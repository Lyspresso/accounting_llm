### Q1 — CORE — Meridian Hardware: fire loss, markup conversion, GP schedule, casualty & insurance JEs
**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Convert markup on cost; gross profit estimation schedule; initial recognition of inventory casualty loss and salvage; insurance settlement JE; presentation of remaining inventory  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Hardware Co.** uses a **periodic** inventory system. On **September 14, Year 1**, a fire destroyed inventory in the main warehouse. Undamaged merchandise that can still be sold was moved to a temporary location and counted at **cost of $52,000**. Accounting records show:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Inventory, September 1 | $540,000 |
| Net purchases, September 1–14 | 295,000 |
| Net sales, September 1–14 | 720,000 |

Past experience shows a consistent **markup of 25% of cost**. Meridian will pursue insurance; recovery of the book inventory loss is considered **probable and reasonably estimable**. On **October 5**, the insurer pays **$195,000** cash as final settlement of the claim (the company had recognized an insurance receivable equal to the expected recovery of **$195,000** on September 14).

**Required:**  
(a) Convert the markup on cost to **gross profit as a percentage of sales**.  
(b) Prepare the **gross profit method schedule** estimating inventory on hand at the fire date (before salvage).  
(c) Compute the **inventory loss** from the fire.  
(d) Prepare the **September 14 journal entry** to reclassify salvage inventory, write off destroyed inventory, and recognize the casualty loss (**before** insurance).  
(e) Prepare the **September 14 journal entry** to recognize the **insurance receivable** for the expected recovery, reducing the net casualty loss.  
(f) Prepare the **October 5 journal entry** for the cash insurance settlement.  
(g) Show the **balance sheet presentation** of inventory immediately after the September 14 entries (before the insurance cash is received).  
(h) State **one use** and **one limitation** of the gross profit method.
