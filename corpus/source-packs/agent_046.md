### Q1 — CORE — Harborline Retail: cash drawer, petty cash life cycle, and bank reconciliation
**LO:** LO 8-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of cash over/short and petty cash; subsequent petty cash replenishment; period-end bank recon schedule and book AJEs  
**Scenario:**  
Harborline Retail Group uses cash drawers, a petty cash fund, and a monthly bank reconciliation as core cash controls. All amounts below are original to this problem.

**Part A — Cash drawer reconciliation (March 15)**  
At the end of a shift, Harborline accumulated cash-drawer counts and POS (point-of-sale) recorded sales:

| Source | Cash drawer count | Sales recorded (POS) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash collected | $12,480.50 | $12,615.20 |
| Checks collected | 3,890.00 | 3,890.00 |
| Credit cards collected | 28,750.00 | 28,750.00 |
| **Total** | **$45,120.50** | **$45,255.20** |

Sales have already been recorded automatically as a debit to Cash and a credit to Sales Revenue from the POS system.

**Part B — Petty cash fund**  
1. On March 1, Harborline establishes a **$1,800** petty cash fund (check written to the custodian; beginning fund balance $0).  
2. On March 31, cash remaining in the fund is **$295**. Vouchers: postage **$520**, office supplies **$480**, delivery **$410**. The fund is replenished to **$1,800**.  
3. On April 1, the fund is increased to **$2,500** because of higher office needs.

**Part C — Bank reconciliation (September 30)**  
Unadjusted bank balance, September 30: **$41,250**  
Unadjusted book (Cash ledger) balance, September 30: **$37,890**

Additional data:  
- Undeposited cash on hand (will be deposited October 1): **$850**  
- Deposits in transit, September 30: **$3,400**  
- Outstanding checks, September 30: **$7,050**  
- Bank collected a note receivable: principal **$1,000**, interest **$50** (not yet on books)  
- NSF check from customer T. Lang returned by bank: **$450**  
- Bank service charges: **$85**  
- Book error: Harborline recorded a check for repairs as **$480** on the books, but the check that cleared was **$360** (correct cash outflow and correct repair cost). Books therefore **overstated cash disbursement and Repair Expense by $120** each. Correction: **debit Cash $120 / credit Repair Expense $120** (no payable — true cost is $360 and is fully settled).  
- After listing book reconciling items above, a **$75** unexplained cash shortage remains that management writes off (Cash Over and Short or Miscellaneous Expense).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the March 15 cash-drawer adjusting entry for Cash Over and Short.  
b. Prepare journal entries for (1) establishing the petty cash fund March 1, (2) replenishing March 31 (including any shortage), and (3) increasing the fund April 1.  
c. Prepare the September 30 bank reconciliation proving a single correct cash balance.  
d. Prepare **all** September 30 adjusting journal entries required from the book side of the reconciliation. Prove the net change in the Cash ledger.  
e. State how the **petty cash fund balance** and **Cash Over and Short** are classified/presented in the financial statements.

**e. Classification / presentation**  
- **Petty Cash** (fund balance $2,500 after April 1) is reported as part of **Cash and cash equivalents** (current assets) on the balance sheet — not a separate noncash asset.  
- **Cash Over and Short** is reported in **other revenues and expenses** on the income statement (debit balance = expense; credit balance = revenue).  
- Bank-side items (DIT, outstanding checks, undeposited cash) are **timing differences** — **no** book AJEs.

**Key insight:** Cash controls produce three recurring journal patterns: (1) Cash Over and Short when drawer/POS amounts differ, (2) expense recognition on **replenishment** of petty cash (not when the custodian spends), and (3) book-only AJEs from the bank reconciliation that bring the Cash ledger to the true ending balance.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Meridian Craft: activity-based bank recon, book AJEs, and closing the petty cash fund
**LO:** LO 8-8  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement via bank recon schedule from activity data; period-end book AJEs; settlement/close of petty cash fund; classification of reconciling items  
**Scenario:**  
Meridian Craft Co. ends its fiscal month on June 30. The accounting department prepares the bank reconciliation (someone other than the cashier). Meridian is also eliminating its petty cash fund in favor of a company card.

**Part A — Bank reconciliation from activity data**  
Unadjusted bank balance, June 30: **$19,840**  
Unadjusted book balance, June 30: **$17,520**

From the **May 31** bank reconciliation:  
- Deposits in transit: **$1,600**  
- Outstanding checks: **$3,200**

June activity:  
| | Bank statement | Company books |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deposits / receipts recorded | $47,200 | $48,000 |
| Checks / disbursements recorded | 48,100 (cleared) | 46,800 |
| Other bank items not on books | see below | — |

Additional June 30 items:  
- Undeposited cash on hand: **$500**  
- Bank collected note: principal **$3,000**, interest **$200**  
- Book error: advertising check of **$1,260** recorded as **$1,620** (books understated cash by **$360**; correct advertising cost is $1,260)  
- NSF check returned: **$180**  
- Bank service charge: **$40**  
- Unexplained cash shortage after other book items: **$20**

Assume the May 31 deposit in transit cleared in June and the May 31 outstanding checks cleared in June.

**Part B — Close petty cash fund (June 30)**  
Petty cash fund balance on books: **$600**.  
Count on June 30: cash **$85**; vouchers for office supplies **$500**.  
Management closes the fund (return remaining cash to the general cash account; remove Petty Cash).

**Required:**  
a. Compute June 30 deposits in transit and outstanding checks from the activity data.  
b. Prepare the complete June 30 bank reconciliation.  
c. Prepare all book adjusting entries from the bank reconciliation.  
d. Prepare the entry (or entries) to record the final petty cash expenses/shortage and **close** the petty cash fund.  
e. Classify each of the following as (i) bank-side timing item — no AJE, (ii) book-side item — AJE required: deposits in transit; outstanding checks; NSF check; bank service charge; note collected by bank; undeposited cash.

**Answer key:**

**a. Deposits in transit and outstanding checks**

**Deposits in transit, June 30:**  
June deposits credited by bank that relate only to June = bank deposits − prior DIT  
= 47,200 − 1,600 = **$45,600**  
DIT June 30 = book deposits − June-only bank deposits  
= 48,000 − 45,600 = **$2,400**

**Outstanding checks, June 30:**  
June checks cleared that relate only to June = bank cleared − prior O/S  
= 48,100 − 3,200 = **$44,900**  
Outstanding checks June 30 = book disbursements − June-only clears  
= 46,800 − 44,900 = **$1,900**

**e. Classification of reconciling items**

| Item | Classification |
|---|---|
| Deposits in transit | (i) Bank-side timing — **no AJE** |
| Outstanding checks | (i) Bank-side timing — **no AJE** |
| Undeposited cash | (i) Bank-side timing — **no AJE** |

| Bank service charge | (ii) Book-side — **AJE required** |
| Note collected by bank | (ii) Book-side — **AJE required** |

**Key insight:** Deposits in transit and outstanding checks are derived by rolling prior-period reconciling items through current-period bank and book activity. Only **book-side** differences adjust the Cash ledger. Closing petty cash removes the Petty Cash asset and settles residual cash and any final expenses/shortages.

### Q4 — MC (classification)
**LO:** LO 8-8  
**Concept:** Classification of bank-reconciling items and cash-control presentation  

**Question 1:**  
In preparing a bank reconciliation, which item is **added to the unadjusted bank balance** and does **not** require a book journal entry?

- A) NSF check returned by the bank  
- B) Bank service charge  
- C) Outstanding checks  
- D) Deposits in transit  

**Answer:** **D.** Deposits in transit are added on the **bank** side as a timing difference; the company already recorded the deposit. NSF checks and service charges require book AJEs that **reduce** cash. Outstanding checks are **deducted** from the bank balance (also no book AJE).

**Question 2:**  
A company establishes a $2,000 petty cash fund. At month-end the fund is replenished; the Petty Cash account balance immediately after a normal replenishment (fund size unchanged) is:

- A) Zero, because all cash was spent  
- B) Equal to the sum of the month’s expense vouchers  
- C) Still $2,000; replenishment debits expenses (and any shortage) and credits Cash, not Petty Cash  
- D) Equal to cash remaining in the drawer before replenishment  

**Answer:** **C.** The Petty Cash control account changes only when the fund is established, increased, decreased, or closed. Replenishment restores the **cash on hand** by writing a check from the general Cash account and records expenses; Petty Cash remains at the authorized fund amount ($2,000).
