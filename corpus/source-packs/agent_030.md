# Agent 30 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-5

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Explain how to record a valuation allowance and report deferred taxes  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Valuation allowance as a **contra asset** to deferred tax assets when it is **more likely than not (>50%)** that some or all of the DTA will not be realized
- Initial recognition JE: Dr Income Tax Expense / Cr Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset
- Period-end remeasurement of the allowance to the amount needed so net DTA equals the benefit more likely than not to be realized
- Multi-year schedule: gross DTA rollforward, target VA, adjustment, net DTA
- Release (debit) of valuation allowance when realizability improves or when the related DTA reverses / settles
- Balance sheet presentation: offset DTA, VA, and DTL **within the same tax-paying component and jurisdiction** into a single **noncurrent** net deferred tax asset or liability
- Classification rule: compare (DTA − VA) to DTL

### Q1 — CORE — Harbor Ridge Appliances: establish, adjust, and release valuation allowance (full life cycle)
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of valuation allowance; subsequent measurement schedule; period-end adjusting JEs; release on full DTA reversal  
**Scenario:**  
**Harbor Ridge Appliances Inc.** accrues product warranties for GAAP purposes before the costs are deductible for tax. The enacted tax rate is **25%** for all years. Accounting and tax years both end December 31. Beginning balances in deferred tax accounts at January 1, Year 1 are **zero**.

| Item | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|------|-------:|-------:|-------:|
| Taxable income | $400,000 | $360,000 | $420,000 |
| Cumulative deductible temporary difference (warranty) at **year-end** | $80,000 | $48,000 | $0 |
| Management’s estimate: portion of **ending gross DTA** more likely than not **not** realizable | $6,000 of DTA (fixed $ amount) | **40%** of ending gross DTA | **0%** (fully realizable / DTA gone) |

Additional facts:
- Year 1 originates the full $80,000 warranty temporary difference.
- Year 2 and Year 3 changes in the cumulative temporary difference arise solely from warranty claims paid (tax-deductible) that reverse prior accruals; no new originating warranty amounts in Years 2–3 beyond what is implied by the ending cumulative balances.
- Harbor Ridge has no deferred tax **liabilities** and no permanent differences.
- Record current tax, deferred tax (gross DTA), and valuation allowance effects for each year-end.

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **gross deferred tax asset** ending balance for Years 1–3. Prepare a **three-year schedule** of: beginning gross DTA, change in DTA, ending gross DTA, beginning VA, **target** ending VA, VA adjustment (Dr/Cr), ending VA, and **net** DTA.  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 1** income tax journal entry(ies), including initial recognition of the valuation allowance.  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 2** period-end adjusting entry for income taxes (current, deferred DTA change, and VA adjustment).  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 3** entry when the temporary difference fully reverses and the valuation allowance is released.  
e. State the **net noncurrent deferred tax asset** reported on the balance sheet at December 31 of Year 1 and Year 2.

**Answer key:**

**a. Gross DTA and valuation allowance schedule**

Gross DTA = cumulative deductible temporary difference × 25%.

| Year | Beg. gross DTA | Change in DTA | End. gross DTA | Beg. VA | Target end VA | VA adjustment | End. VA | Net DTA (End) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | +20,000 | **20,000** | 0 | **6,000** | Cr **6,000** | 6,000 | **14,000** |
| 2 | 20,000 | −8,000 | **12,000** | 6,000 | 12,000 × 40% = **4,800** | Dr **1,200** | 4,800 | **7,200** |
| 3 | 12,000 | −12,000 | **0** | 4,800 | **0** | Dr **4,800** | 0 | **0** |

Checks:  
Year 1 DTA: $80,000 × 25% = **$20,000**.  
Year 2 DTA: $48,000 × 25% = **$12,000**; decrease = $20,000 − $12,000 = **$8,000**.  
Year 3 DTA: $0; decrease = **$12,000**.

**b. December 31, Year 1 — initial recognition (current + DTA + VA)**

Current tax payable = $400,000 × 25% = **$100,000**.  
Deferred tax benefit (gross DTA increase) = **$20,000**.  
Valuation allowance increase = **$6,000** (increases income tax expense).  
Total income tax expense = $100,000 − $20,000 + $6,000 = **$86,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 86,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 20,000 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | | 6,000 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 100,000 |
| *To record Year 1 current tax, recognize DTA on warranty TD, and establish valuation allowance* | | |

**Check:** Dr 86,000 + 20,000 = **106,000**; Cr 6,000 + 100,000 = **106,000**. Balanced.

Alternatively as two entries (also acceptable):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 80,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 20,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 100,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 6,000 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | | 6,000 |

**c. December 31, Year 2 — period-end adjustment**

Current tax payable = $360,000 × 25% = **$90,000**.  
Gross DTA decrease = **$8,000** → deferred tax expense.  
VA decrease = **$1,200** → reduces tax expense (benefit).  
Total income tax expense = $90,000 + $8,000 − $1,200 = **$96,800**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 96,800 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | 1,200 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 8,000 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 90,000 |
| *To record Year 2 current tax, reverse portion of DTA, and reduce VA to $4,800 target* | | |

**Check:** Dr 96,800 + 1,200 = **98,000**; Cr 8,000 + 90,000 = **98,000**. Balanced.  
Ending VA T-account: 6,000 − 1,200 = **4,800**. Ending net DTA = 12,000 − 4,800 = **7,200**.

**d. December 31, Year 3 — full reversal / settlement of DTA and VA**

Current tax payable = $420,000 × 25% = **$105,000**.  
Gross DTA decrease = **$12,000**.  
VA released in full = **$4,800**.  
Total income tax expense = $105,000 + $12,000 − $4,800 = **$112,200**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 112,200 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | 4,800 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 12,000 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 105,000 |
| *To record Year 3 current tax, eliminate remaining DTA on full warranty reversal, and release VA* | | |

**Check:** Dr 112,200 + 4,800 = **117,000**; Cr 12,000 + 105,000 = **117,000**. Balanced.  
Ending gross DTA = 0; ending VA = 0.

**e. Balance sheet presentation (net)**  
December 31, Year 1: **Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net = $14,000** ($20,000 − $6,000).  
December 31, Year 2: **Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net = $7,200** ($12,000 − $4,800).  
December 31, Year 3: **$0** (no deferred taxes).

**Key insight:** The valuation allowance is a **contra asset** adjusted each period so the **net** DTA equals the amount **more likely than not** to be realized. Establishing or increasing the allowance increases income tax expense; decreasing or releasing it decreases tax expense. When the related deductible temporary difference fully reverses, both the gross DTA and any remaining allowance are cleared.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Cedar Peak Tools: DTA increase with partial valuation allowance
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition of DTA and valuation allowance; one-period subsequent VA remeasurement  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedar Peak Tools LLC** has a deductible temporary difference from accrued litigation (recognized for GAAP; not yet deductible for tax). Enacted tax rate is **21%**. Years end December 31. Deferred tax accounts begin at **zero** on January 1, Year 1.

| Item | Amount |
|------|-------:|
| Year 1 taxable income | $1,200,000 |
| Year 1 ending cumulative deductible temporary difference (litigation) | $200,000 |
| Year 1: portion of ending gross DTA more likely than not **not** realized | **25%** of ending gross DTA |
| Year 2 taxable income | $950,000 |
| Year 2 ending cumulative deductible temporary difference | $140,000 |
| Year 2: portion of ending gross DTA more likely than not **not** realized | **10%** of ending gross DTA |

No deferred tax liabilities. No permanent differences.

**Required:**  
a. Compute Year 1 and Year 2 **gross DTA**, **target valuation allowance**, and **net DTA**.  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 1** combined income tax entry (current, DTA, VA).  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 2** combined income tax entry (current, DTA change, VA adjustment).  
d. Prepare a two-year rollforward of the Valuation Allowance account (beg, adjustments, end).

**Answer key:**

**a. Computations**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gross DTA (TD × 21%) | $200,000 × 21% = **$42,000** | $140,000 × 21% = **$29,400** |
| Target VA | 25% × 42,000 = **$10,500** | 10% × 29,400 = **$2,940** |
| Net DTA | 42,000 − 10,500 = **$31,500** | 29,400 − 2,940 = **$26,460** |
| Change in gross DTA | +42,000 (from 0) | 29,400 − 42,000 = **−12,600** |
| VA adjustment needed | Cr **10,500** (from 0) | Target 2,940 − beg 10,500 → Dr **7,560** |

**b. December 31, Year 1**

Current payable = $1,200,000 × 21% = **$252,000**.  
Tax expense = $252,000 − $42,000 + $10,500 = **$220,500**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 220,500 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 42,000 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | | 10,500 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 252,000 |

**Check:** Dr 220,500 + 42,000 = **262,500**; Cr 10,500 + 252,000 = **262,500**. Balanced.

**c. December 31, Year 2**

Current payable = $950,000 × 21% = **$199,500**.  
DTA decrease = **$12,600** (deferred expense).  
VA decrease = **$7,560** (deferred benefit).  
Tax expense = $199,500 + $12,600 − $7,560 = **$204,540**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 204,540 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | 7,560 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 12,600 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 199,500 |

**Check:** Dr 204,540 + 7,560 = **212,100**; Cr 12,600 + 199,500 = **212,100**. Balanced.

**d. Valuation Allowance rollforward**

| Date | Debit | Credit | Balance (Cr) |
|------|------:|-------:|-------------:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | — | — | 0 |
| Dec 31, Y1 establish | — | 10,500 | 10,500 |
| Dec 31, Y2 adjust | 7,560 | — | **2,940** |

**Key insight:** Same measurement logic as Q1 with different rate and percentages: always set VA so ending net DTA = gross DTA × (1 − unrealizable %), and plug the **change** in VA against income tax expense.

### Q3 — CORE — Balance sheet classification and net presentation of deferred taxes
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Classification / presentation — offset DTA, valuation allowance, and DTL into one noncurrent net amount  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Forge Industrial Corp.** has a single tax-paying component in one U.S. jurisdiction. At December 31, Year 4, temporary differences and related tax effects (enacted rate **25%**) are:

| Temporary difference | Future (taxable)/deductible amount | Deferred tax effect |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------:|--------------------:|
| Excess tax depreciation (MACRS > SL) | Taxable $180,000 | DTL **$45,000** |
| Installment gross profit (accrual vs cash) | Taxable $60,000 | DTL **$15,000** |
| Warranty accrual | Deductible $100,000 | DTA **$25,000** |
| Bad debt allowance (allowance method vs write-offs) | Deductible $40,000 | DTA **$10,000** |

Management concludes it is more likely than not that **$12,000** of the **gross deferred tax assets** will not be realized (valuation allowance required = $12,000). There are no beginning balances needed for the presentation question (use ending amounts only).

**Required:**  
a. Compute total **gross DTA**, total **DTL**, valuation allowance, and the **single noncurrent amount** reported on the classified balance sheet. State whether the line is a net deferred tax **asset** or **liability**.  
b. Assume instead the valuation allowance is **$28,000** (more negative evidence). Recompute the net amount and classification.  
c. **Period-end adjusting JE only for the valuation allowance:** On January 1, Year 4 the valuation allowance was **$8,000**. At December 31, Year 4 the required allowance is **$12,000** (part a). Gross DTA and DTL accounts have already been adjusted to the balances above through other tax entries. Record the **valuation allowance adjusting entry only**.  
d. Briefly state the ASC 740 **offset rule** (same component / same jurisdiction; noncurrent presentation).

**Answer key:**

**a. Base case (VA = $12,000)**

Gross DTA = $25,000 + $10,000 = **$35,000**.  
Gross DTL = $45,000 + $15,000 = **$60,000**.  
DTA net of VA = $35,000 − $12,000 = **$23,000**.  
Compare: DTL $60,000 **>** net DTA $23,000 →  
**Noncurrent deferred tax liability, net = $60,000 − $23,000 = $37,000**.

**b. Alternative (VA = $28,000)**

Net DTA = $35,000 − $28,000 = **$7,000**.  
**Noncurrent deferred tax liability, net = $60,000 − $7,000 = $53,000**.  
(Still a net liability; larger allowance increases the net DTL reported.)

**c. VA adjusting entry only (increase $4,000)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 4,000 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | | 4,000 |
| *To increase valuation allowance from $8,000 to $12,000 target* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 4,000. Balanced.

**d. Offset / presentation rule**  
For a **particular tax-paying component** within a **particular tax jurisdiction**, all deferred tax assets, deferred tax liabilities, and any related valuation allowance are **offset and presented as a single noncurrent amount**. Do **not** offset amounts attributable to different components or different jurisdictions.

**Key insight:** Presentation is always **net** after the valuation allowance. Classification depends on whether (DTA − VA) exceeds DTL (net asset) or DTL is larger (net liability)—never report gross DTA and gross DTL separately for the same component/jurisdiction under current U.S. GAAP classified BS rules.

### Q4 — MC — Net deferred tax classification
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Classification of net deferred taxes after valuation allowance  

**Question 1:**  
Pine River Media has deferred tax assets of $90,000, a valuation allowance of $30,000, and deferred tax liabilities of $40,000 (same component and jurisdiction). How should deferred taxes appear on a classified balance sheet?

- A) Noncurrent deferred tax asset $90,000 and noncurrent deferred tax liability $40,000  
- B) Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net $50,000  
- C) Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net $20,000  
- D) Noncurrent deferred tax liability, net $40,000  

**Answer:** C.  
Net DTA = $90,000 − $30,000 = $60,000. DTL = $40,000. Because net DTA > DTL, report a single **noncurrent deferred tax asset, net = $60,000 − $40,000 = $20,000**. Gross presentation (A) is incorrect; ignoring the liability (B) or ignoring the net asset (D) is incorrect.

**Question 2:**  
Which journal entry correctly **establishes** a valuation allowance of $15,000 against a deferred tax asset?

- A) Debit Deferred Tax Asset $15,000; Credit Income Tax Expense $15,000  
- B) Debit Income Tax Expense $15,000; Credit Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset $15,000  
- C) Debit Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset $15,000; Credit Deferred Tax Asset $15,000  
- D) Debit Income Tax Payable $15,000; Credit Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset $15,000  

**Answer:** B.  
The valuation allowance is a **contra asset**; establishing it increases **income tax expense** and credits **Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset**. It does not write off the gross DTA directly (C), does not create a DTA (A), and does not affect Income Tax Payable (D).
