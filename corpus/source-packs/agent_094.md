# Agent 94 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-1

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Record accounts payable and sales taxes payable  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition** of accounts payable from supplier purchases (gross method, perpetual inventory)
- **Cash purchase discounts** (terms such as 2/10, n/30) reduce Inventory (and cash paid) when taken under the gross method
- **Freight-in** paid by the buyer added to Inventory cost at purchase
- **Sales taxes payable** — company acts as agent for taxing authorities; liability between collection and remittance
- Record sales taxes **separately at the point of sale** vs reclassify via **period-end adjusting entry**
- Gross-up when tax is **embedded** in total cash/AR collected: Sales = Total ÷ (1 + tax rate); tax = Total − Sales
- **Settlement** of AP (payment to supplier) and **remittance** of sales taxes (payment to tax authority)
- **Subsequent measurement / rollforward** of AP and Sales Taxes Payable balances
- **Classification / presentation:** AP and Sales Taxes Payable are **current liabilities** (liquidation with current assets or other current liabilities)

### Q1 — CORE — AP purchase (gross method) + sales taxes at point of sale + settlement/remittance
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for accounts payable and sales taxes payable; AP payment within discount period; customer collection; remittance of sales taxes; liability rollforward schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverbend Outfitters Inc.** uses a **perpetual** inventory system and the **gross method** for purchases. The company’s policy is to **record sales taxes payable separately with each sale**. Fiscal year-end is December 31. Selected March–April 2026 activity:

| Date | Transaction |
|---|---|
| Mar 1 | Purchased merchandise for resale on account. Invoice price **$48,000**; terms **2/10, n/45**. Buyer paid freight-in in cash **$720** the same day (FOB shipping point; control passes at shipment). |
| Mar 5 | Week’s sales: merchandise **cost $12,000**, selling price **$30,000** (before tax), sales tax rate **7%**. Of total amounts billed/collected from customers (sales + tax), **40% cash**, **60% on account** (n/30). |
| Mar 9 | Paid the Mar 1 supplier invoice **in full** within the discount period. |
| Mar 28 | Collected **all** Mar 5 credit sales from customers. |
| Apr 15 | Remitted **all** sales taxes related to the Mar 5 sales to the state taxing authority. |

Beginning balances on Mar 1 (before the purchase): Accounts Payable **$0**; Sales Taxes Payable **$0**.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **Mar 1** purchase (inventory + AP) and freight-in (initial recognition of AP).  
b. Record the **Mar 5** sales (including sales taxes payable) and cost of goods sold.  
c. Record the **Mar 9** payment to the supplier (settlement of AP within discount period).  
d. Record the **Mar 28** customer collection and the **Apr 15** remittance of sales taxes (settlement of sales taxes payable).  
e. Prepare a **subsequent measurement / rollforward schedule** for Accounts Payable and Sales Taxes Payable from Mar 1 through Apr 15.  
f. Classify Accounts Payable and Sales Taxes Payable on the balance sheet (current vs noncurrent) and briefly support.

**Answer key:**

**a. Mar 1, 2026 — initial recognition of inventory purchase and AP**  
Inventory cost = invoice + freight-in = $48,000 + $720 = **$48,720**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 48,720 | |
| Cash | | 720 |
| Accounts Payable | | 48,000 |
| *Purchase on account (gross method); freight-in capitalized to inventory* | | |

**Check:** Dr = **48,720**; Cr 720 + 48,000 = **48,720**. Balanced.

**b. Mar 5, 2026 — sales and sales taxes payable at point of sale; COGS**  
Sales tax = $30,000 × 0.07 = **$2,100**  
Total customer billings = $30,000 + $2,100 = **$32,100**  
Cash portion = $32,100 × 0.40 = **$12,840**  
Accounts receivable = $32,100 × 0.60 = **$19,260**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 12,840 | |
| Accounts Receivable | 19,260 | |
| Sales | | 30,000 |
| Sales Taxes Payable | | 2,100 |
| *Record sales and sales taxes payable separately at point of sale* | | |

**Check:** Dr 12,840 + 19,260 = **32,100**; Cr 30,000 + 2,100 = **32,100**. Balanced.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 12,000 | |
| Inventory | | 12,000 |
| *Perpetual COGS for merchandise sold* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **12,000**. Balanced.

**c. Mar 9, 2026 — settlement of AP within discount period (gross method)**  
Purchase discount = $48,000 × 0.02 = **$960**  
Cash paid = $48,000 × 0.98 = **$47,040**  
(Under the gross method, the discount reduces Inventory.)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Payable | 48,000 | |
| Inventory | | 960 |
| Cash | | 47,040 |
| *Pay supplier within 2/10; reduce inventory for cash discount taken* | | |

**Check:** Dr = **48,000**; Cr 960 + 47,040 = **48,000**. Balanced.

**d. Mar 28 and Apr 15 — settlement of AR and sales taxes payable**

*Mar 28 — collection from customers*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 19,260 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 19,260 |
| *Collect Mar 5 credit sales (including tax portion billed)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **19,260**. Balanced.

*Apr 15 — remittance to taxing authority*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Taxes Payable | 2,100 | |
| Cash | | 2,100 |
| *Remit sales taxes collected; settle liability to tax authority* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **2,100**. Balanced.

**e. Subsequent measurement — liability rollforward schedule**

**Accounts Payable**

| Date | Description | Debit (settle) | Credit (recognize) | Balance |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1 open | Beginning | | | $0 |
| Mar 1 | Purchase on account | | 48,000 | 48,000 |
| Mar 9 | Payment (full settlement) | 48,000 | | 0 |
| Apr 15 close | Ending | | | **$0** |

**Sales Taxes Payable**

| Date | Description | Debit (settle) | Credit (recognize) | Balance |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1 open | Beginning | | | $0 |
| Mar 5 | Point-of-sale tax accrual | | 2,100 | 2,100 |
| Apr 15 | Remittance to tax authority | 2,100 | | 0 |
| Apr 15 close | Ending | | | **$0** |

Rollforwards close at zero after settlement/remittance; no interest accretion is required for ordinary short-term trade AP or sales taxes payable (ASC 835-30 scope exception for normal supplier payables due within one year).

**f. Classification / presentation**  
Both **Accounts Payable** and **Sales Taxes Payable** are **current liabilities**. They are present obligations reasonably expected to be liquidated with current assets (cash) or by creating other current liabilities within the operating cycle / one year. Sales taxes payable is an agency liability until remitted; it is not revenue.

**Key insight:** At **initial recognition**, trade purchases create AP at invoice face (gross method), and sales create an **agency** liability for taxes collected. **Settlement** clears AP (often with a cash discount that reduces Inventory under the gross method) and clears Sales Taxes Payable when remitted to the taxing authority—not when the customer pays the retailer.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — AP + sales taxes twin (all numbers changed)

**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition of AP and sales taxes payable; discount settlement; remittance; rollforward  
**Scenario:**

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end sales-tax adjusting entry; tax-included total; AP paid after discount window
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE to reclassify sales taxes from Sales; embedded-tax gross-up; settlement of AP after discount period (no discount); classification of current operating liabilities  
**Scenario:**  
**Northlake Provisions LLC** uses a perpetual inventory system and the **gross method**. Unlike Riverbend, Northlake’s policy is to **not** record sales taxes payable at the point of sale. Cash and receivables are recorded for the **total** amount received/billed; at month-end an **adjusting entry** isolates Sales Taxes Payable by reducing Sales.

| Date | Fact |
|---|---|
| Nov 2 | Purchased inventory on account: invoice **$22,000**, terms **2/10, n/30**. No separate freight. |
| Nov 6 | Cash sales for the day: customers paid **$10,600** total (this amount **includes** 6% sales tax). Cost of goods sold **$6,400**. Per policy, the entire **$10,600** was credited to **Sales** (no tax liability recorded yet). |
| Nov 18 | Paid the Nov 2 supplier invoice **in full** (**after** the discount period). |
| Nov 30 | Month-end: prepare the **adjusting entry** to reclassify the sales-tax portion of the Nov 6 cash sales from Sales to Sales Taxes Payable. (No other taxable sales in November.) |
| Dec 12 | Remitted November sales taxes to the state. |

**Required:**  
a. Nov 2 purchase JE (initial recognition of AP).  
b. Nov 6 cash sale and COGS JEs as recorded under company policy (tax **not** broken out).  
c. Nov 18 settlement of AP (no discount).  
d. Nov 30 **period-end adjusting JE** for sales taxes payable; show the computational schedule (total ÷ 1.06).  
e. Dec 12 remittance JE.  
f. Classification: which statement line items report (1) the Nov 2 obligation before payment and (2) the sales-tax agency obligation after the adjusting entry?  
g. Optional comparison: if Northlake **had** recorded tax at the point of sale on Nov 6, what would the Nov 6 sales entry have been, and what would the Nov 30 adjusting entry be?

**Answer key:**

**a. Nov 2 — initial recognition of AP**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 22,000 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 22,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **22,000**. Balanced.

**b. Nov 6 — cash sale recorded gross into Sales (policy); COGS**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 10,600 | |
| Sales | | 10,600 |
| *Total collections credited to Sales; tax not yet isolated* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **10,600**. Balanced.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 6,400 | |
| Inventory | | 6,400 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **6,400**. Balanced.

**c. Nov 18 — settlement of AP after discount window**  
Discount window expired; pay full **$22,000** (no inventory reduction for discount).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Payable | 22,000 | |
| Cash | | 22,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **22,000**. Balanced.

**d. Nov 30 — period-end adjusting JE (sales taxes reclassification)**  

**Computational schedule (embedded tax)**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total cash collected (includes tax) | $10,600 |
| ÷ (1 + tax rate) = ÷ 1.06 | |
| = **Sales revenue (net of tax)** | **$10,000** |
| Sales taxes payable = $10,600 − $10,000 | **$600** |
| Check: $10,000 × 0.06 | **$600** |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales | 600 | |
| Sales Taxes Payable | | 600 |
| *Reclassify sales-tax portion out of Sales into current liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **600**. Balanced.  
After adjustment: Sales revenue recognized = $10,600 − $600 = **$10,000**; Sales Taxes Payable = **$600**.

**e. Dec 12 — remittance (settlement of sales taxes payable)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Taxes Payable | 600 | |
| Cash | | 600 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **600**. Balanced.

**f. Classification / presentation**  
(1) The Nov 2 supplier obligation is **Accounts Payable — current liability** until paid Nov 18.  
(2) After the Nov 30 adjusting entry, the **$600** is **Sales Taxes Payable — current liability** (agency obligation to the tax authority), not revenue and not a long-term liability.

**g. Comparison — point-of-sale method (what Q1 used)**  
If tax had been recorded at sale:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 10,600 | |
| Sales | | 10,000 |
| Sales Taxes Payable | | 600 |

**Check:** 10,600 = 10,000 + 600. Balanced.  
Nov 30 adjusting entry under that policy: **none** for this sale (liability already correct). Remittance Dec 12 is identical.

**Key insight:** Two acceptable **initial recognition** paths exist for sales taxes: (1) credit Sales Taxes Payable at each sale, or (2) book the gross total to Sales and use a **period-end adjusting JE** to move the tax portion into Sales Taxes Payable. Either way, Sales should end at the pre-tax amount and the **current liability** must equal taxes owed until remittance. AP settlement after the discount period simply clears the full face amount with cash—no Inventory credit for a discount.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of AP and sales taxes payable
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Classification / presentation — current liability nature of accounts payable and sales taxes payable; agency role for sales taxes  
**Question:**  
Which statement best describes the balance-sheet classification and nature of **accounts payable** arising from ordinary inventory purchases due in 30 days and **sales taxes payable** collected from customers but not yet remitted?

- A) Accounts payable is a current liability; sales taxes payable is deferred revenue (contract liability) until remitted.  
- B) Both are current liabilities; sales taxes payable is an agency obligation to the taxing authority, not revenue of the seller.  
- C) Both are noncurrent liabilities because the exact remittance date to suppliers and tax authorities may extend beyond the operating cycle.  
- D) Accounts payable is recorded at present value of future cash flows; sales taxes payable is an equity appropriation until paid.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Ordinary trade accounts payable due within the operating cycle/year are **current liabilities** measured at settlement/face amount (no separate interest for normal short-term supplier payables). Sales taxes collected are held as an **agent** for the taxing authority and reported as **Sales Taxes Payable (current liability)** until remitted—they are **not** the seller’s revenue and are not deferred revenue from customers for goods/services.

### Q4b — MC — Measurement and discount accounting under the gross method
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Classification of method — gross-method treatment when a purchase discount is taken  
**Question:**  
Under the **gross method** in a perpetual system, a company records a $40,000 inventory purchase on account (terms 2/10, n/30) by debiting Inventory and crediting Accounts Payable for $40,000. If the company pays within 10 days and takes the 2% discount, the payment entry:

- A) Debits Accounts Payable $40,000; credits Purchase Discounts (revenue) $800; credits Cash $39,200.  
- B) Debits Accounts Payable $39,200; credits Cash $39,200 only (AP was initially recorded net).  
- C) Debits Accounts Payable $40,000; credits Inventory $800; credits Cash $39,200.  
- D) Debits Accounts Payable $40,000; debits Interest Expense $800; credits Cash $40,800.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Gross method records AP at **invoice face**. When the cash discount is taken, AP is removed at face, Cash is paid at the reduced amount, and the discount **reduces Inventory** (not revenue). Option A incorrectly treats the discount as revenue; B describes the **net method** initial measurement; D invents interest on a normal short-term trade payable.
