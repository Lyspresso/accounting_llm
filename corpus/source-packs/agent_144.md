# Agent 144 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-3

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for Bonds Issued at Face Value  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** when stated rate = market rate, Cash = Bonds Payable at face; no discount or premium
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** carrying value remains at face every period; interest expense equals cash coupon (amortization always zero)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** accrue Interest Payable when fiscal year-end falls between interest dates (face bonds: expense = stated interest × fraction of period)
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JE:** pay face cash and derecognize Bonds Payable; settle final coupon separately if not already recorded
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** noncurrent Bonds Payable until within one year of maturity; reclassify current maturities; total interest expense over term equals total cash interest paid
- **Number-variant twin:** same face-value path with all amounts, rates, and terms changed

### Q1 — CORE — Face-value bonds: issuance, full subsequent measurement schedule, interest payments, maturity
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of bonds issued at face value; subsequent measurement schedule (constant carrying value; interest expense = cash interest); settlement at maturity  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Products Co.** (calendar-year) authorizes and issues **$800,000** face of **6%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1**. The **market rate** for similar debt on the issue date is also **6%**, so the bonds sell at **face value**. The bonds mature on **December 31, Year 5** (5-year term) and pay cash interest **semiannually on June 30 and December 31**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition journal entry**.  
b. Compute the **semiannual cash interest** payment and the **total interest expense** over the entire bond term.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (interest / carrying-value) schedule** for **all 10 semiannual periods**. Columns: period, beginning carrying value, interest expense, cash interest, amortization of discount/premium, ending carrying value.  
d. Prepare the **June 30, Year 1** interest-payment journal entry (same form each payment date).  
e. Prepare the **December 31, Year 5 maturity (derecognition) journal entry**. Assume the final semiannual interest payment has already been recorded.  
f. State the **balance-sheet carrying amount** of the bonds at December 31, Year 1, Year 3, and immediately before maturity (after the final interest entry but before principal repayment).

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition at face value**

Stated rate = market rate ⇒ present value of principal and interest = face ⇒ proceeds = **$800,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 800,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 800,000 |
| *Issue $800,000 face bonds at par (stated = market = 6%)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 800,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Semiannual cash interest and total interest expense**

\[
\text{Semiannual cash interest} = \$800{,}000 \times 6\% \times \tfrac{1}{2} = \$800{,}000 \times 3\% = \mathbf{\$24{,}000}
\]

Number of semiannual periods = 5 years × 2 = **10**.

\[
\text{Total interest expense over term} = 10 \times \$24{,}000 = \mathbf{\$240{,}000}
\]

(Equals total cash interest paid; no discount or premium amortization.)

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — face-value bonds (emphasis)**

Cash interest each period = **$24,000**. Interest expense each period = stated semiannual rate × beginning carrying value = 3% × $800,000 = **$24,000**. Amortization always **$0**. Carrying value remains **$800,000** through maturity.

| Period | Dates | Beg. carrying value | Interest expense | Cash interest | Amort. (disc./prem.) | End. carrying value |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Jan 1–Jun 30, Y1 | $800,000 | $24,000 | $24,000 | $0 | $800,000 |
| 2 | Jul 1–Dec 31, Y1 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| 3 | Jan 1–Jun 30, Y2 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| 4 | Jul 1–Dec 31, Y2 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| 5 | Jan 1–Jun 30, Y3 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| 6 | Jul 1–Dec 31, Y3 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| 7 | Jan 1–Jun 30, Y4 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| 8 | Jul 1–Dec 31, Y4 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| 9 | Jan 1–Jun 30, Y5 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| 10 | Jul 1–Dec 31, Y5 | 800,000 | 24,000 | 24,000 | 0 | 800,000 |
| **Totals** | | | **$240,000** | **$240,000** | **$0** | |

**Schedule checks:**  
- Each period: interest expense $24,000 − cash interest $24,000 = amort **$0**; end CV = beg CV + 0 = **$800,000**.  
- Sum of interest expense = sum of cash interest = **$240,000**.  
- End CV after period 10 (before principal payment) = face = **$800,000**.

**d. June 30, Year 1 — interest payment (identical form each coupon date)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 24,000 | |
| Cash | | 24,000 |
| *Semiannual cash interest: $800,000 × 3%* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 24,000. **Balanced.**

(Same entry on Dec 31 Year 1 and every subsequent interest date through Dec 31 Year 5.)

**e. December 31, Year 5 — maturity / settlement of principal**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 800,000 | |
| Cash | | 800,000 |
| *Pay face at maturity; derecognize Bonds Payable* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 800,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Carrying amounts**

| Date | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|
| December 31, Year 1 | **$800,000** |
| December 31, Year 3 | **$800,000** |
| Immediately before principal repayment Dec 31, Year 5 | **$800,000** |

**Key insight:** When bonds are issued at **face value**, subsequent measurement is simple: **interest expense always equals the cash coupon**, amortization is zero, and the **carrying amount never moves** from face until cash settlement of principal at maturity.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Annual coupon face-value twin (schedule + life-cycle JEs)
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — bonds issued at face with **annual** interest; subsequent measurement schedule; interest JE; maturity settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborcrest Logistics Inc.** (calendar-year) issues **$1,500,000** of **5%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1** at **face value** (market rate also **5%**). Cash interest is paid **annually on December 31**. The bonds mature on **December 31, Year 8** (8-year term).

**Required:**  
a. **January 1, Year 1** issuance journal entry.  
b. Annual cash interest amount and total interest expense over the 8-year term.  
c. **Subsequent measurement schedule** for **all 8 annual periods** (beg CV, interest expense, cash interest, amort., end CV).  
d. **December 31, Year 1** interest-payment journal entry.  
e. **December 31, Year 8** combined entry for **final interest** and **principal maturity** (or two separate balanced entries).  
f. Verify: total interest expense over life equals total cash interest paid.

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — issuance at face**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 1,500,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 1,500,000 |
| *Issue $1,500,000 face bonds at par (stated = market = 5%)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,500,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Annual cash interest and lifetime totals**

\[
\text{Annual cash interest} = \$1{,}500{,}000 \times 5\% = \mathbf{\$75{,}000}
\]

\[
\text{Total interest expense (8 years)} = 8 \times \$75{,}000 = \mathbf{\$600{,}000}
\]

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — annual face-value bonds**

| Period (Year) | Beg. CV | Interest expense | Cash interest | Amort. | End. CV |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $1,500,000 | $75,000 | $75,000 | $0 | $1,500,000 |
| 2 | 1,500,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 | 1,500,000 |
| 3 | 1,500,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 | 1,500,000 |
| 4 | 1,500,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 | 1,500,000 |
| 5 | 1,500,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 | 1,500,000 |
| 6 | 1,500,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 | 1,500,000 |
| 7 | 1,500,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 | 1,500,000 |
| 8 | 1,500,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 | 1,500,000 |
| **Totals** | | **$600,000** | **$600,000** | **$0** | |

**Checks:** Each year IE = $1,500,000 × 5% = $75,000 = cash; amort = 0; CV constant at face.

**d. December 31, Year 1 — interest payment**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 75,000 | |
| Cash | | 75,000 |
| *Annual cash interest at stated rate* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 75,000. **Balanced.**

**e. December 31, Year 8 — final interest and maturity**

*Final interest:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 75,000 | |
| Cash | | 75,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 75,000. **Balanced.**

*Principal settlement:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 1,500,000 | |
| Cash | | 1,500,000 |
| *Derecognize bonds at face on maturity* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,500,000. **Balanced.**

*(Combined form also acceptable: Dr Interest Expense 75,000, Dr Bonds Payable 1,500,000; Cr Cash 1,575,000.)*

**f. Lifetime equality**

Total interest expense **$600,000** = total cash interest paid **$600,000**. For face-value bonds, this equality always holds (LO 16-3 overview: total interest expense = total cash interest payments over the bond term).

**Key insight:** Changing face, rate, term, and payment frequency does not change the face-value pattern: **schedule is flat**, amortization is zero, and maturity always retires **face** for cash equal to face.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Year-end accrual, payment after accrual, reclassification, maturity
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE for face-value bonds when interest dates do not fall on year-end; subsequent cash settlement of Interest Payable; current-maturity reclassification; maturity settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Ridgeway Metals Corp.** (calendar-year fiscal year ending **December 31**) issues **$400,000** of **4%** bonds on **October 1, Year 1** at **face value** (market rate also **4%**). Interest is payable **semiannually on April 1 and October 1**. The bonds mature on **October 1, Year 5** (4-year term). No reversing entries are used.

**Required:**  
a. **October 1, Year 1** issuance journal entry.  
b. **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting entry** for accrued bond interest (3 months).  
c. **April 1, Year 2** journal entry for the first full semiannual cash interest payment (clear Interest Payable and record remaining interest expense).  
d. Compute the **semiannual cash interest** and complete a **partial subsequent measurement schedule** for the first **three** full semiannual periods (Oct 1–Apr 1, Apr 1–Oct 1, Oct 1–Apr 1), showing that CV stays at face.  
e. **December 31, Year 4** reclassification entry for **current maturities of long-term debt** (bonds mature within one year on Oct 1, Year 5).  
f. **October 1, Year 5** maturity entry for principal (assume the final interest payment of $8,000 was recorded the same day in a separate entry).  
g. **Presentation:** how the bonds appear on the December 31, Year 1 and December 31, Year 4 balance sheets (current vs noncurrent), and state Year 1 interest expense on the income statement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. October 1, Year 1 — issuance at face**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 400,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 400,000 |
| *Issue $400,000 face bonds at par* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 400,000. **Balanced.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE (accrual)**

Accrued interest for Oct 1–Dec 31 = 3 months:

\[
\$400{,}000 \times 4\% \times \tfrac{3}{12} = \mathbf{\$4{,}000}
\]

(Equivalently: semiannual cash interest $8,000 × 3/6 = $4,000.)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 4,000 | |
| Interest Payable | | 4,000 |
| *Accrue 3 months of stated interest on face-value bonds* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 4,000. **Balanced.**

**c. April 1, Year 2 — first semiannual cash interest payment**

Semiannual cash interest = $400,000 × 4% × 1/2 = **$8,000**.  
Of that amount, $4,000 was accrued at Dec 31 Year 1; remaining 3 months (Jan–Mar) = **$4,000** interest expense.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 4,000 | |
| Interest Payable | 4,000 | |
| Cash | | 8,000 |
| *Pay $8,000 coupon; clear accrual; expense remaining half-period* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 + 4,000 = Cr 8,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Semiannual cash interest and partial subsequent measurement schedule**

Semiannual cash interest = **$8,000**. Interest expense per full half-year = **$8,000** (face × 2%). Amortization = **$0**.

| Period | Interval | Beg. CV | Interest expense | Cash interest | Amort. | End. CV |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Oct 1 Y1 – Apr 1 Y2 | $400,000 | $8,000 | $8,000 | $0 | $400,000 |
| 2 | Apr 1 Y2 – Oct 1 Y2 | 400,000 | 8,000 | 8,000 | 0 | 400,000 |
| 3 | Oct 1 Y2 – Apr 1 Y3 | 400,000 | 8,000 | 8,000 | 0 | 400,000 |

**Note on Period 1 cash vs expense timing:** Economic interest expense for the full half-year is still $8,000 ($4,000 in Y1 + $4,000 in Y2), matching the schedule; the **cash** coupon of $8,000 is paid on Apr 1 Y2. Carrying value is unaffected by accruals.

**e. December 31, Year 4 — reclassify current maturity**

Bonds mature October 1, Year 5 ⇒ entire **$400,000** face is due within one year of Dec 31, Year 4.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable (Long-Term) | 400,000 | |
| Current Maturities of Long-Term Debt (or Bonds Payable—Current) | | 400,000 |
| *Reclassify face due within one year as current liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 400,000. **Balanced.**

(Also accrue 3 months interest Dec 31 Year 4: same $4,000 pattern as part b — Interest Expense / Interest Payable.)

**f. October 1, Year 5 — maturity settlement of principal**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current Maturities of Long-Term Debt (Bonds Payable) | 400,000 | |
| Cash | | 400,000 |
| *Pay face at maturity* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 400,000. **Balanced.**

**g. Classification / presentation**

| Balance sheet date | Presentation of $400,000 face |
|---|---|
| December 31, Year 1 | **Noncurrent** liability (Bonds Payable); maturity is more than one year away. Also report **Interest Payable $4,000** as a **current** liability. |
| December 31, Year 4 | **Current** liability (current maturities of LTD / bonds payable—current) $400,000; plus Interest Payable $4,000 current. |

**Income statement Year 1:** Interest expense on these bonds = **$4,000** (three months from issue to year-end).

**Lifetime interest over full term:** 4 years × 2 semiannual payments × $8,000 = **$64,000** total interest expense = total cash interest.

**Key insight:** Face-value bonds still need **period-end accruals** when coupon dates and fiscal year-end differ; the accrual changes **Interest Payable**, not the **carrying amount of Bonds Payable**, which stays at face until reclassification and cash maturity.

### Q4 — MC — Financial statement effect of bonds issued at face
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Classification / presentation — issuance at face does not reduce stockholders’ equity; liability equals face  
**Question:**  
On January 1 of Year 1, **Solstice Apparel Co.** authorizes and issues **$250,000**, **7%** interest-bearing bonds at **par** (stated rate equals market rate). The bonds mature December 31 of Year 8 and pay interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. By what amount is Solstice’s **stockholders’ equity reduced on January 1 of Year 1** solely because of the bond issuance transaction?

- A) $0  
- B) $8,750  
- C) $17,500  
- D) $250,000  

**Answer:** **A.** Issuing bonds at face increases Cash and Bonds Payable by the same amount ($250,000). There is no gain, loss, expense, or equity account in the issuance entry. Stockholders’ equity is **unchanged** on the issue date. (Interest expense reduces equity only in later periods when interest is recognized.)

### Q5 — MC — Subsequent measurement identity for face-value bonds
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement — for bonds at face, interest expense equals cash interest each period; carrying amount stays at face  
**Question:**  
**Pinnacle Ridge Inc.** issued **$600,000** of **5-year, 6%** bonds on January 1 at **face value**. Interest is paid annually each December 31. Immediately after the December 31 interest payment at the end of Year 2 (two years after issuance), which statement is correct?

- A) Carrying amount is $600,000 and Year 2 interest expense is $36,000.  
- B) Carrying amount is less than $600,000 because two years of principal amortization have been recorded.  
- C) Year 2 interest expense exceeds the cash interest of $36,000 because of discount amortization.  
- D) Year 2 interest expense is less than cash interest of $36,000 because of premium amortization.  

**Answer:** **A.** Face-value bonds have no discount or premium. Cash interest = $600,000 × 6% = **$36,000** = interest expense each year. Carrying amount remains **$600,000** until maturity. Options B–D describe discount or premium subsequent measurement (other LOs), not face-value bonds.
