# Agent 146 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-5

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for bonds issued at a premium  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Initial recognition of bonds issued at a premium (Cash; Premium on Bonds Payable adjunct; Bonds Payable)
- Measuring issue proceeds at PV using market (effective) rate when stated rate > market rate
- **Subsequent measurement schedule** (effective interest): cash interest − interest expense = premium amortization; carrying amount decreases toward face
- Period-end interest/amortization JEs each payment date (Dr Interest Expense; Dr Premium; Cr Cash)
- Period-end **adjusting JE** when reporting date ≠ interest payment date (prorate next schedule row)
- Balance sheet presentation: Bonds payable + unamortized premium = Bonds payable, net
- Derecognition at maturity (final interest + pay face; premium fully amortized)
- Number-variant twin of the core premium life cycle
- Classification: Premium on Bonds Payable is an **adjunct liability** (not a contra)

### Q1 — CORE — Harborview premium bonds (issue → full schedule → Year 1 → maturity)
**LO:** LO 16-5  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule and full effective-interest life cycle for premium bonds  
**Scenario:**  
Harborview Engineering authorizes and issues **$250,000** face of **6%** interest-bearing bonds on **January 1, Year 1**. The bonds mature **December 31, Year 3** and pay cash interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The bonds are sold to yield a **market rate of 4%**. Harborview amortizes the bond premium using the **effective interest method**. Amounts are rounded to the nearest dollar (textbook style).

**Given computations (semiannual):**
- Semiannual market rate: 4% ÷ 2 = **2%**
- Semiannual periods: 3 × 2 = **6**
- Semiannual cash interest: 6% ÷ 2 × $250,000 = **$7,500**
- Issue price = PV of principal + PV of interest annuity at 2%, 6 periods = **$264,004**
- Initial premium = $264,004 − $250,000 = **$14,004**

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **effective interest amortization schedule** over the full bond term (all 6 periods). **(emphasis)**  
b. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 issuance journal entry**.  
c. Prepare the interest/amortization JEs for **June 30, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 1**.  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet** presentation of the bonds and **Year 1 interest expense**.  
e. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** entries for the final interest payment and maturity settlement (assume no Interest Payable balance).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Amortization schedule — effective interest (stated 6%, market 4%)**  

| Date | Cash (3% × face) | Interest expense (2% × beg. carry) | Premium amort. | Unamortized premium | Carrying amount |
|------|------------------|-------------------------------------|----------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Jan 1, Y1 | — | — | — | 14,004 | 264,004 |
| Jun 30, Y1 | 7,500 | 5,280 | 2,220 | 11,784 | 261,784 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | 7,500 | 5,236 | 2,264 | 9,520 | 259,520 |
| Jun 30, Y2 | 7,500 | 5,190 | 2,310 | 7,210 | 257,210 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 7,500 | 5,144 | 2,356 | 4,854 | 254,854 |
| Jun 30, Y3 | 7,500 | 5,097 | 2,403 | 2,451 | 252,451 |
| Dec 31, Y3 | 7,500 | 5,049 | 2,451 | 0 | 250,000 |
| **Totals** | **45,000** | **30,996** | **14,004** | | |

*Notes:* Interest expense each period = beginning carrying amount × 2%. Premium amort. = Cash − Interest expense. Final period amort. clears remaining unamortized premium. Total interest expense ($30,996) is **less than** total cash interest ($45,000) by the premium ($14,004). Carrying amount **declines** each period toward face.

**b. Issuance at a premium (initial recognition)**  
January 1, Year 1 — To record bonds sold at a premium  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 264,004 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Premium on Bonds Payable | | 14,004 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bonds Payable | | 250,000 |

*Check:* Dr 264,004 = Cr 14,004 + 250,000. Carrying amount at issue = **$264,004**. **Balanced.**

**c. Interest and premium amortization (Year 1)**  
June 30, Year 1 — To record payment of interest and bond premium amortization  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Interest Expense | 5,280 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 2,220 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash | | 7,500 |

*Check:* 5,280 + 2,220 = 7,500. Carry after entry = 264,004 − 2,220 = **261,784**. **Balanced.**

December 31, Year 1 — To record payment of interest and bond premium amortization  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Interest Expense | 5,236 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 2,264 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash | | 7,500 |

*Check:* 5,236 + 2,264 = 7,500. Carry = 261,784 − 2,264 = **259,520**. Unamortized premium = **9,520**. **Balanced.**

**d. Financial statement presentation — December 31, Year 1**  

Balance sheet (liabilities excerpt):  
- Bonds payable — $250,000  
- Plus: Premium on bonds payable — 9,520  
- **Bonds payable, net — $259,520**

Income statement (Year 1):  
- Interest expense — **$(10,516)** [= 5,280 + 5,236]

**e. Maturity — December 31, Year 3**  
Final interest payment and amortization:  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Interest Expense | 5,049 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 2,451 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash | | 7,500 |

*Check:* 5,049 + 2,451 = 7,500. **Balanced.**

Derecognize bonds at face:  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Bonds Payable | 250,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash | | 250,000 |

*After final amort., unamortized premium = 0; carrying amount = face.*

**Key insight:** Stated rate > market rate → proceeds > face → **Premium on Bonds Payable** (adjunct liability). Effective interest: expense = market rate × **beginning carrying amount**, so expense **decreases** and premium amortization **increases** each period as the premium is amortized and carrying amount falls to face. Total interest cost over the life = total cash interest − original premium.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Meridian Pipeline premium bonds
**LO:** LO 16-5  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — issue price, subsequent measurement schedule, and premium amortization JEs  
**Scenario:**  
Meridian Pipeline Co. issues **$400,000** of **5%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1**. Bonds mature **December 31, Year 4** and pay interest **semiannually** (June 30 and December 31). Market yield at issuance is **3%**. Effective interest method; round to nearest dollar.

**Semiannual setup:**
- Market rate per period: 3% ÷ 2 = **1.5%**
- Periods: 4 × 2 = **8**
- Cash interest: 5% ÷ 2 × $400,000 = **$10,000**
- Issue price (PV at 1.5%, 8 periods) = **$429,944**

**Required:**

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Northpeak period-end accrual, presentation, maturity
**LO:** LO 16-5  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE (interest date ≠ reporting date), classification/presentation of premium bonds, and maturity settlement  
**Scenario:**  
Northpeak Logistics issued **$300,000** of **8%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1**, maturing **December 31, Year 3**. Interest is paid **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. Market rate at issuance was **6%**. Effective interest method; round to nearest dollar.

**Semiannual setup:**
- Market rate per period: 6% ÷ 2 = **3%**
- Periods: 3 × 2 = **6**
- Cash interest: 8% ÷ 2 × $300,000 = **$12,000**
- Issue price = **$316,252**; Premium = **$16,252**

Partial amortization (first three interest dates) used for this problem:

| Date | Cash | Interest expense | Premium amort. | Unamortized premium | Carrying amount |
|------|-----:|-----------------:|---------------:|--------------------:|----------------:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | — | — | — | 16,252 | 316,252 |
| Jun 30, Y1 | 12,000 | 9,488 | 2,512 | 13,740 | 313,740 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | 12,000 | 9,412 | 2,588 | 11,152 | 311,152 |
| Jun 30, Y2 | 12,000 | 9,335 | 2,665 | 8,487 | 308,487 |

*(Full-term totals for reference: cash interest $72,000; interest expense $55,748; premium amort. $16,252. Final Dec 31, Y3 period: cash 12,000; expense 9,086; amort. 2,914 → carry 300,000.)*

**Required:**  
a. Issuance JE on January 1, Year 1.  
b. December 31, Year 1 balance sheet **classification/presentation** of the bonds (long-term liability plus unamortized premium).  
c. Northpeak prepares **monthly** financial statements. Record the **January 31, Year 2** adjusting entry for one month of bond interest and premium amortization (prorate the June 30, Year 2 schedule row).  
d. Record maturity entries on **December 31, Year 3** for the final interest payment and repayment of face (no Interest Payable).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Issuance**  
January 1, Year 1  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 316,252 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Premium on Bonds Payable | | 16,252 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Bonds Payable | | 300,000 |

*Dr = Cr = 316,252. **Balanced.***

**b. Classification / presentation — December 31, Year 1**  
Long-term liabilities:  
- Bonds payable — $300,000  
- Plus: Premium on bonds payable — 11,152  
- **Bonds payable, net — $311,152**

*Premium is an **adjunct** account added to Bonds Payable (not deducted). Year 1 interest expense for reference = 9,488 + 9,412 = **$18,900**.*

**c. January 31, Year 2 — period-end adjusting JE (1/6 of next semiannual period)**  

From the June 30, Year 2 schedule row:  
- Cash interest for full semi period: $12,000 → 1 month: $12,000 ÷ 6 = **$2,000**  
- Interest expense: $9,335 ÷ 6 = **$1,556** (rounded)  
- Premium amortization: $2,665 ÷ 6 = **$444** (rounded)  
- Check: 1,556 + 444 = 2,000  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Interest Expense | 1,556 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 444 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Interest Payable | | 2,000 |

*Check:* 1,556 + 444 = 2,000. **Balanced.**  
*(When interest is paid on June 30, reverse/clear Interest Payable for months already accrued and record the remainder of the semi-period amortization.)*

**d. Maturity — December 31, Year 3**  
Final interest payment and amortization:  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Interest Expense | 9,086 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 2,914 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash | | 12,000 |

*Check:* 9,086 + 2,914 = 12,000. **Balanced.**

Derecognize bonds at face:  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Bonds Payable | 300,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash | | 300,000 |

*Premium fully amortized; only face is paid at maturity.*

**Key insight:** Between interest dates, accrue interest expense and amortize premium for the fraction of the period elapsed (Dr Expense, Dr Premium, Cr Interest Payable). On the balance sheet, always show **face + remaining unamortized premium**. At maturity, cash out equals face only—the premium has already reduced interest expense over the bond term.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of Premium on Bonds Payable
**LO:** LO 16-5  
**Concept:** Classification and presentation of Premium on Bonds Payable  
**Question:**  
When bonds are issued at a premium, how is **Premium on Bonds Payable** classified and presented on the issuer’s balance sheet?

- A) As a contra-liability deducted from Bonds Payable  
- B) As an adjunct liability added to Bonds Payable to report Bonds payable, net  
- C) As a deferred credit in stockholders’ equity until amortized  
- D) As other comprehensive income until the bonds mature  

**Answer:** B. Premium on Bonds Payable is an **adjunct liability** account. It increases the carrying amount of the bonds above face and is added to Bonds Payable on the balance sheet. (Discount is the contra-liability; premium is not equity or OCI.)

### Q5 — MC — Pattern of interest expense under effective interest (premium)
**LO:** LO 16-5  
**Concept:** Relationship of cash interest, interest expense, and premium amortization under the effective interest method  
**Question:**  
For bonds issued at a **premium** and amortized under the **effective interest method**, which statement is correct?

- A) Interest expense increases each period as the carrying amount increases toward face  
- B) Interest expense decreases each period as the carrying amount decreases toward face; premium amortization equals cash interest minus interest expense  
- C) Interest expense equals cash interest every period because the stated rate is fixed  
- D) Premium amortization is constant each period under the effective interest method  

**Answer:** B. Effective interest expense = beginning carrying amount × market rate. For a premium bond, carrying amount falls toward face, so **interest expense declines** each period. Cash interest is constant; the growing difference is premium amortization. (Constant amortization is the straight-line method, not effective interest.)
