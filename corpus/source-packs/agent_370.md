# Agent 370 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-3

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Account for reacquisition of common stock  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Treasury stock — cost method (initial recognition):** Debit Treasury Stock (contra equity) at acquisition cost; credit Cash. Assets and equity both decrease; shares remain **issued** but are no longer **outstanding**.
- **Reissuance above cost:** Cash at selling price; credit Treasury Stock at cost; excess credited to **Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock** (not gain on the income statement).
- **Reissuance below cost (period-end / equity adjusting effect):** Debit Cash; remove Treasury Stock at cost; first absorb excess of cost over proceeds against **PIC—Treasury Stock** to the extent of any prior credit balance; any remainder debited to **Retained Earnings** (not a realized loss on the income statement).
- **Subsequent-measurement / equity rollforward schedule:** Track treasury share units, treasury cost balance, PIC—TS balance, issued vs outstanding shares, and period-end stockholders’ equity presentation (TS deducted from total equity).
- **Subsequent retirement of treasury stock:** Derecognize Common Stock and PIC in Excess of Par at **original issuance** amounts; credit Treasury Stock at cost; balance with PIC—TS / PIC—Retired Stock (if available) then Retained Earnings. Net equity unchanged; **issued** shares decline.
- **Direct retirement (alternative path):** On reacquisition for retirement, debit CS and PIC—CS at original issue amounts; credit Cash at repurchase price; excess of cash over original issue → PIC—Retired Stock (if any credit balance) then RE; excess of original issue over cash → credit PIC—Retired Stock. Shares return to **authorized but unissued**.
- **Number-variant twin:** Same LO path with all companies and amounts changed.
- **Method classification:** Cost-method treasury vs direct retirement; equity (not P&L) treatment of reissuance “gains/losses.”

### Q1 — CORE — Harborline Marine: treasury cost method — purchase, reissue above/below cost, period-end equity, retirement, direct-retirement alternative

**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of treasury stock at cost; reissuance JEs (above and below cost); **period-end equity-adjusting reissuance** and year-end stockholders’ equity presentation (emphasis); subsequent-measurement / share-and-cost schedule; subsequent retirement of remaining treasury; direct-retirement alternative  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Marine Inc.** has **80,000** shares of **$2 par** common stock authorized. On **January 1, 20X1**, Harborline issued **20,000** shares for **$18** cash per share. On **June 30, 20X1**, Harborline reacquired **3,000** of those shares for the treasury at **$22** per share (cost method). On **September 15, 20X1**, Harborline sold **800** treasury shares for **$25** per share. On **December 31, 20X1** (period-end), Harborline sold **1,200** treasury shares for **$16** per share. Retained earnings before **any** treasury-related debits was **$95,000**. No other equity transactions occurred in 20X1.

On **March 1, 20X2**, Harborline formally **retires** the remaining treasury shares and returns them to unissued status.

**Additional fact for Part (e):** Instead of the treasury path, assume that on June 30, 20X1 Harborline reacquired **3,000** shares at **$22** and **immediately retired** them (direct retirement). No prior balance exists in Paid-In Capital—Retired Stock.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **initial recognition JE** for the June 30, 20X1 treasury purchase. Prove the entry balances.  
(b) Record the September 15, 20X1 reissuance (sales price **>** cost). Prove balances.  
(c) Record the **December 31, 20X1 period-end** reissuance (sales price **<** cost). Limit the debit to PIC—Treasury Stock to its available credit balance; charge any remainder to Retained Earnings. Prove balances.  
(d) Prepare a **subsequent-measurement schedule** of treasury shares, treasury cost, and PIC—Treasury Stock through December 31, 20X1. Present the **stockholders’ equity section** at December 31, 20X1 (show authorized / issued / outstanding).  
(e) Record the **March 1, 20X2 subsequent retirement** of remaining treasury shares. Prove balances.  
(f) Record the **direct retirement** alternative on June 30, 20X1 (Part additional fact). Prove balances.

**Answer key:**

**Background — original issue (context only; not required unless asked):**  
Cash received = 20,000 × $18 = **$360,000**.  
Common stock = 20,000 × $2 = **$40,000**.  
PIC in excess of par—CS = 20,000 × ($18 − $2) = **$320,000**.  
Original APIC per share = **$16**.

**(a) June 30, 20X1 — initial recognition of treasury stock (cost method)**

Treasury Stock = 3,000 × $22 = **$66,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 66,000 | |
| Cash | | 66,000 |
| *Reacquire 3,000 common shares for treasury at cost $22* | | |

**Check:** Dr 66,000 = Cr 66,000. **Balanced.**  
Issued shares unchanged at 20,000; outstanding = 20,000 − 3,000 = **17,000**.

**(b) September 15, 20X1 — reissuance above cost**

Cash = 800 × $25 = **$20,000**.  
Treasury Stock removed = 800 × $22 = **$17,600**.  
Excess to PIC—Treasury Stock = 20,000 − 17,600 = **$2,400**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 20,000 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 17,600 |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | | 2,400 |
| *Reissue 800 treasury shares; selling price > cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 20,000 = Cr 17,600 + 2,400 = 20,000. **Balanced.**  
PIC—TS credit balance after this entry = **$2,400**.

**(c) December 31, 20X1 — period-end reissuance below cost (emphasis)**

Cash = 1,200 × $16 = **$19,200**.  
Treasury Stock removed = 1,200 × $22 = **$26,400**.  
Cost exceeds proceeds by 26,400 − 19,200 = **$7,200**.  
Debit PIC—Treasury Stock limited to prior credit **$2,400**.  
Remainder to Retained Earnings = 7,200 − 2,400 = **$4,800**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 19,200 | |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | 2,400 | |
| Retained Earnings | 4,800 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 26,400 |
| *Period-end reissue 1,200 treasury shares below cost; absorb excess against PIC—TS then RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 19,200 + 2,400 + 4,800 = 26,400 = Cr 26,400. **Balanced.**  
PIC—TS ending balance = **$0**. No income-statement loss is recognized.

**(d) Subsequent-measurement schedule and period-end equity presentation**

**Treasury / PIC—TS rollforward**

| Date / event | TS shares | TS cost balance | PIC—TS balance | Outstanding shares |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| After Jan 1 issue | 0 | $0 | $0 | 20,000 |
| June 30 — purchase @ $22 | 3,000 | 66,000 | 0 | 17,000 |
| Sept 15 — reissue 800 @ $25 | 2,200 | 48,400 | 2,400 | 17,800 |
| Dec 31 — reissue 1,200 @ $16 | 1,000 | 22,000 | 0 | 19,000 |

Proof of TS cost at year-end: $66,000 − $17,600 − $26,400 = **$22,000** (= 1,000 × $22).  
Proof of RE after treasury debit: $95,000 − $4,800 = **$90,200**.

**Stockholders’ equity — December 31, 20X1**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $2 par, 80,000 shares authorized, 20,000 issued, 19,000 outstanding | $40,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 320,000 |
| Retained earnings | 90,200 |
| Total paid-in capital and retained earnings | 450,200 |
| Less: Treasury stock, 1,000 shares (at cost) | (22,000) |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$428,200** |

Proof: $40,000 + $320,000 + $90,200 − $22,000 = **$428,200**.

**(e) March 1, 20X2 — subsequent retirement of remaining 1,000 treasury shares**

Original issue amounts for 1,000 shares:  
Common Stock = 1,000 × $2 = **$2,000**.  
PIC—CS = 1,000 × $16 = **$16,000**.  
Treasury Stock cost = 1,000 × $22 = **$22,000**.  
PIC—TS available = **$0**.  
To balance: 22,000 − 2,000 − 16,000 = **$4,000** debit to Retained Earnings.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 2,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 16,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | 4,000 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 22,000 |
| *Retire remaining 1,000 treasury shares; return to unissued* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,000 + 16,000 + 4,000 = 22,000 = Cr 22,000. **Balanced.**  
After retirement: authorized 80,000; **issued 19,000**; outstanding 19,000; treasury 0. Net stockholders’ equity is **unchanged** by the retirement itself.

**(f) Direct retirement alternative — June 30, 20X1 (3,000 shares @ $22)**

Original issue amounts for 3,000 shares:  
CS = 3,000 × $2 = **$6,000**.  
PIC—CS = 3,000 × $16 = **$48,000**.  
Cash paid = 3,000 × $22 = **$66,000**.  
Excess of cash over original issue = 66,000 − 54,000 = **$12,000** → Retained Earnings (no PIC—Retired Stock credit balance).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 6,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 48,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | 12,000 | |
| Cash | | 66,000 |
| *Direct retirement of 3,000 common shares at $22* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,000 + 48,000 + 12,000 = 66,000 = Cr 66,000. **Balanced.**  
After direct retirement: issued = 17,000; outstanding = 17,000; treasury = 0 (shares unissued).

**Key insight:** Under the **cost method**, reacquisition is recorded entirely in **Treasury Stock** at cost (contra equity). Reissuance differences stay in **equity** (PIC—TS, then RE)—never as P&L gains/losses. A **period-end** reissue below cost is an equity-adjusting entry that first drains PIC—TS then hits RE. **Retirement** (subsequent or direct) removes capital accounts at **original issue** amounts and settles the cash/cost difference through PIC—Retired/TS then RE; retirement reduces **issued** shares, while holding treasury does not.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Redwood Circuit: treasury purchase, reissue above/below, period-end equity, retirement, direct retirement below issue price

**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial treasury recognition at cost; reissuance above and below cost; **period-end equity presentation and below-cost reissue**; treasury rollforward schedule; subsequent retirement; direct retirement when cash paid **less than** original issue price  
**Scenario:**  
**Redwood Circuit Co.** has **100,000** shares of **$1 par** common authorized. On **January 1, 20X4**, Redwood issued **15,000** shares at **$30** per share. ### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — period-end equity close and dual-lot cost flow; reissue below cost with FIFO

**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** **Period-end adjusting / presentation emphasis** — year-end stockholders’ equity after multi-lot treasury purchases; FIFO cost assignment on period-end reissuance; incomplete absorption of “loss” against PIC—TS then RE  
**Scenario:**  
**Sable Peak Holdings** (calendar year-end) had the following equity accounts **before** any 20X6 treasury activity (all from common stock originally issued at **$12** per share, **$1 par**):

| Account | Balance |
|---|---:|
| Common stock ($1 par), 40,000 shares issued | $40,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 440,000 |
| Retained earnings | 210,000 |
| Treasury stock | 0 |

20X6 treasury activity (cost method; company uses **FIFO** for treasury cost):  
1. **March 8** — purchase **2,000** shares @ **$14**  
2. **July 22** — purchase **1,500** shares @ **$17**  
3. **December 28** — reissue **2,400** shares @ **$13** (period-end-adjacent reissuance; treat as the **period-end equity-adjusting** reissue for this problem)  
4. No other equity transactions. Board does **not** declare year-end dividends.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the two **initial recognition** purchase JEs (March 8 and July 22). Prove balances.  
(b) Assign **FIFO cost** to the 2,400 shares reissued on December 28. Record the **period-end reissuance JE** (sales price vs layered cost). Prove balances.  
(c) Prepare the **subsequent-measurement schedule** of treasury lots after each event and the **December 31, 20X6 stockholders’ equity section** (authorized shares = 100,000).  
(d) Briefly state whether any separate “amortization-style” period-end AJ is required for treasury stock under the cost method and what the true period-end tasks are.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Initial recognition JEs**

March 8 — purchase 2,000 @ $14; cost = **$28,000**:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 28,000 | |
| Cash | | 28,000 |
| *Purchase 2,000 treasury shares @ $14* | | |

**Check:** Dr 28,000 = Cr 28,000. **Balanced.**

July 22 — purchase 1,500 @ $17; cost = **$25,500**:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 25,500 | |
| Cash | | 25,500 |
| *Purchase 1,500 treasury shares @ $17* | | |

**Check:** Dr 25,500 = Cr 25,500. **Balanced.**  
Total TS cost before reissue = 28,000 + 25,500 = **$53,500** (3,500 shares).

**(b) FIFO cost of 2,400 shares reissued December 28**

FIFO layers used:  
- All 2,000 from March lot @ $14 = **$28,000**  
- 400 from July lot @ $17 = **$6,800**  
**Total cost removed** = 28,000 + 6,800 = **$34,800**.

Cash proceeds = 2,400 × $13 = **$31,200**.  
Cost exceeds proceeds by 34,800 − 31,200 = **$3,600**.  
No prior PIC—TS credit balance → entire **$3,600** debited to Retained Earnings.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 31,200 | |
| Retained Earnings | 3,600 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 34,800 |
| *Period-end FIFO reissue of 2,400 treasury shares below cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 31,200 + 3,600 = 34,800 = Cr 34,800. **Balanced.**

**(c) Subsequent-measurement schedule and Dec 31 equity**

| Date / event | Lot detail (shares @ cost) | Total TS shares | Total TS cost | PIC—TS | RE impact |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Start 20X6 | — | 0 | $0 | $0 | — |
| Mar 8 buy | 2,000 @ $14 | 2,000 | 28,000 | 0 | — |
| Jul 22 buy | 2,000 @ $14; 1,500 @ $17 | 3,500 | 53,500 | 0 | — |
| Dec 28 reissue | remaining: 1,100 @ $17 | 1,100 | 18,700 | 0 | (3,600) |

Remaining TS cost proof: 1,100 × $17 = **$18,700** (= $53,500 − $34,800).  
RE at year-end = $210,000 − $3,600 = **$206,400**.  
Outstanding at year-end = 40,000 − 1,100 = **38,900**.

**Stockholders’ equity — December 31, 20X6**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $1 par, 100,000 authorized, 40,000 issued, 38,900 outstanding | $40,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 440,000 |
| Retained earnings | 206,400 |
| Total paid-in capital and retained earnings | 686,400 |
| Less: Treasury stock, 1,100 shares (at cost) | (18,700) |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$667,700** |

Proof: $40,000 + $440,000 + $206,400 − $18,700 = **$667,700**.

**(d) Period-end AJ nature for treasury (cost method)**  
Treasury stock under the cost method is **not amortized**. There is no recurring “interest-style” or “amortization-style” adjusting entry. Period-end work is: (1) record any **year-end reissuance/retirement** JEs with correct cost-flow (FIFO/LIFO/WA), (2) ensure reissue differences hit **PIC—TS then RE** (not P&L), and (3) **present** treasury as a deduction in the equity section with correct issued/outstanding counts.

**Key insight:** Multiple purchase lots require a **cost-flow assumption** (here FIFO) to measure the credit to Treasury Stock on reissue. Period-end emphasis is **equity presentation + correct absorption of below-cost reissues**, not amortization.

### Q4 — MC — absorption order when treasury reissued below cost

**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Cost-method reissuance below cost — debit Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock first, then Retained Earnings; no income-statement loss  
**Question:**  
Under the **cost method**, a company reissues treasury shares for cash **less than** the acquisition cost of those shares. Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock has a **credit balance smaller than** the excess of cost over proceeds. How should the company record the difference?

- A) Debit a loss on the income statement for the full excess of cost over proceeds  
- B) Debit Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock for its entire credit balance and debit **Retained Earnings** for any remaining excess; credit Treasury Stock at cost  
- C) Debit Common Stock for the par of the shares reissued and debit Retained Earnings for the rest  
- D) Debit Accumulated Other Comprehensive Income for the full excess of cost over proceeds  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Under ASC 505 / cost-method practice demonstrated in LO 19-3, “losses” on treasury reissuance are charged first against **Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock** to the extent of prior net “gains” from treasury transactions of the same class; any remainder is charged to **Retained Earnings**. The difference is **not** reported as an income-statement loss, not charged against AOCI, and does not reverse Common Stock on a mere reissuance (shares remain issued while held as treasury and return to outstanding on reissue).

### Q5 — MC — treasury holding vs direct retirement (issued shares)

**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Classification/method — treasury stock keeps shares **issued**; direct retirement returns shares to **unissued**  
**Question:**  
A corporation reacquires 1,000 of its own common shares. Which statement is correct?

- A) Whether accounted for as treasury stock or by direct retirement, the number of **issued** shares decreases immediately by 1,000  
- B) Under the cost method of treasury stock, the repurchase reduces **issued** shares but not outstanding shares  
- C) Holding the shares as treasury stock (cost method) leaves the number of **issued** shares unchanged and reduces **outstanding** shares; direct retirement reduces both issued and outstanding shares (shares become unissued)  
- D) Treasury stock is reported as an asset equal to acquisition cost until resold  

**Answer:** **C.**  
Treasury shares remain **issued** but are not outstanding; cost is a **contra-equity** deduction, not an asset. Direct retirement derecognizes capital accounts and reclassifies shares as **authorized but unissued**, so both issued and outstanding decline.
