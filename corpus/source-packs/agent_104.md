# Agent 104 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-8

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Explain and demonstrate accounting for uncertainty in income tax decisions  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Two-step process (ASC 740):** (1) **Recognition** — more likely than not (>50%) the tax position will be sustained on technical merits (assume full audit knowledge; ignore detection risk); (2) **Measurement** — largest amount of tax benefit with **cumulative probability >50%** of being realized upon settlement
- **Initial recognition JE (emphasis):** Income Tax Expense = Income Tax Payable + **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits (UTB)** when a portion of the filed benefit is not recognized for financial reporting
- **Fails recognition threshold:** recognize **no** financial-statement benefit for the uncertain position; full tax effect of the position is recorded as UTB liability
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** roll-forward of Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits (beginning balance → additions → remeasurement → settlements → ending balance)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** change in estimate of the unrecognized amount as new information becomes available (prospective; not an error restatement)
- **Settlement / resolution JE:** (a) full benefit lost, (b) full benefit realized, (c) partial realization — remove UTB; record Income Tax Payable and/or Income Tax Expense as needed
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** UTB liability on the balance sheet; note reconciliation of beginning to ending unrecognized tax benefits; interest/penalties often in income tax expense

### Q1 — CORE — CedarForge: initial UTB recognition, measurement table, subsequent schedule, remeasurement, settlement
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for uncertain tax position (meets MLTN; measure largest amount with cumulative probability >50%); subsequent UTB schedule; period-end remeasurement JE; settlement JE; classification/disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**CedarForge Technologies Inc.** (calendar-year) claims a **$80,000** research-cost deduction on its Year 1 tax return. Management concludes the position meets the **more-likely-than-not** recognition threshold based on technical merits (assuming the taxing authority examines the position with full knowledge of all relevant information). Taxable income for Year 1, **after** taking the full $80,000 deduction, is **$640,000**. The enacted tax rate is **25%** for all years. CedarForge has no temporary differences other than the uncertain tax position effects illustrated here.

Management’s Year 1 estimate of possible settlement outcomes:

| Possible estimated outcome (allowable tax deduction) | Individual probability | Cumulative probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $80,000 | 20% | 20% |
| 60,000 | 35% | 55% |
| 40,000 | 25% | 80% |
| 20,000 | 15% | 95% |
| 0 | 5% | 100% |

At **December 31, Year 2**, before any settlement, new information causes management to revise the outcome table as follows (change in estimate):

| Possible estimated outcome (allowable tax deduction) | Individual probability | Cumulative probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $80,000 | 10% | 10% |
| 50,000 | 20% | 30% |
| 40,000 | 30% | 60% |
| 20,000 | 30% | 90% |
| 0 | 10% | 100% |

On **June 30, Year 3**, after negotiations, CedarForge and the taxing authority settle: **$48,000** of the original $80,000 deduction is sustained (and **$32,000** is disallowed). Any additional tax is currently payable.

**Required:**  
a. Apply the **two-step** process: does the position meet recognition? What **amount of deduction benefit** is measured for financial reporting at December 31, Year 1?  
b. Compute **Income Tax Payable**, **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits**, and **Income Tax Expense** at December 31, Year 1. Prepare the **initial recognition journal entry** (emphasis). Show Dr = Cr.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement / UTB liability roll-forward schedule** from December 31, Year 1 through June 30, Year 3 settlement (include the Year 2 remeasurement).  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2 period-end adjusting JE** for the change in estimate.  
e. Prepare the **June 30, Year 3 settlement journal entry**.  
f. State how the UTB liability is **classified/presented** and what **disclosure** is typically required for the roll-forward.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Two-step recognition and measurement (Year 1)**

- **Step 1 — Recognition:** Yes. Management concludes it is **more likely than not (>50%)** the position will be sustained on technical merits → financial-statement benefit **may** be recognized; proceed to measurement.  
- **Step 2 — Measurement:** Largest allowable deduction with **cumulative probability >50%** is **$60,000** (cumulative 55%).  
- Recognized benefit (deduction base) = **$60,000**  
- Unrecognized deduction base = $80,000 − $60,000 = **$20,000**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — initial recognition JE (emphasis)**

Income Tax Payable = \(640{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$160{,}000}\)  
Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits = \(20{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$5{,}000}\)  
Income Tax Expense = \(160{,}000 + 5{,}000 = \mathbf{\$165{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 165,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 160,000 |
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | | 5,000 |
| *Record Year 1 tax expense; UTB for unrecognized portion of uncertain deduction* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$165,000**. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits**

| Date | Description | UTB liability |
|---|---|---:|
| 12/31/Y1 | Initial recognition (unrecognized base $20,000 × 25%) | **$5,000** |
| 12/31/Y2 | Remeasurement (see part d) | **10,000** |
| 6/30/Y3 | Settlement — reverse remaining UTB | **0** |

Year 2 remeasurement computation:  
Largest amount with cumulative probability >50% under revised table = **$40,000** (cumulative 60%).  
Unrecognized deduction base = $80,000 − $40,000 = **$40,000**  
Required UTB liability = \(40{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\)  
Increase from beginning Y2 balance = \(10{,}000 - 5{,}000 = \mathbf{\$5{,}000}\)

**d. December 31, Year 2 — period-end adjusting JE (change in estimate)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 5,000 | |
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | | 5,000 |
| *Remeasure UTB for new information; prospective change in estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$5,000**. **Balanced.**

**e. June 30, Year 3 — settlement JE**

Deduction sustained = **$48,000**  
Deduction disallowed = $80,000 − $48,000 = **$32,000**  
Additional Income Tax Payable = \(32{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$8{,}000}\)  
Remove UTB liability of **$10,000**  
Difference reduces Income Tax Expense: \(10{,}000 - 8{,}000 = \mathbf{\$2{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 10,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 8,000 |
| Income Tax Expense | | 2,000 |
| *Settle uncertain tax position; reclass UTB; true-up tax expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 10,000 = Cr 8,000 + 2,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Classification / presentation / disclosure**  
- **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits** is reported as a liability (often **noncurrent** unless settlement is expected within one year).  
- Notes typically include a **reconciliation** of beginning to ending unrecognized tax benefits (additions for current-year positions, additions/reductions for prior-year positions, settlements, and other changes), consistent with ASC 740 disclosures.  
- Interest and penalties associated with UTBs are commonly recorded **within income tax expense** (policy election disclosure).

**Key insight:** Meeting the MLTN **recognition** threshold does **not** mean the full tax-return benefit is recognized. Measurement selects the **largest** benefit amount with **cumulative** probability **greater than 50%**; the tax effect of the remainder is accrued as **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits**. Subsequent remeasurement and settlement are treated as **changes in estimate**.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — NorthPeak: twin uncertain-position measurement and full-cycle JEs
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition of UTB after two-step process; subsequent UTB schedule; period-end remeasurement; settlement when full benefit is realized  
**Scenario:**  
**NorthPeak Analytics Corp.** claims a **$120,000** deduction on its Year 1 return related to classification of certain project costs. Management determines the position is **more likely than not** to be sustained. Taxable income after the full deduction is **$900,000**. Tax rate is **25%**. No other temporary differences.

Year 1 possible outcomes:

| Possible estimated outcome (allowable tax deduction) | Individual probability | Cumulative probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $120,000 | 15% | 15% |
| 90,000 | 25% | 40% |
| 75,000 | 30% | 70% |
| 45,000 | 20% | 90% |
| 0 | 10% | 100% |

At **December 31, Year 2**, new information revises the table so the largest amount with cumulative probability >50% is now **$90,000** (management documents cumulative probability of 55% at $90,000).  
On **September 15, Year 3**, NorthPeak **fully sustains** the entire $120,000 deduction after examination.

**Required:**  
a. Measure the Year 1 recognized deduction benefit and unrecognized base; compute tax payable, UTB liability, and tax expense.  
b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 initial recognition JE**.  
c. Prepare a **UTB liability subsequent measurement schedule** through settlement.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2 period-end remeasurement JE**.  
e. Prepare the **September 15, Year 3 settlement JE** (full benefit realized).  
f. Compute what **Income Tax Expense** would have been in Year 1 if the individual probability of the full $120,000 outcome had been **55%** instead of 15% (with other outcomes reallocated so probabilities still sum to 100%—for this counterfactual, assume only that the full position has 55% individual probability so cumulative at $120,000 exceeds 50%).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1 measurement**

Largest amount with cumulative probability >50% = **$75,000** (cumulative 70%).  
Unrecognized deduction base = $120,000 − $75,000 = **$45,000**  
Income Tax Payable = \(900{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$225{,}000}\)  
Liability for UTB = \(45{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$11{,}250}\)  
Income Tax Expense = \(225{,}000 + 11{,}250 = \mathbf{\$236{,}250}\)

**b. December 31, Year 1 — initial recognition JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 236,250 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 225,000 |
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | | 11,250 |
| *Record tax expense and UTB for unrecognized portion* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$236,250**. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — UTB liability**

Year 2 required liability: unrecognized base = $120,000 − $90,000 = **$30,000**; UTB = \(30{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$7{,}500}\)

| Date | Description | UTB liability |
|---|---|---:|
| 12/31/Y1 | Initial recognition | **$11,250** |
| 12/31/Y2 | Remeasurement (decrease) | **7,500** |
| 9/15/Y3 | Settlement — full benefit realized | **0** |

**d. December 31, Year 2 — period-end remeasurement JE**

Decrease in UTB = \(11{,}250 - 7{,}500 = \mathbf{\$3{,}750}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 3,750 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 3,750 |
| *Reduce UTB for improved measurement estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$3,750**. **Balanced.**

**e. September 15, Year 3 — settlement (full tax benefit realized)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 7,500 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 7,500 |
| *Favorable resolution; reverse remaining UTB; reduce tax expense (change in estimate)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$7,500**. **Balanced.**  
No Income Tax Payable is recorded because the full return position is sustained (no additional tax due).

**f. Counterfactual — full $120,000 has >50% likelihood**

If individual probability of the full $120,000 outcome is **55%**, the largest amount with cumulative probability >50% is **$120,000**.  
Unrecognized base = **$0**; UTB = **$0**  
Income Tax Expense = Income Tax Payable = **$225,000** only.

**Key insight:** The **cumulative** probability column—not the individual probability of the top outcome—drives measurement once recognition is met. Improving estimates reduce UTB and tax expense prospectively; full success settles UTB entirely against tax expense.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — fails recognition threshold + three settlement outcomes
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition when position **fails** MLTN threshold; settlement/resolution JEs for full loss, full sustain, and negotiated partial realization; presentation of UTB  
**Scenario:**  
**Part A — Fails recognition threshold**  
**PineHearth Retail Co.** takes a **$50,000** deduction on its Year 1 tax return. Management assesses that the probability the position will be sustained on technical merits is **only 40%** (does **not** meet more-likely-than-not). Taxable income after taking the $50,000 deduction is **$200,000**. Tax rate is **25%**.

**Part B — Settlement outcomes (separate case; meets threshold previously)**  
**RidgeLine Materials Inc.** previously recorded a **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits of $7,500** at December 31, Year 1 related to a Year 1 uncertain deduction of **$100,000**. That liability reflected an unrecognized deduction base of **$30,000** at 25% (i.e., financial statements recognized benefit of a **$70,000** deduction). In December of Year 2, consider each resolution **separately**:

1. **Full tax benefit is lost** — none of the $100,000 deduction is sustained.  
2. **Full tax benefit is realized** — the entire $100,000 deduction is sustained.  
3. **Portion realized as previously measured** — taxing authority allows **$70,000** of the deduction (exactly the measured amount).  
4. **Portion realized differently** — taxing authority allows only **$55,000** of the deduction.

**Required:**  
a. (Part A) Explain why no benefit is measured under step two. Compute amounts and prepare PineHearth’s **December 31, Year 1 income tax JE**.  
b. (Part B) Prepare the **Year 2 settlement JE** for each of cases 1–4.  
c. For case 3 only, explain the **income statement effect** relative to cases 1, 2, and 4.  
d. State the **balance-sheet classification** of a material UTB liability expected to be negotiated over multi-year examinations.

**Answer key:**  

**a. PineHearth — fails recognition threshold (initial recognition JE)**

- **Step 1:** Probability of sustainment = 40% **≤ 50%** → does **not** meet MLTN recognition threshold.  
- **Step 2 measurement is not applied** to recognize any financial-statement benefit of the position.  
- Full tax effect of the $50,000 deduction is treated as unrecognized.

Income Tax Payable (as filed) = \(200{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$50{,}000}\)  
Liability for UTB = \(50{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$12{,}500}\)  
Income Tax Expense = \(50{,}000 + 12{,}500 = \mathbf{\$62{,}500}\)

(Equivalent view: tax expense as if the deduction is fully disallowed = \((200{,}000 + 50{,}000) \times 25\% = \$62{,}500\).)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 62,500 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 50,000 |
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | | 12,500 |
| *Position fails MLTN; no FS benefit recognized for the $50,000 deduction* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$62,500**. **Balanced.**

**b. RidgeLine — Year 2 settlement JEs**

**Case 1 — Full tax benefit lost**

Additional tax due on full $100,000 disallowance = \(100{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$25{,}000}\)  
Prior UTB accrual = **$7,500**  
Incremental Income Tax Expense in Year 2 = \(25{,}000 - 7{,}500 = \mathbf{\$17{,}500}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 7,500 | |
| Income Tax Expense | 17,500 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 25,000 |
| *Fail to sustain tax position; settle UTB and accrue remaining tax* | | |

**Check:** Dr 7,500 + 17,500 = Cr 25,000. **Balanced.**

**Case 2 — Full tax benefit realized**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 7,500 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 7,500 |
| *Favorable resolution; reverse UTB; reduce tax expense prospectively* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$7,500**. **Balanced.**

**Case 3 — Portion realized as previously measured ($70,000 allowed)**

Additional tax due = unrecognized base \(30{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$7{,}500}\)  
Equals existing UTB → pure reclassification; **no** income-statement effect.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 7,500 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 7,500 |
| *Negotiated resolution matches prior measurement; reclass UTB to payable* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$7,500**. **Balanced.**

**Case 4 — Portion realized differently ($55,000 allowed)**

Disallowed base = $100,000 − $55,000 = **$45,000**  
Additional tax due = \(45{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$11{,}250}\)  
Prior UTB = **$7,500**  
Incremental Income Tax Expense = \(11{,}250 - 7{,}500 = \mathbf{\$3{,}750}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 7,500 | |
| Income Tax Expense | 3,750 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 11,250 |
| *Settlement worse than prior measurement; true-up tax expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 7,500 + 3,750 = Cr 11,250. **Balanced.**

**c. Income statement effect — case 3 vs others**  
- Case 3: **$0** Year 2 income-statement effect (settlement equals prior estimate).  
- Case 1: **+$17,500** tax expense (worse than estimate).  
- Case 2: **−$7,500** tax expense (better than estimate; full reverse of UTB).  
- Case 4: **+$3,750** tax expense (somewhat worse than the $70,000 measured benefit).  
Resolution effects are **changes in accounting estimates**, not prior-period error corrections.

**d. Classification**  
A material UTB liability related to multi-year examinations is typically presented as a **noncurrent liability** unless management expects settlement (and payment) within one year of the balance-sheet date; the notes disclose the roll-forward and reasonably possible near-term changes when estimable.

**Key insight:** If step 1 **fails**, the entire tax benefit of the position is unrecognized (full UTB). If step 1 **passes** and a UTB was accrued, settlement either reclasses to payable (estimate correct), increases tax expense (worse outcome), or decreases tax expense (better outcome).

### Q4 — MC (classification / measurement method)
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Measurement method for a tax position that meets the more-likely-than-not recognition threshold  

**Question:**  
Lakewood Biosystems Inc. meets the more-likely-than-not recognition threshold for an uncertain tax deduction of $40,000. Management’s cumulative probability table shows: 20% chance of sustaining $40,000; 45% chance of sustaining at least $28,000; 75% chance of sustaining at least $16,000; 100% chance of sustaining at least $0. The tax rate is 25%. What amount of **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits** should Lakewood record related to this position?

- A) $0  
- B) $3,000  
- C) $6,000  
- D) $10,000  

**Answer:** **C.** Once recognition is met, measure the **largest** benefit with **cumulative** probability **>50%**. Cumulative probability at $28,000 is only **45%** (does not exceed 50%); cumulative probability at $16,000 is **75%**. Recognized deduction = **$16,000**; unrecognized base = $40,000 − $16,000 = **$24,000**; UTB = \(24{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\).  
Option B ($3,000) is the common trap if students incorrectly treat the $28,000 row (45% cumulative) as meeting the >50% cumulative measurement rule (\(12{,}000 \times 25\%\)). Option D ($10,000) treats the position as if it failed recognition entirely (\(40{,}000 \times 25\%\)). Option A would require the full $40,000 to clear the cumulative >50% test.

### Q4b — MC (recognition threshold / method)
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Effect of failing the more-likely-than-not recognition threshold on initial measurement  

**Question:**  
Summit Vale Foods Co. claims a $20,000 uncertain deduction. Management is **only 45%** certain the position will be sustained on technical merits if examined. Taxable income after the deduction is $320,000; tax rate 25%. Summit paid (or accrued payable) taxes based on the tax position taken. What **Income Tax Expense** should Summit record for the year related to current taxes and this uncertain position (ignore other temporary differences)?

- A) $80,000  
- B) $80,500  
- C) $85,000  
- D) $75,000  

**Answer:** **C.** Recognition threshold is **not** met (45% ≯ 50%). No financial-statement benefit is recognized for the $20,000 deduction.  
Tax payable as filed = \(320{,}000 \times 25\% = \$80{,}000\)  
UTB = \(20{,}000 \times 25\% = \$5{,}000\)  
Income Tax Expense = \(80{,}000 + 5{,}000 = \mathbf{\$85{,}000}\)  
(Alternatively: taxable income without the deduction \(340{,}000 \times 25\% = \$85{,}000\).)  
A is tax payable only (incorrectly ignores UTB). B has no conceptual support. D is as if a larger benefit were recognized.
