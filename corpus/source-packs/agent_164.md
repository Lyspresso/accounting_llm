# Agent 164 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-1

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Identify operating, investing, and financing activities, and the statement of cash flows format  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Operating activities:** cash effects of transactions that generally enter into net income (sales collections, supplier/salary/tax/interest payments; dividends and interest received; trading securities held for resale; trade notes related to inventory)
- **Investing activities:** cash for PPE and intangibles (purchase/sale), nontrade loans made/collected (principal), debt/equity investments not held for trading resale
- **Financing activities:** cash from owners and creditors (stock issuance, treasury stock, dividends paid, debt principal borrowed/repaid, finance-lease principal)
- **Initial recognition JEs:** record cash transactions that generate SCF line items (PPE purchase/sale, debt issue/repay, stock issue, dividends)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** section-by-section cash flow schedule that rolls beginning cash to ending cash (O + I + F = Δ cash)
- **Period-end format:** formal SCF order (operating → investing → financing); report investing/financing **gross** inflows and outflows separately; reconcile cash (including cash equivalents and restricted cash)
- **Disposal / settlement classification:** sale of land/PPE → investing inflow; debt principal settlement → financing outflow; related gains/losses affect NI but cash proceeds/principal go outside operating
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** three-section framework; interest paid/received and dividends received = operating (U.S. GAAP); dividends paid = financing
- **Number-variant twin:** same skills with all company names and amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Full O/I/F classification, cash-event JEs, and cash reconciliation schedule

The controller is building the **2025 statement of cash flows** framework (classification and format only — not a full indirect-method reconciliation from net income). Cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash are treated as the **cash** total for SCF purposes.

**e. Interest vs principal; dividends received vs paid**

Under U.S. GAAP, **interest paid** is an **operating** cash outflow because interest expense enters the determination of net income and ASC 230 classifies interest as operating. **Principal repayment** reduces a borrowing and is a **financing** outflow. **Dividends received** are **operating** inflows (return on investment that enters income), whereas **dividends paid** are **financing** outflows (return *of* resources to owners). Classification separates repetitive operating cash capacity from long-term investing strategy and capital-structure financing.

**Key insight:** Every cash movement lands in **exactly one** of O, I, or F. The three section totals must explain the **entire** change in cash (including cash equivalents and restricted cash). Investing and financing lines are shown **gross** (purchases separate from sales; borrowings separate from repayments).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Edge classifications, period-end cash total, and gross presentation
**LO:** LO 22-1  
**Concept:** Period-end cash (cash + equivalents + restricted cash) reconciliation; edge-case classification (trade note vs nontrade; trading securities; interest vs principal); gross presentation requirement; disposal of patent and settlement of finance-lease principal  
**Scenario:**  
**Maplewick Analytics LLC** is closing its books for the year ended **December 31, 2025**. Staff prepared a draft list of “cash flow items” that includes several classification traps.

**Part A — Edge classification (select for each: O inflow, O outflow, I inflow, I outflow, F inflow, F outflow)**

1. Payment of **principal** on a finance lease liability.  
2. Cash purchase of **trading** equity securities acquired specifically for short-term resale profits.  
3. Cash proceeds from sale of those **same trading** securities.  
4. Cash purchase of **available-for-sale** debt securities held for long-term yield (not trading).  
5. Collection of **principal** on a **short-term trade note** receivable arising from sale of inventory to a customer.  
6. Collection of **interest** on that same trade note.  
7. Cash paid for **early extinguishment** of long-term bonds issued to finance a plant expansion (principal and any cash premium paid treated as financing settlement for this LO).  
8. Cash proceeds from sale of a **patent**.  
9. Cash received from issuance of a **short-term nontrade** note payable used for general financing (not to purchase inventory).  
10. Payment of a cash dividend declared in the prior year (dividends payable settled this year).

**Part B — Period-end cash total and format**  
Maplewick’s December 31, 2025 balances:

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash in bank (unrestricted) | $48,000 |
| Money-market cash equivalents | 12,000 |
| Restricted cash (compensating balance, long-term debt agreement) | 5,000 |
| Short-term AFS equity investments (not cash equivalents) | 22,000 |

During 2025, net cash from operating activities was **$90,000**, net cash **used** by investing was **$(70,000)**, and net cash **used** by financing was **$(15,000)**.

**Part C — Disposal and settlement JEs with SCF classification**  
- Sold a patent with book value **$18,000** for **$22,500** cash.  
- Made a **$9,000** cash principal payment on a finance lease (ignore interest portion for this entry; interest paid separately as operating).

**Required:**  
a. Classify items 1–10 in Part A.  
b. Compute the **ending cash total** that appears on the SCF reconciliation. Using that total and the section nets, solve for **beginning cash**.  
c. Prepare the **patent sale JE** and the **finance-lease principal payment JE**; state each item’s SCF section.  
d. True/False with correction: “Investing cash flows may be reported net (purchases net of sales of equipment) to simplify the SCF.”  
e. List the **required section order** on the face of the statement of cash flows.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Part A classifications**

| # | Classification | Brief support |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Financing outflow** | Principal payments on finance leases are financing |
| 2 | **Operating outflow** | Trading securities acquired for resale → operating |
| 3 | **Operating inflow** | Sale of trading securities held for resale → operating |
| 4 | **Investing outflow** | Non-trading debt investment purchase → investing |
| 5 | **Operating inflow** | Trade note principal tied to inventory sale → operating |
| 6 | **Operating inflow** | Interest received → operating |
| 7 | **Financing outflow** | Debt principal settlement / extinguishment cash → financing |
| 8 | **Investing inflow** | Sale of intangible productive asset → investing |
| 9 | **Financing inflow** | Nontrade borrowing → financing |
| 10 | **Financing outflow** | Dividend payment to owners (when paid) → financing |

**b. Period-end cash and beginning cash**

SCF “cash” total at Dec 31, 2025:  
$48,000 + $12,000 + $5,000 = **$65,000**  
*(Exclude $22,000 AFS equity — not cash, cash equivalents, or restricted cash.)*

Net change in cash = $90,000 − $70,000 − $15,000 = **$5,000** increase  

Beginning cash = Ending − Net change = $65,000 − $5,000 = **$60,000**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net cash provided by operating activities | $90,000 |
| Net cash used by investing activities | (70,000) |
| Net cash used by financing activities | (15,000) |
| Net increase in cash | 5,000 |
| Cash at beginning of period | 60,000 |
| **Cash at end of period** | **$65,000** |

**c. Disposal and settlement JEs**

*Patent sale (disposal)*  
Gain = $22,500 − $18,000 = **$4,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 22,500 | |
| Patent (net) | | 18,000 |
| Gain on Sale of Patent | | 4,500 |
| *Sell patent for cash* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,500 = Cr 18,000 + 4,500. **Balanced.**  
**SCF:** **Investing inflow $22,500** (gain is removed from operating if indirect method is used later — LO 22-2; cash still investing).

*Finance-lease principal payment (settlement)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Finance Lease Liability | 9,000 | |
| Cash | | 9,000 |
| *Pay principal portion of finance lease* | | |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.**  
**SCF:** **Financing outflow $9,000**.

**d. Gross presentation**  
**False.** Except for limited items specified in ASC 230, **investing and financing cash inflows and outflows shall be reported separately** (e.g., equipment purchases separate from equipment sale proceeds; borrowings separate from repayments; stock issuances separate from treasury purchases).

**e. Section order**  
1. **Operating activities**  
2. **Investing activities**  
3. **Financing activities**  
Then: net change in cash → beginning cash → **ending cash** (cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash).

**Key insight:** Classification traps cluster around (1) **trading vs investing securities**, (2) **trade vs nontrade notes**, and (3) **interest/dividends received (operating) vs dividends paid and debt principal (financing)**. Period-end, the SCF cash total is broader than “cash in bank” alone when equivalents or restricted cash exist.

### Q4 — MC — Classification choice (two items max)
**LO:** LO 22-1  
**Concept:** Classification of cash flows — interest/dividends and PPE sale proceeds  

**Question 1:**  
**Lumenport Marine Corp.** paid **$14,000** cash interest on its bonds and **$40,000** cash dividends to common shareholders in the same year. How should these amounts be classified on the statement of cash flows under U.S. GAAP?

- A) Interest paid: financing outflow; dividends paid: financing outflow  
- B) Interest paid: operating outflow; dividends paid: financing outflow  
- C) Interest paid: operating outflow; dividends paid: operating outflow  
- D) Interest paid: investing outflow; dividends paid: financing outflow  

**Answer:** **B.** Under U.S. GAAP (ASC 230), cash interest paid is classified as an **operating** activity; cash dividends paid to owners are a **financing** outflow. (Interest is not investing; dividends paid are not operating.)

---

**Question 2:**  
**Sable Peak Co.** sold equipment for **$55,000** cash. The equipment’s book value was **$48,000**, so a **$7,000** gain was included in net income. On the statement of cash flows, the **$55,000** cash is reported as:

- A) An operating inflow of $55,000  
- B) An operating inflow of $7,000 and an investing inflow of $48,000  
- C) An investing inflow of $55,000  
- D) An investing inflow of $48,000 only (gain is noncash)  

**Answer:** **C.** The **full cash proceeds** from sale of PPE are an **investing inflow**. The $7,000 gain is part of net income (and would be **deducted** in the **indirect** operating section under LO 22-2), but it does not split the cash proceeds into operating and investing pieces on the face of the SCF.
