# Agent 175 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-6

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Account for the sale of receivables and use as collateral for borrowing  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Secured borrowing (assignment of AR as collateral): initial loan proceeds, flat finance fee, and note payable; AR remains on books
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** monthly interest on unpaid note balance; remittances applied interest-first then principal; note roll-forward
- Period-end collection, sales-adjustment, write-off, and remittance entries while assigned AR stays recognized
- Settlement/payoff of remaining note payable (principal + final-period interest)
- Sale (factoring) **without recourse**: derecognize AR; finance fee as loss; factor holdback as receivable from factor
- Sale (factoring) **with recourse**: same structure plus **recourse liability**; loss includes estimated recourse cost
- Settlement of factor holdback (actual sales adjustments) and recourse obligation
- Discounting a note receivable as a sale: accrue interest to discount date; bank fee on maturity value for remaining term; remove note at carrying amount
- Classification: sale vs secured borrowing (control / ASC 860 criteria)

### Q1 — CORE — Rivergate Apparel: assignment of AR as collateral with multi-period note schedule
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of secured borrowing; subsequent interest-and-remittance schedule; period-end collection/write-off; settlement of note payable  
**Scenario:**  
**Rivergate Apparel Co.** finances seasonal inventory by assigning trade receivables as collateral for a short-term loan from **Horizon Finance LLC**. Rivergate continues to bill and collect from customers and must remit collections to Horizon as payments on the loan. Assigned receivables remain on Rivergate’s books. Cash discounts are recorded under the **gross method**.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Date of assignment | **October 1, Year 1** |
| Accounts receivable assigned (face) | **$160,000** |
| Loan advance | **75%** of assigned AR |
| Flat finance charge | **1.5%** of the **accounts receivable assigned** (deducted from proceeds) |
| Interest | **12%** per year on the **unpaid note (loan) balance**, computed and settled when cash is remitted |
| October 31, Year 1 | Collect **$52,000** cash on **$56,000** of assigned accounts after **$2,500** sales returns (previously recorded as a refund liability) and **$1,500** sales discounts. Remit the **$52,000** to Horizon. |
| November, Year 1 | Write off **$1,800** of assigned accounts (against Allowance for Doubtful Accounts). Collect an additional **$45,000** cash on assigned accounts. |
| November 30, Year 1 | Pay the **remaining loan balance in full**, including November interest (separate payoff; do not net the $45,000 customer collection into the payoff computation unless instructed—customer cash is available but the required payoff entry is for remaining principal + interest). |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **October 1** journal entry to record receipt of loan proceeds (initial recognition).  
b. Prepare the **October 31** entries to record collection on account and remittance to Horizon.  
c. Prepare a **note payable subsequent-measurement schedule** for October–November showing: beginning balance, interest, cash paid, principal reduction, and ending balance.  
d. Prepare the November collection/write-off entry and the **November 30** loan payoff entry.  
e. Compute the **total cost of borrowing** (finance charge + all interest).

**Answer key:**  

**a. October 1, Year 1 — initial recognition of secured borrowing**  
Note payable = \(0.75 \times \$160{,}000 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}\)  
Finance expense = \(0.015 \times \$160{,}000 = \mathbf{\$2{,}400}\)  
Cash proceeds = \(120{,}000 - 2{,}400 = \mathbf{\$117{,}600}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 117,600 | |
| Finance Expense | 2,400 | |
| Note Payable | | 120,000 |
| *Loan at 75% of assigned AR less flat finance charge; AR not derecognized* | | |

**Check:** Dr 117,600 + 2,400 = Cr 120,000. Balanced.  
(This is a **borrowing**, not a sale—receivables stay on Rivergate’s books.)

**b. October 31 — collection and remittance**  

*Collection on assigned accounts:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 52,000 | |
| Sales Discount | 1,500 | |
| Refund Liability | 2,500 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 56,000 |
| *Cash $52,000 on $56,000 face after $1,500 discounts and $2,500 returns* | | |

**Check:** 52,000 + 1,500 + 2,500 = 56,000. Balanced.

*Remittance to Horizon (interest first, remainder to principal):*  
Interest expense = \(120{,}000 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$1{,}200}\)  
Principal reduction = \(52{,}000 - 1{,}200 = \mathbf{\$50{,}800}\)  
Ending note payable after October = \(120{,}000 - 50{,}800 = \mathbf{\$69{,}200}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 50,800 | |
| Interest Expense | 1,200 | |
| Cash | | 52,000 |
| *Apply October remittance to interest then principal* | | |

**Check:** 50,800 + 1,200 = 52,000. Balanced.

**c. Note payable subsequent-measurement schedule**

| Date | Beg. NP | Interest (12% × 1/12) | Cash paid | Principal reduction | End. NP |
|------|--------:|----------------------:|----------:|--------------------:|--------:|
| Oct 1 (loan recorded) | — | — | — | — | 120,000 |
| Oct 31 | 120,000 | 1,200 | 52,000 | 50,800 | 69,200 |
| Nov 30 (payoff) | 69,200 | 692 | 69,892 | 69,200 | **0** |

November interest = \(69{,}200 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$692}\).  
Cash to pay off = \(69{,}200 + 692 = \mathbf{\$69{,}892}\).

**d. November collection/write-off and loan settlement**  

*Collection and write-off (AR still on books):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 45,000 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | 1,800 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 46,800 |
| *Collect $45,000 and write off $1,800 of assigned receivables* | | |

**Check:** 45,000 + 1,800 = 46,800. Balanced.

*November 30 — payoff of remaining loan:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 69,200 | |
| Interest Expense | 692 | |
| Cash | | 69,892 |
| *Settle remaining principal plus November interest* | | |

**Check:** 69,200 + 692 = 69,892. Balanced.

**e. Total cost of borrowing**  
Finance charge **$2,400** + October interest **$1,200** + November interest **$692** = **$4,292**.  
Verification of remittances to lender for principal and interest: \(52{,}000 + 69{,}892 = 121{,}892\); of which principal \(120{,}000\) and interest \(1{,}892\); plus the initial finance fee embedded in reduced proceeds.

**Key insight:** Under a **secured borrowing / assignment**, AR remains an asset and a **note payable** is recognized. Each remittance is subsequent measurement of the liability: compute interest on the **beginning unpaid balance**, reduce principal by the residual cash, and roll the note forward until final settlement.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Trailhead Outfitters: assignment of AR as collateral
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition of secured borrowing; multi-period note schedule; full settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Trailhead Outfitters Inc.** assigns accounts receivable to **Prairie Capital Finance** as collateral.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Date | **March 1, Year 1** |
| AR assigned | **$300,000** |
| Advance rate | **80%** of assigned AR |
| Finance fee | **$3,000** flat (deducted from proceeds) |
| Interest | **12%** per year on unpaid note balance |
| March 31 | Collect and remit **$110,000** cash from customers on assigned accounts (ignore returns/discounts this month for simplicity). |
| April 30 | Pay remaining loan balance in full (including April interest). |

**Required:**  
a. March 1 journal entry for loan proceeds.  
b. March 31 entry to record remittance of $110,000 (interest + principal).  
c. Note payable roll-forward through April 30 payoff; April 30 payoff entry.  
d. Total finance fee + interest cost.

**Answer key:**  

**a. March 1 — initial recognition**  
Note payable = \(0.80 \times \$300{,}000 = \mathbf{\$240{,}000}\)  
Cash = \(240{,}000 - 3{,}000 = \mathbf{\$237{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 237,000 | |
| Finance Expense | 3,000 | |
| Note Payable | | 240,000 |

**Check:** 237,000 + 3,000 = 240,000. Balanced.

**b. March 31 — remittance**  
Interest = \(240{,}000 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$2{,}400}\)  
Principal reduction = \(110{,}000 - 2{,}400 = \mathbf{\$107{,}600}\)  
Ending NP = \(240{,}000 - 107{,}600 = \mathbf{\$132{,}400}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 107,600 | |
| Interest Expense | 2,400 | |
| Cash | | 110,000 |

**Check:** 107,600 + 2,400 = 110,000. Balanced.

**c. Schedule and April 30 payoff**

| Date | Beg. NP | Interest | Cash paid | Principal red. | End. NP |
|------|--------:|---------:|----------:|---------------:|--------:|
| Mar 1 | — | — | — | — | 240,000 |
| Mar 31 | 240,000 | 2,400 | 110,000 | 107,600 | 132,400 |
| Apr 30 | 132,400 | 1,324 | 133,724 | 132,400 | 0 |

April interest = \(132{,}400 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$1{,}324}\).  
Cash to pay off = \(132{,}400 + 1{,}324 = \mathbf{\$133{,}724}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 132,400 | |
| Interest Expense | 1,324 | |
| Cash | | 133,724 |

**Check:** 132,400 + 1,324 = 133,724. Balanced.

**d. Total cost**  
Finance fee **$3,000** + interest \(2{,}400 + 1{,}324 = 3{,}724\) = **$6,724**.

**Key insight:** Same lifecycle as Q1 with different advance rate, fee amount, and remittance size—always recompute interest on the **remaining** principal each period before reducing principal.

### Q3 — CORE — Lakeshore Components: factoring without/with recourse; holdback & recourse settlement; note discounting
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Sale of receivables without and with recourse (initial JEs); settlement of holdback and recourse; discounting a note receivable (sale)  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakeshore Components Corp.** is evaluating cash-acceleration alternatives on **June 1, Year 1**. Treat each case as independent unless linked.

**Case A — Sale without recourse**  
Factors **$250,000** of accounts receivable **without recourse**. Factor charges an **8%** finance fee and retains **12%** of face AR for potential sales adjustments. Arrangement qualifies as a **sale**.

**Case B — Sale with recourse**  
Factors the same **$250,000** **with recourse**. Factor charges a **4%** finance fee and retains **10%** of face AR for sales adjustments. Lakeshore estimates a **recourse liability** of **$3,500** (no prior allowance has been recorded for these specific accounts). Arrangement qualifies as a **sale**.

**Case C — Subsequent settlement (extends Case A)**  
On **July 15**, actual sales returns and allowances on the factored accounts total **$18,000**. The factor remits the remaining holdback in cash.

**Case D — Settlement of recourse (extends Case B)**  
Later, actual uncollectible accounts on the factored portfolio equal the estimate of **$3,500**. Lakeshore settles the recourse obligation by paying the factor **$3,500** cash (no remaining holdback available for this settlement).

**Case E — Discounting a note receivable (sale)**  
On **January 1, Year 1**, Lakeshore accepted a **$36,000, 10%, one-year** note receivable (interest due at maturity) from a customer. On **April 1, Year 1**, Lakeshore discounts the note **with recourse** to **Metro State Bank**. The bank charges **12%** on the **maturity value** for the **9 months** remaining to maturity. The discounting **qualifies as a sale**.

**Required:**  
a. Case A: seller’s June 1 journal entry.  
b. Case B: seller’s June 1 journal entry.  
c. Case C: July 15 entry to settle the receivable from factor.  
d. Case D: entry to settle the recourse liability.  
e. Case E: (i) April 1 entry to accrue interest to the discount date; (ii) computation of bank proceeds; (iii) entry to record the discounting.  
f. Briefly contrast balance-sheet effects of Case A vs a secured borrowing of the same AR.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Case A — sale without recourse (June 1)**  
Finance fee (loss) = \(0.08 \times \$250{,}000 = \mathbf{\$20{,}000}\)  
Holdback (Receivable from Factor) = \(0.12 \times \$250{,}000 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000}\)  
Cash = \(250{,}000 - 30{,}000 - 20{,}000 = \mathbf{\$200{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 200,000 | |
| Receivable from Factor | 30,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Receivables | 20,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 250,000 |
| *Derecognize AR sold without recourse; holdback for sales adjustments* | | |

**Check:** 200,000 + 30,000 + 20,000 = 250,000. Balanced.

**b. Case B — sale with recourse (June 1)**  
Finance fee = \(0.04 \times \$250{,}000 = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\)  
Holdback = \(0.10 \times \$250{,}000 = \mathbf{\$25{,}000}\)  
Cash = \(250{,}000 - 25{,}000 - 10{,}000 = \mathbf{\$215{,}000}\)  
Loss on sale = finance fee + estimated recourse = \(10{,}000 + 3{,}500 = \mathbf{\$13{,}500}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 215,000 | |
| Receivable from Factor | 25,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Receivables | 13,500 | |
| Recourse Liability | | 3,500 |
| Accounts Receivable | | 250,000 |
| *Sale with recourse: loss includes finance fee and estimated recourse obligation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 215,000 + 25,000 + 13,500 = 253,500; Cr 3,500 + 250,000 = 253,500. Balanced.

**c. Case C — settle factor holdback (July 15)**  
Holdback remaining after actual adjustments: \(30{,}000 - 18{,}000 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\) cash from factor.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 12,000 | |
| Sales Returns and Allowances | 18,000 | |
| Receivable from Factor | | 30,000 |
| *Actual sales adjustments charged against holdback; remainder remitted* | | |

**Check:** 12,000 + 18,000 = 30,000. Balanced.

**d. Case D — settle recourse liability**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recourse Liability | 3,500 | |
| Cash | | 3,500 |
| *Pay factor for actual uncollectibles under with-recourse provision* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 3,500. Balanced.  
(If actual defaults had differed from the estimate, the difference would adjust the loss/gain on settlement; here actual equals estimate.)

**e. Case E — discounting the note (April 1)**  

*(i) Accrue interest January 1 → April 1 (3 months):*  
\(\$36{,}000 \times 0.10 \times 3/12 = \mathbf{\$900}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Receivable | 900 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 900 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 900.

*(ii) Proceeds computation:*

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|-------:|
| Principal | 36,000 |
| Interest to maturity (\(36{,}000 \times 0.10 \times 12/12\)) | 3,600 |
| **Maturity value** | **39,600** |
| Bank discount (\(39{,}600 \times 0.12 \times 9/12\)) | 3,564 |
| **Proceeds to Lakeshore** | **36,036** |

*(iii) Record discounting as a sale:*  
Carrying amount of note + accrued interest = \(36{,}000 + 900 = \mathbf{\$36{,}900}\)  
Loss = \(36{,}900 - 36{,}036 = \mathbf{\$864}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 36,036 | |
| Loss on Sale of Note Receivable | 864 | |
| Note Receivable | | 36,000 |
| Interest Receivable | | 900 |
| *Remove note and accrued interest; difference vs proceeds is loss* | | |

**Check:** 36,036 + 864 = 36,000 + 900 = 36,900. Balanced.

**f. Presentation contrast**  
- **Case A (sale):** AR is **derecognized**; cash and receivable-from-factor recognized; loss on income statement; **no** loan liability for the transfer.  
- **Secured borrowing of same AR:** AR **remains** an asset (disclose as collateral/assigned); cash and **note payable** recognized; finance fee and subsequent interest are **borrowing costs**, not a “loss on sale of receivables.”

**Key insight:** Without recourse, the factor’s fee is the primary seller loss at transfer (later sales adjustments hit the holdback). With recourse, estimated uncollectibles create a **recourse liability** added into the loss. Note discounting mirrors sale accounting: accrue to the transfer date, price the bank fee on **maturity value** for the **remaining** term, and remove the note at carrying amount.

### Q4 — MC — Sale vs secured borrowing classification
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Classification of a receivables arrangement as a sale vs secured borrowing  
**Question:**  
On September 30, **Northwind Distributors** transfers a portfolio of trade receivables to a finance company and receives cash. Which set of facts most strongly supports accounting for the transfer as a **sale** (rather than a secured borrowing)?

- A) Northwind pledges the receivables, continues to collect from customers, remits collections to the lender, and remains fully obligated to repay a note payable regardless of collections.  
- B) Northwind surrenders control: the receivables are isolated from Northwind, the finance company may pledge or exchange the receivables, and Northwind has no obligation to repurchase the receivables; any separate estimated recourse obligation is recognized as a liability.  
- C) Northwind assigns specific receivables under a promissory note that allows the lender to seek payment from receivable proceeds if Northwind defaults, but title and customer billing remain with Northwind.  
- D) The finance company withholds a holdback labeled as a “margin” and charges monthly interest on an unpaid “loan” balance that Northwind must repay even if customers never pay.

**Answer:** B.  
Under ASC 860, a transfer is accounted for as a **sale** only if the transferor **surrenders control** (isolation from the seller, buyer’s right to pledge or exchange, and no effective obligation to repurchase). A recourse obligation **by itself** does not preclude sale accounting. A, C, and D describe **secured borrowing / assignment / pledge** patterns: receivables remain the transferor’s assets and a liability is recognized for the financing.

### Q5 — MC — With-recourse vs without-recourse loss measurement
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Loss on sale measurement — finance fee only vs finance fee plus estimated recourse  
**Question:**  
**Oak & Iron Fabricators** factors **$80,000** of receivables under two alternative contracts that both qualify as sales. Under Contract W (without recourse), the factor charges **10%** and holds back **5%**. Under Contract R (with recourse), the factor charges **6%**, holds back **5%**, and Oak & Iron estimates a **$2,000** recourse liability for expected defaults. Which statement is correct?

- A) Loss under Contract W is $8,000; loss under Contract R is $4,800; both leave AR on the books.  
- B) Loss under Contract W is $8,000; loss under Contract R is $6,800 (finance fee + recourse); AR is derecognized under both.  
- C) Loss under Contract W is $12,000; loss under Contract R is $2,000; holdback is never recognized as an asset.  
- D) Loss under Contract R is only the $2,000 recourse amount because the finance fee is capitalized as a deferred charge.

**Answer:** B.  
Without-recourse loss = finance fee only: \(0.10 \times 80{,}000 = \$8{,}000\). With-recourse loss = finance fee + estimated recourse: \(0.06 \times 80{,}000 + 2{,}000 = \$6{,}800\). In both sale cases AR is **derecognized**; the holdback is a **receivable from the factor**, not left in customer AR.
