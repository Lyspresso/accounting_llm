# Agent 188 — CORE DEMO — LO 10-1

**Chapter:** 10  
**LO title:** Apply lower of cost or net realizable value rule to inventory  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- LCNRV applies to inventory measured by methods **other than LIFO and the retail inventory method** (e.g., FIFO, average cost)
- **NRV** = estimated selling price − reasonably predictable costs of completion, disposal, and transportation
- Three application levels: **individual item** (most common; lowest inventory), **classification/category**, and **inventory in total**
- When NRV < cost: reduce assets (Inventory directly or **Allowance to Reduce Inventory to NRV**) and reduce equity (**COGS** or **Holding Loss on Inventory**)
- No subsequent write-up if NRV recovers on the same goods; reduced amount is “cost” for later periods
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period rollforward of cost, NRV, required LCNRV, required allowance, unadjusted allowance, and period-end adjustment
- Period-end adjusting JE sets allowance to the required credit balance
- On **disposal/sale** of written-down inventory: close remaining allowance into COGS so net COGS equals net carrying amount

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-period LCNRV subsequent measurement schedule, initial write-down, period-end adjusts, sale close-out
**LO:** LO 10-1  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for LCNRV allowance (multi-year rollforward); initial recognition of write-down; period-end adjusting JEs; disposal close of allowance  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Kitchenware Inc.** measures merchandise inventory using **FIFO** (not LIFO or the retail inventory method). It applies the **lower of cost or net realizable value (LCNRV)** rule at the **individual-item** level and maintains an **Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value**. The allowance is remeasured only at each **year-end** reporting date (not at each sale). Charges for write-downs are recorded through **Cost of Goods Sold**.

**January 5, Year 1 — initial inventory purchases (for recognition tracking):** Meridian purchases inventory on account. The purchases that remain on hand at December 31, Year 1 cost **$104,500** in total (detail below). Other purchases during Year 1 are sold before year-end and are already reflected in pre-adjustment COGS.

At **December 31, Year 1**, cost and NRV of ending inventory:

| Item | Category | Cost | NRV |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Cast Skillet | Cookware | $28,000 | $25,500 |
| Sauce Pan Set | Cookware | 15,000 | 16,200 |
| Chef Knife | Cutlery | 42,000 | 44,000 |
| Bread Knife | Cutlery | 19,500 | 17,000 |
| **Total** | | **$104,500** | **$102,700** |

Additional facts:
- Cost of goods sold **before** any LCNRV adjustment for Year 1: **$312,000**.
- All **January 1, Year 2** inventory (Year 1 ending inventory) is sold during Year 2. The Year 1 allowance balance remains on the books until the **December 31, Year 2** year-end adjustment.
- At **December 31, Year 2**, cost of ending inventory is **$118,000** and NRV is **$109,200**.
- All **January 1, Year 3** inventory is sold during Year 3. Allowance is again adjusted only at year-end.
- At **December 31, Year 3**, cost of ending inventory is **$76,000** and NRV is **$72,400**.
- On **April 12, Year 4**, Meridian sells **all** remaining Year 3 ending inventory for cash of **$74,000**. Inventory still carries **$76,000** cost on the books; the allowance still has its December 31, Year 3 credit balance until closed at sale.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **January 5, Year 1** purchase of the inventory that remains at year-end (**$104,500** on account).  
(b) Prepare an **LCNRV measurement schedule** at December 31, Year 1 for application to **(1) individual items**, **(2) each category**, and **(3) inventory in total**. Identify the write-down under the **item** approach used by Meridian.  
(c) Record the **December 31, Year 1** period-end adjusting entry (item approach; charge COGS; credit allowance). Also show the alternative entry charging **Holding Loss on Inventory**.  
(d) Prepare a multi-year **subsequent measurement schedule** of the allowance (emphasis) for Year 1–Year 3 year-ends, showing: inventory cost, NRV, required LCNRV (item approach for Y1; for Y2–Y3 use total cost vs total NRV as given), required allowance, unadjusted allowance balance, and the debit or credit adjustment.  
(e) Record the **December 31, Year 2** and **December 31, Year 3** period-end adjusting entries.  
(f) Record the **April 12, Year 4** sale and the entry to **close the allowance** into COGS. Compute net COGS on this inventory layer and gross profit.

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 5, Year 1 — initial recognition of inventory at cost**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 104,500 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 104,500 |
| *Purchase ending-inventory layer at cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 104,500 = Cr 104,500. **Balanced.**

**(b) December 31, Year 1 — LCNRV schedule**

| Item | Category | Cost | NRV | LCNRV — Item | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Cast Skillet | Cookware | 28,000 | 25,500 | **25,500** | NRV < cost |
| Sauce Pan Set | Cookware | 15,000 | 16,200 | **15,000** | Cost lower |
| Chef Knife | Cutlery | 42,000 | 44,000 | **42,000** | Cost lower |
| Bread Knife | Cutlery | 19,500 | 17,000 | **17,000** | NRV < cost |
| **Totals** | | **104,500** | **102,700** | **99,500** | |

**By category:**  
- Cookware: cost \(28{,}000 + 15{,}000 = 43{,}000\); NRV \(25{,}500 + 16{,}200 = 41{,}700\) → LCNRV **$41,700**  
- Cutlery: cost \(42{,}000 + 19{,}500 = 61{,}500\); NRV \(44{,}000 + 17{,}000 = 61{,}000\) → LCNRV **$61,000**  
- Category total LCNRV = \(41{,}700 + 61{,}000 =\) **$102,700**

**By total inventory:** \(\min(104{,}500,\ 102{,}700) =\) **$102,700**

| Application level | Inventory at LCNRV | Write-down vs cost $104,500 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Individual items | **$99,500** | **$5,000** |
| Categories | $102,700 | $1,800 |
| Total inventory | $102,700 | $1,800 |

Item approach produces the **lowest** inventory (Sauce Pan Set and Chef Knife “surpluses” cannot offset Cast Skillet and Bread Knife deficits).

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE (write-down $5,000)**

(1) Charge COGS (Meridian’s policy):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 5,000 | |
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | | 5,000 |
| *Reduce FIFO inventory to LCNRV (item approach)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,000 = Cr 5,000. **Balanced.**

(2) Alternative — charge separate holding loss:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Holding Loss on Inventory | 5,000 | |
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | | 5,000 |
| *Recognize inventory holding loss; allowance contra to inventory* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,000 = Cr 5,000. **Balanced.**

Year 1 presentation (COGS method):  
Inventory, net = \(104{,}500 - 5{,}000 =\) **$99,500**  
COGS after adjustment = \(312{,}000 + 5{,}000 =\) **$317,000**

**(d) Subsequent measurement schedule — allowance rollforward (emphasis)**

Year 1 required allowance (item approach) = \(104{,}500 - 99{,}500 =\) **$5,000**  
Year 2 required allowance = \(118{,}000 - 109{,}200 =\) **$8,800**  
Year 3 required allowance = \(76{,}000 - 72{,}400 =\) **$3,600**

| Year-end | Inventory cost | NRV | Required LCNRV (net) | Required allowance (Cr) | Unadjusted allow. (Cr) | Period-end adjustment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 12/31/Y1 | $104,500 | $102,700* | **$99,500** (item) | **$5,000** | $0 | **Cr $5,000** (to COGS) |
| 12/31/Y2 | 118,000 | 109,200 | **109,200** | **8,800** | 5,000 | **Cr $3,800** (to COGS) |
| 12/31/Y3 | 76,000 | 72,400 | **72,400** | **3,600** | 8,800 | **Dr $5,200** (from COGS) |

\*Total NRV shown for reference; Year 1 **item** LCNRV of $99,500 drives the $5,000 required allowance.

**Allowance T-account rollforward**

| Date | Description | Allowance Dr | Allowance Cr | Balance (Cr) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 12/31/Y1 | Initial LCNRV write-down | | 5,000 | 5,000 |
| 12/31/Y2 | Period-end top-up | | 3,800 | 8,800 |
| 12/31/Y3 | Period-end reduction (new layer needs less reserve) | 5,200 | | 3,600 |
| 4/12/Y4 | Close on sale of inventory | 3,600 | | 0 |

**Schedule math checks:**  
- Y2 adj: \(8{,}800 - 5{,}000 = 3{,}800\) credit.  
- Y3 adj: \(8{,}800 - 3{,}600 = 5{,}200\) debit.  
- Ending net inventory Y2: \(118{,}000 - 8{,}800 = 109{,}200\).  
- Ending net inventory Y3: \(76{,}000 - 3{,}600 = 72{,}400\).

**(e) Period-end adjusting JEs — Years 2 and 3**

December 31, Year 2:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 3,800 | |
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | | 3,800 |
| *Adjust allowance to Year 2 required LCNRV balance* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,800 = Cr 3,800. **Balanced.** Ending allowance **$8,800** credit.

December 31, Year 3:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | 5,200 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 5,200 |
| *Reduce allowance to Year 3 required balance after inventory turn* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,200 = Cr 5,200. **Balanced.** Ending allowance **$3,600** credit.

**(f) April 12, Year 4 — disposal/settlement of inventory and allowance**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 74,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 74,000 |
| *Cash sale of remaining inventory* | | |

**Check:** Dr 74,000 = Cr 74,000. **Balanced.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 76,000 | |
| Inventory | | 76,000 |
| *Remove inventory at cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 76,000 = Cr 76,000. **Balanced.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | 3,600 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 3,600 |
| *Close allowance; reduces COGS to net carrying amount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,600 = Cr 3,600. **Balanced.**

Net COGS on this layer = \(76{,}000 - 3{,}600 =\) **$72,400** (equals prior net inventory).  
Gross profit = \(74{,}000 - 72{,}400 =\) **$1,600**.

**Key insight:** LCNRV is a subsequent measurement rule: inventory starts at cost, is remeasured at each reporting date when NRV is lower, and the allowance is rolled forward so net inventory always equals the current required LCNRV. When goods are sold, closing the allowance into COGS aligns expense with the already-reduced carrying amount.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Subsequent measurement schedule and LCNRV twin
**LO:** LO 10-1  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — multi-year subsequent LCNRV allowance schedule; item/category/total measurement; initial and period-end JEs  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakewood Appliance Distributors LLC** uses **average cost** for inventory (not LIFO/retail). Policy: **individual-item** LCNRV; **Allowance to Reduce Inventory to NRV**; write-downs (and allowance adjustments) charged or credited through **COGS** at year-end only.

At **December 31, Year 1**:

| Item | Category | Cost | NRV |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Mixer Pro | Small Appliances | $33,000 | $30,000 |
| Blender X | Small Appliances | 14,500 | 15,800 |
| Range Hood | Built-ins | 51,000 | 53,000 |
| Dishwasher | Built-ins | 27,000 | 24,500 |
| **Total** | | **$125,500** | **$123,300** |

- COGS before LCNRV adjustment, Year 1: **$405,000**.
- All Year 1 ending inventory is sold during Year 2; allowance is adjusted only at **December 31, Year 2**.
- At **December 31, Year 2**, cost of ending inventory is **$140,000** and NRV is **$131,000**.
- On **February 28, Year 3**, Lakewood sells **all** Year 2 ending inventory for cash of **$136,500**. Close the allowance at sale.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute LCNRV by item, by category, and in total; state the Year 1 write-down under the item approach.  
(b) Record the Dec 31, Year 1 adjusting entry.  
(c) Prepare the **subsequent measurement schedule** for the allowance through Feb 28, Year 3 (required allowance, unadjusted balance, adjustment; then close-out).  
(d) Record Dec 31, Year 2 adjust and the Feb 28, Year 3 sale + allowance close; state net COGS and gross profit on the Year 2 layer.

**(a) LCNRV schedule**

| Item | Cost | NRV | LCNRV — Item |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mixer Pro | 33,000 | 30,000 | **30,000** |
| Blender X | 14,500 | 15,800 | **14,500** |
| Range Hood | 51,000 | 53,000 | **51,000** |
| Dishwasher | 27,000 | 24,500 | **24,500** |

**By category:**

- Built-ins: cost \(51{,}000 + 27{,}000 = 78{,}000\); NRV \(53{,}000 + 24{,}500 = 77{,}500\) → LCNRV **$77,500**  
- Category total = \(45{,}800 + 77{,}500 =\) **$123,300**

**By total:** \(\min(125{,}500,\ 123{,}300) =\) **$123,300**

|---|---:|---:|

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 3,500 | |
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | | 3,500 |
| *Adjust allowance to Year 2 required balance* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,500 = Cr 3,500. **Balanced.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 140,000 | |
| Inventory | | 140,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | 9,000 | |

### Q3 — CORE — Unit NRV from selling price; period-end JE; direct write-down; method-scope classification
**LO:** LO 10-1  
**Concept:** Compute NRV from SP − disposal costs; item-level LCNRV period-end adjusting JE; direct inventory credit alternative; FIFO/avg use LCNRV vs LIFO/retail use LCM  
**Scenario:**  
**Harbor Point Sporting Goods Co.** uses **FIFO**. It applies LCNRV to **each inventory item** at fiscal year-end **September 30**. Data:

| Item No. | Quantity | Est. selling price per unit | Disposal cost per unit | Cost per unit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SP-40 | 120 | $62 | $4 | $52 |
| SP-55 | 80 | 48 | 5 | 50 |
| SP-70 | 200 | 35 | 2 | 28 |

COGS before any LCNRV adjustment: **$188,000**. Policy: allowance account; charge COGS.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute NRV per unit, extended cost, extended LCNRV (item approach), and the required write-down.  
(b) Record the September 30 period-end adjusting entry using the **allowance**.  
(c) Prepare the alternative entry that **credits Inventory directly** (no allowance) for the same write-down.  
(d) State whether Harbor Point would apply **LCNRV** or **lower of cost or market (LCM)** if it switched to **LIFO**, and why.  
(e) Assume the entire written-down SP-55 lot (80 units) is sold on **October 15** for **$47** per unit cash, and the related portion of the allowance is closed at sale. Record the sale, COGS removal at cost, and allowance close for SP-55 only. (Other items remain on hand; their allowance stays open.)

**Answer key:**

**(a) Unit NRV and totals**

NRV/u = selling price − disposal cost.

| Item | Qty | Cost/u | SP/u | Disp./u | **NRV/u** | LCNRV/u | Extended cost | Extended LCNRV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SP-40 | 120 | 52 | 62 | 4 | **58** | **52** | 6,240 | 6,240 |
| SP-55 | 80 | 50 | 48 | 5 | **43** | **43** | 4,000 | 3,440 |
| SP-70 | 200 | 28 | 35 | 2 | **33** | **28** | 5,600 | 5,600 |
| **Total** | | | | | | | **15,840** | **15,280** |

Write-down = \(15{,}840 - 15{,}280 =\) **$560** (entirely from SP-55: \(80 \times (50 - 43) = 560\)).

**(b) September 30 — period-end adjusting JE (allowance)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 560 | |
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | | 560 |
| *LCNRV write-down, item approach* | | |

**Check:** Dr 560 = Cr 560. **Balanced.**  
Inventory, net = \(15{,}840 - 560 =\) **$15,280**  
COGS after adj = \(188{,}000 + 560 =\) **$188,560**

**(c) Alternative — reduce Inventory directly**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 560 | |
| Inventory | | 560 |
| *Direct write-down of inventory to LCNRV* | | |

**Check:** Dr 560 = Cr 560. **Balanced.**  
Inventory carrying amount = **$15,280** (same net result; no contra account).

**(d) Method-scope classification**  
If Harbor Point switched to **LIFO**, it would apply the **lower of cost or market (LCM)** rule (LO 10-2), **not** LCNRV. LCNRV applies only to methods **other than LIFO and the retail inventory method** (e.g., FIFO, average cost).

**(e) October 15 — disposal of SP-55 layer only**

Cash proceeds = \(80 \times 47 =\) **$3,760**.  
SP-55 cost removed = \(80 \times 50 =\) **$4,000**.  
SP-55 allowance closed = **$560**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 3,760 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 3,760 |
| *Cash sale of SP-55* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,760 = Cr 3,760. **Balanced.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 4,000 | |
| Inventory | | 4,000 |
| *Remove SP-55 at cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 = Cr 4,000. **Balanced.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allowance to Reduce Inventory to Net Realizable Value | 560 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 560 |
| *Close SP-55 allowance into COGS* | | |

**Check:** Dr 560 = Cr 560. **Balanced.**

Net COGS on SP-55 = \(4{,}000 - 560 =\) **$3,440** (= prior net carrying amount).  
Gross profit (loss) = \(3{,}760 - 3{,}440 =\) **$320** gross profit.

**Key insight:** NRV is not selling price alone—completion/disposal/transport costs reduce the ceiling. Only items with NRV below cost create a write-down under the item approach. Closing the related allowance at sale keeps net COGS equal to the LCNRV carrying amount already recognized.

### Q4 — MC (classification / application method)
**LO:** LO 10-1  
**Concept:** Classification of LCNRV vs LCM by inventory cost method; which application level yields the lowest inventory  

**Question 4-1:**  
Which inventory cost method(s) require application of the **lower of cost or net realizable value** rule under ASC 330?

- A) LIFO only  
- B) Retail inventory method only  
- C) Any method **other than** LIFO or the retail inventory method (e.g., FIFO, average cost)  
- D) All inventory methods, including LIFO and retail  

**Answer:** C. ASC 330 requires LCNRV for inventory measured using methods other than LIFO or the retail inventory method. LIFO and retail use lower of cost or **market** (LO 10-2).

---

**Question 4-2:**  
A company applies LCNRV three ways to the same inventory data. Which application level generally produces the **lowest** reported inventory?

- A) Inventory in total (single comparison)  
- B) By major category/classification  
- C) By individual inventory item  
- D) All three levels always produce the same amount  

**Answer:** C. The individual-item approach is most common and produces the lowest inventory because items with NRV > cost cannot offset items with NRV < cost.
