# Agent 97 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-4

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Account for short-term debt and classify debt on the balance sheet  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition** of short-term interest-bearing notes payable at face (when stated = market)
- **Period-end interest accrual** on interest-bearing short-term notes (Interest Expense / Interest Payable)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule** of accrued interest over the note term
- **Maturity / settlement JE** (principal + full cash interest; reverse Interest Payable)
- **Balance sheet presentation** of Note Payable and Interest Payable as current liabilities
- **Noninterest-bearing short-term notes**: Discount on Note Payable, cash proceeds at PV, SL discount amortization to interest expense
- **Carrying amount** of note net of unamortized discount
- **Current maturities of long-term debt** classification
- **Callable long-term debt** / covenant violation classification
- **Debt expected to be refinanced** — intent + ability (post-BS-date long-term financing or agreement) before statements issued
- Short-term debt repaid from **noncurrent assets** (e.g., sinking fund) remains noncurrent

### Q1 — CORE — Interest-bearing short-term note: issue, accrue, present, settle
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for interest-bearing short-term note; period-end adjusting JE; subsequent interest schedule; maturity settlement JE; current liability presentation  
**Scenario:**  
On **November 1, Year 1**, **Meridian Forge Co.** borrows cash and signs a **9-month**, **interest-bearing** note payable. Cash interest is due **in full at maturity**. The stated rate equals the market rate for similar risk. Meridian’s fiscal year-end is **December 31**. Year 1 financial statements are issued on **March 5, Year 2**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Cash proceeds / face of note | $80,000 |
| Stated (and market) annual interest rate | 9% |
| Issuance date | November 1, Year 1 |
| Maturity date | July 31, Year 2 |
| Interest payment pattern | All cash interest paid at maturity with principal |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **November 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry for issuance of the note.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (interest accrual) schedule** showing interest for (i) Nov 1–Dec 31 Year 1, (ii) Jan 1–Jul 31 Year 2, and (iii) total interest over the 9-month term.  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting** journal entry.  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet presentation** of amounts related to this note (ignore other liabilities).  
e. Prepare the **July 31, Year 2 maturity / settlement** journal entry when Meridian pays principal and all interest in cash.

**Answer key:**

**a. November 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (interest-bearing; stated = market)**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 80,000 | |
| Notes Payable | | 80,000 |
| *Record short-term interest-bearing note at face; proceeds equal PV when stated = market* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **80,000**.

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — interest over the note term**

Annual interest if full year: $80,000 × 0.09 = **$7,200**.  
Term = 9 months → total cash interest at maturity: $80,000 × 0.09 × 9/12 = **$5,400**.

| Period | Months | Computation | Interest expense |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Nov 1 – Dec 31, Year 1 | 2 | $80,000 × 0.09 × 2/12 | **$1,200** |
| Jan 1 – Jul 31, Year 2 | 7 | $80,000 × 0.09 × 7/12 | **$4,200** |
| **Total (term)** | **9** | $80,000 × 0.09 × 9/12 | **$5,400** |

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 1,200 | |
| Interest Payable | | 1,200 |
| *Accrue 2 months’ interest: $80,000 × 0.09 × 2/12* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **1,200**.

**d. December 31, Year 1 — classification / presentation**

| Balance sheet (excerpt) — Dec 31, Year 1 | Amount |
|---|---:|
| **Current liabilities** | |
| Notes payable | $80,000 |
| Interest payable | 1,200 |
| **Income statement (excerpt) — Year 1** | |
| Interest expense | $(1,200) |

Both the note (due within one year of the balance sheet date) and accrued interest payable are **current liabilities**.

**e. July 31, Year 2 — maturity / settlement JE**  

Cash paid = principal $80,000 + total interest $5,400 = **$85,400**.  
Year 2 interest expense (remaining 7 months) = **$4,200**.  
Remove Interest Payable from Year 1 accrual = **$1,200**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 80,000 | |
| Interest Payable | 1,200 | |
| Interest Expense ($80,000 × 0.09 × 7/12) | 4,200 | |
| Cash ($80,000 + $5,400) | | 85,400 |
| *Settle face + full cash interest; recognize remaining interest expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 80,000 + 1,200 + 4,200 = **85,400**; Cr = **85,400**. Balanced.  
Roll-forward: Interest Payable beginning $1,200 + new expense $4,200 = $5,400 cash interest paid; Notes Payable removed at face.

**Key insight:** When stated rate = market rate, **initially recognize** the interest-bearing note at **face** for cash. **Accrue** interest at year-end with Interest Payable. At **maturity**, remove Notes Payable and Interest Payable, record remaining Interest Expense, and pay face + full cash interest. The note is a **current liability** because settlement requires current resources within one year.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Interest-bearing short-term note twin

b. Prepare the **interest schedule** for Year 1 accrual months, Year 2 remaining months, and total term interest.  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 1 adjusting** entry.  
d. State Year 1 **balance sheet** amounts for Notes Payable and Interest Payable.

|---|---:|---|---:|

- Notes payable: **$120,000**

Cash paid = $120,000 + $8,000 = **$128,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|

**Key insight:** Same core path as Q1 with different principal, rate, and term: **issue at face → accrue at YE → settle principal + total cash interest**, removing any Interest Payable built up from prior periods.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Noninterest-bearing note (discount) + debt classification scenarios
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of noninterest-bearing short-term note with Discount on Notes Payable; SL amortization schedule; period-end adjusting JE; maturity settlement; balance sheet classification of current maturities, callable debt, refinancing, and noncurrent-asset settlement  
**Scenario — Part A (noninterest-bearing note):**  
On **October 1, Year 1**, **Harborline Metals Inc.** signs a **one-year, noninterest-bearing** note and receives cash equal to the note’s present value. Harborline amortizes the discount using the **straight-line method**. Fiscal year-end is **December 31**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Face (maturity) amount of note | $42,400 |
| Cash proceeds (present value) | $40,000 |
| Discount on note payable at issuance | $2,400 |
| Term | 12 months (matures September 30, Year 2) |
| Stated rate | 0% (noninterest-bearing) |
| Discount amortization | Straight-line over 12 months |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Scenario — Part B (classification only; ignore interest):**  
**Ridgeway Appliance Co.** prepares its **December 31, Year 10** balance sheet. Year 10 financial statements will be issued **March 1, Year 11**. For each independent scenario, determine the amount classified as **current** vs **noncurrent** (ignore Interest Payable).

| # | Debt scenario at December 31, Year 10 |
|---|---|
| 1 | $600,000 installment note; principal installments of $120,000 due each December 31 for the next five years (next installment due Dec 31, Year 11). Remaining principal after next installment is noncurrent. |
| 2 | $150,000 note due in four years. Debt covenant requires current ratio ≥ 1.50. At Dec 31 Year 10, current ratio is 1.20 (CA $360,000 / CL $300,000, note classified long-term in that ratio). Creditor has **not** waived call rights; cure within the contractual grace period is **not** probable before statements are issued. |
| 3 | Same facts as #2, except Ridgeway obtains a written **waiver** from the bank of the right to demand repayment for more than one year from Dec 31, Year 10 (waiver received before statements issued). |
| 4 | $90,000 short-term note due February 15, Year 11. Before statements are issued, Ridgeway **issues long-term debt** for $90,000 and uses those proceeds to refinance (intent and ability demonstrated by actual post-balance-sheet long-term issuance). |
| 5 | $90,000 short-term note due February 15, Year 11. On February 10, Year 11 Ridgeway **pays the note with operating cash**, then on February 20 issues a long-term note for $90,000 (before Year 10 statements are issued). |
| 6 | $200,000 note due December 31, Year 11 that will be paid entirely from a **noncurrent bond sinking fund** investment (not current assets). |

**Required:**  
**Part A**  
a. Record the **October 1, Year 1 initial recognition** entry.  
b. Prepare a **discount amortization / carrying-amount schedule** for Oct–Dec Year 1 and Jan–Sep Year 2; show Dec 31 Year 1 carrying amount.  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 1 adjusting** entry (discount amortization).  
d. Show **December 31, Year 1** balance sheet presentation of the note (net).  
e. Record the **September 30, Year 2 maturity** entry.  

**Part B**  
f. Complete a table of **current** vs **noncurrent** classification amounts for scenarios 1–6. Briefly support each.

**Answer key:**

**Part A**

**a. October 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (noninterest-bearing)**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 40,000 | |
| Discount on Notes Payable | 2,400 | |
| Notes Payable | | 42,400 |
| *Record face of note; discount = face − PV; cash at present value* | | |

**Check:** Dr 40,000 + 2,400 = **42,400**; Cr = **42,400**. Balanced.  
Initial carrying amount = $42,400 − $2,400 = **$40,000**.

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — SL discount amortization**

Monthly amortization = $2,400 ÷ 12 = **$200**.

| Period | Months | Discount amortized (Int. Exp.) | Unamortized discount (end) | Carrying amount (face − unamort. disc.) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| At issuance (Oct 1, Y1) | — | — | $2,400 | $40,000 |
| Oct 1 – Dec 31, Year 1 | 3 | **$600** | $1,800 | **$40,600** |
| Jan 1 – Sep 30, Year 2 | 9 | **$1,800** | $0 | **$42,400** |
| **Total term** | **12** | **$2,400** | — | — |

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 600 | |
| Discount on Notes Payable ($200 × 3) | | 600 |
| *Straight-line amortization of discount for 3 months* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **600**.

**d. Presentation — December 31, Year 1**

| Balance sheet (excerpt) | Amount |
|---|---:|
| **Current liabilities** | |
| Notes payable, net ($42,400 − $1,800 unamortized discount) | **$40,600** |
| **Income statement** | |
| Interest expense | $(600) |

(Alternatively show face $42,400 less Discount $1,800 = net $40,600.)

**e. September 30, Year 2 — maturity / settlement**  

Remaining discount amortization (9 months): $200 × 9 = **$1,800**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 42,400 | |
| Interest Expense | 1,800 | |
| Cash | | 42,400 |
| Discount on Notes Payable | | 1,800 |
| *Pay face; amortize remaining discount to interest expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 42,400 + 1,800 = **44,200**; Cr 42,400 + 1,800 = **44,200**. Balanced.  
Total interest over life = $600 + $1,800 = **$2,400** = original discount (cost of borrowing). Cash out at maturity = face only (**$42,400**); interest was embedded in the discount.

**Part B — classification / presentation**

| # | Current liability | Noncurrent liability | Support |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | **$120,000** | **$480,000** | Current maturity of LTD: next installment current; $600,000 − $120,000 = $480,000 noncurrent. |
| 2 | **$150,000** | **$0** | Covenant violation makes debt **callable**; no waiver and cure not probable → **current** (ASC 470-10-45-11). |
| 3 | **$0** | **$150,000** | Creditor **waived** right to demand repayment for >1 year → may avoid current classification. |
| 4 | **$0** | **$90,000** | Intent + ability: post-BS-date **issuance of long-term debt** refinances the short-term obligation before statements issued (ASC 470-10-45-14). |
| 5 | **$90,000** | **$0** | Paid with **operating cash** before obtaining long-term financing → refinancing criteria **not** met; remains **current**. |
| 6 | **$0** | **$200,000** | Short-term maturity extinguished from **noncurrent assets** (sinking fund) → classified **noncurrent**. |

**Key insight:** Noninterest-bearing short-term notes are recorded at **PV of cash proceeds**, with **Discount on Notes Payable** amortized to interest expense; carrying amount rises to face. Separately, **classification** turns on timing of settlement, **callability / covenants**, and whether **refinancing intent and ability** (or noncurrent-asset settlement) is demonstrated before statements are issued—not merely management’s hope.

### Q4 — MC (classification)
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Balance sheet classification of short-term debt expected to be refinanced  
**Question:**  
At December 31, Year 1, **Summit Bindery Corp.** has a $200,000 note payable due March 15, Year 2. Summit intends to refinance the note on a long-term basis. Before the Year 1 financial statements are issued on February 28, Year 2, which of the following **alone** best supports classifying the entire $200,000 as a **noncurrent** liability at December 31, Year 1?

- A) Management has a board resolution documenting its intent to refinance, but no financing agreement and no post-balance-sheet long-term issuance or equity offering has occurred by the issue date of the statements.  
- B) On February 10, Year 2, Summit issues $200,000 of 10-year bonds and uses the proceeds to pay off the short-term note (before financial statements are issued).  
- C) On January 20, Year 2, Summit pays the $200,000 note with excess cash from operations; on February 15 it issues $200,000 of long-term debt for general corporate purposes (before statements are issued).  
- D) The note is callable by the lender on demand at any time after December 31, Year 1, regardless of refinancing plans.

**Answer:** **B**.  
Under ASC 470-10-45-14, exclusion of a short-term obligation from current liabilities requires **intent** to refinance on a long-term basis **and ability**, demonstrated by (a) post-balance-sheet issuance of a long-term obligation or equity securities, or (b) a qualifying financing agreement, before the financial statements are issued. **B** shows actual long-term issuance used to refinance before the statements are issued. **A** shows intent only—insufficient. **C** settles the short-term debt with **current resources** first, so the short-term note is still reported as **current** at year-end. **D** describes **callable** debt, which is generally **current** because the debtor does not control the payment date.

### Q5 — MC (classification)
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Classification of callable long-term debt after covenant violation  
**Question:**  
**Northlake Logistics LLC** has a $400,000 note due in six years. The note becomes callable if the debt-to-equity ratio exceeds 1.25. At December 31, Year 1, the ratio is 1.40. The agreement allows a 45-day grace period to cure. Financial statements are issued February 20, Year 2. Which treatment is **correct**?

- A) Classify $400,000 as noncurrent because the contractual maturity is still six years away.  
- B) Classify $400,000 as current unless the creditor has waived (or lost) the right to demand repayment for more than one year from the balance sheet date, or it is probable the violation will be cured within the grace period (and before statements are issued).  
- C) Always classify half as current and half as noncurrent when a covenant is violated.  
- D) Classify as noncurrent if management plans to cure the violation after the financial statements are issued.

**Answer:** **B**.  
Long-term obligations that are or will be **callable** because of a debt-agreement violation at the balance sheet date are **current liabilities** unless the creditor has waived/lost the right to demand repayment for more than one year, or (with a grace period) cure is **probable** within that period so the obligation does not become callable (ASC 470-10-45-11). Contractual final maturity alone (**A**) does not control. Arbitrary 50/50 (**C**) is incorrect. Cure plans **after** statements are issued (**D**) do not support noncurrent classification at the balance sheet date.
