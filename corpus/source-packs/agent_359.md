# Agent 359 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-4

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Account for short-term debt and classify debt on the balance sheet  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE** of short-term interest-bearing notes payable at face when stated rate = market rate
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** accrue Interest Expense / Interest Payable for months outstanding before year-end
- **Subsequent measurement schedule** of interest over the note term (pre-YE vs post-YE periods)
- **Maturity / settlement JE:** clear Notes Payable and Interest Payable; record remaining interest expense; pay face + full cash interest
- **Balance sheet presentation** of Note Payable and Interest Payable as current liabilities
- **Noninterest-bearing short-term notes:** Discount on Notes Payable; cash at PV; **straight-line** discount amortization as period-end adjusting entry
- **Carrying amount** = face − unamortized discount
- **Balance sheet classification of debt:** current maturities of long-term debt; callable / covenant-violation debt; short-term debt expected to be refinanced (intent + ability); debt settled from noncurrent assets

### Q1 — CORE — Interest-bearing short-term note: issue, accrue (emphasis), present, settle
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE for interest accrual on an interest-bearing short-term note; initial recognition at face; subsequent interest schedule; maturity settlement JE; current liability presentation  
**Scenario:**  
On **August 1, Year 1**, **Harborline Components LLC** borrows cash and signs a **one-year, interest-bearing** note payable. Cash interest is due **in full at maturity** with principal. The stated rate equals the market rate for similar risk. Harborline’s fiscal year-end is **December 31**. Year 1 financial statements are issued on **March 8, Year 2**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Cash proceeds / face of note | **$120,000** |
| Stated (and market) annual interest rate | **8%** |
| Issuance date | August 1, Year 1 |
| Maturity date | July 31, Year 2 |
| Interest payment pattern | All cash interest paid at maturity with principal |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **August 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry for issuance of the note.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (interest accrual) schedule** showing interest for (i) Aug 1–Dec 31 Year 1, (ii) Jan 1–Jul 31 Year 2, and (iii) total interest over the 12-month term.  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting** journal entry (**emphasis**).  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet presentation** of amounts related to this note (ignore other liabilities) and Year 1 interest expense.  
e. Prepare the **July 31, Year 2 maturity / settlement** journal entry when Harborline pays principal and all interest in cash.

**Answer key:**

**a. August 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (interest-bearing; stated = market)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 120,000 | |
| Notes Payable | | 120,000 |
| *Record short-term interest-bearing note at face; proceeds equal PV when stated = market* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **120,000**. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — interest over the note term**

Annual interest if full year: \(120{,}000 \times 0.08 = \mathbf{\$9{,}600}\).  
Total cash interest at maturity (12 months): \(120{,}000 \times 0.08 \times 12/12 = \mathbf{\$9{,}600}\).

| Period | Months | Computation | Interest expense |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Aug 1 – Dec 31, Year 1 | 5 | \(120{,}000 \times 0.08 \times 5/12\) | **$4,000** |
| Jan 1 – Jul 31, Year 2 | 7 | \(120{,}000 \times 0.08 \times 7/12\) | **$5,600** |
| **Total (term)** | **12** | \(120{,}000 \times 0.08\) | **$9,600** |

**Roll-forward check:** \(4{,}000 + 5{,}600 = 9{,}600\). Cash paid at maturity = face + total interest = \(120{,}000 + 9{,}600 = \mathbf{\$129{,}600}\).

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE (emphasis)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 4,000 | |
| Interest Payable | | 4,000 |
| *Accrue 5 months’ interest: $120,000 × 0.08 × 5/12* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **4,000**. **Balanced.**

**d. December 31, Year 1 — classification / presentation**

| Balance sheet (excerpt) — Dec 31, Year 1 | Amount |
|---|---:|
| **Current liabilities** | |
| Notes payable | **$120,000** |
| Interest payable | **4,000** |
| **Income statement (excerpt) — Year 1** | |
| Interest expense | **$(4,000)** |

Both the note and accrued interest are **current** (the note is due within one year of the balance sheet date; interest payable will be settled with the note).

**e. July 31, Year 2 — maturity / settlement JE**

Remaining interest for Year 2 (7 months) = **$5,600**.  
Cash paid = \(120{,}000 + 9{,}600 = \mathbf{\$129{,}600}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 120,000 | |
| Interest Payable | 4,000 | |
| Interest Expense | 5,600 | |
| Cash | | 129,600 |
| *Pay face + full cash interest; clear prior Interest Payable; record remaining interest expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(120{,}000 + 4{,}000 + 5{,}600 = 129{,}600\); Cr = **129,600**. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** When cash interest is paid only at maturity, the **period-end adjusting entry** is critical—it allocates interest expense to the correct reporting period via Interest Payable. At maturity, reverse (clear) the accrued Interest Payable and expense only the remaining months.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Cedarwick: interest-bearing 6-month note twin
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — period-end Interest Payable adjusting JE; initial recognition at face; interest schedule; maturity settlement  
**Scenario:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |

| Account | Debit | Credit |

| Balance sheet (excerpt) — Dec 31, Year 1 | Amount |
|---|---:|

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 90,000 | |

**Key insight:** Same life-cycle as Q1 with all numbers changed. Half the term falls before year-end and half after—so Year 1 and Year 2 interest expense are equal, but the **period-end adjusting entry** is still required so Year 1 does not omit interest.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Noninterest-bearing note + multi-scenario debt classification
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Period-end SL discount amortization adjusting JE on a noninterest-bearing short-term note; initial recognition at PV; carrying-amount schedule; maturity settlement; multi-scenario balance sheet classification  
**Scenario:**  
**Part A — Noninterest-bearing short-term note**  
On **May 1, Year 1**, **Northfork Packaging Co.** (calendar-year company) signs a **one-year, noninterest-bearing** note and receives cash equal to the note’s present value. The company amortizes the discount using the **straight-line method**. Fiscal year-end is **December 31**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Face (maturity) amount of note | **$51,000** |
| Cash proceeds (present value) | **$48,000** |
| Discount at issuance (face − PV) | **$3,000** |
| Stated rate | **0%** (noninterest-bearing) |
| Market rate implicit in PV | ≈ **6.25%** (given by PV; face due in one year) |
| Term | **12 months** (matures **April 30, Year 2**) |
| Discount amortization | Straight-line over **12** months |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Part B — Balance sheet classification scenarios**  
For each independent scenario at **December 31, Year 1**, state the amount classified as **current** and **noncurrent** (ignore interest payable). Year 1 financial statements are issued **March 1, Year 2**.

| # | Debt scenario (at Dec 31, Year 1) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Northfork has a **$400,000**, 5-year installment note. Principal installments of **$80,000** are due each December 31 for the next five years (including Dec 31 of next year). |
| 2 | Northfork has a **$150,000** note due in **four years**. The debt agreement requires a minimum current ratio of **1.40**. On Dec 31 Year 1 the current ratio is **1.15**, excluding this note from current liabilities. The lender has **not** waived call rights, and it is **not** probable the violation will be cured within the contractual grace period before statements are issued. |
| 3 | Northfork has a **$75,000** short-term note due February 15, Year 2. Management **intends** to refinance on a long-term basis. On **February 20, Year 2** (before statements are issued), Northfork issues **$75,000** of 10-year bonds and uses the proceeds to refinance the short-term note (no interim cash settlement from working capital). |
| 4 | Northfork has a **$60,000** note due June 30, Year 2 that will be paid entirely from a **bond sinking fund** classified as a **noncurrent** asset. |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **May 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry (Part A).  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for the note term: months, discount amortized (interest expense), ending unamortized discount, and ending **carrying amount**.  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting** entry (Part A — emphasis).  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1** net presentation of the note and Year 1 interest expense.  
e. Record the **April 30, Year 2 maturity / settlement** journal entry.  
f. Complete the **classification table** for Part B scenarios 1–4 (current vs noncurrent amounts).

**Answer key:**

**a. May 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (noninterest-bearing)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 48,000 | |
| Discount on Notes Payable | 3,000 | |
| Notes Payable | | 51,000 |
| *Record face; discount = face − PV; cash at present value* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(48{,}000 + 3{,}000 = 51{,}000\); Cr = **51,000**. **Balanced.**  
Initial carrying amount = \(51{,}000 - 3{,}000 = \mathbf{\$48{,}000}\).

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — SL discount amortization**

Monthly amortization = \(3{,}000 \div 12 = \mathbf{\$250}\).

| Period | Months | Discount amortized (Int. exp.) | Ending unamortized discount | Ending carrying amount (face − unamort. disc.) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| At issuance (May 1, Y1) | — | — | 3,000 | **48,000** |
| May 1 – Dec 31, Year 1 | 8 | **2,000** | 1,000 | **50,000** |
| Jan 1 – Apr 30, Year 2 | 4 | **1,000** | 0 | **51,000** |
| **Total term** | **12** | **3,000** | — | — |

**Roll-forward check:** \(48{,}000 + 3{,}000 = 51{,}000\) (beginning CA + total interest = face).  
Unamortized discount Dec 31 Y1: \(3{,}000 - 2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}000}\); CA = \(51{,}000 - 1{,}000 = \mathbf{\$50{,}000}\).

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE (emphasis)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 2,000 | |
| Discount on Notes Payable ($250 × 8) | | 2,000 |
| *Amortize 8 months of discount (May–Dec)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **2,000**. **Balanced.**

**d. December 31, Year 1 — classification / presentation**

| Balance sheet (excerpt) — Dec 31, Year 1 | Amount |
|---|---:|
| **Current liabilities** | |
| Notes payable, net ($51,000 − $1,000 unamortized discount) | **$50,000** |
| **Income statement (excerpt) — Year 1** | |
| Interest expense | **$(2,000)** |

The entire note is **current** (due within one year). Discount is a **contra** to Notes Payable; report the **net** carrying amount.

**e. April 30, Year 2 — maturity / settlement JE**

Remaining discount amortization (4 months) = \(250 \times 4 = \mathbf{\$1{,}000}\) = Year 2 interest expense.  
Cash paid = face **$51,000** (no separate cash interest on a noninterest-bearing note).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 51,000 | |
| Interest Expense | 1,000 | |
| Cash | | 51,000 |
| Discount on Notes Payable | | 1,000 |
| *Pay face; amortize remaining discount to interest expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(51{,}000 + 1{,}000 = 52{,}000\); Cr \(51{,}000 + 1{,}000 = 52{,}000\). **Balanced.**  
Total interest over term: \(2{,}000 + 1{,}000 = 3{,}000\) = original discount. **Proven.**

**f. Part B — classification table**

| # | Current liability | Noncurrent liability | Reasoning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | **$80,000** | **$320,000** | Current maturities of long-term debt: next installment is current; remainder noncurrent (\(400{,}000 - 80{,}000\)). |
| 2 | **$150,000** | **$0** | Covenant violation makes the debt **callable** → classify entire amount as **current** (no waiver; cure not probable within grace period). |
| 3 | **$0** | **$75,000** | Intent + ability demonstrated by **post-balance-sheet-date** issuance of long-term debt used to refinance before statements issued → exclude from current liabilities. |
| 4 | **$0** | **$60,000** | Short-term obligation extinguished from a **noncurrent asset** (sinking fund) → report as **noncurrent**. |

**Key insight:** Noninterest-bearing notes still create interest expense through **discount amortization**—the period-end adjusting entry amortizes Discount on Notes Payable rather than accruing Interest Payable. Separately, classification can flip current ↔ noncurrent for callable debt, refinancings, current maturities, and settlement from noncurrent assets.

### Q4 — MC — Short-term debt expected to be refinanced
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Balance sheet classification of short-term debt expected to be refinanced (intent and ability)  
**Question:**  
At December 31, Year 1, **Quarry Ridge Foods** has a **$200,000** note payable due February 28, Year 2. Management intends to refinance the note on a long-term basis. Which of the following facts allows Quarry Ridge to classify the **entire $200,000** as a **noncurrent** liability on the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet? Year 1 financial statements are issued March 15, Year 2.

- A) On January 10, Year 2, Quarry Ridge pays the $200,000 note from existing cash, then on March 1, Year 2 issues $200,000 of 8-year bonds.  
- B) On March 5, Year 2 (before statements are issued), Quarry Ridge issues $200,000 of 8-year bonds and uses the proceeds to refinance the short-term note.  
- C) Management has only a verbal plan to refinance; no financing agreement exists and no long-term debt or equity has been issued by the statement issuance date.  
- D) On March 20, Year 2 (after statements are issued), Quarry Ridge enters a noncancelable long-term refinancing agreement with a viable lender.

**Answer:** **B.**  
To reclassify short-term debt as noncurrent, the entity must have **intent** to refinance on a long-term basis **and** demonstrate **ability** before the financial statements are issued—by actually issuing long-term debt or equity (or entering a qualifying financing agreement). Option B meets both tests.  
A fails because repayment from **current cash** before obtaining long-term financing means the short-term obligation was settled with current assets—classification remains **current**.  
C has intent only, not ability.  
D occurs **after** statements are issued, so it cannot support Year 1 noncurrent classification.

### Q5 — MC — Callable debt due to covenant violation
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Classification of long-term debt that is callable because of a covenant violation  
**Question:**  
**Pine Harbor Distillers** has a **$500,000** note due in seven years. The loan requires a minimum current ratio of **1.50**. At December 31, Year 1, Pine Harbor’s current ratio is **1.20** (computed excluding the $500,000 note from current liabilities). The agreement provides a 45-day grace period to cure. Which conclusion is correct for the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet if the lender has **not** waived its call rights and cure within the grace period is **not** probable before the financial statements are issued?

- A) Classify $0 as current and $500,000 as noncurrent because the contractual maturity is seven years away.  
- B) Classify $500,000 as current because the covenant violation makes the obligation **callable** by the creditor.  
- C) Classify only one-seventh ($≈71,429) as current based on straight-line allocation of the term.  
- D) Classify $500,000 as noncurrent if management merely intends to cure the ratio after year-end, even if cure is not probable within the grace period.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Long-term obligations that are or will be **callable** because of a covenant violation at the balance sheet date are classified as **current** unless the creditor has waived/lost the right to demand repayment for more than one year, or it is **probable** the violation will be cured within the grace period (preventing callability). Neither exception applies here, so the **entire** $500,000 is current. Contractual final maturity (A) and arbitrary pro-rata allocation (C) do not control. Mere intent without probable cure (D) is insufficient.
