# Agent 262 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-8

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for internally developed software and cloud computing arrangements  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Software to be sold/leased (ASC 985-20):** expense as R&D until **technological feasibility**; capitalize product-master costs after feasibility until product release
- **Amortization of capitalized software to be sold:** commence when product is marketed; annual amortization = **greater of** revenue method or straight-line method
- **Revenue method:** (current product revenue ÷ total anticipated product revenue) × capitalized software costs (apply to remaining unamortized cost after Year 1 of marketing)
- **Internal-use software (ASC 350-40):** preliminary project stage → expense; application development stage → capitalize; postimplementation/operations → expense; training costs always expensed
- **Internal-use amortization:** generally straight-line once software is substantially complete and ready for intended use
- **Cloud hosting arrangement:** software purchase only if customer has right to take possession **without significant penalty** **and** can run/host elsewhere; otherwise **service contract** (more common)
- **Service-contract cloud implementation costs:** preliminary stage expense; development/implementation stage capitalize and amortize (usually SL over hosting term); post-implementation expense; subscription fees expensed
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** year-end amortization of capitalized software / cloud implementation costs
- **Classification / presentation (cloud service contract):** capitalized implementation costs presented like a prepayment of hosting fees; amortization in same IS line as hosting fees; cash flows classified like hosting fees
- **Disposal / end-of-life:** write off remaining unamortized software or implementation costs when abandoned or when hosting term ends with zero residual

### Q1 — CORE — Software developed for sale: initial recognition, greater-of amortization schedule, period-end adjusting JEs
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of software-to-be-sold costs (R&D until technological feasibility; capitalize product masters after); subsequent measurement using greater of revenue vs straight-line; period-end amortization adjusting JEs (emphasis); classification of the intangible  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Analytics Inc.** (calendar-year) develops a commercial data-analytics package expected to be **sold** to third parties (not solely for internal use).

| Timing / event | Amount (all cash) |
|---|---:|
| Years 1–2: planning, designing, coding, and testing to establish **technological feasibility** (working model completed Dec. 31, Year 2) | **$2,400,000** |
| Year 3: final coding, testing, debugging, and production of **product masters** (after technological feasibility; before release) | **$800,000** |
| Jan. 1, Year 4: product **available for sale / marketing begins**; estimated economic life | **4 years** |

| Year | Current product revenue | Total anticipated product revenue *remaining at beginning of year (including current year)* | Unamortized capitalized cost at beginning of year |

- Greater = **150,000** → fully amortized

**Roll-forward check:** \(200{,}000 + 300{,}000 + 150{,}000 + 150{,}000 = 800{,}000\). ✓

| Statement | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Balance sheet** | Software intangible asset (or computer software costs) reported among **intangible assets**, net of accumulated amortization / reductions: carrying amount **$600,000** |
| **Income statement** | Software amortization expense **$200,000** (operating expense; not R&D once product is released) |
| **Contrast** | Pre-feasibility **$2,400,000** was **R&D expense** in Years 1–2 |

**Key insight:** For software developed for **sale**, costs before technological feasibility are **R&D expense**; after feasibility, product-master costs are **capitalized**. Amortization begins when marketing starts and each year equals the **greater of** the revenue method and straight-line — so the period-end adjusting entry can switch which method “wins” as the revenue pattern changes (Year 4 SL, Year 5 revenue method).

### Q4 — MC — Cloud arrangement classification and cost treatment
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Classification of a hosting arrangement as service contract vs software; which costs capitalize  
**Question:**  
**Northwind Retail Group** signs a four-year contract giving it online access to a vendor’s ERP system hosted by the vendor. Northwind **cannot** take possession of the software without significant penalty. During the setup phase Northwind pays: (1) $40,000 to evaluate vendor options, (2) $120,000 to external contractors for configuration and testing before go-live, (3) $25,000 to train employees, and (4) $80,000 annual access fee (first year paid at signing for Year 1 access). How should Northwind account for the arrangement, and what total amount is **capitalized** at initial setup (before amortization)?

- A) Software purchase; capitalize $265,000 (all costs including first-year access fee)  
- B) Service contract; capitalize $120,000 (configuration/testing implementation only)  
- C) Service contract; capitalize $185,000 (implementation + training + evaluation)  
- D) Software purchase; capitalize $160,000 (evaluation + configuration/testing)

**Answer:** **B.**  
Because Northwind lacks a right to take possession without significant penalty, the arrangement is a **service contract**, not a software purchase. Under the service-contract path: **preliminary** evaluation costs are expensed; **development-stage implementation** (configuration/testing) **$120,000** is capitalized; **training** is expensed; the **annual access/subscription fee** is expensed (or prepaid and expensed over the access period) — not capitalized as software. Thus only **$120,000** is capitalized as implementation costs at setup.

### Q5 — MC — Software-to-be-sold cost stage classification
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Classification of software development costs before vs after technological feasibility and after product release  
**Question:**  
**Lakeshore Codeworks** is developing software it will **sell** to customers. Which statement is correct under ASC 985-20?

- A) All coding costs from project start until first customer sale are capitalized as an intangible asset.  
- B) Costs after technological feasibility to produce product masters are capitalized; costs before technological feasibility are R&D expense; post-release maintenance and customer support are expensed as operating (non-R&D) costs.  
- C) Costs after technological feasibility are always expensed as R&D until the product has positive net cash flows.  
- D) Capitalized software costs are amortized using only the revenue method; straight-line is not considered.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Technological feasibility is the bright line: **before** → R&D expense; **after** (to product masters, until release) → **capitalize**; **after release** → further costs are generally **operating expenses**, not R&D. Amortization of capitalized amounts uses the **greater of** revenue method and straight-line, so D is incorrect. A ignores the feasibility cutoff. C misstates post-feasibility treatment.
