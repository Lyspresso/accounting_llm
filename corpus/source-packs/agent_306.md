# Agent 306 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-6

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Account for the sale of receivables and use as collateral for borrowing  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Secured borrowing (assignment/pledge):** receivables remain on books; record Note Payable for loan proceeds; finance fee expensed at inception
- **Initial recognition JE:** loan proceeds = advance % × face AR − flat finance fee; Dr Cash, Dr Finance Expense, Cr Note Payable
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** unpaid loan balance rolls forward after each remittance (principal reduction = cash remitted − interest on unpaid balance)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** at each month-end, accrue interest on unpaid Note Payable and allocate customer collections remitted between interest and principal
- **Collections / write-offs while assigned:** borrower still owns AR → record cash collections, discounts, returns, and write-offs on its own books
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** final payoff of remaining Note Payable + final period interest (loan settled)
- **Sale without recourse:** derecognize AR; Loss = finance fee % × face; holdback = Receivable from Factor
- **Sale with recourse:** same as without + Recourse Liability; Loss = finance fee + estimated recourse
- **Discounting a note receivable:** accrue interest to discount date, remove NR + interest receivable, record cash proceeds and Loss (or Gain)
- **Classification:** sale only if seller surrenders control (ASC 860); otherwise secured borrowing
- **Number-variant twin:** same secured-borrowing life cycle with all different amounts/rates

### Q1 — CORE — Secured borrowing life cycle: initial recognition, collection remittances, loan schedule, period-end interest JEs (emphasis), final settlement

**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Using AR as collateral for borrowing — initial loan JE; subsequent loan-balance schedule; **period-end adjusting JEs** for interest and remittance allocation (emphasis); settlement of remaining Note Payable  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Components LLC** (calendar year) needs short-term cash. On **September 30, Year 1**, Harborline assigns **$150,000** of accounts receivable as collateral for a loan from Meridian Finance. Harborline retains title to the receivables, continues collections, bears bad-debt risk, and remits customer collections as payments on the loan.

**Contract terms:**  
- Loan advance = **80%** of assigned receivables  
- Flat finance fee = **$3,000** (deducted from proceeds at inception)  
- Interest = **12%** per year on the **unpaid loan balance**, payable at the **end of each month**  

**Subsequent events:**  
1. **October 31, Year 1 (period-end):** Harborline collected **$65,000 cash** on **$72,000** of assigned accounts after **$5,000** sales returns (already recorded as Refund Liability) and **$2,000** sales discounts (gross method). Harborline remits the entire **$65,000** to Meridian Finance.  
2. **November:** **$4,000** of assigned accounts are written off against Allowance for Doubtful Accounts; another **$40,000** is collected on account (still assigned).  
3. **November 30, Year 1 (period-end / settlement):** Harborline pays the remaining loan balance **in full** with cash (from collections and/or other sources). Interest for November is paid with the final settlement.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **September 30, Year 1** journal entry to record the **initial recognition** of the secured borrowing (loan proceeds).  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (loan amortization) schedule** from Sept 30 through final payoff showing beginning balance, interest, principal reduction, cash remitted, and ending balance.  
c. Prepare the **October 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting / remittance entries**: (1) collection on assigned accounts and (2) remittance to Meridian allocating cash between interest and principal.  
d. Prepare the **November** entry for collections and write-off of assigned accounts.  
e. Prepare the **November 30, Year 1 settlement JE** paying off the remaining Note Payable (including November interest).  
f. Compute **total financing cost** of the arrangement (finance fee + all interest).  
g. Briefly explain why this arrangement is a **secured borrowing**, not a sale (control / ownership).

**Answer key:**  

**a. September 30, Year 1 — initial recognition of secured borrowing**  
Note Payable = \(0.80 \times \$150{,}000 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}\).  
Cash proceeds = \(120{,}000 - 3{,}000 = \mathbf{\$117{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 117,000 | |
| Finance Expense | 3,000 | |
| Note Payable | | 120,000 |
| *To record loan secured by $150,000 AR; fee deducted from proceeds* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(117{,}000 + 3{,}000 = 120{,}000\) = Cr 120,000. **Balanced.**  
Receivables **remain** on Harborline’s books (no AR derecognition).

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (loan balance roll-forward)**  
Monthly interest rate = \(12\% \times 1/12 = 1\%\).

| Date | Beg. NP | Interest (1% × beg.) | Cash remitted | Principal reduction | End. NP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sept 30, Y1 | — | — | — | — | **120,000** |
| Oct 31, Y1 | 120,000 | **1,200** | **65,000** | **63,800** | **56,200** |
| Nov 30, Y1 | 56,200 | **562** | **56,762** | **56,200** | **0** |

Interest Oct: \(120{,}000 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$1{,}200}\).  
Principal Oct: \(65{,}000 - 1{,}200 = \mathbf{\$63{,}800}\).  
End Oct NP: \(120{,}000 - 63{,}800 = \mathbf{\$56{,}200}\).  
Interest Nov: \(56{,}200 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$562}\).  
Final cash: \(56{,}200 + 562 = \mathbf{\$56{,}762}\).

**Roll-forward:** \(120{,}000 - 63{,}800 - 56{,}200 = 0\).  
Total cash to lender: \(65{,}000 + 56{,}762 = 121{,}762 = 120{,}000\) principal \(+ 1{,}762\) interest. ✓

**c. October 31, Year 1 — period-end collection and remittance (emphasis)**  

**(1) Collection on assigned accounts**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 65,000 | |
| Sales Discount | 2,000 | |
| Refund Liability | 5,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 72,000 |
| *Collect assigned AR net of discounts and returns previously in refund liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(65{,}000 + 2{,}000 + 5{,}000 = 72{,}000\) = Cr 72,000. **Balanced.**

**(2) Period-end remittance to finance company (interest + principal)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 63,800 | |
| Interest Expense | 1,200 | |
| Cash | | 65,000 |
| *Period-end: remit collections; allocate interest on unpaid NP then reduce principal* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(63{,}800 + 1{,}200 = 65{,}000\) = Cr 65,000. **Balanced.**

**d. November — collection and write-off (AR still on books)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 40,000 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | 4,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 44,000 |
| *Collect $40,000 and write off $4,000 of assigned AR* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(40{,}000 + 4{,}000 = 44{,}000\) = Cr 44,000. **Balanced.**

**e. November 30, Year 1 — disposal / settlement of remaining loan**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 56,200 | |
| Interest Expense | 562 | |
| Cash | | 56,762 |
| *Pay remaining NP + November interest; loan fully settled* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(56{,}200 + 562 = 56{,}762\) = Cr 56,762. **Balanced.**  
Note Payable ending balance = **$0**.

**f. Total financing cost**  
Finance fee \(3{,}000\) + Oct interest \(1{,}200\) + Nov interest \(562\) = **\$4,762**.

**g. Sale vs secured borrowing**  
Harborline **retains title**, continues collections, bears credit risk, and is **obligated to repay** Meridian. Receivables are **not isolated** from the seller and control has not been surrendered → **secured borrowing**: AR stays on books; **liability** recognized. Not a sale (no AR derecognition; no loss on sale of receivables).

**Key insight:** In a secured borrowing, AR **never leaves** the books. Each **period-end**, interest is measured on the **unpaid Note Payable**, and remitted collections are split: first interest, then principal. The loan schedule must roll to zero at settlement; total cash to the lender = principal advanced + all interest.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Secured borrowing life cycle (all numbers changed)

**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Same path as Q1 — initial secured-borrowing JE, loan schedule, **period-end interest/remittance adjusting JEs**, final settlement — with all different amounts and rate  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarpoint Metals Corp.** assigns **$200,000** of AR as collateral on **March 1, Year 1**.

**Contract terms:**  
- Advance = **75%** of assigned AR  
- Flat finance fee = **$2,250** (1.5% of face AR, deducted at inception)  
- Interest = **12%** per year on unpaid balance, paid monthly at period-end  

**Subsequent events:**  
1. **March 31, Year 1:** Collected **$74,000 cash** on **$82,000** assigned AR after **$5,000** sales returns (Refund Liability) and **$3,000** sales discounts. Remits **$74,000** to the finance company.  
2. **April:** Write off **$2,500** assigned AR against AFDA; collect another **$50,000** on assigned accounts.  
3. **April 30, Year 1:** Pays remaining loan **in full** (including April interest).

**Required:**  
a. March 1 initial recognition JE.  
b. Loan amortization schedule (Mar 1 → final payoff).  
c. March 31 period-end collection + remittance JEs.  
d. April collection/write-off JE.  
e. April 30 settlement JE.  
f. Total financing cost.

**Answer key:**  

**a. March 1 — initial recognition**  
Note Payable = \(0.75 \times \$200{,}000 = \mathbf{\$150{,}000}\).  
Cash = \(150{,}000 - 2{,}250 = \mathbf{\$147{,}750}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 147,750 | |
| Finance Expense | 2,250 | |
| Note Payable | | 150,000 |
| *Loan secured by $200,000 AR; $2,250 finance fee* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(147{,}750 + 2{,}250 = 150{,}000\) = Cr 150,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule**  
Monthly interest rate = 1%.

| Date | Beg. NP | Interest | Cash remitted | Principal reduction | End. NP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1, Y1 | — | — | — | — | **150,000** |
| Mar 31, Y1 | 150,000 | **1,500** | **74,000** | **72,500** | **77,500** |
| Apr 30, Y1 | 77,500 | **775** | **78,275** | **77,500** | **0** |

Interest Mar: \(150{,}000 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$1{,}500}\).  
Principal Mar: \(74{,}000 - 1{,}500 = \mathbf{\$72{,}500}\).  
End Mar NP: \(150{,}000 - 72{,}500 = \mathbf{\$77{,}500}\).  
Interest Apr: \(77{,}500 \times 0.01 = \mathbf{\$775}\).  
Final cash: \(77{,}500 + 775 = \mathbf{\$78{,}275}\).

**Roll-forward:** \(150{,}000 - 72{,}500 - 77{,}500 = 0\).  
Total cash to lender: \(74{,}000 + 78{,}275 = 152{,}275 = 150{,}000 + 2{,}275\) interest. ✓

**c. March 31 — period-end collection and remittance (emphasis)**  

**(1) Collection**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 74,000 | |
| Sales Discount | 3,000 | |
| Refund Liability | 5,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 82,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(74{,}000 + 3{,}000 + 5{,}000 = 82{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**(2) Remittance (period-end interest + principal)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 72,500 | |
| Interest Expense | 1,500 | |
| Cash | | 74,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(72{,}500 + 1{,}500 = 74{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**d. April — collection and write-off**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 50,000 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | 2,500 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 52,500 |

**Check:** Dr \(50{,}000 + 2{,}500 = 52{,}500\). **Balanced.**

**e. April 30 — settlement**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 77,500 | |
| Interest Expense | 775 | |
| Cash | | 78,275 |

**Check:** Dr \(77{,}500 + 775 = 78{,}275\). **Balanced.** NP = **$0**.

**f. Total financing cost**  
Fee \(2{,}250\) + Mar interest \(1{,}500\) + Apr interest \(775\) = **\$4,525**.

**Key insight:** Changing advance %, fee, AR face, and remittance amounts changes every line of the schedule, but the **period-end logic is invariant**: interest = unpaid NP × periodic rate; principal reduction = cash remitted − interest; final settlement clears NP to zero.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Factoring without/with recourse (initial recognition + holdback/recourse settlement) and discounting a note receivable

**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Sale of AR without recourse and with recourse (initial recognition JEs); **settlement** of factor holdback and recourse liability; discounting a note receivable (accrual + disposal as sale)  
**Scenario:**  
**Northridge Outfitters Inc.** accelerates cash through three independent financing arrangements (treat separately).

**Arrangement 1 — Sale without recourse (Dec 1, Year 1)**  
Factors **$250,000** of AR **without recourse**. Factor charges a **10%** finance fee and retains **8%** holdback for sales adjustments. Arrangement qualifies as a **sale**.

**Arrangement 2 — Sale with recourse (Dec 1, Year 1)**  
Factors **$250,000** of AR **with recourse**. Factor charges a **5%** finance fee and retains **6%** holdback. Northridge estimates **recourse liability** for bad debts of **$4,000**. Arrangement qualifies as a **sale**.

**Arrangement 3 — Settlement of Arrangement 1 holdback (Jan 15, Year 2)**  
Actual sales returns/allowances against the factored AR = **$12,000**. Factor remits the remaining holdback in cash. Northridge had not previously accrued the returns.

**Arrangement 4 — Discounting a note (disposal path)**  
On **April 1**, Northridge received a **$60,000, 8%, one-year** note receivable (interest due at maturity) from a customer. On **July 1**, Northridge **discounts the note with recourse** to State Bank. The discounting **qualifies as a sale**. Bank charges **10%** on the **maturity value** for the **9 months** remaining to maturity.

**Required:**  
a. Dec 1 JE — sale **without** recourse (seller only).  
b. Dec 1 JE — sale **with** recourse (seller only).  
c. Jan 15 settlement JE for holdback under Arrangement 1.  
d. For Arrangement 4: (1) July 1 interest accrual to discount date; (2) July 1 discounting JE; show proceeds calculation.  
e. Compare **loss on sale** without recourse vs with recourse and explain the difference.  
f. If Arrangement 2’s actual credit losses equal the $4,000 estimate, prepare the settlement of the **recourse liability** when paid in cash.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Sale without recourse — Dec 1 initial recognition**  
Finance fee = \(0.10 \times 250{,}000 = \$25{,}000\).  
Holdback (Receivable from Factor) = \(0.08 \times 250{,}000 = \$20{,}000\).  
Cash = \(250{,}000 - 20{,}000 - 25{,}000 = \mathbf{\$205{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 205,000 | |
| Receivable from Factor | 20,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Receivables | 25,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 250,000 |
| *Factor without recourse; AR derecognized; fee = loss* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(205{,}000 + 20{,}000 + 25{,}000 = 250{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**b. Sale with recourse — Dec 1 initial recognition**  
Finance fee = \(0.05 \times 250{,}000 = \$12{,}500\).  
Holdback = \(0.06 \times 250{,}000 = \$15{,}000\).  
Cash = \(250{,}000 - 15{,}000 - 12{,}500 = \mathbf{\$222{,}500}\).  
Loss = fee \(12{,}500\) + recourse estimate \(4{,}000\) = **\$16,500**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 222,500 | |
| Receivable from Factor | 15,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Receivables | 16,500 | |
| Recourse Liability | | 4,000 |
| Accounts Receivable | | 250,000 |
| *Factor with recourse; AR derecognized; loss includes estimated recourse* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(222{,}500 + 15{,}000 + 16{,}500 = 254{,}000\); Cr \(4{,}000 + 250{,}000 = 254{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**c. Settlement of holdback (Arrangement 1) — Jan 15**  
Actual returns \(12{,}000\); remaining cash from factor \(20{,}000 - 12{,}000 = \$8{,}000\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 8,000 | |
| Sales Returns and Allowances | 12,000 | |
| Receivable from Factor | | 20,000 |
| *Settle factor holdback for actual sales adjustments* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(8{,}000 + 12{,}000 = 20{,}000\). **Balanced.** Receivable from Factor → **$0**.

**d. Discounting note receivable**  

**Proceeds calculation**  
Principal — $60,000  
Add: interest to maturity \(60{,}000 \times 0.08 \times 12/12\) — 4,800  
**Maturity value** — **$64,800**  
Less: bank discount \(64{,}800 \times 0.10 \times 9/12\) — **4,860**  
**Proceeds** — **$59,940**

**(1) July 1 — accrue interest Apr 1 → July 1 (3 months)**  
\(60{,}000 \times 0.08 \times 3/12 = \mathbf{\$1{,}200}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Receivable | 1,200 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 1,200 |
| *Accrue interest to date of discounting* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,200 = Cr 1,200. **Balanced.**

**(2) July 1 — record sale (discounting) of note**  
Carrying amount = \(60{,}000 + 1{,}200 = \$61{,}200\).  
Loss = \(61{,}200 - 59{,}940 = \mathbf{\$1{,}260}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 59,940 | |
| Loss on Sale of Note Receivable | 1,260 | |
| Note Receivable | | 60,000 |
| Interest Receivable | | 1,200 |
| *Discount note with recourse (qualifies as sale); remove NR and accrued interest* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(59{,}940 + 1{,}260 = 61{,}200\); Cr \(60{,}000 + 1{,}200 = 61{,}200\). **Balanced.**  
After July 1, the note is **no longer an asset** of Northridge.

**e. Loss comparison**  
- Without recourse loss = **$25,000** (finance fee only; factor bears credit risk → higher fee).  
- With recourse loss = **$16,500** (lower fee $12,500 + estimated recourse $4,000).  
With-recourse fee is lower because seller retains credit risk, but the **estimated recourse** increases loss; net loss can be lower or higher depending on fee differential vs recourse estimate.

**f. Settlement of recourse liability (actual losses = estimate)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recourse Liability | 4,000 | |
| Cash | | 4,000 |
| *Pay factor under recourse provision; settles estimated obligation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 = Cr 4,000. **Balanced.**  
(If actual losses differed from $4,000, adjust Loss on Sale of Receivables for the difference.)

**Key insight:** A **sale** derecognizes AR (or NR) and records a **loss** equal to fees plus any estimated **recourse**. Holdback is a **receivable from the factor**, settled when actual sales adjustments are known. Discounting a note requires **accruing interest to the discount date** first, then comparing proceeds to carrying amount to measure gain/loss.

### Q4 — MC — Sale vs secured borrowing classification

**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Classify arrangement as sale of receivables vs secured borrowing (control criteria)  
**Question:**  
Which arrangement is accounted for as a **sale of receivables** (derecognize AR and recognize gain/loss) rather than a secured borrowing?

- A) Company pledges all AR as collateral, retains collections, and remains obligated to repay the bank loan principal plus interest.  
- B) Company assigns specific AR, receives 80% advance less a finance fee, remits collections to the lender, and keeps AR on its books.  
- C) Company transfers AR to a factor **without recourse**, the factor may pledge or exchange the receivables, receivables are isolated from the seller, and the seller has no repurchase obligation; factor retains a holdback for sales adjustments.  
- D) Company uses AR as collateral for a note payable; only disclosure of the pledge is required and AR is reclassified to “AR assigned.”

**Answer:** **C.**  
Under ASC 860, a transfer is a **sale** only if the seller **surrenders control**: (1) receivables isolated from the seller, (2) buyer may pledge/exchange the assets, and (3) seller is not obligated to repurchase. Option C meets these conditions (nonrecourse factoring with isolation). A holdback for sales adjustments does **not** by itself prevent sale accounting.  
A and B are classic **secured borrowings** (AR remains; liability recorded). D describes a pledge/assignment disclosure pattern, not sale accounting.

### Q5 — MC — With-recourse factoring measurement

**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Measure loss on sale of receivables **with recourse**  
**Question:**  
Pinnacle Co. factors **$80,000** of AR **with recourse**. Factor fee = **6%**; holdback = **10%**; estimated recourse liability = **$2,500**. The transfer qualifies as a sale. What is the **loss on sale of receivables**?

- A) $4,800  
- B) $7,300  
- C) $10,500  
- D) $13,000  

**Answer:** **B. $7,300**  
Loss = finance fee + estimated recourse = \((0.06 \times 80{,}000) + 2{,}500 = 4{,}800 + 2{,}500 = \mathbf{\$7{,}300}\).  
Holdback (\(0.10 \times 80{,}000 = 8{,}000\)) is **Receivable from Factor**, not part of the loss.  
Cash = \(80{,}000 - 8{,}000 - 4{,}800 = 67{,}200\).  
Entry check: Dr Cash 67,200 + Rec from Factor 8,000 + Loss 7,300 = 82,500; Cr Recourse Liab 2,500 + AR 80,000 = 82,500. **Balanced.**
