# Agent 316 — CORE DEMO — LO 9-7

**Chapter:** 9  
**LO title:** Describe and compute the effect of LIFO liquidation  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **LIFO liquidation** = under periodic LIFO, ending inventory **quantity** falls below beginning quantity so prior-year layer(s) are eliminated and charged to COGS
- In **rising prices**, liquidated layers carry **lower historical unit costs** → COGS is below current replacement cost → pretax and after-tax income are **higher** than if all units sold were costed at current RC
- **Pretax liquidation effect** = Σ[(current replacement cost − liquidated layer unit cost) × units liquidated from that layer]; equivalently COGS@RC − COGS with liquidation
- **After-tax effect** = pretax × (1 − tax rate); incremental tax = pretax × tax rate (LIFO conformity)
- **Initial recognition JE:** summary Purchases entry (inventory cost first enters Purchases under periodic LIFO)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** multi-year LIFO **layer roll-forward** (base + incremental layers) through a liquidation year; COGS-by-layer schedule
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** (1) year-end periodic close (BI + Purchases → EI + COGS) that first books liquidated-layer COGS; (2) interim/quarterly accrual when temporary liquidation is expected to be restored before year-end so interim readers are not misled
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** replacement purchase that restores the LIFO base quantity and clears the interim replacement liability
- **Voluntary vs involuntary** liquidation; material income effect requires **footnote or parenthetical disclosure** (ASC 330-10-S99-3)

### Q1 — CORE — Ironwood Packaging: multi-year LIFO layers, Year 3 liquidation, period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis)

**LO:** LO 9-7  
**Concept:** Initial purchases JE; subsequent multi-year LIFO layer schedule; **period-end adjusting close** each year (emphasis); Year 3 liquidation COGS-by-layer; pretax/after-tax income effect; disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Ironwood Packaging Inc.** uses a **periodic inventory system** and **LIFO** for tax and external reporting. Calendar year-end. Purchase prices have been rising. Income tax rate is **25%**. Ironwood records inventory cost-flow **only at each December 31** (period-end adjusting close).

On **January 1, Year 1**, beginning inventory is a **single base layer**:

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Base (pre–Year 1) | 40,000 | $3.00 | $120,000 |

Activity (all purchases at that year’s current unit cost):

| Year | Units purchased | Unit cost | Ending inventory (physical units) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 | 50,000 | $4.50 | 48,000 |
| Year 2 | 55,000 | 6.00 | 58,000 |
| Year 3 | 35,000 | 8.00 | 22,000 |

In Year 3, ending quantity falls below beginning quantity (**LIFO liquidation**). Current replacement cost in Year 3 equals the Year 3 purchase price of **$8.00**. Management did not rebuild inventory before year-end (late-year supplier constraint).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** of LIFO inventory **layers** at December 31 of Year 1, Year 2, and Year 3 (units and dollars by layer; total EI). Show units sold and COGS each year under periodic LIFO.  
b. For **Year 3 only**, prepare the **COGS-by-layer** schedule (newest first) identifying fully and partially liquidated beginning layers.  
c. Compute the **effect of the Year 3 LIFO liquidation on pretax income** and on **after-tax income**. Show both the by-layer (RC − layer cost) method and the full-COGS comparison method (Demo 9-7 style).  
d. Record **journal entries**: (1) Year 3 summary of **purchases** (initial recognition); (2) **December 31, Year 1**, **Year 2**, and **Year 3 period-end adjusting closing entries** recognizing ending inventory and COGS under LIFO (emphasis — must balance each year).  
e. State the **disclosure** required if the Year 3 income effect is material.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Subsequent measurement — LIFO layer roll-forward**

**Year 1**

| | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory (base) | 40,000 | $3.00 | $120,000 |
| Purchases | 50,000 | 4.50 | 225,000 |
| **Cost of goods available** | **90,000** | | **$345,000** |
| Ending inventory (LIFO) | 48,000 | | **$156,000** |
| Cost of goods sold | 42,000 | | **$189,000** |

Ending layers at Dec. 31, Year 1:

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Base | 40,000 | $3.00 | $120,000 |
| Year 1 increment | 8,000 | 4.50 | 36,000 |
| **Total EI** | **48,000** | | **$156,000** |

COGS Year 1 = 42,000 × $4.50 = **$189,000** (all from current purchases; no liquidation).  
Check: $345,000 − $156,000 = **$189,000**.

**Year 2**

| | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | 48,000 | | $156,000 |
| Purchases | 55,000 | $6.00 | 330,000 |
| **Cost of goods available** | **103,000** | | **$486,000** |
| Ending inventory (LIFO) | 58,000 | | **$216,000** |
| Cost of goods sold | 45,000 | | **$270,000** |

Ending layers at Dec. 31, Year 2:

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Base | 40,000 | $3.00 | $120,000 |
| Year 1 | 8,000 | 4.50 | 36,000 |
| Year 2 increment | 10,000 | 6.00 | 60,000 |
| **Total EI** | **58,000** | | **$216,000** |

COGS Year 2 = 45,000 × $6.00 = **$270,000** (no liquidation).  
Check: $486,000 − $216,000 = **$270,000**.

**Year 3 (liquidation year)**

| | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | 58,000 | | $216,000 |
| Purchases | 35,000 | $8.00 | 280,000 |
| **Cost of goods available** | **93,000** | | **$496,000** |
| Ending inventory (LIFO — base remainder) | 22,000 | 3.00 | **$66,000** |
| Cost of goods sold | 71,000 | | **$430,000** |

Ending layers at Dec. 31, Year 3:

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Base (remainder of original 40,000) | 22,000 | $3.00 | $66,000 |
| Year 1 | 0 | — | 0 |
| Year 2 | 0 | — | 0 |
| **Total EI** | **22,000** | | **$66,000** |

Units liquidated from beginning inventory = 58,000 − 22,000 = **36,000**.  
Check: $496,000 − $66,000 = **$430,000**.

**b. Year 3 COGS by layer (LIFO — newest first)**

| Source | Units | Unit cost | Total cost | Liquidation status |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Year 3 purchases | 35,000 | $8.00 | $280,000 | Current layer (not a prior-year liquidation) |
| Year 2 layer | 10,000 | 6.00 | 60,000 | **Fully liquidated** |
| Year 1 layer | 8,000 | 4.50 | 36,000 | **Fully liquidated** |
| Base layer | 18,000 | 3.00 | 54,000 | **Partially liquidated** (18,000 of 40,000) |
| **Cost of goods sold** | **71,000** | | **$430,000** | |

Layer sum: 280,000 + 60,000 + 36,000 + 54,000 = **$430,000**.

**c. Income effect of Year 3 LIFO liquidation**

*Method 1 — by liquidated layer (RC − old unit cost):*

| Liquidated layer | Units | RC − layer cost | Pretax effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 2 | 10,000 | $8.00 − $6.00 = $2.00 | $20,000 |
| Year 1 | 8,000 | 8.00 − 4.50 = 3.50 | 28,000 |
| Base | 18,000 | 8.00 − 3.00 = 5.00 | 90,000 |
| **Total pretax increase** | **36,000** | | **$138,000** |

*Method 2 — full COGS comparison (Demo 9-7):*

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| COGS if liquidation had **not** occurred (71,000 × $8.00) | $568,000 |
| COGS **with** liquidation (from schedule) | 430,000 |
| **Increase in pretax income** | **$138,000** |

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Increase in pretax income | $138,000 |
| Increase in income tax ($138,000 × 0.25) | 34,500 |
| **After-tax increase in income** ($138,000 × 0.75) | **$103,500** |

**d. Journal entries**

*(1) Purchases during Year 3 — initial recognition (summary)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 280,000 | |
| Accounts Payable (or Cash) | | 280,000 |
| *35,000 units × $8.00* | | |

**Check:** Dr 280,000 = Cr 280,000. **Balanced.**

*(2) Period-end adjusting closing entries (emphasis) — Dec. 31 each year*

**December 31, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 156,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 189,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 120,000 |
| Purchases | | 225,000 |
| *Period-end only: close BI and Purchases; recognize LIFO EI and COGS (no liquidation)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = 156,000 + 189,000 = **345,000**; Cr = 120,000 + 225,000 = **345,000**. **Balanced.**

**December 31, Year 2**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 216,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 270,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 156,000 |
| Purchases | | 330,000 |
| *Period-end only: true up inventory and COGS under periodic LIFO* | | |

**Check:** Dr = 216,000 + 270,000 = **486,000**; Cr = 156,000 + 330,000 = **486,000**. **Balanced.**

**December 31, Year 3 — period-end close that books liquidated-layer COGS**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 66,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 430,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 216,000 |
| Purchases | | 280,000 |
| *Period-end only: recognize LIFO EI and COGS including fully/partially liquidated prior layers* | | |

**Check:** Dr = 66,000 + 430,000 = **496,000**; Cr = 216,000 + 280,000 = **496,000**. **Balanced.**  
After this entry, Inventory on the books equals LIFO EI of **$66,000**.

**e. Disclosure**  
If the income effect of LIFO liquidation is **material**, disclose the income realized from LIFO inventory liquidation so the financial statements are not misleading. Disclosure may be in a **footnote** or **parenthetically on the face of the income statement** (ASC 330-10-S99-3). Example: “Net income includes approximately $103,500 after tax from liquidation of LIFO inventory layers.”

**Key insight:** Under periodic LIFO, **liquidated-layer COGS is first recognized in the period-end adjusting close** (BI + Purchases → EI + COGS). Layers build only when ending quantity rises; when quantity falls, old costs hit COGS and pretax income rises by Σ[(RC − layer cost) × liquidated units]. After-tax income rises by pretax × (1 − t).

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Brass Harbor Foundry: multi-layer liquidation twin

**LO:** LO 9-7  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — multi-layer LIFO liquidation schedule; pretax/after-tax effect at 30% tax; balanced period-end adjusting closing JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Brass Harbor Foundry LLC** uses **periodic LIFO**. Tax rate is **30%**. Current replacement cost is **$36** per unit.

Beginning inventory, January 1:

| Layer (oldest first) | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Base layer | 100,000 | $18.00 | $1,800,000 |
| Intermediate layer | 25,000 | 22.00 | 550,000 |
| Recent layer | 15,000 | 28.00 | 420,000 |
| **Total beginning inventory** | **140,000** | | **$2,770,000** |

Purchases during the year: **110,000** units @ **$36** = **$3,960,000**.  
Ending inventory physical count: **55,000** units.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the COGAS / EI / COGS schedule and the **COGS-by-layer** schedule; identify units liquidated by layer.  
b. Compute pretax and after-tax income effect of the LIFO liquidation (both methods).  
c. Prepare the December 31 **period-end adjusting** closing JE.  
d. Compute the **incremental income tax** attributable solely to the liquidation effect.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Schedules**

Units available = 140,000 + 110,000 = **250,000**  
Units sold = 250,000 − 55,000 = **195,000**

| | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | 140,000 | | $2,770,000 |
| Purchases | 110,000 | $36.00 | 3,960,000 |
| **COGAS** | **250,000** | | **$6,730,000** |
| Ending inventory (LIFO, base remainder) | 55,000 | 18.00 | 990,000 |
| **COGS** | **195,000** | | **$5,740,000** |

**COGS by layer:**

| Source | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current purchases | 110,000 | $36.00 | $3,960,000 |
| Recent layer (**fully liquidated**) | 15,000 | 28.00 | 420,000 |
| Intermediate layer (**fully liquidated**) | 25,000 | 22.00 | 550,000 |
| Base layer (**partially liquidated**) | 45,000 | 18.00 | 810,000 |
| **COGS** | **195,000** | | **$5,740,000** |

EI: 55,000 × $18 = **$990,000** (of original 100,000 base).  
Units liquidated from BI: 140,000 − 55,000 = **85,000** (15k + 25k + 45k).

Checks: 6,730,000 − 990,000 = **5,740,000**; layer sum 3,960 + 420 + 550 + 810 = **5,740,000**.

**b. Income effect**

| Liquidated layer | Units | RC − cost | Pretax |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Recent | 15,000 | $36 − $28 = $8 | $120,000 |
| Intermediate | 25,000 | 36 − 22 = 14 | 350,000 |
| Base | 45,000 | 36 − 18 = 18 | 810,000 |
| **Total pretax increase** | **85,000** | | **$1,280,000** |

Cross-check: COGS if no liquidation = 195,000 × $36 = **$7,020,000**;  
7,020,000 − 5,740,000 = **$1,280,000**.

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Pretax increase | $1,280,000 |
| After-tax increase ($1,280,000 × 0.70) | **$896,000** |

**c. December 31 period-end adjusting closing JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 990,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 5,740,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 2,770,000 |
| Purchases | | 3,960,000 |
| *Period-end only: close BI and Purchases; book LIFO EI and COGS with liquidated layers* | | |

**Check:** Dr = 990,000 + 5,740,000 = **6,730,000**; Cr = 2,770,000 + 3,960,000 = **6,730,000**. **Balanced.**

**d. Incremental tax from liquidation**  
$1,280,000 × 0.30 = **$384,000** additional income tax (LIFO conformity applies the same inventory method for tax).

**Key insight:** Same LO 9-7 mechanics with all new quantities, unit costs, and a **30%** tax rate. Always prove pretax two ways (by-layer gap vs. full COGS at RC) and prove the **period-end** closing JE balances to COGAS.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Hearthstone Culinary: interim period-end adjusting JE, settlement on replacement, permanent year-end liquidation

**LO:** LO 9-7  
**Concept:** **Period-end/interim adjusting JE** when temporary LIFO liquidation is expected to be restored (emphasis); settlement JE on replacement; permanent year-end liquidation schedule, period-end close, and disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Hearthstone Culinary Supply Co.** tracks units continuously but reports inventory under **LIFO**. Purchase prices have been rising. Tax rate is **25%**.

**Part A — Temporary (interim) involuntary liquidation expected to be restored**  
On **September 30, Year 6** (third-quarter interim period-end), a carrier strike forces Hearthstone to dip **5,000 units** into its LIFO base layer. Those units had a LIFO carrying cost of **$22** each. Management **expects full replacement before December 31** at the current replacement cost of **$34** per unit. Sales of the 5,000 units have already reduced Inventory and charged COGS at the **$22** LIFO cost under perpetual LIFO tracking.

**Part B — Settlement when replaced**  
On **November 12, Year 6**, Hearthstone purchases **5,000** replacement units for **$34** cash each, restoring the LIFO base layer quantities.

**Part C — Permanent year-end liquidation (Year 7)**  
In **Year 7**, beginning LIFO inventory is a **single base layer** of **28,000 units @ $22 = $616,000**. Purchases are **52,000 @ $35**. Ending count is **16,000 units**. Liquidation is **permanent** (not restored before year-end). Current replacement cost = **$35**.

**Required:**  
a. **September 30, Year 6 interim period-end adjusting entry** so interim statements do not overstate income from the temporary liquidation (accrue the excess of current RC over LIFO cost).  
b. **November 12, Year 6 settlement / replacement JE** that restores the LIFO layer and clears the liability (and records cash paid).  
c. For **Year 7 permanent liquidation**: prepare the COGAS/EI/COGS and COGS-by-layer schedules; compute pretax and after-tax income effect; prepare the **December 31, Year 7 period-end adjusting** closing JE.  
d. Classify the Year 6 event as voluntary or involuntary; state required **presentation/disclosure** if Year 7’s effect is material.

**Answer key:**  

**a. September 30, Year 6 — interim period-end adjusting JE (anticipated replacement)**

Excess of current cost over LIFO cost on liquidated units:  
5,000 × ($34 − $22) = **$60,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 60,000 | |
| Accrued Liability for Inventory Replacement | | 60,000 |
| *Raise interim COGS to current RC for temporary LIFO liquidation expected to be restored before year-end* | | |

**Check:** Dr 60,000 = Cr 60,000. **Balanced.**  
Combined with the $22 LIFO cost already in COGS, the 5,000 units carry **$170,000** (= 5,000 × $34) of COGS in interim results — matching current revenue to current cost.

**b. November 12, Year 6 — settlement of temporary liquidation (replacement)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 110,000 | |
| Accrued Liability for Inventory Replacement | 60,000 | |
| Cash | | 170,000 |
| *Restore 5,000 base-layer units at LIFO cost $22; clear liability; pay current RC $34* | | |

**Check:** Dr = 110,000 + 60,000 = **170,000**; Cr = **170,000**. **Balanced.**  
After replacement: LIFO base quantity is restored (no permanent year-end liquidation from this episode); cash outlay equals current cost of the temporary dip.

**c. Year 7 — permanent LIFO liquidation**

Units available = 28,000 + 52,000 = **80,000**  
Units sold = 80,000 − 16,000 = **64,000**

| | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory (base) | 28,000 | $22.00 | $616,000 |
| Purchases | 52,000 | 35.00 | 1,820,000 |
| **COGAS** | **80,000** | | **$2,436,000** |
| Ending inventory (LIFO base remainder) | 16,000 | 22.00 | 352,000 |
| **COGS** | **64,000** | | **$2,084,000** |

**COGS by layer:**

| Source | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 52,000 | $35.00 | $1,820,000 |
| Base (liquidated) | 12,000 | 22.00 | 264,000 |
| **COGS** | **64,000** | | **$2,084,000** |

Liquidated units = 28,000 − 16,000 = **12,000**  
Pretax effect = 12,000 × ($35 − $22) = **$156,000**  
Cross-check: COGS if no liquidation = 64,000 × $35 = $2,240,000; 2,240,000 − 2,084,000 = **$156,000**.  
After-tax effect = $156,000 × 0.75 = **$117,000**.  
Incremental tax = $156,000 × 0.25 = **$39,000**.

*December 31, Year 7 period-end adjusting closing JE:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 352,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 2,084,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 616,000 |
| Purchases | | 1,820,000 |
| *Period-end only: permanent LIFO liquidation remains in year-end COGS at old layer cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = 352,000 + 2,084,000 = **2,436,000**; Cr = 616,000 + 1,820,000 = **2,436,000**. **Balanced.**

**d. Classification and disclosure**  
- Year 6 carrier-strike dip: **involuntary** liquidation (uncontrollable cause). (A **voluntary** liquidation would be management’s deliberate decision to run inventory down—e.g., product change or lower expected demand.)  
- Year 7 permanent liquidation: if the **$117,000 after-tax** (or pretax $156,000) effect is material, disclose income realized from LIFO liquidation in the **notes** or **parenthetically** on the income statement so statements are not misleading.

**Key insight:** Temporary liquidations expected to be restored should not inflate interim earnings—at the **interim period-end**, accrue the RC−LIFO gap, then **settle** by restoring Inventory at old LIFO cost and clearing the liability when cash is paid at current prices. Permanent year-end liquidations stay in COGS at old layer costs via the **period-end close**; quantify and **disclose** the income effect.

### Q4 — MC — Classification and after-tax effect computation

**LO:** LO 9-7  
**Concept:** Voluntary vs involuntary LIFO liquidation classification and material disclosure; after-tax liquidation income effect  

**Question 1:**  
Which statement best describes a **voluntary** LIFO liquidation and the related reporting obligation when the income effect is material?

- A) Uncontrollable causes (strike, shortage, delayed deliveries) force inventory below beginning LIFO layers; disclosure is never required for voluntary events.  
- B) Management deliberately reduces inventory quantity (e.g., anticipated decline in demand, lower replenishment costs, or product change); if the income effect is material, disclose income realized from LIFO liquidation in a footnote or parenthetically on the income statement.  
- C) Voluntary liquidation is defined as any year in which the LIFO reserve decreases and must be reported as a separate current liability.  
- D) Voluntary liquidation occurs only when ending inventory quantity exceeds beginning quantity and always decreases pretax income when prices are rising.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Voluntary liquidation is management’s intentional reduction of inventory quantity that eliminates prior LIFO layers. In rising prices that lowers COGS and raises income; material amounts require disclosure (footnote or parenthetical) so statements are not misleading (ASC 330-10-S99-3). A describes **involuntary** liquidation and wrongly denies disclosure. C confuses LIFO **reserve** mechanics with unit-layer liquidation. D is the opposite of liquidation (quantity increase builds layers) and misstates the income direction.

**Question 2:**  
**Mesa Ridge Outdoor Co.** has beginning LIFO inventory of **48,000 units @ $40**. During the year it sells more units than it purchases; ending inventory is **30,000 units**. Current replacement cost is **$55**. Tax rate is **25%**. What is the **after-tax** LIFO liquidation effect on income?

- A) $202,500  
- B) $270,000  
- C) $810,000  
- D) $675,000  

**Answer:** **A.**  
Units liquidated = 48,000 − 30,000 = **18,000**.  
Pretax effect = 18,000 × ($55 − $40) = 18,000 × $15 = **$270,000**.  
After-tax = $270,000 × (1 − 0.25) = **$202,500**.  
(B is pretax; C is 54,000 × $15-style error; D is 18,000 × $37.50-style error.)
