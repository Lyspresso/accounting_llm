# Agent 45 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-7

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Describe receivables disclosures and ratio analyses  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Required receivables disclosures under ASC 310-10-50 (policies, major categories, allowance balance and activity, collateral, past-due/nonaccrual, methodologies, significant purchases/sales, fair value when applicable)
- Concentration of credit risk disclosure (common economic risk or major-customer concentration)
- Allowance for doubtful accounts **rollforward schedule** (beg + provision + recoveries − write-offs = end) as a core note disclosure
- Initial recognition of credit sales (AR) and settlement of receivables (write-offs; recovery reinstate-and-collect)
- Period-end adjusting entry to set the allowance to management’s estimate of expected credit losses
- Accounts receivable turnover = Net sales ÷ Average net accounts receivable
- Average days to collect receivables = 365 ÷ Accounts receivable turnover
- Balance sheet presentation: Accounts receivable, **net** of allowance
- Comparative ratio analysis and interpretation of collection efficiency trends

### Q1 — CORE — Cedarline Outdoor Gear: allowance rollforward, disclosure note, ratios (initial recognition emphasis)
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of credit sales; write-offs/recoveries; period-end ADA adjusting JE; allowance disclosure rollforward; AR turnover and days to collect  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Outdoor Gear Co.** sells outdoor equipment primarily on account. Fiscal year ends **December 31**. Selected information:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Accounts receivable, **gross** — Dec 31, Year 0 | $380,000 |
| Allowance for doubtful accounts — Dec 31, Year 0 | 15,200 |
| Accounts receivable, **gross** — Dec 31, Year 1 | 420,000 |
| Allowance for doubtful accounts — Dec 31, Year 1 | 18,000 |
| Net credit sales — Year 1 | 2,600,000 |
| Net credit sales — Year 2 | 2,850,000 |

**Year 2 activity (in chronological summary form):**  
1. Recorded **credit sales** of **$2,850,000**.  
2. Collected **$2,640,000** of accounts receivable (includes cash from the recovery in item 4 after reinstatement).  
3. Wrote off **$22,000** of specifically identified uncollectible accounts.  
4. Recovered **$3,500** of accounts previously written off in prior years (reinstated and collected in cash the same day).  
5. At December 31, Year 2, after analyzing current conditions and reasonable and supportable forecasts, management estimates the **allowance for doubtful accounts should be $26,000**.

**Additional disclosure facts (Year 2):**  
- Two large regional farm-supply retailers (agribusiness channel) together represent **38%** of ending gross trade receivables; remaining customers are geographically dispersed specialty retailers.  
- **$140,000** of accounts receivable is pledged as collateral under a revolving bank line of credit.  
- Accounting policy: trade receivables measured at the net amount expected to be collected; expected credit losses estimated using a pooling approach by risk characteristics with historical loss experience adjusted for current conditions and forecasts.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the **initial recognition** journal entry for Year 2 credit sales.  
(b) Prepare the journal entries for the **write-off** and for the **recovery** (reinstate and collect).  
(c) Compute ending **gross** accounts receivable at December 31, Year 2. Prepare the **period-end adjusting** entry for bad debt expense.  
(d) Prepare the **allowance for doubtful accounts rollforward** schedule for Year 2 (the activity disclosure).  
(e) Show the December 31, Year 2 **balance sheet presentation** of accounts receivable (gross, allowance, net).  
(f) Compute for **Year 1 and Year 2**: (1) accounts receivable turnover and (2) average days to collect receivables. Round ratios to **two** decimals; round days to **two** decimals. Briefly interpret the Year 1 → Year 2 trend.  
(g) List the **receivables disclosures** Cedarline should make (or consider) based on ASC 310-10-50 and the facts given; specifically address **concentration of credit risk** and **collateral**.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Initial recognition — Year 2 credit sales**  
```
Dr Accounts Receivable .................... 2,850,000
   Cr Sales Revenue ................................. 2,850,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 2,850,000)

**(b) Settlement / write-off and recovery**  
Write-off of uncollectible accounts:  
```
Dr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts ........ 22,000
   Cr Accounts Receivable ........................... 22,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 22,000)

Recovery — reinstate:  
```
Dr Accounts Receivable .................... 3,500
   Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts ............... 3,500
```
(Dr = Cr = 3,500)

Recovery — collect cash:  
```
Dr Cash ................................... 3,500
   Cr Accounts Receivable ........................... 3,500
```
(Dr = Cr = 3,500)

**(c) Ending gross AR and period-end adjusting JE**  
Ending gross AR:  
\[
420{,}000 + 2{,}850{,}000 - 2{,}640{,}000 - 22{,}000 + 3{,}500 = \mathbf{\$611{,}500}
\]

Allowance before Year 2 provision:  
\[
18{,}000 + 3{,}500 - 22{,}000 = \mathbf{\$(500)}\ \text{(debit balance)}
\]  
Required ending allowance **$26,000**.  
Bad debt expense (provision) = \(26{,}000 - (-500) = \mathbf{\$26{,}500}\).

```
Dr Bad Debt Expense ....................... 26,500
   Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts ............... 26,500
```
(Dr = Cr = 26,500)

**(d) Allowance rollforward — Year 2 (disclosure schedule)**  

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Balance, beginning of year | $18,000 |
| Current-period provision (credit loss expense) | 26,500 |
| Recoveries of amounts previously charged off | 3,500 |
| Direct write-downs charged against the allowance | (22,000) |
| **Balance, end of year** | **$26,000** |

Check: \(18{,}000 + 26{,}500 + 3{,}500 - 22{,}000 = 26{,}000\).

**(e) Balance sheet presentation — December 31, Year 2**  

| | |
|---|---:|
| Accounts receivable | $611,500 |
| Less: Allowance for doubtful accounts | (26,000) |
| **Accounts receivable, net** | **$585,500** |

**(f) Ratio analysis**  

Net AR:  
- Dec 31, Year 0: \(380{,}000 - 15{,}200 = \mathbf{\$364{,}800}\)  
- Dec 31, Year 1: \(420{,}000 - 18{,}000 = \mathbf{\$402{,}000}\)  
- Dec 31, Year 2: \(611{,}500 - 26{,}000 = \mathbf{\$585{,}500}\)

| Year | Net sales | Average net AR | AR turnover | Days to collect |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 2 | $2,850,000 | \((585{,}500 + 402{,}000)/2 = 493{,}750\) | \(2{,}850{,}000 / 493{,}750 = \mathbf{5.77}\) | \(365 / 5.77 = \mathbf{63.26}\) days |
| Year 1 | $2,600,000 | \((402{,}000 + 364{,}800)/2 = 383{,}400\) | \(2{,}600{,}000 / 383{,}400 = \mathbf{6.78}\) | \(365 / 6.78 = \mathbf{53.83}\) days |

*(If unrounded turnovers are used for days: Year 2 \(365/5.77215 \approx 63.23\); Year 1 \(365/6.78143 \approx 53.82\). Either consistent approach is acceptable; pack uses turnover rounded to two decimals then \(365 \div\) turnover.)*

**Interpretation:** Turnover **declined** and days to collect **increased** from Year 1 to Year 2. Collections slowed relative to sales (net AR grew faster than sales), which may signal looser credit policy, slower customer payments, or mix shift—users would compare to credit terms and industry peers.

**(g) Receivables disclosures (core ASC 310-10-50 themes)**  
Cedarline should disclose, as applicable:  
1. **Accounting policies** for trade receivables and for estimating the allowance (including methodology/factors and any policy changes).  
2. **Major categories** of receivables if not shown separately on the face of the balance sheet.  
3. **Allowance balance** and full **activity** (beg, provision, write-offs, recoveries, end)—as in part (d).  
4. Policy for **charging off** uncollectibles, policy for determining **past due**, and amounts past due / on nonaccrual if material.  
5. Receivables serving as **collateral** — disclose that **$140,000** of AR secures the revolving line.  
6. **Significant purchases or sales** of receivables (none indicated here).  
7. **Fair value** of receivables (practical exception often applies to short-term trade receivables due within one year).  
8. **Concentration of credit risk:** yes — **38%** of ending gross trade receivables with two agribusiness farm-supply retailers whose ability to pay is subject to **common economic risks** (agribusiness channel). Disclose the concentration and the nature of the shared risk even if management believes overall credit quality is acceptable.

**Key insight:** Disclosure of **allowance activity** is a formal rollforward that must reconcile to the period-end adjusting entry and write-offs/recoveries. Ratio analysis uses **net** AR (after allowance) and **average** net AR, so ending presentation and the allowance schedule feed the ratios users compute from the statements and notes.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Harborlight Appliances twin
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Allowance rollforward, period-end ADA JE, write-off/recovery settlement, AR ratios (all numbers changed)  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborlight Appliances Inc.** reports the following:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Accounts receivable, gross — Dec 31, Year 0 | $465,000 |
| Allowance for doubtful accounts — Dec 31, Year 0 | 19,800 |
| Accounts receivable, gross — Dec 31, Year 1 | 510,000 |
| Allowance for doubtful accounts — Dec 31, Year 1 | 22,500 |
| Net credit sales — Year 1 | 3,120,000 |
| Net credit sales — Year 2 | 3,420,000 |

**Year 2 activity:**  
- Credit sales **$3,420,000**  
- Collections on account **$3,185,000** (includes cash collected on the recovery below)  
- Write-offs **$28,500**  
- Recoveries of previously written-off accounts **$4,200** (reinstate and collect)  
- Desired ending allowance at Dec 31, Year 2: **$31,500**  

Also: one national big-box customer represents **22%** of ending gross AR; **$95,000** of AR is pledged under a short-term borrowing arrangement.

**Required:**  
(a) Journal entry for Year 2 credit sales (initial recognition).  
(b) Journal entries for write-offs and for recovery (reinstate + collect).  
(c) Ending gross AR; period-end bad debt adjusting entry.  
(d) Allowance rollforward for Year 2.  
(e) AR turnover and average days to collect for Year 1 and Year 2 (turnover and days to two decimals).  
(f) Identify two **required or highly relevant** note disclosures beyond the allowance rollforward from the facts given.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Credit sales**  
```
Dr Accounts Receivable .................... 3,420,000
   Cr Sales Revenue ................................. 3,420,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 3,420,000)

**(b) Write-off and recovery**  
```
Dr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts ........ 28,500
   Cr Accounts Receivable ........................... 28,500
```
```
Dr Accounts Receivable .................... 4,200
   Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts ............... 4,200
```
```
Dr Cash ................................... 4,200
   Cr Accounts Receivable ........................... 4,200
```
(Each entry balances.)

**(c) Ending gross AR and adjusting entry**  
\[
510{,}000 + 3{,}420{,}000 - 3{,}185{,}000 - 28{,}500 + 4{,}200 = \mathbf{\$720{,}700}
\]  
Allowance before provision: \(22{,}500 + 4{,}200 - 28{,}500 = \mathbf{\$(1{,}800)}\) debit.  
Provision: \(31{,}500 - (-1{,}800) = \mathbf{\$33{,}300}\).

```
Dr Bad Debt Expense ....................... 33,300
   Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts ............... 33,300
```
(Dr = Cr = 33,300)

**(d) Allowance rollforward — Year 2**  

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning balance | $22,500 |
| Provision | 33,300 |
| Recoveries | 4,200 |
| Write-offs | (28,500) |
| **Ending balance** | **$31,500** |

Check: \(22{,}500 + 33{,}300 + 4{,}200 - 28{,}500 = 31{,}500\).

**(e) Ratios**  
Net AR:  
- Year 0: \(465{,}000 - 19{,}800 = \mathbf{\$445{,}200}\)  
- Year 1: \(510{,}000 - 22{,}500 = \mathbf{\$487{,}500}\)  
- Year 2: \(720{,}700 - 31{,}500 = \mathbf{\$689{,}200}\)

| Year | Average net AR | Turnover | Days to collect |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 2 | \((689{,}200 + 487{,}500)/2 = 588{,}350\) | \(3{,}420{,}000 / 588{,}350 = \mathbf{5.81}\) | \(365 / 5.81 = \mathbf{62.82}\) days |
| Year 1 | \((487{,}500 + 445{,}200)/2 = 466{,}350\) | \(3{,}120{,}000 / 466{,}350 = \mathbf{6.69}\) | \(365 / 6.69 = \mathbf{54.56}\) days |

Trend: slower collections in Year 2 (lower turnover, higher days).

**(f) Disclosures from facts**  
1. **Collateral:** $95,000 of AR pledged under short-term borrowing.  
2. **Concentration of credit risk:** ~22% of ending gross AR with one national big-box customer (major-customer concentration).  
(Also: accounting policies for receivables/allowance; past-due/charge-off policies as applicable.)

**Key insight:** Changing only the numbers, the same structure holds: **gross AR rollforward → ADA rollforward → net AR → average net AR → turnover and days**, with note disclosures for collateral and concentrations layered on top.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Comparative ratios + disclosure classification
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Comparative AR turnover and days to collect; interpret peer differences; map facts to ASC 310-10-50 disclosure requirements  
**Scenario:**  
Selected data from recent Form 10-K–style summaries for two competitors in wholesale hard goods (**amounts in thousands**):

| ($ thousands) | AR, net End Y3 | AR, net End Y2 | AR, net End Y1 | Net sales Y3 | Net sales Y2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Pinecrest Tools Co.** | $2,840 | $2,610 | $2,490 | $48,600 | $45,200 |
| **Rivermead Distributors Inc.** | 5,180 | 4,920 | 4,750 | 31,200 | 29,850 |

**Additional facts for Rivermead only (Year 3 notes excerpt — incomplete draft):**  
- Trade receivables policy: “Receivables are recorded at the net amount expected to be collected.”  
- Beginning ADA $410; provision $285; write-offs $260; recoveries $35; ending ADA **?**  
- Approximately **31%** of Year 3 consolidated net sales and **44%** of ending trade receivables relate to five large home-center retailers.  
- $1,200 of receivables are pledged as security for notes payable.  
- Rivermead sold **$800** of receivables without recourse during Year 3 (significant sale of receivables).

**Required:**  
(a) For **both** companies and for **Year 2 and Year 3**, compute (1) accounts receivable turnover and (2) average days to collect receivables. Round turnover and days to **two** decimals.  
(b) Briefly analyze the results (relative collection speed and year-over-year trend).  
(c) Complete Rivermead’s **ending allowance** and present the **allowance activity disclosure** table.  
(d) For Rivermead, identify which of the following must be **disclosed** in the notes (yes/no + brief reason): (i) concentration of credit risk; (ii) receivables as collateral; (iii) significant sale of receivables; (iv) detailed listing of every customer balance.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Ratios**  

**Pinecrest Tools Co.**  
Average net AR Y3: \((2{,}840 + 2{,}610)/2 = 2{,}725\)  
Average net AR Y2: \((2{,}610 + 2{,}490)/2 = 2{,}550\)

| Year | Turnover | Days to collect |
|---|---:|---:|
| Year 3 | \(48{,}600 / 2{,}725 = \mathbf{17.83}\) | \(365 / 17.83 = \mathbf{20.47}\) days |
| Year 2 | \(45{,}200 / 2{,}550 = \mathbf{17.73}\) | \(365 / 17.73 = \mathbf{20.59}\) days |

**Rivermead Distributors Inc.**  
Average net AR Y3: \((5{,}180 + 4{,}920)/2 = 5{,}050\)  
Average net AR Y2: \((4{,}920 + 4{,}750)/2 = 4{,}835\)

| Year | Turnover | Days to collect |
|---|---:|---:|
| Year 3 | \(31{,}200 / 5{,}050 = \mathbf{6.18}\) | \(365 / 6.18 = \mathbf{59.06}\) days |
| Year 2 | \(29{,}850 / 4{,}835 = \mathbf{6.17}\) | \(365 / 6.17 = \mathbf{59.16}\) days |

**(b) Analysis**  
Pinecrest turns receivables roughly **18×** per year (~**20–21 days**), much faster than Rivermead (~**6.2×**, ~**59 days**). Composition of receivables and credit terms often explain peer gaps (e.g., card processors / quick-pay channels vs. traditional trade terms). Both companies show a **slight improvement** from Year 2 to Year 3 (marginally higher turnover / lower days). Users should not conclude Pinecrest is “better” without understanding receivable mix and sales terms.

**(c) Rivermead allowance rollforward ($ thousands)**  
Ending ADA:  
\[
410 + 285 + 35 - 260 = \mathbf{\$470}
\]

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Balance, beginning | $410 |
| Current-period provision | 285 |
| Recoveries | 35 |
| Write-downs charged against allowance | (260) |
| **Balance, end of year** | **$470** |

**(d) Disclosure mapping**  
| Item | Disclose? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Concentration of credit risk | **Yes** | Five home-center retailers = **44%** of ending trade receivables (and large share of sales)—significant concentration with common retail-channel risks. |
| (ii) Receivables as collateral | **Yes** | ASC 310-10-50 requires disclosure of receivables serving as collateral (**$1,200** pledged). |
| (iii) Significant sale of receivables | **Yes** | Significant purchases/sales of receivables are a specified disclosure item (**$800** sold without recourse). |
| (iv) Detailed listing of every customer balance | **No** | Standards require **significant concentrations** and categories/policies—not a customer-by-customer schedule of all balances. |

**Key insight:** Demo 8-7 style analysis pairs **ratio computation** with **note reading**: peers can have very different days-to-collect because of receivable composition, while the same notes that support ratios also require **allowance activity**, **collateral**, **sales of receivables**, and **credit-risk concentrations**.

### Q4 — MC — Concentration of credit risk (classification/disclosure)
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Identify when a concentration of credit risk disclosure is required  
**Question:**  
Which situation **best** illustrates a **concentration of credit risk** that generally requires disclosure related to receivables?

- A) A company holds trade receivables from hundreds of customers across many industries and regions, with no customer over 2% of the balance.  
- B) A company has 40% of its accounts receivable from several independent grape growers and wineries in one geographic region whose cash flows depend heavily on the same weather and commodity-price conditions.  
- C) A company invests temporarily idle cash in a diversified portfolio of short-term U.S. Treasury bills classified as cash equivalents.  
- D) A company reports accounts receivable gross of $500,000 and an allowance of $20,000, so net receivables are $480,000.

**Answer:** **B.** A concentration of credit risk exists when receivables are subject to **common economic risks** (here, regional agricultural/commodity conditions) or are concentrated with major counterparties. Option A describes diversification (limited concentration). Option C is about cash equivalents, not receivable credit concentration. Option D is ordinary gross/net presentation of AR, not a concentration disclosure trigger.

### Q5 — MC — AR ratio formulas
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Correct computation of accounts receivable turnover and days to collect  
**Question:**  
Northbridge Supply reports net sales of **$4,000,000**, beginning net accounts receivable of **$350,000**, and ending net accounts receivable of **$450,000**. What are accounts receivable turnover and average days to collect receivables (use 365 days; round turnover to two decimals and days to nearest whole day)?

- A) Turnover **10.00**; days **37**  
- B) Turnover **8.89**; days **41**  
- C) Turnover **11.43**; days **32**  
- D) Turnover **8.00**; days **46**

**Answer:** **A.** Average net AR = \((350{,}000 + 450{,}000)/2 = 400{,}000\).  
Turnover = \(4{,}000{,}000 / 400{,}000 = \mathbf{10.00}\).  
Days = \(365 / 10.00 = \mathbf{36.5} \approx \mathbf{37}\) days.  
B incorrectly uses ending AR only (\(4{,}000{,}000/450{,}000 \approx 8.89\)). C uses beginning AR only. D uses gross mis-averaging or similar error.
