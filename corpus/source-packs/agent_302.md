# Agent 302 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-2

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Account for sales and collections on account including the impact of cash discounts  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE (gross method):** Dr Accounts Receivable / Cr Sales Revenue at **full invoice (gross)**; simultaneous COGS when control of inventory transfers
- **Initial recognition JE (net method):** Dr Accounts Receivable / Cr Sales Revenue at **gross less available cash discount**
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** customer-level AR rollforward (gross or net carrying amounts) through sales, early collections, year-end estimates, and final settlement
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):**  
  - **Gross method:** reduce revenue and AR (or credit **Allowance for Sales Discounts**) for cash discounts **expected to be taken** on open year-end receivables still inside the discount window  
  - **Net method:** increase AR and **Sales Discount Forfeited** for cash discounts **expected to be forfeited** on open year-end receivables
- **Settlement (disposal) JE within discount period — gross:** Dr Cash + Dr Sales Discount / Cr AR (gross relieved)
- **Settlement after discount period — gross:** Dr Cash / Cr AR for remaining gross
- **Settlement within discount period — net:** Dr Cash / Cr AR at net carrying amount (no separate discount account)
- **Settlement after discount period — net:** Dr Cash (remaining gross) / Cr Sales Discount Forfeited + Cr AR (net), or clear AR already stepped up by the YE forfeit accrual
- **Presentation:** gross method → Sales − Sales discounts = Net sales; net method → Sales + Sales discounts forfeited (other revenue); when outcomes are known, total cash-based revenue for a customer is the same under either method’s final economics
- **Number-variant twin:** same year-straddle gross-method path with all amounts, rates, and splits changed

**Method note (core exam path):** Cash discounts are **variable consideration**. The **gross method** records AR/Sales at invoice gross and records **Sales Discount** only when (or when expected to be) taken. The **net method** records AR/Sales as if the discount will be taken and records **Sales Discount Forfeited** only when (or when expected to be) lost. **Both methods require period-end adjusting entries** on open receivables (Hanlon Demo 8-2 / LO 8-2 body). Do **not** use Expanding Your Knowledge trade-discount or annualized-cost sidebars as the core path.

### Q1 — CORE — Gross method year-straddle: sale, partial early collection, period-end discount accrual (emphasis), schedule, post-YE settlement

**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition under gross method; mid-period collection within discount window; **period-end adjusting JE** for expected discounts on open AR (emphasis); subsequent AR / allowance schedule; settlement after year-end (with and without discount)  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborwick Trading Inc.** (calendar year-end) accounts for cash discounts using the **gross method**. All credit terms are **2/10, n/30**. Beginning AR for the customers below is **$0**. Harborwick uses a contra-asset **Allowance for Sales Discounts** for the year-end estimate (AR reported net of expected prompt-payment discounts).

| Date | Customer | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 16, Year 1 | Kestrel Co. | Credit sale, gross invoice **$48,000**; cost **$28,800** |
| Dec 22, Year 1 | Kestrel Co. | Pays **50%** of its gross invoice (**within** the 10-day discount period) |
| Dec 28, Year 1 | Lumen Goods LLC | Credit sale, gross invoice **$32,000**; cost **$19,200** (discount window runs through **Jan 7, Year 2**) |
| Dec 31, Year 1 | — | Year-end: management expects **Lumen** will take the **2%** cash discount on **100%** of its open balance. **Kestrel’s** remaining balance is **past** the discount window (window ended Dec 26) — **no** discount expected. |
| Jan 4, Year 2 | Lumen Goods LLC | Pays **in full** and takes the cash discount |
| Jan 18, Year 2 | Kestrel Co. | Pays remaining balance **after** the discount period (no discount) |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare **initial recognition** JEs on Dec 16 and Dec 28 (sale and COGS for each).  
b. Prepare the **Dec 22 settlement** JE (partial collection within discount period).  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** of gross AR by customer through Dec 31, the **Allowance for Sales Discounts**, and **AR net** of the allowance.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** for expected cash discounts (emphasis).  
e. Prepare the **Year 2 settlement** JEs on Jan 4 and Jan 18 (disposal of the receivables).  
f. Show the **Year 1 income-statement excerpt** (gross method) for these customers and the **Dec 31 Year 1 balance-sheet presentation** of the related receivables.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition (gross method)**

*Dec 16, Year 1 — Kestrel Co.*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 48,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 48,000 |
| *Credit sale at full gross invoice* | | |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 28,800 | |
| Inventory | | 28,800 |
| *Remove inventory; recognize COGS* | | |

**Check:** Sale Dr = Cr = 48,000; COGS Dr = Cr = 28,800. **Balanced.**

*Dec 28, Year 1 — Lumen Goods LLC*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 32,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 32,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 19,200 | |
| Inventory | | 19,200 |

**Check:** 32,000 = 32,000; 19,200 = 19,200. **Balanced.**

**b. Dec 22 — Kestrel pays 50% within discount period**  
Gross AR relieved = \(0.50 \times 48{,}000 = \mathbf{\$24{,}000}\)  
Sales discount = \(24{,}000 \times 0.02 = \mathbf{\$480}\)  
Cash = \(24{,}000 - 480 = \mathbf{\$23{,}520}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 23,520 | |
| Sales Discount | 480 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 24,000 |
| *Partial collection within 2/10; Sales Discount is contra-revenue* | | |

**Check:** \(23{,}520 + 480 = 24{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — gross AR and year-end allowance**

Amounts in AR columns are **gross method** carrying amounts. Collections reduce AR by the **gross** amount relieved.

| Date / Item | Kestrel Co. | Lumen Goods | Total gross AR | Allow. for sales disc. | AR, net |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dec 1, Y1 beginning | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Dec 16 sale | 48,000 | — | 48,000 | — | 48,000 |
| Dec 22 collection (AR relieved) | (24,000) | — | 24,000 | — | 24,000 |
| Dec 28 sale | — | 32,000 | 56,000 | — | 56,000 |
| Dec 31 YE estimate (Lumen 2%) | — | — | 56,000 | **640** | **55,360** |
| **Dec 31, Y1 ending** | **$24,000** | **$32,000** | **$56,000** | **$640** | **$55,360** |

**Discount-window analysis at Dec 31:**  
- Kestrel remaining **$24,000**: discount period ended **Dec 26** → expected discount **$0**.  
- Lumen open **$32,000**: still inside 2/10 through Jan 7 → expected discount \(32{,}000 \times 0.02 = \mathbf{\$640}\).

**Roll-forward check:** \(48{,}000 - 24{,}000 + 32{,}000 = 56{,}000\); net \(56{,}000 - 640 = 55{,}360\). ✓

**d. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE (emphasis)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Discount | 640 | |
| Allowance for Sales Discounts | | 640 |
| *YE: reduce revenue and net AR for cash discounts expected on open receivables still in the discount window* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 640. **Balanced.**

*(If the firm credits AR directly instead of an allowance, same economics: Dr Sales Discount 640 / Cr Accounts Receivable 640.)*

**e. Year 2 settlement (disposal) JEs**

*Jan 4, Year 2 — Lumen pays in full and takes the discount*  
Cash = \(32{,}000 \times 0.98 = \mathbf{\$31{,}360}\)  
Apply the YE allowance against the gross AR:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 31,360 | |
| Allowance for Sales Discounts | 640 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 32,000 |
| *Settle Lumen AR; allowance absorbs the 2% discount taken* | | |

**Check:** \(31{,}360 + 640 = 32{,}000\). **Balanced.** Allowance balance after entry = **$0**.

*Jan 18, Year 2 — Kestrel remaining after discount period*  
Remaining gross AR = **$24,000** (no discount)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 24,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 24,000 |
| *Final collection after discount window; full remaining gross* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 24,000. **Balanced.** Ending AR for both customers = **$0**.

**f. Presentation**

*Year 1 income statement excerpt (these customers)*

| Income Statement — Excerpt (Year 1) | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales revenue ($48,000 + $32,000) | $80,000 |
| Less: Sales discounts ($480 + $640) | (1,120) |
| **Net sales revenue** | **$78,880** |

*Dec 31, Year 1 balance sheet (related receivables)*

| Balance Sheet — Current assets excerpt | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Accounts receivable (gross) | $56,000 |
| Less: Allowance for sales discounts | (640) |
| **Accounts receivable, net of expected cash discounts** | **$55,360** |

*(Separate allowance for credit losses, if any, is LO 8-4 and is ignored here.)*

**Proof (full lifecycle cash):**  
Cash = \(23{,}520 + 31{,}360 + 24{,}000 = \mathbf{\$78{,}880}\)  
Cash + all sales discounts (\(480 + 640 = 1{,}120\)) = \(78{,}880 + 1{,}120 = \mathbf{\$80{,}000}\) = original combined gross sales. ✓

**Key insight:** Under the gross method, AR stays at **invoice gross** until settlement or a YE estimate. The **period-end adjusting JE** (emphasis) anticipates discounts still available on open AR so **Year 1 net sales and net AR** reflect expected cash. When Lumen pays early in Year 2, the **Allowance for Sales Discounts** is closed against AR (not a second hit to Year 2 revenue). Kestrel’s late balance was never in the YE estimate and settles at full remaining gross.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Net method lifecycle with period-end forfeit accrual (emphasis) and settlement

**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Net-method initial recognition; partial early settlement; **period-end adjusting JE** for expected forfeitures (emphasis); subsequent AR schedule at net then stepped-up; late settlement after YE; revenue presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Sable Peak Distributors Inc.** (calendar year-end) accounts for cash discounts using the **net method**. Terms **2/10, n/30**. Beginning AR for these customers is **$0**.

| Date | Customer | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 18, Year 1 | Quill & Co. | Credit sale, **gross invoice $50,000**; cost **$30,000** |
| Dec 26, Year 1 | Quill & Co. | Pays **40%** of the **net** receivable (**within** discount period) |
| Dec 29, Year 1 | Mariner Retail | Credit sale, **gross invoice $20,000**; cost **$12,000** (discount window through **Jan 8, Year 2**) |
| Dec 31, Year 1 | — | YE: **Quill’s** remaining balance is **past** the discount window (ended Dec 28) — management expects **100% forfeiture**. **Mariner** is still inside the discount window and is expected to **take** the discount — **no** forfeit accrual. |
| Jan 5, Year 2 | Mariner Retail | Pays **in full within** the discount period |
| Jan 12, Year 2 | Quill & Co. | Pays remaining balance **after** the discount period (full remaining gross) |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare initial recognition JEs on Dec 18 and Dec 29 (sale at **net** + COGS).  
b. Prepare Dec 26 settlement JE.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** of net AR by customer through the YE forfeit accrual and final settlements.  
d. Prepare the **December 31 period-end adjusting JE** for expected forfeitures (emphasis).  
e. Prepare Jan 5 and Jan 12 settlement JEs.  
f. Show Year 1 total revenue presentation under the net method for these customers, and Dec 31 AR after the YE entry.

|---|---:|---:|

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 30,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 30,000 |
| *Late settlement clears AR already increased by YE forfeit accrual* | | |

**Check:** 30,000 = 30,000. **Balanced.** Ending AR = **$0**.

*(If no YE entry had been made, the Jan 12 entry would have been: Dr Cash 30,000 / Cr Sales Discount Forfeited 600 / Cr AR 29,400 — same total revenue and cash.)*

**f. Presentation**

*Year 1 net-method revenue (these customers)*

| Income Statement — Excerpt (Year 1) | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales revenue ($49,000 + $19,600) | $68,600 |
| Sales discounts forfeited (YE accrual) | 600 |
| **Total revenue** | **$69,200** |

*(Sales discounts forfeited is typically reported in **other revenues (expenses)**, not as a reduction of sales.)*

*Dec 31, Year 1 AR (after YE adj.)* = **$49,600** (Quill $30,000 + Mariner $19,600).

**Lifecycle cash proof:**  
Cash = \(19{,}600 + 19{,}600 + 30{,}000 = \mathbf{\$69{,}200}\) = total revenue recognized (\(68{,}600 + 600\)). ✓  
Original combined gross invoices = \(50{,}000 + 20{,}000 = \$70{,}000\); discounts **taken** (not forfeited) = \(400 + 400 = \$800\); cash \(70{,}000 - 800 = \$69{,}200\). ✓

**Key insight:** The net method **assumes the discount will be taken** at initial recognition. Early payment simply clears net AR. At period-end (emphasis), only open balances **expected to forfeit** are stepped up with **Sales Discount Forfeited**. After that accrual, late collection is a pure cash-for-AR settlement. Gross and net methods both need YE adjustments; they pull **opposite** directions (gross: reduce for expected takes; net: increase for expected forfeits).

### Q4 — MC — Period-end cash-discount adjusting entry (method choice)

**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Classification of the correct **period-end adjusting JE** under the gross method vs net method for cash discounts  
**Question:**  
At year-end, **Tideglass Co.** has open trade receivables of **$80,000** (recorded at **gross** invoice) that are still inside their cash-discount windows. Management expects customers will take the **2%** cash discount on these balances. Tideglass uses the **gross method**. Which journal entry correctly records the **period-end adjusting entry**?

- A) Debit Accounts Receivable $1,600; credit Sales Discount Forfeited $1,600  
- B) Debit Sales Discount $1,600; credit Accounts Receivable (or Allowance for Sales Discounts) $1,600  
- C) Debit Bad Debt Expense $1,600; credit Allowance for Doubtful Accounts $1,600  
- D) Debit Sales Returns $1,600; credit Refund Liability $1,600  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Under the **gross method**, the YE entry **reduces** revenue and AR (or credits a contra-AR allowance) for cash discounts **expected to be taken**: \(80{,}000 \times 0.02 = \$1{,}600\).  
A is the **net-method** forfeit-style entry (and would apply only if AR were already at net and forfeiture were expected).  
C is the allowance for **credit losses** (LO 8-4), not cash discounts.  
D is the **sales returns / refund liability** estimate (LO 8-3), not cash discounts.

### Q5 — MC — Gross vs net initial measurement

**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Method classification — initial measurement of AR and sales when a cash discount is offered  
**Question:**  
**Windspire Outfitters** sells merchandise with a **gross invoice price of $25,000**, terms **2/10, n/30**. If Windspire uses the **net method**, what amounts are debited to Accounts Receivable and credited to Sales Revenue on the sale date (ignore COGS)?

- A) $25,000 and $25,000  
- B) $24,500 and $24,500  
- C) $25,000 debit to AR; $24,500 credit to Sales; $500 credit to Sales Discount  
- D) $24,500 debit to AR; $25,000 credit to Sales; $500 debit to Sales Discount  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Net method records AR and Sales at \(25{,}000 \times 0.98 = \mathbf{\$24{,}500}\).  
A is the **gross method** initial measurement.  
C and D invent split entries that are not used under either standard method at the sale date.
