# Agent 238 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-2

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Account for common stock issuance, including par and no-par, cash and noncash, and issue costs  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** par-value common stock issued for cash; Common Stock credited at par; excess to Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock
- **Stock issue costs:** reduce net cash and reduce PIC in excess of par (not expense; excluded from net income under ASC 505-10-25-2)
- **Noncash issuance / settlement:** measure at fair value of stock or noncash consideration, whichever is more clearly evident
- **Multiple securities (lump-sum):** proportional allocation when both FVs known; incremental when only one FV known
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-event **paid-in capital and shares roll-forward** (composition of CS, PIC-CS, PS, PIC-PS and shares issued/outstanding after each issuance)
- **No-par vs stated-value:** true no-par credits Common Stock for full proceeds; stated-value no-par treated like par stock
- **Period-end settlement JE:** year-end equity issuance for services/assets when measurement is finalized at period end
- Classification: stock issue costs → **equity (PIC reduction)**, not period expense

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-event issuances with paid-in capital roll-forward schedule (emphasis: subsequent measurement schedule)
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Initial cash issuance with issue costs; noncash land settlement; proportional lump-sum common+preferred; year-end noncash services settlement; subsequent paid-in capital / shares schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**Briarstone Components Corp.** is newly incorporated on **January 1, Year 1** (calendar year). The corporate charter authorizes:

| Security | Authorized | Par |
|---|---:|---|
| Common stock | 500,000 shares | **$2** |
| Preferred stock | 50,000 shares | **$10** |

There is **no beginning equity**. During Year 1 the company completes the following **separate** equity transactions:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **Jan 5** | Issues **30,000** common shares for cash at **$25** per share. Stock issue costs (legal, underwriting, registration) total **$9,000**, paid in cash from the proceeds. |
| **Mar 18** | Privately issues **4,000** common shares in exchange for **machinery**. The fair value of the common shares is **not clearly evident**. A recent independent appraisal values the machinery at **$92,000**. |
| **Aug 1** | Issues a **combined (lump-sum)** package of **8,000** common shares and **2,000** preferred shares for total cash of **$288,000**. At issuance, common stock is selling at **$30** per share and preferred stock at **$40** per share. |
| **Dec 31** | Settles a year-end obligation by issuing **1,500** common shares for **consulting services** performed in Q4. The fair value of the services is more clearly evident and equals **$40,500**. (Stock market for Briarstone is thin; use service FV.) |

No treasury stock transactions occur. All issued common shares remain outstanding.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 5** journal entry for the cash common stock issuance **including stock issue costs** (initial recognition).  
b. Record the **March 18** journal entry for the noncash machinery issuance (settlement for noncash consideration).  
c. Compute the **proportional allocation** of the August 1 lump-sum proceeds and record the **August 1** journal entry.  
d. Prepare a complete **subsequent measurement schedule** of **shares issued** and **paid-in capital account balances** after **each** of the three events above (Jan 5, Mar 18, Aug 1). Show beginning zero balances, the effect of each event, and ending balances.  
e. Record the **December 31** period-end settlement entry for shares issued for consulting services.  
f. Extend the subsequent measurement schedule through **December 31** and state total **paid-in capital** and common shares **issued and outstanding** at year-end.  
g. In one or two sentences, explain why the $9,000 stock issue costs are **not** recorded as an expense.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — cash issuance with issue costs**

Gross proceeds = \(30{,}000 \times \$25 = \$750{,}000\).  
Net cash = \(750{,}000 - 9{,}000 = \$741{,}000\).  
Common Stock (par) = \(30{,}000 \times \$2 = \$60{,}000\).  
PIC excess of par (before costs) = \(30{,}000 \times (\$25 - \$2) = \$690{,}000\).  
PIC after issue costs = \(690{,}000 - 9{,}000 = \$681{,}000\).

*January 5, Year 1 — Issue par common stock for cash (net of issue costs)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 741,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 60,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 681,000 |
| *Record 30,000 shares, $2 par, at $25; issue costs reduce PIC* | | |

**Check:** Dr 741,000 = Cr 60,000 + 681,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Settlement — noncash machinery (FV of asset more clearly evident)**

*March 18, Year 1 — Issue common stock for machinery*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Machinery | 92,000 | |
| Common Stock (4,000 × $2) | | 8,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock ($92,000 − $8,000) | | 84,000 |
| *Measure at appraised FV of machinery* | | |

**Check:** Dr 92,000 = Cr 8,000 + 84,000. **Balanced.**

**c. August 1 — proportional lump-sum allocation**

Fair values of securities issued:

| Class | Computation | Fair value |
|---|---|---:|
| Common | \(8{,}000 \times \$30\) | 240,000 |
| Preferred | \(2{,}000 \times \$40\) | 80,000 |
| **Total FV** | | **320,000** |

Proportional allocation of **$288,000** lump-sum cash:

| Class | Fraction | Allocation |
|---|---|---:|
| Common | \(240{,}000 / 320{,}000 = 75\%\) | \(288{,}000 \times 0.75 = \mathbf{216{,}000}\) |
| Preferred | \(80{,}000 / 320{,}000 = 25\%\) | \(288{,}000 \times 0.25 = \mathbf{72{,}000}\) |
| **Total** | | **288,000** |

Par and PIC split:

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common Stock \(8{,}000 \times \$2\) | 16,000 |
| PIC—Common \(216{,}000 - 16{,}000\) | 200,000 |
| Preferred Stock \(2{,}000 \times \$10\) | 20,000 |
| PIC—Preferred \(72{,}000 - 20{,}000\) | 52,000 |

*August 1, Year 1 — Multiple securities issuance (proportional)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 288,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 16,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 200,000 |
| Preferred Stock | | 20,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Preferred Stock | | 52,000 |

**Check:** Dr 288,000 = Cr 16,000 + 200,000 + 20,000 + 52,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — through August 1**

| Date / event | Common shares issued | Common Stock | PIC—CS | Preferred shares | Preferred Stock | PIC—PS | Total paid-in capital |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1 beginning | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Jan 5 cash issue (net costs) | +30,000 | +60,000 | +681,000 | — | — | — | +741,000 |
| **After Jan 5** | **30,000** | **60,000** | **681,000** | **0** | **0** | **0** | **741,000** |
| Mar 18 machinery | +4,000 | +8,000 | +84,000 | — | — | — | +92,000 |
| **After Mar 18** | **34,000** | **68,000** | **765,000** | **0** | **0** | **0** | **833,000** |
| Aug 1 lump-sum | +8,000 | +16,000 | +200,000 | +2,000 | +20,000 | +52,000 | +288,000 |
| **After Aug 1** | **42,000** | **84,000** | **965,000** | **2,000** | **20,000** | **52,000** | **1,121,000** |

**Roll-forward check:** Cash/assets into equity = \(741{,}000 + 92{,}000 + 288{,}000 = \$1{,}121{,}000\) = total paid-in capital.  
Account sum: \(84{,}000 + 965{,}000 + 20{,}000 + 52{,}000 = \$1{,}121{,}000\). **OK.**

**e. Period-end settlement JE — shares for services**

*December 31, Year 1 — Issue common stock for consulting services*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Consulting Expense | 40,500 | |
| Common Stock (1,500 × $2) | | 3,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock ($40,500 − $3,000) | | 37,500 |
| *Measure at more clearly evident FV of services* | | |

**Check:** Dr 40,500 = Cr 3,000 + 37,500. **Balanced.**

**f. Schedule extended through December 31**

| Date / event | Common shares | Common Stock | PIC—CS | Pref. shares | Preferred Stock | PIC—PS | Total paid-in |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| After Aug 1 (from d) | 42,000 | 84,000 | 965,000 | 2,000 | 20,000 | 52,000 | 1,121,000 |
| Dec 31 services | +1,500 | +3,000 | +37,500 | — | — | — | +40,500 |
| **Dec 31 Year 1 ending** | **43,500** | **87,000** | **1,002,500** | **2,000** | **20,000** | **52,000** | **1,161,500** |

**Year-end totals:**  
- Common shares **issued and outstanding** = **43,500** (authorized 500,000; unissued common 456,500).  
- Preferred shares issued and outstanding = **2,000**.  
- Total paid-in capital = **$1,161,500**.  
- Check: \(1{,}121{,}000 + 40{,}500 = 1{,}161{,}500\); accounts \(87{,}000 + 1{,}002{,}500 + 20{,}000 + 52{,}000 = 1{,}161{,}500\).

**g. Issue costs not expense**  
Stock issue costs relate to a capital transaction in the entity’s **own stock**, not operations. Under ASC 505-10-25-2 they are **excluded from net income** and are treated as a **reduction of paid-in capital in excess of par** (netted against the proceeds).

**Key insight:** Equity is not remeasured at fair value each period. The **subsequent measurement schedule** for common stock issuance is a **paid-in capital and shares roll-forward**: after each issuance, Common Stock holds cumulative **par × shares issued**, and PIC holds cumulative excess (net of issue costs). Year-end noncash settlements use the same measurement rule (more clearly evident FV) and feed the same schedule.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Cash, noncash, lump-sum, year-end equipment settlement, equity schedule
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: par cash issue with costs; noncash land; proportional lump-sum; period-end noncash equipment settlement; subsequent paid-in capital schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborlight Logistics Inc.** (calendar year) is newly formed January 1, Year 1. Authorized: **1,000,000** common shares, **$1 par**; **100,000** preferred shares, **$8 par**. Beginning equity = $0.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **Jan 10** | Issues **80,000** common shares for cash at **$11** per share; stock issue costs **$12,000**. |
| **Apr 1** | Issues **10,000** common shares for **land**. Stock FV not clearly evident; land appraised at **$145,000**. |
| **Jun 15** | Lump-sum issue of **15,000** common + **5,000** preferred for cash **$420,000**. Market prices: common **$22**, preferred **$30**. |
| **Dec 31** | Issues **3,000** common shares for **equipment**. Common stock is actively traded at **$24** per share (use stock FV as more clearly evident). |

All issued shares remain outstanding.

**Required:**  
a. Record January 10 cash issuance **including issue costs**.  
b. Record April 1 noncash land issuance.  
c. Allocate June 15 lump-sum proportionally and record the JE.  
d. Build the **subsequent measurement schedule** of shares and paid-in capital accounts after each of Jan 10, Apr 1, and Jun 15.  
e. Record December 31 equipment settlement.  
f. Complete the schedule through December 31; compute total paid-in capital and reconcile to the sum of all consideration received (net cash + land + equipment).

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 10 — cash with issue costs**

Gross = \(80{,}000 \times 11 = \$880{,}000\).  
Net cash = \(880{,}000 - 12{,}000 = \$868{,}000\).  
CS = \(80{,}000 \times 1 = \$80{,}000\).  
PIC-CS = \(80{,}000 \times (11 - 1) - 12{,}000 = 800{,}000 - 12{,}000 = \$788{,}000\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 868,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 80,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 788,000 |

**Check:** Dr 868,000 = Cr 80,000 + 788,000. **Balanced.**

**b. April 1 — land (asset FV more clearly evident)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Land | 145,000 | |
| Common Stock (10,000 × $1) | | 10,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 135,000 |

**Check:** Dr 145,000 = Cr 10,000 + 135,000. **Balanced.**

**c. June 15 — proportional lump-sum**

| Class | FV computation | Fair value |
|---|---|---:|
| Common | \(15{,}000 \times \$22\) | 330,000 |
| Preferred | \(5{,}000 \times \$30\) | 150,000 |
| **Total** | | **480,000** |

| Class | Allocation of $420,000 |
|---|---:|
| Common \(420{,}000 \times 330/480 = 420{,}000 \times 11/16\) | **288,750** |
| Preferred \(420{,}000 \times 150/480 = 420{,}000 \times 5/16\) | **131,250** |
| **Total** | **420,000** |

CS par = \(15{,}000 \times \$1 = \$15{,}000\); PIC-CS = \(288{,}750 - 15{,}000 = \$273{,}750\).  
PS par = \(5{,}000 \times \$8 = \$40{,}000\); PIC-PS = \(131{,}250 - 40{,}000 = \$91{,}250\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 420,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 15,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 273,750 |
| Preferred Stock | | 40,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Preferred Stock | | 91,250 |

**Check:** Dr 420,000 = Cr 15,000 + 273,750 + 40,000 + 91,250. **Balanced.**

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule through June 15**

| Date / event | C shares | Common Stock | PIC—CS | P shares | Preferred Stock | PIC—PS | Total paid-in |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Jan 10 | +80,000 | +80,000 | +788,000 | — | — | — | +868,000 |
| **After Jan 10** | **80,000** | **80,000** | **788,000** | **0** | **0** | **0** | **868,000** |
| Apr 1 | +10,000 | +10,000 | +135,000 | — | — | — | +145,000 |
| **After Apr 1** | **90,000** | **90,000** | **923,000** | **0** | **0** | **0** | **1,013,000** |
| Jun 15 | +15,000 | +15,000 | +273,750 | +5,000 | +40,000 | +91,250 | +420,000 |
| **After Jun 15** | **105,000** | **105,000** | **1,196,750** | **5,000** | **40,000** | **91,250** | **1,433,000** |

**Check:** \(868{,}000 + 145{,}000 + 420{,}000 = 1{,}433{,}000\); \(105{,}000 + 1{,}196{,}750 + 40{,}000 + 91{,}250 = 1{,}433{,}000\).

**e. December 31 — equipment settlement (stock FV more clearly evident)**

Measurement = \(3{,}000 \times \$24 = \$72{,}000\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 72,000 | |
| Common Stock (3,000 × $1) | | 3,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 69,000 |

**Check:** Dr 72,000 = Cr 3,000 + 69,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Schedule through December 31 and reconciliation**

| Date / event | C shares | Common Stock | PIC—CS | P shares | Preferred Stock | PIC—PS | Total paid-in |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| After Jun 15 | 105,000 | 105,000 | 1,196,750 | 5,000 | 40,000 | 91,250 | 1,433,000 |
| Dec 31 equipment | +3,000 | +3,000 | +69,000 | — | — | — | +72,000 |
| **Dec 31 ending** | **108,000** | **108,000** | **1,265,750** | **5,000** | **40,000** | **91,250** | **1,505,000** |

**Reconciliation of total paid-in capital to consideration:**

| Source | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net cash (Jan 10) | 868,000 |
| Land (Apr 1) | 145,000 |
| Cash lump-sum (Jun 15) | 420,000 |
| Equipment (Dec 31) | 72,000 |
| **Total consideration → paid-in capital** | **1,505,000** |

Account sum: \(108{,}000 + 1{,}265{,}750 + 40{,}000 + 91{,}250 = \$1{,}505{,}000\). **OK.**

**Key insight:** Changing every number does not change the framework: **par to Common Stock**, excess (net of issue costs) to **PIC**, lump-sums by **relative FV**, noncash by **more clearly evident FV**, and the **schedule** is the cumulative equity composition after each event.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — No-par, stated-value, and incremental lump-sum allocation
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** True no-par vs stated-value no-par cash issuance; incremental allocation when only one security’s FV is known  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Quill Media Corp.** records three **independent** January 1 issuance scenarios (do not combine them). For (a)–(b) ignore preferred stock. For (c) both classes are issued together.

| Scenario | Facts |
|---|---|
| **(a) True no-par** | Issue **25,000** shares of **true no-par** common for cash at **$16** per share. |
| **(b) Stated value** | Same economics: issue **25,000** shares of **no-par common with $0.50 stated value** for cash at **$16** per share. |
| **(c) Incremental lump-sum** | Issue **4,000** common shares (**$1 par**) and **1,000** preferred shares (**$20 par**) for a **lump-sum cash price of $120,000**. Common stock is selling at **$25** per share; the market price of preferred is **unknown**. |

**Required:**  
a. Record the true **no-par** cash issuance.  
b. Record the **stated-value** no-par cash issuance.  
c. Allocate the lump-sum using the **incremental** method and record the journal entry.  
d. Briefly contrast how much of the proceeds in (a) vs (b) is labeled “Common Stock” vs additional paid-in capital, and state when incremental (vs proportional) allocation is used.

**Answer key:**  

**a. True no-par**

Proceeds = \(25{,}000 \times \$16 = \$400{,}000\). Entire amount → Common Stock; **no PIC**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 400,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 400,000 |

**Check:** Dr 400,000 = Cr 400,000. **Balanced.**

**b. No-par with stated value $0.50 (accounted like par stock)**

Common Stock = \(25{,}000 \times \$0.50 = \$12{,}500\).  
PIC in excess of stated value = \(400{,}000 - 12{,}500 = \$387{,}500\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 400,000 | |
| Common Stock (25,000 × $0.50 stated) | | 12,500 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Stated Value—Common Stock | | 387,500 |

**Check:** Dr 400,000 = Cr 12,500 + 387,500. **Balanced.**

**c. Incremental allocation (only common FV known)**

Common allocation (known FV) = \(4{,}000 \times \$25 = \$100{,}000\).  
Preferred allocation (residual) = \(120{,}000 - 100{,}000 = \$20{,}000\).

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common Stock \(4{,}000 \times \$1\) | 4,000 |
| PIC—Common \(100{,}000 - 4{,}000\) | 96,000 |
| Preferred Stock \(1{,}000 \times \$20\) | 20,000 |
| PIC—Preferred \(20{,}000 - 20{,}000\) | 0 |

*January 1 — Lump-sum issuance (incremental)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 120,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 4,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 96,000 |
| Preferred Stock | | 20,000 |

**Check:** Dr 120,000 = Cr 4,000 + 96,000 + 20,000. **Balanced.**  
(Note: PIC—Preferred is **zero** because residual proceeds equal preferred par.)

**d. Contrast / method choice**  
In (a), **all** $400,000 is Common Stock; in (b), only **stated value** ($12,500) is Common Stock and the remainder is PIC. Economically identical cash, different legal-capital labeling.  
**Proportional** allocation is used when **fair values of both** (all) classes are known; **incremental** is used when the FV of **only one** class is known—assign that known FV first and give the **residual** lump-sum to the other class.

**Key insight:** No-par vs stated-value changes **account labels**, not total equity. Lump-sum method choice (proportional vs incremental) is driven by **which fair values are observable**, not by management preference.

### Q4 — MC (classification / measurement)
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Classification of stock issue costs; measurement basis for noncash stock issuance  

**Question 1:**  
Under U.S. GAAP (as applied in this chapter), how should **stock issue costs** (registration fees, underwriter commissions, legal fees related to issuance) be accounted for when common stock is sold for cash?

- A) Debited to Organization Expense (or Issuance Expense) in the period incurred  
- B) Capitalized as a deferred charge and amortized to expense over a selected period  
- C) Treated as a **reduction of paid-in capital in excess of par** (netted against proceeds); excluded from net income  
- D) Debited directly to Retained Earnings as a prior-period-type charge  

**Answer:** **C.** Stock issue costs reduce net cash and reduce PIC in excess of par. ASC 505-10-25-2 excludes charges from transactions in an entity’s own capital stock from the determination of net income. They are not operating expenses and are not amortized.

**Question 2:**  
When a corporation issues common stock in exchange for a noncash asset (e.g., land or equipment), the transaction should be measured at:

- A) The par value of the shares issued only  
- B) The book value of the asset on the transferor’s books  
- C) The **fair value of the stock issued or of the noncash consideration received, whichever is more clearly evident**  
- D) Always the board of directors’ arbitrary assignment, even when a market price of the stock is clearly evident  

**Answer:** **C.** Core LO 19-2 measurement rule: use the more clearly evident fair value (stock or asset). Appraisals or board valuations are used only when neither market value is clearly evident.
