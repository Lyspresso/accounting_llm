# Agent 288 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-1

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Classifying Differences Between GAAP Income and Taxable Income  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- Book-to-tax differences classified as **taxable temporary**, **deductible temporary**, or **permanent** (nontaxable / nondeductible)
- **Taxable temporary**: reverses by **increasing future taxable income relative to pretax GAAP** (prepaid expensed for tax when paid; installment receivable; excess tax depreciation)
- **Deductible temporary**: reverses by **decreasing future taxable income relative to pretax GAAP** (warranty accrual; estimated bad debts; deferred revenue taxed on receipt)
- **Permanent** differences **never reverse** → no deferred tax asset or liability (municipal interest; fines; nondeductible premiums)
- Balance-sheet view: temporary difference when **GAAP carrying amount ≠ tax basis** of an asset or liability
- **Subsequent measurement schedule**: multi-year origin / reverse / ending **cumulative temporary difference** roll-forward
- **Initial recognition JEs** for difference-creating items
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis)**: year-end income tax entry that records current tax payable and adjusts DTA/DTL to ending cumulative TD × enacted rate
- **Settlement / maturity** of temporary differences (prepaid expires; warranty paid; advances earned; installment collected)
- Classification / presentation: current vs deferred tax expense; DTA/DTL reported **noncurrent** (net within jurisdiction)

### Q4 — MC — Classification of warranty accrual
**LO:** LO 18-1  
**Concept:** Classification choice — taxable temporary vs deductible temporary vs permanent (warranty accrual)  
**Question:**  
**Harborlight Appliances Co.** records a **$50,000** warranty expense and Accrued Warranty Liability at December 31, Year 1 under GAAP. Warranty costs will be paid in Years 2–3. For tax purposes, warranty costs are deductible only when paid. How should Harborlight classify this book-to-tax difference at December 31, Year 1?

- A) Taxable temporary difference (creates a deferred tax liability)  
- B) Deductible temporary difference (creates a deferred tax asset)  
- C) Permanent difference — nondeductible item (never creates deferred taxes)  
- D) Permanent difference — nontaxable item (never creates deferred taxes)  

**Answer:** **B.** The GAAP liability basis ($50,000) exceeds the tax basis ($0). When the difference reverses, taxable income will be **lower** relative to pretax GAAP because tax deductions occur as cash is paid while GAAP already expensed the amount in Year 1. That pattern defines a **deductible temporary difference** and a **deferred tax asset**.

### Q5 — MC — Classification of prepaid expense deductible when paid
**LO:** LO 18-1  
**Concept:** Classification choice — prepaid expense deductible for tax when paid  
**Question:**  
**Prairie Wind Energy Inc.** pays **$12,000** on December 31, Year 1 for a maintenance contract covering Year 2. GAAP capitalizes Prepaid Maintenance; tax deducts the $12,000 in Year 1 when paid. At December 31, Year 1, this difference is:

- A) A deductible temporary difference because future taxable income will decrease relative to pretax GAAP  
- B) A taxable temporary difference because future taxable income will increase relative to pretax GAAP when the prepaid is expensed for books  
- C) A permanent difference because prepaid assets never reverse  
- D) Neither temporary nor permanent until Year 2 when amortization begins  

**Answer:** **B.** GAAP asset basis ($12,000) > tax basis ($0). In Year 2, GAAP recognizes expense while tax has no remaining deduction → future TI is **higher** relative to pretax GAAP. That is a **taxable temporary difference** (deferred tax liability).
