# Agent 339 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-11

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Depreciation under GAAP and under the Internal Revenue Code are different  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **GAAP depreciation** allocates historical cost (less residual value) over **economic useful life** in a systematic and rational manner (expense recognition); residual value is **not** depreciated
- **Tax depreciation (MACRS)** uses **statutory class lives and recovery percentages** set by the IRC; residual value is **ignored**; asset cost is fully recovered for tax
- **Half-year convention** is built into MACRS first- and last-year rates (assume mid-year acquisition on average)
- **Book vs tax** amounts generally **differ each year** and in total (book stops at residual; MACRS goes to zero cost basis)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** only **GAAP** depreciation is recorded on the books (Dr Depreciation Expense / Cr Accumulated Depreciation); MACRS is a **tax-return** deduction, not a book journal entry
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** side-by-side book (SL) and tax (MACRS) depreciation, carrying amount (book) vs tax basis
- **Disposal:** book gain/loss uses **book carrying amount**; tax gain/loss uses **tax basis** (often $0 after full MACRS recovery); lifetime net effect on pretax / taxable income can be the same, but timing differs (time value of money)
- **Bonus depreciation** (when elected) can accelerate tax deduction further; taxpayers may elect out; ignore unless stated
- Deferred tax accounting for the temporary difference is Chapter 18 (acknowledge only)

### Q1 — CORE — Purchase, GAAP SL vs MACRS (3-year) schedules, period-end book JEs (emphasis), disposal book vs tax

**LO:** LO 12-11  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of PPE; subsequent book SL vs MACRS tax schedules; **period-end adjusting JEs for GAAP depreciation only (emphasis)**; disposal with different book vs tax gain  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Forge Co.** (calendar-year reporter) acquires production equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$60,000 cash**. The equipment qualifies as **three-year class** property under MACRS. Summit does **not** elect bonus depreciation.

**GAAP (financial reporting):**
- Estimated useful life: **5 years**
- Estimated residual (salvage) value: **$10,000**
- Method: **straight-line**
- Partial-period: full year in Year 1 (placed in service January 1)

**Tax (Internal Revenue Code / MACRS):**
- Class life: **3-year**
- Cost basis: **$60,000** (residual ignored)
- Use statutory three-year MACRS percentages above

On **December 31, Year 4**, Summit sells the equipment for **$11,500 cash**. At that date, Year 4 book depreciation has already been recorded; MACRS recovery for Years 1–4 is complete (tax basis = $0).

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** of the equipment.  
(b) Compute **annual GAAP straight-line depreciation**. Prepare a **book depreciation schedule** (expense, accumulated depreciation, carrying amount) for Years 1–5 (or through disposal).  
(c) Prepare a **MACRS tax depreciation schedule** for all recovery years (cost × rate = tax deduction).  
(d) Record the **period-end adjusting journal entry for GAAP depreciation** at December 31 of Years 1–4 (emphasis). State whether a journal entry is made for MACRS.  
(e) Prepare a **side-by-side comparison** of book depreciation, tax depreciation, book carrying amount, and tax basis at each year-end through Year 4.  
(f) Record the **December 31, Year 4 disposal** for **book** purposes. Compute the **tax** gain on sale. Compare lifetime net book effect vs lifetime net tax effect of (depreciation ± gain/loss).

**(f) December 31, Year 4 — disposal (book) and tax gain**

Book carrying amount at sale = \(60{,}000 - 40{,}000 = 20{,}000\).  
Cash proceeds = $11,500.  
**Book loss** = \(20{,}000 - 11{,}500 = 8{,}500\).

|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 11,500 | |

| | Book | Tax |
|---|---:|---:|

No lifetime net difference; **timing** of deductions vs gain differs (MACRS front-loads tax depreciation). Deferred tax accounting for temporary differences is covered in Chapter 18.

**Key insight:** GAAP allocates cost − residual over economic life via period-end book adjusting entries; MACRS uses statutory rates, ignores residual, and is not booked as Depreciation Expense. Disposal measures gain/loss against **book CA** vs **tax basis** separately, though lifetime net cost of ownership equals cost − proceeds for both.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — 5-year MACRS vs book SL; period-end JEs; disposal

**LO:** LO 12-11  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial PPE recognition; 5-year MACRS tax schedule vs book SL; period-end GAAP adjusting JEs (emphasis); disposal book vs tax  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakeside Calibration LLC** purchases a specialized analyzer on **January 1, Year 1** for **$80,000 cash**. The asset is **five-year class** MACRS property. No bonus depreciation.

**GAAP:**
- Useful life: **8 years**
- Residual value: **$8,000**
- Method: straight-line

**Tax:** MACRS five-year recovery percentages (table above); residual ignored.

On **January 1, Year 5** (after recording Year 4 book depreciation at Dec 31, Year 4), Lakeside sells the analyzer for **$22,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
(a) Initial recognition JE.  
(b) Annual book SL amount and book schedule through Year 4 (and full-life total if held).  
(c) Full MACRS tax schedule (Years 1–6).  
(d) Period-end GAAP depreciation adjusting entries for Years 1–4 (emphasis).  
(e) Book carrying amount and tax basis at December 31, Year 4; disposal JE on January 1, Year 5; book gain/loss and tax gain/loss.  
(f) Lifetime net book vs tax effect of depreciation through Year 4 plus disposal.

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 80,000 | |
| Cash | | 80,000 |

**Check:** Dr 80,000 = Cr 80,000. **Balanced.**

**(b) GAAP straight-line**

\[
\text{Annual SL} = \frac{\$80{,}000 - \$8{,}000}{8} = \$9{,}000 \text{ per year}
\]

**Book schedule (Years 1–4 used; full life shown for context):**

| Year | Dep. expense | Accum. dep. (EOY) | Carrying amount (EOY) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 9,000 | 9,000 | 71,000 |

**(c) MACRS tax schedule (5-year class)**

| Year | Rate | Tax depreciation (\(80{,}000 \times\) rate) |
|---:|---:|---:|

Tax basis Dec 31, Year 4 = \(80{,}000 - 66{,}176 = \mathbf{13{,}824}\).

**Four-year total book depreciation recorded:** \(4 \times 9{,}000 = 36{,}000\).

No MACRS book JE.

**(e) January 1, Year 5 — disposal**

Book CA = $44,000; proceeds = $22,000 → **book loss = $22,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 22,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 36,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 22,000 | |

**(f) Lifetime net through disposal (after 4 years of use)**

| | Book | Tax |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Y1–Y4 | 36,000 | 66,176 |
| Gain (loss) on disposal | (22,000) | 8,176 |
| **Net reduction** | **58,000** | **58,000** |

Reconciliation: \(80{,}000 - 22{,}000 = 58{,}000\) (cost − cash). Timing still differs: tax has larger early deductions and a taxable gain; book has smaller annual expense and a larger book loss at sale.

**Key insight:** Changing class life (5-year) and numbers does not change the framework: statutory MACRS × cost (ignore residual) for tax; SL on (cost − residual)/life for book; only book amounts hit the period-end adjusting JE; disposal uses different bases.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end emphasis: mid-life true-up of book dep; one-year book JE vs multi-year MACRS already taken; classification of differences

**LO:** LO 12-11  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE emphasis — compute current-year book depreciation only; reconcile cumulative book vs MACRS tax; identify what is / is not journalized; presentation of book carrying amount  
**Scenario:**  
**Northline Packaging Inc.** bought machinery on **January 1, Year 1** for **$45,000**. Facts:

| Item | Amount / policy |
|---|---|
| Cost | $45,000 |
| GAAP residual | $5,000 |
| GAAP useful life | 4 years |
| GAAP method | Straight-line |
| MACRS class | 3-year |
| Bonus depreciation | Not used |

It is now **December 31, Year 2**. Northline has correctly recorded **Year 1** book depreciation. The **unadjusted trial balance** before Year 2 closing adjustments shows:

| Account | Balance |
|---|---:|
| Equipment | 45,000 Dr |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 10,000 Cr |
| Depreciation Expense (Y2 YTD) | 0 |

**Required:**  
(a) Compute the **correct Year 2 GAAP depreciation** and record the **December 31, Year 2 period-end adjusting JE** (emphasis).  
(b) Complete a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–4 (book) and Years 1–4 (MACRS tax).  
(c) At December 31, Year 2, state **book carrying amount**, **tax basis**, and the **cumulative temporary difference** (tax basis vs book CA).  
(d) Show the **balance sheet presentation** of the machinery at December 31, Year 2 (GAAP).  
(e) Briefly explain why MACRS may **not** be used as GAAP depreciation when class life is outside a reasonable range of useful life (ASC 360 guidance in chapter).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Year 2 period-end adjusting JE (emphasis)**

\[
\text{Annual SL} = \frac{\$45{,}000 - \$5{,}000}{4} = \$10{,}000
\]

Unadjusted Accum. Dep. of $10,000 equals one full year → Year 1 was correctly recorded. Year 2 expense = **$10,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 10,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 10,000 |
| *Dec 31, Year 2 period-end GAAP depreciation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 10,000 = Cr 10,000. **Balanced.**

After entry: Accum. Dep. = $20,000; Depreciation Expense Y2 = $10,000.

**(b) Subsequent measurement schedules**

**Book (GAAP SL):**

| Year | Dep. expense | Accum. dep. | Carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 10,000 | 10,000 | 35,000 |
| 2 | 10,000 | 20,000 | 25,000 |
| 3 | 10,000 | 30,000 | 15,000 |
| 4 | 10,000 | 40,000 | 5,000 |
| **Total** | **40,000** | | |

**Tax (MACRS 3-year on $45,000):**

| Year | Rate | Tax dep. | Cum. tax dep. | Tax basis EOY |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 33.33% | **14,998.50** → **14,999\*** | 14,999 | 30,001 |
| 2 | 44.45% | **20,002.50** → **20,003\*** | 35,002 | 9,998 |
| 3 | 14.81% | **6,664.50** → **6,664\*** | 41,666 | 3,334 |
| 4 | 7.41% | **3,334.50** → **3,334\*** | 45,000 | 0 |
| **Total** | | **45,000** | | |

\*Round each year to the nearest dollar so the four years sum exactly to $45,000 (standard practice; slight penny differences from raw \(45{,}000 \times\) rate are absorbed in rounding). Using unrounded then adjusting:

Exact:  
- Y1: \(45{,}000 \times 0.3333 = 14{,}998.50\)  
- Y2: \(45{,}000 \times 0.4445 = 20{,}002.50\)  
- Y3: \(45{,}000 \times 0.1481 = 6{,}664.50\)  
- Y4: \(45{,}000 \times 0.0741 = 3{,}334.50\)  
Sum of exact = \(45{,}000.00\).

For nearest-dollar tax return style without double-counting: keep **one-decimal half-dollar** or report:

| Year | Tax dep. (exact) |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 14,998.50 |
| 2 | 20,002.50 |
| 3 | 6,664.50 |
| 4 | 3,334.50 |
| **Total** | **45,000.00** |

**(Preferred whole-dollar schedule for exams — use rates × cost rounded to nearest dollar, plug final year):**

| Year | Rate | Tax dep. |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 33.33% | 14,999 |
| 2 | 44.45% | 20,003 |
| 3 | 14.81% | 6,665 |
| 4 | plug | **3,333** |
| **Total** | | **45,000** |

Use **exact product** method (first table with .50) for rigor, or whole-dollar plug method — either is acceptable if total = cost. Below for part (c) use **exact half-dollars** then state whole dollars:

**(c) December 31, Year 2 positions**

Using exact MACRS:  
Cum. tax dep. Y1–Y2 = \(14{,}998.50 + 20{,}002.50 = 35{,}001\).  
Tax basis = \(45{,}000 - 35{,}001 = \mathbf{9{,}999}\).

Book CA = \(45{,}000 - 20{,}000 = \mathbf{25{,}000}\).

**Cumulative temporary difference (book CA − tax basis)** = \(25{,}000 - 9{,}999 = \mathbf{15{,}001}\) (taxable temporary difference: tax basis lower → future taxable amounts when reversed / on disposal; deferred tax liability setup is Ch. 18).

Whole-dollar alternative: tax dep Y1–Y2 = 14,999 + 20,003 = 35,002; tax basis = 9,998; difference vs book CA 25,000 = 15,002.

**(d) Balance sheet presentation (December 31, Year 2) — GAAP only**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Equipment | $45,000 |
| Less: Accumulated depreciation—equipment | (20,000) |
| **Equipment, net** | **$25,000** |

Tax basis and MACRS are **not** reported on the GAAP balance sheet as the carrying amount of PPE.

**(e) Why MACRS may not equal GAAP depreciation**

Per chapter/ASC 360 guidance: if the number of years specified by ACRS/MACRS recovery does **not** fall within a **reasonable range** of the asset’s **useful life**, those recovery deductions shall **not** be used as depreciation expense for **financial reporting**. MACRS also **ignores residual value** and uses **statutory rates** (including half-year convention), which often fail the GAAP “systematic and rational allocation of cost less salvage over economic life” objective. Tax policy goals (incentive for investment) differ from financial reporting goals (expense recognition / allocation).

**Key insight:** The only period-end **journal entry** is the GAAP adjusting entry. MACRS is computed for the tax return and for measuring tax basis / temporary differences; it does not replace book depreciation on the financial statements.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method choice)

**LO:** LO 12-11  
**Concept:** Classification of book vs tax depreciation attributes; what is journalized at period-end  

**Question 1:**  
Which statement best describes **MACRS** relative to **U.S. GAAP** depreciation for the same asset?

- A) MACRS and GAAP always produce the same annual expense and the same total depreciation over the asset’s life.  
- B) MACRS allocates cost less residual value over economic useful life using management’s estimates; residual is never ignored.  
- C) MACRS multiplies cost by **statutory recovery percentages** for a **property class**, generally **ignores residual value**, and often differs from GAAP annual and total depreciation.  
- D) MACRS is recorded each year as a period-end adjusting entry that credits Accumulated Depreciation for the tax amount.

**Answer:** **C.** MACRS uses class lives and statutory rates, ignores residual, and typically differs from GAAP both yearly and in total (GAAP stops at residual). A is false. B describes GAAP, not MACRS. D is false — only GAAP depreciation is journalized; MACRS is a tax deduction.

---

**Question 2:**  
At year-end, a company that uses straight-line for books and MACRS for tax should record which **period-end adjusting entry** related to depreciation of PPE held for use?

- A) Debit Tax Depreciation Expense and credit Accumulated Depreciation for the MACRS amount.  
- B) Debit Depreciation Expense and credit Accumulated Depreciation for the **GAAP** amount only.  
- C) Debit Depreciation Expense for the **higher** of GAAP or MACRS and credit Accumulated Depreciation.  
- D) Debit Deferred Tax Expense and credit Equipment for the difference between MACRS and GAAP.

**Answer:** **B.** Period-end book adjusting entry uses **GAAP** depreciation only. MACRS is not booked as Depreciation Expense. Deferred taxes (Ch. 18) involve deferred tax assets/liabilities and tax expense, not a direct write-down of Equipment for the MACRS difference.
