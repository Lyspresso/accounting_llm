# Agent 373 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-6

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Account for Stock Dividends and Stock Splits  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Small stock dividend** (generally &lt;20–25% of shares previously outstanding): capitalize **fair value** — Dr Retained Earnings (FV), Cr Common Stock Dividends Distributable (par), Cr PIC—Common (excess)
- **Large stock dividend / stock split effected in the form of a dividend** (≥ ~20–25%): capitalize **par (or stated) value only** — Dr Retained Earnings, Cr Common Stock Dividends Distributable; fair value is irrelevant
- **Distribution (settlement) JE:** Dr Common Stock Dividends Distributable, Cr Common Stock when shares are issued
- **Fractional shares:** pay cash at FV for fractional entitlements; whole-share portion still at FV with PIC excess
- **True stock split / reverse stock split:** **no journal entry**; share count and par per share change proportionally; total capital stock $ and total equity unchanged
- **Subsequent equity / share schedule:** roll forward CS, CSDD, PIC, RE, shares outstanding, and par from before declaration → after declaration → after distribution
- **Period-end presentation (emphasis):** declaration already records the event — typically **no separate year-end adjusting JE** solely because the reporting date falls between declaration and distribution; **Common Stock Dividends Distributable is equity** (not a liability); fractional cash portion is a **current liability**
- **Classification judgment:** size of dividend drives FV vs par measurement basis

### Q1 — CORE — Year-end straddling small dividend, equity schedule, large dividend, true split
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** Initial recognition (small at FV vs large at par); period-end equity presentation of dividends distributable; distribution settlement; true split (no JE); subsequent equity schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**SummitRidge Packaging Corp.** (calendar-year reporter) has the following stockholders’ equity on **December 15, Year 1**, immediately before any dividend or split action:

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $3 par; 60,000 shares issued and outstanding | $180,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 540,000 |
| Retained earnings | 750,000 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$1,470,000** |

The market price of SummitRidge common stock on December 15 is **$22 per share**. Treat **Scenarios A, B, and C independently**, each starting from the December 15 balances above.

**Scenario A — Small stock dividend straddling year-end**  
On **December 15, Year 1**, the board declares a **12%** common stock dividend. Distribution is scheduled for **January 12, Year 2**, to shareholders of record December 28. All entitlements are whole shares (no fractional shares).

**Scenario B — Large stock dividend (stock split effected in the form of a dividend)**  
On **December 15, Year 1**, the board declares a **50%** common stock dividend (additional shares = 50% of shares previously outstanding). Distribution date is **January 12, Year 2**. Market price remains $22 (do **not** use market for measurement of large dividends).

**Scenario C — True stock split**  
On **December 15, Year 1**, SummitRidge effects a **2-for-1** stock split by reducing par value per share (not structured as a stock dividend). Authorized shares are adjusted as needed for disclosure.

**Required:**  
a. **Scenario A:** Prepare the **December 15 declaration** journal entry (initial recognition at fair value). Show Dr = Cr.  
b. **Scenario A — period-end:** Identify any **December 31, Year 1 adjusting journal entry** related to this stock dividend. State how **Common Stock Dividends Distributable** is **classified/presented** on the December 31 balance sheet.  
c. **Scenario A:** Prepare a **subsequent equity / share schedule** at (1) immediately **before** declaration, (2) **December 31, Year 1** (after declaration, before distribution), and (3) **after** distribution on January 12, Year 2. Include CS, CSDD, PIC—CS, RE, total equity, shares outstanding, and par per share.  
d. **Scenario A:** Prepare the **January 12, Year 2 distribution (settlement)** journal entry.  
e. **Scenario B:** Prepare the **December 15 declaration** and **January 12 distribution** journal entries at **par**. State ending CS, PIC, RE, shares outstanding, and total equity after distribution.  
f. **Scenario C:** Journal entry (if any)? Report shares outstanding, par per share, and total common stock dollar amount after the split.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Scenario A — Declaration at fair value (December 15, Year 1)**

New shares = 60,000 × 12% = **7,200**  
Fair value capitalized = 7,200 × $22 = **$158,400**  
Par of distributable shares = 7,200 × $3 = **$21,600**  
PIC excess = 158,400 − 21,600 = **$136,800**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (7,200 × $22) | 158,400 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable (7,200 × $3) | | 21,600 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 136,800 |
| *Capitalize fair value of 12% small stock dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr 158,400 = Cr 21,600 + 136,800. **Balanced.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Period-end adjusting JE and presentation**

**No additional period-end adjusting journal entry** is required solely because the year closed. The December 15 declaration already transferred the full fair-value amount from retained earnings to paid-in capital (CSDD + PIC). There is no accrual of interest, amortization, or further measurement change between declaration and distribution for a stock dividend.

**Presentation at December 31, Year 1:**  
**Common Stock Dividends Distributable $21,600** is reported in the **stockholders’ equity** section (with or near common stock / additional paid-in capital)—**not** as a liability. Total stockholders’ equity remains **$1,470,000** (unchanged by a pure stock dividend with no cash fractionals).

**c. Scenario A — Subsequent equity / share schedule**

| Item | (1) Before declaration | (2) Dec 31 Y1 (after decl., pre-dist.) | (3) After distribution Jan 12 Y2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Common stock ($3 par) | $180,000 | $180,000 | $201,600 |
| Common stock dividends distributable | 0 | 21,600 | 0 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—CS | 540,000 | 676,800 | 676,800 |
| Retained earnings | 750,000 | 591,600 | 591,600 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$1,470,000** | **$1,470,000** | **$1,470,000** |
| Shares outstanding | 60,000 | 60,000* | 67,200 |
| Par per share | $3.00 | $3.00 | $3.00 |

\*Formal share issuance occurs on distribution; the **right** to 7,200 additional shares is already reflected in equity via Dividends Distributable. Total equity is identical in all three columns.

**Roll-forward checks:**  
RE: 750,000 − 158,400 = **591,600**  
PIC: 540,000 + 136,800 = **676,800**  
CS after issue: 180,000 + 21,600 = **201,600**  
Column (2): 180,000 + 21,600 + 676,800 + 591,600 = **1,470,000**  
Column (3): 201,600 + 676,800 + 591,600 = **1,470,000**

**d. January 12, Year 2 — Distribution (settlement)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 21,600 | |
| Common Stock | | 21,600 |
| *Issue 7,200 shares for stock dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 21,600. **Balanced.**

**e. Scenario B — Large 50% stock dividend (par only)**

New shares = 60,000 × 50% = **30,000**  
Amount capitalized = 30,000 × $3 = **$90,000**  
(Fair value $22 is **not** used.)

**December 15 — Declaration**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (30,000 × $3) | 90,000 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | | 90,000 |
| *Stock split effected in the form of a dividend at par* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 90,000. **Balanced.**

At **December 31, Year 1**, CSDD **$90,000** remains in **equity**; again, **no separate year-end AJJE** beyond the declaration entry.

**January 12 — Distribution**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 90,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 90,000 |
| *Issue 30,000 shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 90,000. **Balanced.**

After full cycle:  
| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock ($3 par; 90,000 shares) | $270,000 |
| PIC—CS (unchanged) | 540,000 |
| Retained earnings (750,000 − 90,000) | 660,000 |
| **Total equity** | **$1,470,000** |

**f. Scenario C — True 2-for-1 stock split**

**Journal entry:** **None** (no change in total dollar amounts of Common stock, PIC, or Retained earnings).

| Item | Before split | After 2-for-1 split |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shares outstanding | 60,000 | **120,000** |
| Par value per share | $3.00 | **$1.50** |
| Common stock (total $) | $180,000 | **$180,000** |
| PIC—CS | 540,000 | 540,000 |
| Retained earnings | 750,000 | 750,000 |
| **Total equity** | **$1,470,000** | **$1,470,000** |

Disclosure: common stock, **$1.50 par**; 120,000 shares issued and outstanding (authorized shares typically doubled for presentation consistency). No period-end adjusting entry—only memorandum/disclosure of share and par changes.

**Key insight:** A small stock dividend declared before year-end is **fully recognized on the declaration date at fair value**; year-end requires **presentation** of Common Stock Dividends Distributable as **equity**, not a new AJJE. Large “split-form” dividends capitalize **par only**; a **true split** needs **no entry**.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Small FV, large par, multi-for-1 split twin
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — small stock dividend at FV with year-end open distributable; large stock dividend at par; true multi-for-1 stock split  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakeshore Analytics Inc.** reports the following equity on **November 20, Year 3**:

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $4 par; 150,000 shares issued and outstanding | $600,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 900,000 |
| Retained earnings | 1,200,000 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$2,700,000** |

Market price of common stock on November 20 is **$30 per share**. Treat each case **independently** from the November 20 balances. Lakeshore’s fiscal year ends **December 31**.

**Case 1:** Declares a **5%** stock dividend on **November 20, Year 3** (all whole shares). Distribution **January 8, Year 4**.  
**Case 2:** Declares a stock split effected in the form of a **100%** stock dividend on **November 20, Year 3**. Distribution **January 8, Year 4**.  
**Case 3:** Effects a **4-for-1** true stock split on **November 20, Year 3** (par reduced proportionally).

**Required:**  
a. **Case 1:** Compute new shares, FV capitalized, par of new shares, and PIC. Prepare **declaration** JE.  
b. **Case 1:** State the December 31, Year 3 **period-end treatment** (AJJE? classification of CSDD?). Prepare a **three-column equity schedule** (before / Dec 31 after declaration / after Jan 8 distribution). Prepare the **January 8 distribution** JE.  
c. **Case 2:** Prepare declaration and distribution JEs at **par**. State shares outstanding and equity components after distribution.  
d. **Case 3:** No JE. Report shares outstanding, par per share, and total common stock $ after the split.  
e. Compute the **difference in ending Retained earnings** between Case 1 and Case 2 after each is fully distributed (both start from RE $1,200,000).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Case 1 — Small 5% stock dividend at FV**

New shares = 150,000 × 5% = **7,500**  
FV = 7,500 × $30 = **$225,000**  
Par = 7,500 × $4 = **$30,000**  
PIC = 225,000 − 30,000 = **$195,000**

**November 20 — Declaration**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (7,500 × $30) | 225,000 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable (7,500 × $4) | | 30,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 195,000 |
| *Small stock dividend at fair value* | | |

**Check:** 225,000 = 30,000 + 195,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Case 1 — Period-end, schedule, distribution**

**December 31, Year 3:** **No additional adjusting entry.** CSDD **$30,000** is **stockholders’ equity** (not a liability). Total SE still **$2,700,000**.

| Item | Before declaration | Dec 31 Y3 (post-decl.) | After Jan 8 Y4 distribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Common stock ($4 par) | $600,000 | $600,000 | $630,000 |
| Common stock dividends distributable | 0 | 30,000 | 0 |
| PIC—CS | 900,000 | 1,095,000 | 1,095,000 |
| Retained earnings | 1,200,000 | 975,000 | 975,000 |
| **Total SE** | **$2,700,000** | **$2,700,000** | **$2,700,000** |
| Shares outstanding | 150,000 | 150,000* | 157,500 |

Checks: RE 1,200,000 − 225,000 = **975,000**; PIC 900,000 + 195,000 = **1,095,000**; CS 600,000 + 30,000 = **630,000**; totals 600+30+1,095+975 = 2,700; 630+1,095+975 = 2,700.

**January 8, Year 4 — Distribution**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 30,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 30,000 |
| *Issue 7,500 shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 30,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Case 2 — 100% stock dividend (large; par only)**

New shares = 150,000 × 100% = **150,000**  
Capitalize = 150,000 × $4 = **$600,000**

**November 20 — Declaration**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings | 600,000 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | | 600,000 |
| *Stock split effected in the form of a 100% stock dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 600,000. **Balanced.**

At Dec 31 Y3: CSDD **$600,000** in equity; **no extra AJJE**.

**January 8 — Distribution**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 600,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 600,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 600,000. **Balanced.**

Shares outstanding after distribution = 150,000 + 150,000 = **300,000**  
Equity: CS **$1,200,000**; PIC **$900,000**; RE **$600,000**; total **$2,700,000**.

**d. Case 3 — 4-for-1 true stock split**

**No journal entry.**

| Item | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shares outstanding | 150,000 | **600,000** |
| Par per share | $4.00 | **$1.00** |
| Common stock total $ | $600,000 | **$600,000** |
| Total equity | $2,700,000 | **$2,700,000** |

**e. RE difference after full distribution**  
Case 1 ending RE = **$975,000**  
Case 2 ending RE = **$600,000**  
Difference = 975,000 − 600,000 = **$375,000** higher RE under Case 1 than Case 2 in these independent scenarios.  
(Percentages and bases differ by design; the comparison reinforces that **measurement basis and size**—FV small vs par large—drive how much RE is capitalized, not merely “stock dividend” as a single rule.)

**Key insight:** Same framework, new numbers: year-end between declaration and distribution is a **presentation** problem (CSDD in equity), not a new measurement AJJE; small still hits RE at **market**, large “split-form” at **par**, true splits **memo only**.

### Q3 — CORE — Period-end focus: fractional cash + large dividend open at year-end + reverse split
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting/presentation for stock dividends open at year-end; fractional-share cash liability; large dividend at par declared late December; reverse split (no JE); settlement after year-end  
**Scenario:**  
**BrightPath Medical Devices Inc.** is a calendar-year company. On **December 20, Year 1**, before any dividend action, equity is:

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $2.50 par; 40,000 shares issued and outstanding | $100,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 280,000 |
| Retained earnings | 520,000 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$900,000** |

**Part I — Small stock dividend with fractional shares (year-end open)**  
On **December 20, Year 1**, BrightPath declares a **10%** common stock dividend when the market price is **$16 per share**. Distribution is scheduled for **February 5, Year 2**.

Of the theoretical **4,000** stock-dividend shares (40,000 × 10%), management determines that **3,750** will be issued as **whole shares** and **250** share-equivalents are **fractional**. BrightPath’s policy is to pay **cash** equal to fair value for fractional entitlements.

On **February 5, Year 2**, BrightPath issues the 3,750 whole shares and pays cash for the fractional portion.

**Part II — Independent large dividend at year-end**  
Ignore Part I. Starting again from the December 20 equity above, assume instead that on **December 28, Year 1**, BrightPath declares a **30%** stock split effected in the form of a stock dividend (par remains $2.50). Distribution is **January 25, Year 2**. Market price is still $16 (ignore for measurement).

**Part III — Independent reverse split**  
Ignore Parts I–II. Assume BrightPath has **40,000** shares of **$2.50 par** common outstanding and effects a **1-for-2 reverse stock split** on **March 1, Year 2**.

**Required:**  
a. **Part I:** Prepare the **December 20 declaration** journal entry (small dividend + fractional cash).  
b. **Part I — period-end:** Identify any **December 31, Year 1 adjusting JE**. Classify **Common Stock Dividends Distributable** and **Dividends Payable** (fractional). Prepare the **December 31 equity (and related liability) snapshot**. Confirm the effect on total equity vs pre-declaration.  
c. **Part I:** Prepare the **February 5, Year 2 settlement** entries (issue stock + pay fractional cash).  
d. **Part II:** Prepare **December 28 declaration** and **January 25 distribution** JEs. State Dec 31 presentation of CSDD.  
e. **Part III:** Journal entry for the reverse split? Shares outstanding and par per share after the reverse split?

**Answer key:**  

**a. December 20, Year 1 — Declaration (small + fractional cash)**

Theoretical shares = 40,000 × 10% = **4,000**  
Whole shares to issue = **3,750**  
Fractional share-equivalents = **250**  
Total FV assigned = 4,000 × $16 = **$64,000**  
Cash for fractionals = 250 × $16 = **$4,000**  
CS Dividends Distributable = 3,750 × $2.50 = **$9,375**  
PIC on whole shares = 3,750 × ($16 − $2.50) = 3,750 × $13.50 = **$50,625**

**Component check:** 4,000 + 9,375 + 50,625 = **64,000** ✓

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (4,000 × $16) | 64,000 | |
| Dividends Payable (250 × $16) | | 4,000 |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable (3,750 × $2.50) | | 9,375 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 50,625 |
| *Small stock dividend; cash for fractional shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr 64,000 = Cr 4,000 + 9,375 + 50,625. **Balanced.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Period-end**

**No additional adjusting journal entry** is required solely because the reporting date fell between declaration and distribution. Declaration already recorded capitalization and the fractional cash liability.

**Classification / presentation at December 31, Year 1:**  
- **Common Stock Dividends Distributable $9,375** → **stockholders’ equity** (paid-in capital category; **not** a liability).  
- **Dividends Payable $4,000** → **current liability** (cash obligation for fractionals).

**December 31 snapshot (post-declaration):**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock ($2.50 par; 40,000 shares) | $100,000 |
| Common stock dividends distributable | 9,375 |
| PIC—CS (280,000 + 50,625) | 330,625 |
| Retained earnings (520,000 − 64,000) | 456,000 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$896,000** |
| Dividends payable (current liability) | 4,000 |

**Equity effect:** Pre-declaration SE = $900,000. Post-declaration SE = **$896,000** — a decrease of **$4,000**, equal to the fractional cash liability. RE fell by $64,000 while equity credits for CSDD + PIC rose by only $9,375 + $50,625 = $60,000; the remaining $4,000 left equity and sits in liabilities. Assets are unchanged until cash is paid; the balance sheet balances via Dividends Payable.

**c. February 5, Year 2 — Settlement / distribution**

**Issue whole shares:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 9,375 | |
| Common Stock | | 9,375 |
| *Distribute 3,750 shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 9,375. **Balanced.**

**Pay fractional cash:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Dividends Payable | 4,000 | |
| Cash | | 4,000 |
| *Settle fractional-share cash* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 4,000. **Balanced.**

After settlement: CS = 100,000 + 9,375 = **$109,375** (43,750 shares × $2.50); PIC **$330,625**; RE **$456,000**; total SE **$896,000**; cash and dividends payable both down $4,000.

**d. Part II — Large 30% stock dividend**

New shares = 40,000 × 30% = **12,000**  
Capitalize at par = 12,000 × $2.50 = **$30,000**  
(Market $16 is **not** used.)

**December 28 — Declaration**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (12,000 × $2.50) | 30,000 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | | 30,000 |
| *Stock split effected in the form of a 30% stock dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 30,000. **Balanced.**

**December 31 presentation:** CSDD **$30,000** in **equity**; **no separate AJJE**. Total SE still **$900,000** (RE 490,000; CS 100,000; CSDD 30,000; PIC 280,000).

**January 25 — Distribution**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 30,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 30,000 |
| *Issue 12,000 shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 30,000. **Balanced.**

After distribution: CS **$130,000** (52,000 × $2.50); PIC **$280,000**; RE **$490,000**; total SE **$900,000**.

**e. Part III — 1-for-2 reverse stock split**

**Journal entry:** **None.**

| Item | Before | After 1-for-2 reverse |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shares outstanding | 40,000 | **20,000** |
| Par per share | $2.50 | **$5.00** |
| Common stock total $ | $100,000 | **$100,000** |
| Total equity | unchanged | unchanged |

**Key insight (period-end emphasis):** Closing the books between declaration and distribution does **not** create a new stock-dividend AJJE—declaration already did the work. Exam traps: (1) treating CSDD as a **liability**, (2) capitalizing a large dividend at **FV**, (3) recording a pure reverse split as a RE transfer, (4) forgetting that **fractional cash** reduces total equity (via liability) while pure stock dividends do not.

### Q4 — MC (classification / measurement)
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** Classification of small vs large stock dividend measurement basis; CSDD equity vs liability; pure stock split no-entry rule  

**Question 1:**  
On December 18, Year 1, a company declares a 10% common stock dividend on 100,000 shares of $1 par common stock when the market price is $8. Distribution is January 15, Year 2. What amount of **Retained Earnings** is debited on the **declaration date**, and how is **Common Stock Dividends Distributable** presented at **December 31, Year 1**?

- A) Debit RE $10,000; present CSDD as a current liability  
- B) Debit RE $80,000; present CSDD as a current liability  
- C) Debit RE $80,000; present CSDD in stockholders’ equity  
- D) Debit RE $10,000; present CSDD in stockholders’ equity  

**Answer:** **C.** A 10% dividend is a **small** stock dividend → capitalize **fair value** 100,000 × 10% × $8 = **$80,000**. Common Stock Dividends Distributable is **equity**, not a liability. Year-end does not reclassify CSDD to liabilities merely because shares are not yet issued.

**Question 2:**  
Which statement is **correct** regarding stock dividends and stock splits?

- A) A 2-for-1 true stock split requires a debit to Retained Earnings for the par of the additional shares.  
- B) A 50% stock split effected in the form of a dividend is measured at the fair value of the additional shares.  
- C) A pure reverse stock split requires no journal entry; shares decrease and par per share increases proportionally.  
- D) After a small stock dividend is declared but before distribution, total stockholders’ equity decreases by the fair value of the shares to be issued (assuming no fractional cash).  

**Answer:** **C.** Pure stock splits and reverse splits need **no JE**—only share counts and par per share change. A is wrong (no RE debit for a true split). B is wrong (large / split-form dividends use **par**, not FV). D is wrong: pure stock dividends **rearrange** equity accounts; **total SE is unchanged** when there is no cash fractional payout.
