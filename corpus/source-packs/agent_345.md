# Agent 345 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-7

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Account for impairment of property, plant, and equipment  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Indicators only:** PPE held for use is reviewed for impairment when events/circumstances suggest carrying amount may not be recoverable (not a routine annual revaluation)
- **Step 1 — Recoverability test:** compare **undiscounted** expected future net cash inflows (recoverable cost) to carrying amount; proceed to measurement only if recoverable cost **<** carrying amount
- **Step 2 — Impairment measurement:** loss = carrying amount − **fair value** (not recoverable cost)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** when the indicator is assessed at the reporting date, record **Loss on Impairment** (Dr) and **Accumulated Depreciation** (Cr) as a year-end adjusting entry (Hanlon / Demo 12-7 convention); gross cost remains; fair value becomes **new cost basis**
- **Initial recognition JE:** purchase of PPE at cost; later, the impairment write-down itself is the impairment initial recognition
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** depreciate new carrying amount over **revised remaining useful life** (revised residual as given); **no restoration** of prior impairment for assets held for use
- **Disposal after impairment:** remove gross cost and accumulated depreciation; recognize gain/loss vs proceeds
- **Presentation / disclosure:** impairment loss in **income from continuing operations** (other expenses); notes describe the asset, circumstances, amount, and fair-value method

### Q1 — CORE — Full cycle with period-end impairment adjusting JE (emphasis)
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Purchase (initial recognition); pre-impairment depreciation; **period-end impairment adjusting JE** (recoverability + measurement); subsequent depreciation schedule; disposal after impairment  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Thermoforming LLC** (calendar year-end) uses specialized forming equipment **held for use** in operations.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| **January 1, Year 1** — purchase and install equipment for cash | **$960,000** |
| Original useful life / residual | **10 years**, residual **$60,000** |
| Depreciation method | Straight-line; full year in year of acquisition |
| Through **December 31, Year 5** | Five full years of depreciation already recorded |
| **December 31, Year 5 (period-end)** | Major customer permanently lost; demand for the related product falls sharply — **impairment indicator** assessed at year-end |
| Estimated remaining undiscounted future net cash inflows (use + eventual disposal) | **$280,000** |
| Fair value of equipment (Dec 31, Year 5) | **$200,000** |
| Revised remaining useful life / residual after write-down | **4 years**, residual **$20,000** |

On **December 31, Year 7** (after two full years of post-impairment depreciation), Meridian sells the equipment for **$95,000** cash. Assume no further impairment indicators after Year 5 and no change to residual/life after the write-down. Year 5 regular depreciation is already on the books before the impairment test.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1, Year 1** purchase (initial recognition) and the **December 31, Year 1** depreciation adjusting entry. Compute annual depreciation under the original estimates.  
b. Compute carrying amount at **December 31, Year 5** immediately before the impairment analysis. Perform the **recoverability test** and, if required, measure the **impairment loss**.  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 5 period-end adjusting JE** to record any impairment (textbook accumulated-depreciation credit convention). State post-impairment carrying amount (new cost basis) and the cumulative AD balance.  
d. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (depreciation) schedule** for the four years after impairment (Years 6–9 planned). Record the **December 31, Year 6** depreciation adjusting entry.  
e. Prepare the **December 31, Year 7 disposal JE** (after recording Year 7 depreciation).  
f. State how the Year 5 impairment loss is **classified** on the income statement and whether a later recovery of fair value (while still held for use) may reverse the impairment.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition and Year 1 depreciation**

Annual depreciation (original):  
\[
\frac{\$960{,}000 - \$60{,}000}{10} = \$90{,}000
\]

*January 1, Year 1 — Purchase equipment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 960,000 | |
| Cash | | 960,000 |
| *Acquire forming equipment at cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 960,000 = Cr 960,000. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 1 — Depreciation adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 90,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 90,000 |
| *Year 1 SL depreciation on original basis* | | |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Carrying amount, recoverability, measurement (Dec 31, Year 5)**

Accumulated depreciation after 5 years: \(5 \times \$90{,}000 = \mathbf{\$450{,}000}\)  
Carrying amount: \(\$960{,}000 - \$450{,}000 = \mathbf{\$510{,}000}\)

**Recoverability test (Step 1):**  
Recoverable cost **$280,000** < carrying amount **$510,000** → asset is **not recoverable** → proceed to Step 2.

**Impairment measurement (Step 2):**  
\[
\text{Impairment loss} = \$510{,}000 - \$200{,}000 = \mathbf{\$310{,}000}
\]

**c. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 5 (emphasis)**

*To record impairment of equipment held for use at year-end*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 310,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 310,000 |
| *Period-end write-down of carrying amount to fair value; increase AD* | | |

**Check:** Dr 310,000 = Cr 310,000. **Balanced.**

Post-impairment balances:  
- Equipment (gross cost) remains **$960,000**  
- Accumulated depreciation: \(450{,}000 + 310{,}000 = \mathbf{\$760{,}000}\)  
- **Carrying amount / new cost basis** = \(960{,}000 - 760{,}000 = \mathbf{\$200{,}000}\) (= fair value)

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule and Year 6 adjusting entry**

New depreciable base = \(\$200{,}000 - \$20{,}000 = \$180{,}000\); remaining life **4 years**.  
Annual depreciation = \(180{,}000 / 4 = \mathbf{\$45{,}000}\)

| Year | Beginning CV | Depreciation | Ending CV | Ending AD (from original cost) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 200,000 | 45,000 | 155,000 | 805,000 |
| 7 | 155,000 | 45,000 | 110,000 | 850,000 |
| 8 | 110,000 | 45,000 | 65,000 | 895,000 |
| 9 | 65,000 | 45,000 | 20,000 | 940,000 |

*Schedule roll-forward check:* ending residual CV Year 9 = **$20,000**; total post-impairment depreciation \(4 \times 45{,}000 = 180{,}000\); ending AD \(760{,}000 + 180{,}000 = 940{,}000\); cost − AD = \(960{,}000 - 940{,}000 = 20{,}000\).

*December 31, Year 6 — Depreciation (post-impairment basis)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 45,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 45,000 |
| *Period-end dep on new cost basis* | | |

**Check:** Dr 45,000 = Cr 45,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Disposal — December 31, Year 7**

After Year 7 depreciation: CV = **$110,000**; AD = **$850,000**.  
Cash proceeds **$95,000** → loss on disposal = \(110{,}000 - 95{,}000 = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\)

*December 31, Year 7 — Dispose of equipment (after Y7 dep)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 95,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 850,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 15,000 | |
| Equipment | | 960,000 |
| *Remove cost and AD; recognize disposal loss* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(95{,}000 + 850{,}000 + 15{,}000 = 960{,}000\) = Cr 960,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Classification and restoration**

- Year 5 impairment loss is reported in **income from continuing operations** (other expenses).  
- For PPE **held for use**, restoration (write-up) of a previously recognized impairment loss is **prohibited**. Fair value becomes the new cost basis for subsequent depreciation only.

**Key insight:** At period-end, first complete ordinary depreciation for the year, then test for impairment if an indicator is present. The recoverability gate uses **undiscounted** cash flows; only if that test fails is the loss measured with **fair value**. The impairment entry is a **period-end adjusting JE** that credits **Accumulated Depreciation**, leaving gross cost unchanged.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Period-end impairment, post-impairment schedule, disposal
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — purchase; recoverability; **period-end impairment adjusting JE**; subsequent depreciation schedule; disposal with gain  
**Scenario:**  
**Atlas Conveyor Systems Inc.** (calendar year-end) owns a custom conveyor system **held for use**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| **January 1, Year 1** — purchase and install for cash | **$1,250,000** |
| Original useful life / residual | **12 years**, residual **$50,000** |
| Depreciation method | Straight-line; full year in year of acquisition |
| Through **December 31, Year 7** | Seven full years of depreciation already recorded |
| **December 31, Year 7 (period-end)** | Regulatory change permanently reduces demand — **impairment indicator** at year-end |
| Estimated remaining undiscounted future net cash inflows | **$320,000** |
| Fair value (Dec 31, Year 7) | **$240,000** |
| Revised remaining useful life / residual after write-down | **5 years**, residual **$0** |

On **December 31, Year 10** (after three full years of post-impairment depreciation), Atlas sells the system for **$100,000** cash. No further impairment indicators after Year 7.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1, Year 1** purchase and compute original annual depreciation. State AD and CA at Dec 31, Year 7 **before** impairment.  
b. Perform recoverability and measure any impairment. Prepare the **December 31, Year 7 period-end impairment adjusting JE**.  
c. Prepare the **subsequent depreciation schedule** for Years 8–12. Record the **December 31, Year 8** depreciation adjusting entry.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 10 disposal JE** (after Year 10 depreciation).  
e. Confirm that total depreciation + impairment + residual disposition reconciling to cost equals original cost (proof schedule).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Purchase, original dep, pre-impairment CA**

Annual depreciation (original):  
\[
\frac{\$1{,}250{,}000 - \$50{,}000}{12} = \$100{,}000
\]

*January 1, Year 1 — Purchase*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 1,250,000 | |
| Cash | | 1,250,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,250,000. **Balanced.**

AD after 7 years: \(7 \times \$100{,}000 = \mathbf{\$700{,}000}\)  
CA before impairment: \(\$1{,}250{,}000 - \$700{,}000 = \mathbf{\$550{,}000}\)

**b. Recoverability, measurement, period-end JE (emphasis)**

**Recoverability:** recoverable cost **$320,000** < CA **$550,000** → not recoverable → Step 2.  
**Impairment loss:** \(550{,}000 - 240{,}000 = \mathbf{\$310{,}000}\)

*December 31, Year 7 — Period-end impairment adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 310,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 310,000 |

**Check:** Dr 310,000 = Cr 310,000. **Balanced.**

Post-impairment: gross cost **$1,250,000**; AD \(700{,}000 + 310{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}010{,}000}\); CA / new cost basis **$240,000**.

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule and Year 8 dep**

New depreciable base = **$240,000**; residual **$0**; life **5 years**.  
Annual depreciation = \(240{,}000 / 5 = \mathbf{\$48{,}000}\)

| Year | Beginning CV | Depreciation | Ending CV | Ending AD (from original cost) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 240,000 | 48,000 | 192,000 | 1,058,000 |
| 9 | 192,000 | 48,000 | 144,000 | 1,106,000 |
| 10 | 144,000 | 48,000 | 96,000 | 1,154,000 |
| 11 | 96,000 | 48,000 | 48,000 | 1,202,000 |
| 12 | 48,000 | 48,000 | 0 | 1,250,000 |

*December 31, Year 8 — Depreciation adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 48,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 48,000 |

**Check:** Dr 48,000 = Cr 48,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Disposal — December 31, Year 10**

After Year 10 depreciation: CV = **$96,000**; AD = **$1,154,000**.  
Cash **$100,000** → gain on disposal = \(100{,}000 - 96{,}000 = \mathbf{\$4{,}000}\)

*December 31, Year 10 — Dispose of equipment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 100,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 1,154,000 | |
| Equipment | | 1,250,000 |
| Gain on Disposal of Equipment | | 4,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(100{,}000 + 1{,}154{,}000 = 1{,}254{,}000\) = Cr \(1{,}250{,}000 + 4{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**e. Cost reconciliation proof**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Pre-impairment depreciation (7 × $100,000) | 700,000 |
| Impairment loss (period-end Y7) | 310,000 |
| Post-impairment depreciation (3 × $48,000 through Y10) | 144,000 |
| Carrying amount at disposal (before sale) | 96,000 |
| **Sum** | **1,250,000** |

Sum equals original cost **$1,250,000**. (If held to end of Year 12 with residual $0: pre-impairment dep $700,000 + impairment $310,000 + post-impairment dep $240,000 = $1,250,000.)

**Key insight:** Changing only the numbers, the same two-step model applies: **undiscounted** cash flows gate the loss; **fair value** measures it. Period-end impairment is an **adjusting JE** that does not reverse later for assets held for use.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Multi-asset period-end screen (pass vs fail) and combined adjusting JE
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Period-end multi-asset recoverability (some pass); combined impairment adjusting JE only for failed assets; subsequent dep on written-down asset; disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Prairie Glassworks Co.** (calendar year-end) reviews three machines **held for use** at **December 31, Year 4**. Management concludes that **indicators of impairment are present** for all three (underutilization and adverse industry trends). Data at year-end (after Year 4 depreciation has already been recorded):

| Plant asset | Cost | Accumulated depreciation | Carrying amount | Recoverable cost (undiscounted) | Fair value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cutter A | $400,000 | $160,000 | $240,000 | $270,000 | $210,000 |
| Polisher B | 550,000 | 275,000 | 275,000 | 200,000 | 160,000 |
| Oven C | 300,000 | 90,000 | 210,000 | 180,000 | 175,000 |

After any required write-down, **Polisher B** will be used for **5 more years** with a revised residual of **$10,000**. **Oven C** will be used for **3 more years** with residual **$0**. No other estimate changes.

**Required:**  
a. For each asset, perform the recoverability test and, if required, measure the impairment loss. Which asset(s) require a loss, and for what amount(s)?  
b. Prepare the **combined December 31, Year 4 period-end adjusting JE** for all impairment losses (one compound entry is acceptable).  
c. For **Polisher B** only, prepare the **Year 5 subsequent depreciation** amount and the **December 31, Year 5** depreciation adjusting entry.  
d. In one sentence, explain why **Cutter A** has **no impairment** even though fair value ($210,000) is below carrying amount ($240,000).  
e. List **two note disclosures** required when an impairment loss is recognized.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Recoverability and measurement by asset**

| Asset | Recoverable cost vs CA | Impaired? | Loss = CA − FV |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Cutter A | $270,000 **>** $240,000 | **No** (recoverable) | **$0** |
| Polisher B | $200,000 **<** $275,000 | **Yes** | \(275{,}000 - 160{,}000 = \mathbf{\$115{,}000}\) |
| Oven C | $180,000 **<** $210,000 | **Yes** | \(210{,}000 - 175{,}000 = \mathbf{\$35{,}000}\) |

Total impairment loss = \(115{,}000 + 35{,}000 = \mathbf{\$150{,}000}\).

**b. Combined period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 4**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 150,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Polisher B | | 115,000 |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Oven C | | 35,000 |
| *Period-end write-down of impaired assets held for use to fair value* | | |

**Check:** Dr 150,000 = Cr \(115{,}000 + 35{,}000\). **Balanced.**

Post-impairment carrying amounts:  
- Cutter A: **$240,000** (unchanged)  
- Polisher B: **$160,000** (new cost basis)  
- Oven C: **$175,000** (new cost basis)

**c. Polisher B — subsequent Year 5 depreciation**

New depreciable base = \(160{,}000 - 10{,}000 = 150{,}000\); life **5 years**.  
Annual depreciation = \(150{,}000 / 5 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000}\)

*December 31, Year 5 — Depreciation adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 30,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Polisher B | | 30,000 |

**Check:** Dr 30,000 = Cr 30,000. **Balanced.**  
Ending CV of B after Year 5 = \(160{,}000 - 30{,}000 = \mathbf{\$130{,}000}\).

**d. Why Cutter A is not impaired**

Because recoverable cost (**undiscounted** future net cash inflows) **exceeds** carrying amount, the asset is considered recoverable under ASC 360; no impairment is recognized **even though fair value is less than carrying amount**.

**e. Disclosure (any two)**

Per ASC 360 note requirements when a loss is recognized:  
1. Description of the impaired asset (group) and facts/circumstances leading to impairment  
2. Amount of the loss and the income-statement caption (if not separately presented)  
3. Method(s) used to determine fair value  
4. Segment (if applicable under Topic 280)

**Key insight:** The recoverability test is a **gate**. Assets that pass it take **no loss** at period-end even if FV < CA. Only failed assets get a period-end impairment adjusting JE measured with **fair value**.

### Q4 — MC — Recoverability vs measurement amounts
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Recoverability uses undiscounted cash flows; impairment loss measured with fair value  
**Question:**  
At December 31, equipment held for use has a carrying amount of $400,000. An impairment indicator is present. Estimated undiscounted future net cash inflows are $360,000. Fair value is $275,000. What amount of impairment loss, if any, should be recognized in the period-end adjusting entry?

- A) $0, because fair value is not used until disposal  
- B) $40,000 (carrying amount − recoverable cost)  
- C) $125,000 (carrying amount − fair value)  
- D) $85,000 (recoverable cost − fair value)

**Answer:** **C.**  
Recoverable cost $360,000 < CA $400,000 → recoverability **fails**, so a loss is measured.  
Impairment loss = CA − FV = \(400{,}000 - 275{,}000 = \$125{,}000\).  
Choice B incorrectly measures the loss with recoverable cost. Choice D mixes the two steps. Choice A is wrong because held-for-use assets are written down when both steps require a loss.

### Q5 — MC — New cost basis and no restoration (held for use)
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** New cost basis after period-end impairment; restoration prohibited for PPE held for use  
**Question:**  
On December 31, Year 3, a company records a $90,000 impairment loss on equipment held for use (period-end adjusting entry), writing the asset down to fair value of $210,000 with a 3-year remaining life and zero residual. On December 31, Year 4, fair value of the same equipment (still held for use) has risen to $250,000. Which statement is correct?

- A) Record a $40,000 recovery gain on December 31, Year 4, limited to prior impairment  
- B) Record a $40,000 recovery gain only if the recoverability test would now pass  
- C) Record no recovery; depreciate the $210,000 new cost basis over the remaining life (Year 4 depreciation $70,000 if SL, residual $0, 3 years)  
- D) Reclassify the equipment as held for sale so that recovery is allowed

**Answer:** **C.**  
After a held-for-use impairment, fair value is the **new cost basis** and is depreciated over remaining useful life. **Restoration of a previously recognized impairment loss is prohibited** for assets held for use. Year 4 depreciation = \(210{,}000 / 3 = \$70{,}000\) (assuming SL, residual $0, and 3 years remaining at the write-down date). Recoveries are a held-for-sale (LO 12-8) concept with a cap, not LO 12-7 held-for-use accounting.
