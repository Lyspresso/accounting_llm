# Agent 389 — CORE DEMO — LO 21-4

**Chapter:** 21  
**LO title:** Demonstrate the accounting for an error correction  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Error vs other events:** an **error** is incorrect recording / non-recording (math mistake, misapplication of GAAP, oversight of facts existing when statements were prepared); a change from non-GAAP to GAAP is an **error correction**, not a change in principle
- **Material error discovered after issuance:** record a **prior period adjustment (PPA)** to Retained Earnings and the affected BS account(s); **restate and reissue** prior financial statements as soon as practicable
- **Initial recognition JE (correction):** reclassify / recognize the asset, liability, or equity impact that should have been on the books (e.g., capitalize improvement; reverse improper capitalization; set up prepaid; set up accrual)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** roll forward **incorrect vs correct** cumulative depreciation/amortization (or inventory COGS path) through discovery; after correction, measure going forward on the **corrected** basis
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** (1) PPA / correcting entries often prepared at or near discovery period-end; (2) after books are corrected, record **current-period** period-end adjusting entries (depreciation, insurance amortization, accruals) on the corrected balances
- **Counterbalancing inventory errors:** self-correct within two consecutive periods; **PPA only if discovered before self-correction**; after self-correction, **no JE** but material errors still require **restatement** of presented periods
- **Exceptions with no PPA to RE:** BS-only misclassification → reclass only; IS-only closed misclassification → no correcting JE; counterbalancing after self-correction → no JE
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** settle a liability that should have been accrued (pay salaries payable set up by PPA); remove misclassified assets when corrected or later disposed
- **Disclosure:** nature of error; effect on each FS line item and EPS for periods presented; cumulative effect on beginning RE of earliest period presented

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-error prior period adjustments, measurement schedules, period-end AJE, settlement
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition correcting JEs (PPA); subsequent measurement (incorrect vs correct) schedules; **period-end adjusting JEs** after correction (emphasis); settlement of unrecorded accrual  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverside Components Co.** has a **December 31** year-end. In **early January of Year 6**, after Year 5 statements have been issued, the controller identifies the following **material** errors. Ignore income taxes.

| # | Fact |
|---|---|
| **A** | **January of Year 3:** Building improvements of **$48,000** were debited to **Maintenance Expense**. The improvements should have been capitalized to **Building Improvements** and depreciated **12.5% per year** (8-year life), straight-line, **no residual**. |
| **B** | **January 1, Year 4:** Ordinary repairs of **$18,000** on equipment were **erroneously capitalized** to Equipment. Related equipment is depreciated SL over **6 years**, no residual. |
| **C** | **January 1, Year 4:** A **four-year** insurance premium of **$8,000** was paid and **fully debited to Insurance Expense**. |
| **D** | **December 31, Year 5:** Accrued salaries of **$11,200** were **not recorded**. The salaries will be **paid in cash on January 15, Year 6**. |
| **E** | Patent purchased **January 1, Year 2** for **$180,000** was amortized over the **legal life of 20 years**. The **economic life** is only **6 years**. The error is discovered at the start of Year 6 (four years of incorrect amortization already recorded). |

The company will correct the accounting records as of **January 1, Year 6**, then record proper **Year 6 period-end adjusting entries**.

**Required:**  
a. For errors **A–E**, compute each **prior period adjustment** (show incorrect cumulative expense vs correct cumulative expense for closed years). Record the **January 1, Year 6 correcting / initial recognition JEs** (all must balance).  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Error **A** (Building Improvements): for Years 3–8, show correct annual depreciation and correct accumulated depreciation; note what was actually recorded before correction.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Error **E** (Patent): incorrect vs correct cumulative amortization through Dec 31, Year 5, and correct net book value after the PPA.  
d. **Period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis):** Record the **December 31, Year 6** adjusting entries for (i) depreciation on the corrected Building Improvements and (ii) amortization of the remaining prepaid insurance for Year 6 (assume annual AJE).  
e. **Settlement JE:** Record payment of the Year 5 salaries on **January 15, Year 6**.  
f. Briefly state whether Year 5 comparative statements must be **restated**, and name one required **disclosure**.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Prior period adjustments and correcting JEs (January 1, Year 6)**

**Error A — Improvements expensed (asset understated; prior expense overstated)**  
Incorrect expense recognized in Year 3: **$48,000**  
Correct depreciation for Years 3–5: \( \$48{,}000 \times 12.5\% \times 3 = \$6{,}000 \times 3 = \mathbf{\$18{,}000} \)  
Prior period adjustment (increase RE): \( \$48{,}000 - \$18{,}000 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000} \)

*January 1, Year 6 — Correct Error A*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Building Improvements | 48,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Building Improvements | | 18,000 |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 30,000 |
| *Capitalize improvements; catch up 3 years of SL depreciation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 48,000 = Cr 18,000 + 30,000. **Balanced.**

**Error B — Ordinary repairs capitalized (asset overstated; prior expense understated)**  
Incorrect depreciation Years 4–5: \( (\$18{,}000 / 6) \times 2 = \mathbf{\$6{,}000} \)  
Correct expense (all in Year 4): **$18,000**  
Prior period adjustment (decrease RE): \( \$18{,}000 - \$6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000} \)

*January 1, Year 6 — Correct Error B*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 12,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 6,000 | |
| Equipment | | 18,000 |
| *Remove improperly capitalized repairs and related AD* | | |

**Check:** Dr 12,000 + 6,000 = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**

**Error C — Prepaid insurance fully expensed**  
Incorrect expense Year 4: **$8,000**  
Correct expense Years 4–5: \( (\$8{,}000 / 4) \times 2 = \mathbf{\$4{,}000} \)  
Remaining prepaid at Jan 1, Year 6 (Years 6–7 coverage): \( \$8{,}000 \times 2/4 = \mathbf{\$4{,}000} \)  
Prior period adjustment (increase RE): \( \$8{,}000 - \$4{,}000 = \mathbf{\$4{,}000} \)

*January 1, Year 6 — Correct Error C*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Prepaid Insurance | 4,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 4,000 |
| *Set up remaining prepaid for Years 6–7* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 = Cr 4,000. **Balanced.**

**Error D — Failure to accrue salaries at Dec 31, Year 5 (period-end omission)**  
Correct Year 5 salaries expense / payable: **$11,200**  
Prior period adjustment (decrease RE): **$(11,200)**

*January 1, Year 6 — Correct Error D (initial recognition of omitted accrual)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 11,200 | |
| Salaries Payable | | 11,200 |
| *Record omitted Year 5 period-end accrual* | | |

**Check:** Dr 11,200 = Cr 11,200. **Balanced.**

**Error E — Patent amortized over legal life instead of economic life**  
Years of amortization through Dec 31, Year 5: **4**  
Incorrect cumulative amortization: \( (\$180{,}000 / 20) \times 4 = \$9{,}000 \times 4 = \mathbf{\$36{,}000} \)  
Correct cumulative amortization: \( (\$180{,}000 / 6) \times 4 = \$30{,}000 \times 4 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000} \)  
Prior period adjustment (decrease RE): \( \$120{,}000 - \$36{,}000 = \mathbf{\$84{,}000} \)

*January 1, Year 6 — Correct Error E*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 84,000 | |
| Patent | | 84,000 |
| *Catch up under-amortization (economic life 6 years)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 84,000 = Cr 84,000. **Balanced.**

**Net PPA effect on beginning RE, Year 6 (ignore tax):**  
\( +30{,}000 - 12{,}000 + 4{,}000 - 11{,}200 - 84{,}000 = \mathbf{-\$73{,}200} \)

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — Building Improvements (Error A)**  
Annual correct depreciation: \( \$48{,}000 \times 12.5\% = \mathbf{\$6{,}000} \)

| Year | Correct dep. expense | Correct accum. dep. (EOY) | Recorded before correction |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | 6,000 | 6,000 | Full $48,000 expensed; **no** asset/AD |
| 4 | 6,000 | 12,000 | No depreciation on improvements |
| 5 | 6,000 | 18,000 | No depreciation on improvements |
| **PPA at 1/1/Y6** | — | **18,000** set up | Correcting entry in (a) |
| 6 | 6,000 | 24,000 | Record Year 6 AJE on corrected basis |
| 7 | 6,000 | 30,000 | Continue |
| 8 | 6,000 | 36,000 | Fully depreciated end Year 8 (if placed in service Jan Y3) |

After PPA: carrying amount Jan 1, Year 6 = \( \$48{,}000 - \$18{,}000 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000} \) (5 years remaining × $6,000).

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — Patent (Error E)**

| Basis | Annual amort. | Cum. through Y5 (4 yrs) | NBV end Y5 / after PPA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Incorrect (legal 20 yrs) | 9,000 | 36,000 | \( 180{,}000 - 36{,}000 = 144{,}000 \) (on books pre-PPA) |
| Correct (economic 6 yrs) | 30,000 | 120,000 | \( 180{,}000 - 120{,}000 = \mathbf{60{,}000} \) |

PPA writes patent down by **$84,000** so NBV after correction = **$60,000** (2 years × $30,000 remaining economic life).  
Year 6 correct amortization (subsequent): **$30,000**.

**d. Period-end adjusting JEs — December 31, Year 6 (emphasis)**

*(i) Depreciation on corrected Building Improvements*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Building Improvements | 6,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Building Improvements | | 6,000 |
| *Year 6 SL depreciation on corrected improvements* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,000 = Cr 6,000. **Balanced.**

*(ii) Amortize prepaid insurance for Year 6*  
Annual insurance expense: \( \$8{,}000 / 4 = \mathbf{\$2{,}000} \)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Insurance Expense | 2,000 | |
| Prepaid Insurance | | 2,000 |
| *Year 6 portion of multi-year premium* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,000 = Cr 2,000. **Balanced.**  
Prepaid balance after AJE: \( \$4{,}000 - \$2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$2{,}000} \) (Year 7 coverage remaining).

*(Optional current-period patent amort. if recorded as AJE)*  
Dr Amortization Expense 30,000 / Cr Patent 30,000 — **Balanced.**

**e. Settlement JE — January 15, Year 6**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Salaries Payable | 11,200 | |
| Cash | | 11,200 |
| *Pay Year 5 salaries previously set up by PPA* | | |

**Check:** Dr 11,200 = Cr 11,200. **Balanced.**  
(Do **not** debit Salaries Expense again — that would double-count the Year 5 cost already charged to beginning RE via PPA.)

**f. Restatement and disclosure**  
Yes — material error in Year 5 (and earlier affected years) requires **restatement and reissuance** of previously issued statements as soon as practicable; comparative amounts in later filings must show corrected figures.  
**Disclosure:** that prior FS have been restated; **nature of the error**; effect on each FS line item and per-share amounts for periods presented; **cumulative effect on beginning retained earnings** of the earliest period presented.

**Key insight:** Error corrections use a **prior period adjustment** to RE (not current income) for closed periods, fix BS accounts to what they **should** be, then run **normal period-end AJEs** on the corrected balances. Settlement of an omitted accrual hits the **payable**, not expense, if the PPA already charged RE.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Multi-error PPA, schedules, period-end AJE, settlement
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Same skills as Q1 with all numbers changed — initial recognition PPA JEs; subsequent measurement schedules; period-end AJEs; settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Plastics LLC** (December 31 year-end) discovers the following **material** errors on **January 2, Year 7**. Ignore income taxes.

| # | Fact |

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Counterbalancing inventory error; discovery timing; restatement schedule
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Counterbalancing inventory error — when to record correcting JE / PPA vs no entry; income and RE restatement schedule; BS-only reclass contrast  
**Scenario:**  
**Northwind Merchandising Inc.** uses a perpetual system but adjusts to physical counts at each year-end. Purchases and sales cutoffs are otherwise correct. The **December 31, Year 1 physical count understated ending inventory by $15,000**. Year 2 ending inventory is correct. Pretax amounts as **originally recorded** (with the Year 1 error):

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | $40,000 | $70,000 (understated BI) |
| Net purchases | 200,000 | 220,000 |
| Ending inventory | 70,000 (**understated by 15,000**) | 85,000 (correct) |
| Cost of goods sold (as recorded) | 170,000 (**overstated**) | 205,000 (**understated**) |
| Net sales | 310,000 | 340,000 |
| Gross profit (as recorded) | 140,000 | 135,000 |
| Other expenses | 50,000 | 55,000 |
| Pretax income (as recorded) | 90,000 | 80,000 |

Ignore taxes. The error is material for restatement analysis when applicable.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare a **correction schedule** showing correct COGS, gross profit, and pretax income for Year 1 and Year 2.  
b. Record the correcting JE if the error is discovered **in Year 1** (before books close).  
c. Record the correcting JE if discovered **early in Year 2** (before self-correction completes).  
d. Record any JE if discovered **in Year 3** (after self-correction). Explain.  
e. Assume comparative Year 2 statements present Year 1 and Year 2. After restatement, what is correct pretax income for each year, and what is the effect on **beginning RE of Year 2** in the restated statements?  
f. **Contrast:** In a **different** error, WIP of $9,000 was misclassified as Finished Goods at Dec 31, Year 2 (BS only; no income effect). Discovered in Year 3 while both accounts remain. Record the correcting entry and state whether RE is affected.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Correction schedule (counterbalancing inventory)**

| | Year 1 correct | Year 2 correct |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | 40,000 | **85,000** (= correct Y1 EI 70,000 + 15,000) |
| Net purchases | 200,000 | 220,000 |
| Ending inventory | **85,000** | 85,000 |
| **COGS** | \( 40{,}000 + 200{,}000 - 85{,}000 = \mathbf{155{,}000} \) | \( 85{,}000 + 220{,}000 - 85{,}000 = \mathbf{220{,}000} \) |
| Net sales | 310,000 | 340,000 |
| **Gross profit** | \( 310{,}000 - 155{,}000 = \mathbf{155{,}000} \) | \( 340{,}000 - 220{,}000 = \mathbf{120{,}000} \) |
| Other expenses | 50,000 | 55,000 |
| **Pretax income** | \( 155{,}000 - 50{,}000 = \mathbf{105{,}000} \) | \( 120{,}000 - 55{,}000 = \mathbf{65{,}000} \) |

**As-recorded vs correct pretax:**

| Year | As recorded | Correct | Error effect on pretax |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 90,000 | 105,000 | **Understated $15,000** |
| 2 | 80,000 | 65,000 | **Overstated $15,000** |
| **Two-year total** | 170,000 | 170,000 | **Nets to zero (counterbalancing)** |

**b. Discovered in Year 1 (same period — reverse/fix before close)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 15,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 15,000 |
| *Correct understated ending inventory / overstated COGS* | | |

**Check:** Dr 15,000 = Cr 15,000. **Balanced.**  
No prior-period RE impact (still open period).

**c. Discovered early Year 2 (before self-correction — PPA)**  
Ending inventory Year 1 still wrong on books going into Year 2 until corrected; income of Year 1 closed to RE.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 15,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 15,000 |
| *PPA: restore inventory and correct beginning RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 15,000 = Cr 15,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Discovered in Year 3 (after self-correction)**  
**No journal entry.**  
Year 1 understated EI → overstated COGS → understated NI/RE. Year 2 understated BI → understated COGS → overstated NI that restores RE. By Year 3, BS is correct and cumulative RE is correct.  
If material, **restate** comparative statements for presented prior years even though no live JE is needed.

**e. Restatement of comparative amounts**  
Correct pretax: Year 1 **$105,000**; Year 2 **$65,000**.  
Beginning RE of Year 2 (restated) increases by **$15,000** relative to originally reported (for the Year 1 income understatement), holding dividends constant.  
Ending RE after Year 2 is the **same** as originally reported (error has counterbalanced).

**f. BS-only misclassification (no PPA to RE)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Work-in-Process Inventory | 9,000 | |
| Finished Goods Inventory | | 9,000 |
| *Reclassify inventory categories* | | |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.**  
**RE is not affected** (income statement accounts were not misstated; only classification within inventory). Restatement of BS presentation still required if material and statements reissued/compared.

**Key insight:** Counterbalancing inventory errors **flip income between two years** then die out. **Timing of discovery** drives the JE (current-period fix vs PPA vs none). **Restatement** can still be required for material errors even when **no entry** hits the live books.

### Q4 — MC — Classification: error correction treatment vs other accounting changes
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Identify which situation is an **error correction** requiring prior period adjustment / restatement (vs prospective estimate change or retrospective principle change)

**Question:** Which of the following, discovered in Year 5 after Year 4 statements were issued, is accounted for as an **error correction** with a **prior period adjustment** (assume all amounts material)?

- A) Management revises the estimated useful life of a machine from 10 years to 7 years based on new usage data arising in Year 5.  
- B) The company voluntarily changes from FIFO to weighted-average inventory for financial reporting (both GAAP methods; practicable to restate).  
- C) In Year 3 the company expensed $50,000 of equipment that met the capitalization criteria under GAAP then in effect; the equipment is still in use.  
- D) The company consolidates a variable interest entity for the first time because of a change in the composition of entities under common control that creates a different reporting entity.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Expensing a capitalizable asset is a **misapplication of GAAP / oversight** → **error**. Correction: capitalize remaining book value path via **PPA to RE** and BS accounts; **restate** prior FS.  
A is a **change in estimate** (prospective). B is a **change in accounting principle** (retrospective application, not labeled “restatement” for an error). D is a **change in reporting entity** (retrospective), not an error.

### Q5 — MC — When no PPA journal entry is required
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Exceptions — counterbalancing after self-correction; IS-only closed misclassification

**Question:** A company’s Year 1 ending inventory was overstated by $20,000 due to a math error. Year 2 ending inventory is correct. The error is first discovered in **Year 3**. Ignoring taxes and assuming the error was material to Year 1 alone, which statement is **best**?

- A) Debit Retained Earnings—PPA $20,000 and credit Inventory $20,000 in Year 3.  
- B) Debit Inventory $20,000 and credit Retained Earnings—PPA $20,000 in Year 3.  
- C) No correcting journal entry in Year 3; if comparative statements present Year 1–2, restate those periods’ inventory, COGS, and income.  
- D) Prospectively adjust Year 3 COGS by $20,000 as a change in estimate.

**Answer:** **C.**  
The overstatement of Year 1 EI **counterbalanced** in Year 2 (overstated BI → overstated COGS → understated Year 2 income). By Year 3 the error has self-corrected on the live BS and cumulative RE — **no JE**. Material errors still require **restatement** of presented prior periods. A/B would double-distort RE/inventory after self-correction. D misclassifies an error as an estimate change.
