# Agent 170 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-1

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Classify cash, cash equivalents, restricted cash, and compensating balances  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Cash:** currency, demand deposits, savings, petty cash, cashier’s/certified checks, money orders
- **Cash equivalents:** short-term, highly liquid investments with **original maturity to the holder of three months or less** and insignificant risk of value change; company policy election (ASC 230)
- **Restricted cash** (legal or voluntary): report **separately** from cash; **current** if expected to be used in the normal operating cycle, else **noncurrent**
- **Compensating balances:** **legally restricted** → separate current or noncurrent asset; **not legally restricted** → remain in cash with **note disclosure**
- **Not cash:** travel advances, postdated checks, NSF checks, postage, investments with original maturity > 3 months, bond sinking funds (restricted), bank overdraft with no offsetting positive balance at same bank (current liability)
- **Initial recognition JEs:** borrow + reclass legally restricted compensating balance; purchase cash equivalents; transfer to restricted funds
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-date rollforward of unrestricted cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash after each event
- **Period-end adjusting/reclassifying JEs:** NSF, noncash items, misclassified CE/STI, overdraft gross-up
- **Maturity / settlement:** cash-equivalent maturity to cash; release of compensating balance when related debt is paid

**(i) Non-legally-restricted compensating balance**  
If the compensating balance were **not** legally restricted, Ridgeway would report the full Meridian balance in **Cash** and **disclose** the arrangement and amount in the notes. Legally restricted balances are **separated** from cash on the face of the balance sheet.

**Key insight:** The **subsequent measurement schedule** tracks how each event moves amounts among unrestricted cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash. **Original** maturity to the holder (not remaining maturity at year-end) decides cash-equivalent status. Legally restricted ST compensating balances and near-term funds are **current** restricted cash; long-horizon sinking funds are **noncurrent**. Maturity of a cash equivalent returns cash plus any discount interest.

### Q4 — MC — Cash and cash equivalents total
**LO:** LO 8-1  
**Concept:** Compute cash and cash equivalents from mixed cash-related balances  
**Question:**  
Balances for **Brightwater Retail Group** on December 31, Year 1:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash deposit in escrow fund (legal restriction; equipment purchase in 20 months) | $52,000 |
| Cash deposit in checking account | $96,000 |
| Money market fund (highly liquid; original maturity 2 months; low risk) | $28,000 |
| T-bill matures Mar 1, Year 2; purchased **Oct 1, Year 1** (original maturity to Brightwater = 5 months) | $35,000 |
| T-bill matures Mar 1, Year 2; purchased **Dec 10, Year 1** (original maturity to Brightwater = 81 days) | $18,000 |
| Legally restricted compensating balance against short-term loan (included in the $96,000 checking balance) | $12,000 |

What amount should Brightwater report as **cash and cash equivalents** on December 31, Year 1?

- A) $112,000  
- B) $130,000  
- C) $142,000  
- D) $177,000  

**Answer:** **B. $130,000.**  
Cash = checking unrestricted \(96{,}000 - 12{,}000 = 84{,}000\).  
Cash equivalents = money market \(28{,}000\) + Dec 10 T-bill \(18{,}000\) = \(46{,}000\).  
Total cash and cash equivalents = \(84{,}000 + 46{,}000 = \mathbf{\$130{,}000}\).  

Exclude: escrow (restricted noncurrent), Oct 1 T-bill (original maturity **5 months > 3 months** → short-term investment), and the $12,000 compensating balance (restricted cash—current, separate from cash).

### Q5 — MC — Legally restricted compensating balance against long-term debt
**LO:** LO 8-1  
**Concept:** Classification of a legally restricted compensating balance held against long-term debt  
**Question:**  
On December 31, **Cedar Hill Foods** maintains a **$55,000** deposit at Lender First Bank that is **legally restricted** as a compensating balance under a long-term loan agreement (principal due in five years). How should Cedar Hill report the $55,000?

- A) Cash and cash equivalents (current asset)  
- B) Other current asset—restricted cash  
- C) Noncurrent asset—restricted cash (or investment)  
- D) Reduction of the long-term note payable (contra-liability)  

**Answer:** **C.** Legally restricted compensating balances are reported **separately from cash**. Because the related debt is **long-term** and the funds are not expected to be available for current operations, the restricted deposit is a **noncurrent** asset. It is not netted against the note payable.
