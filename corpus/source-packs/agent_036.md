**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Financing section: gross cash inflows (debt proceeds, equity issuances) and outflows (debt principal, dividends, treasury stock, debt issue costs)
- Balance-sheet T-account analysis to reconstruct financing cash flows (bonds, mortgage, common stock, dividends payable)
- Initial recognition JEs for equity issuance and new borrowing (cash financing inflows)
- Settlement JE for early bond retirement (cash paid = financing outflow; loss/gain = operating under indirect method)
- Finance lease: principal portion = financing outflow; interest portion = operating
- Dividends declared vs cash dividends paid (dividends payable roll-forward)
- Installment note: amortization schedule; principal vs interest split on SCF
- Classification/presentation: financing vs operating vs noncash investing/financing disclosure

### Q1 — CORE — Crestwood Fabricators: financing section from comparative data (initial recognition + settlement emphasis)
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Prepare cash flows from financing activities — stock issuance JE, bond retirement settlement, mortgage/lease principal, dividends paid  
**Scenario:**  
**Crestwood Fabricators Inc.** prepares a statement of cash flows for the year ended **December 31, 20X2**. Selected comparative balances and facts follow.

| Account | Dec 31, 20X1 | Dec 31, 20X2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | $320,000 | $185,000 |
| Mortgage payable | 240,000 | 210,000 |
| Bonds payable | 500,000 | 350,000 |
| Premium on bonds payable | 15,000 | 6,000 |
| Finance lease liability | 90,000 | 72,000 |
| Common stock, $5 par | 250,000 | 300,000 |
| Additional paid-in capital | 80,000 | 140,000 |
| Dividends payable | 12,000 | 18,000 |
| Retained earnings | 160,000 | 255,000 |

**Additional information for 20X2:**
1. Net income was **$145,000**.
2. On **January 1, 20X2**, Crestwood retired bonds with face value **$150,000** by paying **$159,000** cash. Unamortized premium related to the retired bonds was **$4,500**. No other bonds were issued or retired during the year.
3. No new mortgage borrowings occurred; the mortgage decrease is entirely principal repayment.
4. Crestwood issued common stock **for cash** only (no stock-for-asset exchanges).
5. No new finance leases were entered. Cash payments on the existing finance lease totaled **$24,000**, of which **$6,000** was interest and the remainder reduced principal.
6. All changes in dividends payable relate to cash dividends declared and paid (no stock dividends).

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **initial recognition** journal entry for the common stock issuance (cash financing inflow).  
(b) Record the **settlement** journal entry for the January 1 bond retirement. Identify the amount that appears in the financing section and the amount that affects the operating section under the **indirect** method.  
(c) Prepare T-account (or schedule) analyses for: mortgage principal paid; finance lease principal paid; dividends declared and **cash dividends paid**.  
(d) Prepare the **Cash flows from financing activities** section of the 20X2 statement of cash flows.  
(e) Briefly classify: (i) bond retirement loss, (ii) finance lease interest of $6,000, (iii) commencement of a new finance lease (assume none this year — state the SCF treatment if one had been signed with no cash at commencement).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Common stock issuance — initial recognition (cash)**

Par increase = $300,000 − $250,000 = **$50,000** → 10,000 shares × $5.  
APIC increase = $140,000 − $80,000 = **$60,000**.  
Cash proceeds = $50,000 + $60,000 = **$110,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 110,000 | |
| Common stock, $5 par | | 50,000 |
| Additional paid-in capital | | 60,000 |

Dr = Cr = $110,000 ✓  
→ **Financing inflow: Proceeds from issuance of common stock $110,000**.

**(b) Bond retirement — settlement JE (January 1, 20X2)**

Carrying amount of retired bonds = $150,000 + $4,500 = **$154,500**.  
Loss on retirement = $159,000 − $154,500 = **$4,500**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds payable | 150,000 | |
| Premium on bonds payable | 4,500 | |
| Loss on bond retirement | 4,500 | |
| Cash | | 159,000 |

Dr = Cr = $159,000 ✓  

- **Financing outflow:** Payment to retire bonds **$(159,000)** (cash paid).  
- **Operating (indirect method):** Add back loss on bond retirement **$4,500** (noncash loss embedded in net income).  
Check bonds payable: $500,000 − $150,000 = **$350,000** ending.  
Premium roll-forward: $15,000 − $4,500 (retired) − amortization of remaining premium = $6,000 ending → remaining premium amortized in 20X2 = **$4,500** (operating reduction of interest expense under effective-interest; not a financing line).

**(c) Supporting analyses**

**Mortgage payable**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Beginning balance | $240,000 |
| Less: principal payments (no new borrowings) | (30,000) |
| Ending balance | **$210,000** |

**Finance lease liability**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Beginning balance | $90,000 |
| Cash lease payments | $24,000 |
| Less: interest portion (operating) | (6,000) |
| Principal reduction (financing) | **(18,000)** |
| Ending balance | **$72,000** |

**Dividends payable / retained earnings**

Dividends declared:  
\[
160{,}000 + 145{,}000 - \text{dividends declared} = 255{,}000 \implies \text{dividends declared} = \mathbf{\$50{,}000}
\]

| Dividends Payable | | |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | | 12,000 |
| Dividends declared | | 50,000 |
| Cash dividends paid | 44,000 | |
| End. bal. | | **18,000** |

Check: $12,000 + $50,000 − $44,000 = $18,000 ✓

**(d) Cash flows from financing activities — 20X2**

```
Cash flows from financing activities
  Proceeds from issuance of common stock .......... $ 110,000
  Payment to retire bonds .........................  (159,000)
  Principal payments on mortgage ..................   (30,000)
  Principal payments on finance lease .............   (18,000)
  Cash dividends paid .............................   (44,000)
  Net cash used by financing activities ........... $(141,000)
```

Cross-check: $110,000 − $159,000 − $30,000 − $18,000 − $44,000 = **$(141,000)**.

**(e) Classification notes**

| Item | SCF classification |
|---|---|
| (i) Loss on bond retirement $4,500 | **Operating** (add back under indirect method); cash paid for retirement is **financing** |
| (ii) Finance lease interest $6,000 | **Operating** (interest); principal $18,000 is **financing** |
| (iii) New finance lease at commencement (ROU ↔ lease liability, no cash) | **Noncash investing and financing** disclosure only — **not** on the face of financing or investing cash flows until principal is paid |

**Key insight:** Financing activities report **cash** changes in nontrade debt and equity and **cash** dividends. Reconstruct amounts with T-accounts; report **gross** inflows and outflows. Bond retirement cash is financing even when a loss hits net income; lease and installment **principal** (not interest) is financing.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Pinecrest Logistics: installment note life cycle + SCF financing presentation
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Installment note — initial recognition JE, amortization schedule, period-end payment JE, principal as financing outflow  
**Scenario:**  
On **January 1, Year 1**, **Pinecrest Logistics LLC** borrows **$100,000** cash by signing a **4-year installment note**. The note bears **6%** interest and requires **four equal annual payments** of **$28,859.15** each on **December 31** of Years 1–4 (payments include interest and principal). Pinecrest’s year-end is December 31. Ignore debt issue costs.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **initial recognition** journal entry on January 1, Year 1. State the SCF line if a statement of cash flows is prepared for Year 1.  
(b) Prepare the **note amortization schedule** for all four years (interest, principal, balance).  
(c) Record the **December 31, Year 1** payment journal entry (interest + principal).  
(d) Show how the Year 1 payment appears on the **statement of cash flows** (financing vs operating).  
(e) On **December 31, Year 4**, record the final payment that **settles/matures** the note. Confirm the liability is zero.  
(f) **Optional extension:** On March 15, Year 2, Pinecrest also reacquires 2,000 shares of its own $1-par common stock for **$28 per share** cash. Record the treasury stock entry and its financing presentation (Year 2).

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 100,000 | |
| Notes payable (installment) | | 100,000 |

Dr = Cr = $100,000 ✓  
**Year 1 SCF:** Proceeds from issuance of installment note — **financing inflow $100,000**.

**(b) Amortization schedule (i = 6%; payment = $28,859.15)**

Payment check: \(PV = 28{,}859.15 \times \dfrac{1-(1.06)^{-4}}{0.06} \approx \mathbf{\$100{,}000}\).

| Date | Payment | Interest (6%) | Principal | Liability balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | — | — | — | 100,000.00 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | 28,859.15 | 6,000.00 | 22,859.15 | 77,140.85 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 28,859.15 | 4,628.45 | 24,230.70 | 52,910.15 |
| Dec 31, Y3 | 28,859.15 | 3,174.61 | 25,684.54 | 27,225.61 |
| Dec 31, Y4 | 28,859.15 | 1,633.54 | 27,225.61 | **0.00** |
| **Totals** | **115,436.60** | **15,436.60** | **100,000.00** | |

Interest checks: \(100{,}000 \times 0.06 = 6{,}000\); \(77{,}140.85 \times 0.06 = 4{,}628.451 \to 4{,}628.45\); \(52{,}910.15 \times 0.06 = 3{,}174.609 \to 3{,}174.61\); \(27{,}225.61 \times 0.06 = 1{,}633.5366 \to 1{,}633.54\).  
Final principal clears the balance (any $0.01 rounding difference is absorbed in the final interest/principal split).

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — period-end payment**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest expense | 6,000.00 | |
| Notes payable (installment) | 22,859.15 | |
| Cash | | 28,859.15 |

Dr = Cr = $28,859.15 ✓

**(d) Year 1 statement of cash flows presentation of the $28,859.15 payment**

| Section | Line | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| **Operating** | Interest paid (or effect through net income under indirect method) | $6,000.00 interest |
| **Financing** | Principal payment on installment note | **$(22,859.15)** |

The **full** $28,859.15 cash out is **not** reported as a single financing line; only **principal** is financing.

**(e) December 31, Year 4 — maturity / settlement payment**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest expense | 1,633.54 | |
| Notes payable (installment) | 27,225.61 | |
| Cash | | 28,859.15 |

Dr = Cr = $28,859.15 ✓  
Liability after entry = **$0**. Financing section Year 4 includes principal outflow **$(27,225.61)**.

**(f) Treasury stock — March 15, Year 2**

Cost = 2,000 × $28 = **$56,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury stock | 56,000 | |
| Cash | | 56,000 |

Dr = Cr = $56,000 ✓  
**Year 2 financing outflow:** Purchase of treasury stock **$(56,000)**.

**Key insight:** Installment (and finance-lease) cash payments are **bifurcated**: interest → operating; principal reduction → financing. New borrowing is a financing **inflow** at initial recognition; maturity payments that extinguish principal remain financing **outflows**.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of financing cash flows
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Classify cash flows — financing vs operating vs noncash  
**Question:**  
During the year, **Lakeview Components Corp.** entered into the following transactions. Which item is reported as a **cash outflow in the financing activities** section of the statement of cash flows?

- A) Interest paid of $14,000 on a long-term bank note  
- B) Principal payment of $45,000 on a finance lease liability  
- C) Purchase of equipment for $80,000 cash  
- D) Acquisition of equipment by signing a $80,000 long-term note (no cash exchanged at acquisition)

**Answer:** **B**.  
Principal payments on finance leases (and other nontrade debt) are **financing** outflows. Interest paid (A) is **operating** under U.S. GAAP. Cash equipment purchase (C) is **investing**. Signing a note for equipment (D) is a **noncash investing and financing** disclosure, not a financing cash flow on the face of the SCF.

### Q5 — MC — Dividends declared vs cash dividends paid
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Compute financing outflow for dividends using dividends payable  
**Question:**  
**Northline Media Inc.** declared cash dividends of **$90,000** during the year. Dividends payable was **$11,000** at the beginning of the year and **$17,000** at year-end. What amount of cash dividends is reported as a financing outflow?

- A) $90,000  
- B) $96,000  
- C) $84,000  
- D) $101,000  

**Answer:** **C**.  
Cash dividends paid = dividends declared − increase in dividends payable  
\(= 90{,}000 - (17{,}000 - 11{,}000) = 90{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$84{,}000}\).  
(Alternatively: beg $11,000 + declared $90,000 − end $17,000 = $84,000.) Financing reports **cash paid**, not dividends declared.
