# Agent 390 — CORE DEMO — LO 21-5

**Chapter:** 21  
**LO title:** Apply tax effects to changes in accounting principle and error corrections  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE (change in principle, books ≠ tax):** cumulative effect **net of tax** to Retained Earnings; **Deferred Tax Asset** when book inventory falls below tax base; **Deferred Tax Liability** when book inventory rises above tax base
- **Initial recognition JE (LIFO conformity, books = tax):** LIFO change forces tax method change; tax effect is **Income Tax Payable** (repay prior LIFO savings), not deferred tax
- **Error correction JE (error on tax return, book = tax):** **Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment** net of tax; tax effect is **Income Tax Receivable** or **Payable** (cash taxes wrong)—no new temporary difference
- **Error correction JE (books wrong, tax correct):** reverse the **DTL** that arose from a taxable temporary difference (e.g., prepaid on books already deducted for tax); PPA is pretax correction net of the deferred-tax reversal
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** multi-year inventory book-vs-tax temporary-difference rollforward; deferred-tax balances after a principle change as layers turn; multi-year pretax error accumulation and net-of-tax PPA schedule; RE rollforward (as reported → cumulative effect/PPA net of tax → adjusted)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** remeasure DTA/DTL at year-end for remaining inventory temporary difference; record multi-year error PPAs and related tax as year-end correcting/adjusting entries after prior statements were issued
- **Settlement JE:** collect Income Tax Receivable (error refund); remit Income Tax Payable arising from LIFO method change
- **Classification:** DTA/DTL vs current tax receivable/payable depends on whether book and tax bases still differ after the adjustment

### Q1 — CORE — FIFO→average with DTA; period-end DTA adjusting JEs (emphasis); multi-year depreciation understatement PPA at year-end; tax-refund settlement
**LO:** LO 21-5  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for FIFO→weighted-average with DTA; subsequent measurement schedule of inventory temporary difference; **period-end adjusting JEs** to remeasure DTA; year-end multi-year depreciation-error PPA with tax receivable; settlement of tax refund  
**Scenario:**  
**Northvale Supply Corp.** is a calendar-year merchandiser. The enacted income tax rate is **25%** for all years presented.

**Change in accounting principle (Parts a–d):**  
On **January 1, Year 5**, Northvale voluntarily changes its inventory costing method from **FIFO to weighted-average** for **financial reporting only**. It continues to use **FIFO for tax purposes**. Management determines that if weighted-average had been applied in all prior periods, **December 31, Year 4 inventory** would have been **$56,000 lower** than the FIFO balance on the books.

**Beginning retained earnings, January 1, Year 5, as previously reported** (before the principle change): **$685,000**.

After the change, Northvale tracks the remaining book–tax inventory temporary difference each year-end as inventory turns (book inventory remains lower than tax inventory until the difference fully reverses):

| Measurement date | Remaining deductible temporary difference (book inv. below tax inv.) |
|---|---:|
| January 1, Year 5 (immediately after change entry) | $56,000 |
| December 31, Year 5 | $35,000 |
| December 31, Year 6 | $14,000 |
| December 31, Year 7 | $0 |

Assume the enacted rate remains 25% and that Northvale remeasures the related deferred tax asset at each **period-end** for the remaining temporary difference (no valuation allowance).

**Error correction — period-end adjusting emphasis (Parts e–f):**  
During the **December 31, Year 4** year-end close (after Year 2 and Year 3 annual statements had already been issued), Northvale discovers that **depreciation expense was understated by $15,000 in Year 2 and $15,000 in Year 3** (total pretax **$30,000**) for **both financial reporting and income tax purposes**. The same method is used for book and tax, and the error carried over to the tax returns. Record the **period-end correcting entry** on December 31, Year 4.

**Settlement (Part g):**  
On **April 15, Year 5**, Northvale receives a cash refund from the taxing authority equal to the full Income Tax Receivable recorded for the depreciation-error correction.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 5 initial recognition JE** for the FIFO → weighted-average change, including tax effects. Show Dr = Cr.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** of the inventory temporary difference and deferred tax asset balance at each date in the table (Jan 1 Y5; Dec 31 Y5; Dec 31 Y6; Dec 31 Y7).  
c. Prepare the **period-end adjusting JEs** at **December 31, Year 5**, **December 31, Year 6**, and **December 31, Year 7** to remeasure the DTA for the decline in the temporary difference (debit Income Tax Expense; credit Deferred Tax Asset).  
d. Prepare the **retained earnings rollforward** for the beginning of Year 5 reflecting the retrospective principle change.  
e. Compute the **multi-year pretax income effect** of the depreciation understatement through December 31, Year 4, and prepare the **period-end correcting JE** including tax effects.  
f. Prepare a one-line **error-effect schedule** (Year 2 pretax, Year 3 pretax, cumulative pretax, tax, net of tax).  
g. Prepare the **April 15, Year 5 settlement JE** when the tax refund cash is received.  
h. In 2–3 sentences, explain why the principle change uses a **Deferred Tax Asset** while the depreciation error uses **Income Tax Receivable**.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — FIFO → weighted-average (January 1, Year 5)**

Pretax cumulative effect (inventory ↓ / cumulative COGS ↑) = **$56,000**  
Deferred tax asset = \( \$56{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$14{,}000} \)  
Net-of-tax debit to retained earnings = \( \$56{,}000 - \$14{,}000 = \mathbf{\$42{,}000} \)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings | 42,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 14,000 | |
| Inventory | | 56,000 |
| *Retrospective change FIFO → weighted-average; books only; tax still FIFO* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(42{,}000 + 14{,}000 = 56{,}000\) = Cr 56,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — inventory temporary difference and DTA**

| Measurement date | Remaining deductible TD (book inv. < tax inv.) | DTA @ 25% | Change in DTA (period) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Year 5 (after change entry) | $56,000 | **$14,000** | — (established) |
| Dec 31, Year 5 | 35,000 | **8,750** | ↓ $5,250 |
| Dec 31, Year 6 | 14,000 | **3,500** | ↓ $5,250 |
| Dec 31, Year 7 | 0 | **$0** | ↓ $3,500 |

Book inventory lower than tax inventory → **deductible temporary difference** → **DTA**. As layers turn, book COGS exceeds tax COGS, pretax book income is lower relative to taxable income, and the DTA **reverses through current tax expense** at each **period-end remeasurement**.

**c. Period-end adjusting JEs — DTA remeasurement (emphasis)**

**December 31, Year 5** (DTA from $14,000 → $8,750):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 5,250 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 5,250 |
| *Period-end remeasure DTA for remaining deductible TD of $35,000 × 25%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,250 = Cr 5,250. **Balanced.**

**December 31, Year 6** (DTA from $8,750 → $3,500):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 5,250 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 5,250 |
| *Period-end remeasure DTA for remaining deductible TD of $14,000 × 25%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,250 = Cr 5,250. **Balanced.**

**December 31, Year 7** (DTA from $3,500 → $0):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 3,500 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 3,500 |
| *Period-end remeasure DTA — temporary difference fully reversed* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,500 = Cr 3,500. **Balanced.**  
**Cumulative DTA reverse:** \(5{,}250 + 5{,}250 + 3{,}500 = 14{,}000\) (matches original DTA).

**d. Retained earnings rollforward (beginning Year 5)**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Retained earnings, Jan 1, Year 5, as previously reported | $685,000 |
| Cumulative effect of change in accounting principle (net of $14,000 tax benefit) | (42,000) |
| **Retained earnings, Jan 1, Year 5, as adjusted** | **$643,000** |

**e. Period-end error correction — multi-year depreciation understatement (December 31, Year 4)**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Year 2 understatement of depreciation (overstatement of pretax income) | $15,000 |
| Year 3 understatement of depreciation (overstatement of pretax income) | 15,000 |
| **Cumulative pretax income overstatement at Dec 31, Year 4** | **$30,000** |
| Income tax receivable ($30,000 × 25%) | $7,500 |
| **Prior period adjustment, net of tax ($30,000 × 75%)** | **$22,500** |

Because the error also appeared on the tax return and book = tax measurement, cash taxes were overpaid → **Income Tax Receivable**, not deferred tax. No DTA/DTL to reverse (book and tax depreciation were identical under the error).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 22,500 | |
| Income Tax Receivable | 7,500 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 30,000 |
| *Period-end PPA: correct Years 2–3 understated depreciation; error on books and tax return* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(22{,}500 + 7{,}500 = 30{,}000\) = Cr 30,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Multi-year error-effect schedule (subsequent measurement of cumulative PPA)**

| Period | Pretax income effect of error (overstatement) | Tax @ 25% | Net-of-tax effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 2 | +$15,000 | +$3,750 | +$11,250 |
| Year 3 | +$15,000 | +$3,750 | +$11,250 |
| **Cumulative at Dec 31, Year 4** | **+$30,000** | **+$7,500** | **+$22,500** |

PPA debits RE for the **cumulative net-of-tax overstatement** of **$22,500**; tax receivable is the cumulative **$7,500**.

**g. Settlement — collect tax refund (April 15, Year 5)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 7,500 | |
| Income Tax Receivable | | 7,500 |
| *Collect refund for overpaid taxes related to depreciation-error restatement* | | |

**Check:** Dr 7,500 = Cr 7,500. **Balanced.**

**h. Why DTA vs Income Tax Receivable**

- **Principle change (books ≠ tax):** After the entry, book inventory is on a different basis than tax inventory until layers reverse → **temporary difference** → **Deferred Tax Asset**, remeasured each **period-end**.  
- **Depreciation error (books = tax, error on return):** Correcting both books and tax does **not** leave a temporary difference; cash taxes were wrong → **Income Tax Receivable** for the refund.

**Key insight:** Retrospective principle changes and error PPAs hit equity **net of tax**. The tax **account** is deferred tax when book and tax bases still differ after the adjustment, and current tax receivable/payable when cash taxes were simply wrong. **Period-end** work for LO 21-5 is both (1) year-end DTA/DTL remeasurement as inventory temporary differences reverse and (2) year-end prior-period-adjustment entries when multi-year errors are discovered after prior statements were issued.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Average→FIFO with DTL; period-end DTL adjusting JEs; books-only prepaid error reversing DTL
**LO:** LO 21-5  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — weighted-average→FIFO with DTL at 30%; multi-year temporary-difference / DTL subsequent measurement schedule; **period-end DTL adjusting JEs**; multi-year prepaid-error PPA (books wrong, tax correct) reversing DTL; RE rollforward  
**Scenario:**  
**Bayridge Trade Group LLC** (calendar year-end). Enacted tax rate is **30%**.

**Principle change:** On **January 1, Year 6**, Bayridge changes from **weighted-average to FIFO** for **financial reporting only**; continues **weighted-average for tax**. If FIFO had always been used, Dec 31, Year 5 inventory would be **$72,000 higher**. Beginning RE as previously reported on Jan 1, Year 6: **$910,000**.

Remaining **taxable** temporary difference (book inventory above tax inventory) after the change:

| Measurement date | Remaining taxable temporary difference |
|---|---:|
| January 1, Year 6 (after change entry) | $72,000 |
| December 31, Year 6 | $45,000 |
| December 31, Year 7 | $18,000 |
| December 31, Year 8 | $0 |

**Error (books wrong, tax correct) — period-end discovery:**  
During the **December 31, Year 4** year-end close, Bayridge discovers that operating expenses for **Year 2 and Year 3** were **understated by $20,000 each year** for **accounting purposes only** (total pretax overstatement **$40,000**). The amounts were incorrectly left in **Prepaid Expense** on the books but were **correctly deducted when paid for tax purposes**. Assume a related **Deferred Tax Liability** of **$12,000** (\( \$40{,}000 \times 30\% \)) is already on the books for this taxable temporary difference. Record the **period-end correcting entry** on December 31, Year 4.

**Required:**  
a. January 1, Year 6 JE for the average-cost → FIFO change (with tax).  
b. Subsequent measurement schedule of remaining taxable TD and DTL at each date.  
c. **Period-end adjusting JEs** at **December 31, Year 6**, **December 31, Year 7**, and **December 31, Year 8** to remeasure the DTL.  
d. Beginning RE rollforward for Year 6.  
e. December 31, Year 4 **period-end correcting JE** for the multi-year prepaid overstatement (with deferred-tax reversal), including pretax computation.  
f. Briefly state why this error uses a **Deferred Tax Liability reversal** rather than Income Tax Receivable.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — average-cost → FIFO (January 1, Year 6)**

Pretax cumulative effect (inventory ↑ / cumulative COGS ↓) = **$72,000**  
Deferred tax liability = \( \$72{,}000 \times 30\% = \mathbf{\$21{,}600} \)  
Net-of-tax credit to retained earnings = \( \$72{,}000 - \$21{,}600 = \mathbf{\$50{,}400} \)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 72,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | | 21,600 |
| Retained Earnings | | 50,400 |
| *Retrospective change average-cost → FIFO; books only; tax still average-cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 72,000 = Cr \(21{,}600 + 50{,}400 = 72{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — taxable temporary difference and DTL**

| Measurement date | Remaining taxable TD (book inv. > tax inv.) | DTL @ 30% | Change in DTL (period) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Year 6 (after change entry) | $72,000 | **$21,600** | — (established) |
| Dec 31, Year 6 | 45,000 | **13,500** | ↓ $8,100 |
| Dec 31, Year 7 | 18,000 | **5,400** | ↓ $8,100 |
| Dec 31, Year 8 | 0 | **$0** | ↓ $5,400 |

Book inventory higher than tax inventory → **taxable temporary difference** → **DTL**. As the difference reverses, the DTL is reduced at each **period-end** with a credit to Income Tax Expense (benefit).

**c. Period-end adjusting JEs — DTL remeasurement (emphasis)**

**December 31, Year 6** (DTL from $21,600 → $13,500):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Tax Liability | 8,100 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 8,100 |
| *Period-end remeasure DTL for remaining taxable TD of $45,000 × 30%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 8,100 = Cr 8,100. **Balanced.**

**December 31, Year 7** (DTL from $13,500 → $5,400):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Tax Liability | 8,100 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 8,100 |
| *Period-end remeasure DTL for remaining taxable TD of $18,000 × 30%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 8,100 = Cr 8,100. **Balanced.**

**December 31, Year 8** (DTL from $5,400 → $0):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Tax Liability | 5,400 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 5,400 |
| *Period-end remeasure DTL — temporary difference fully reversed* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,400 = Cr 5,400. **Balanced.**  
**Cumulative DTL reverse:** \(8{,}100 + 8{,}100 + 5{,}400 = 21{,}600\).

**d. Retained earnings rollforward (beginning Year 6)**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Retained earnings, Jan 1, Year 6, as previously reported | $910,000 |
| Cumulative effect of change in accounting principle (net of $21,600 tax) | 50,400 |
| **Retained earnings, Jan 1, Year 6, as adjusted** | **$960,400** |

**e. Period-end error correction — prepaid overstated (books only; tax correct) (December 31, Year 4)**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Year 2 understatement of operating expense | $20,000 |
| Year 3 understatement of operating expense | 20,000 |
| **Cumulative pretax income overstatement (prepaid overstated)** | **$40,000** |
| Deferred tax liability already recognized ($40,000 × 30%) | $12,000 |
| **Prior period adjustment, net of tax ($40,000 × 70%)** | **$28,000** |

Tax was already correct (expenses properly deducted on the return when paid). The books-only prepaid created a **taxable temporary difference** (book carrying amount of prepaid > tax base of zero) → **DTL**. Correction removes prepaid and **reverses the DTL**; net equity hit is after tax.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 28,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 12,000 | |
| Prepaid Expense | | 40,000 |
| *Period-end PPA: reverse books-only prepaid error; remove related DTL* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(28{,}000 + 12{,}000 = 40{,}000\) = Cr 40,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Why DTL reversal vs Income Tax Receivable**

Cash taxes were **correct** (expense already deducted for tax). The only tax balance from the error is the **deferred** effect of a book–tax temporary difference. Correcting the books eliminates that temporary difference → **debit Deferred Tax Liability**. Income Tax Receivable would apply only if cash taxes had been overpaid because the error also contaminated the tax return.

**Key insight:** Opposite-direction inventory principle changes flip DTA ↔ DTL. **Period-end** DTL remeasurement is the mirror of DTA remeasurement (debit DTL / credit tax expense as taxable TDs reverse). Books-only errors that created prepaid assets already deducted for tax require **PPA + DTL reverse**, not a tax refund receivable.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — LIFO→FIFO with Income Tax Payable; multi-year inventory-gap schedule; period-end PPA for capitalized repairs; settlement of LIFO tax payable
**LO:** LO 21-5  
**Concept:** LIFO→FIFO initial recognition with **Income Tax Payable** (conformity); multi-year LIFO/FIFO inventory difference subsequent measurement schedule feeding the cumulative effect; period-end capitalized-repair error PPA with tax receivable; settlement of LIFO-related tax payable  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Components Inc.** (calendar year-end). Enacted tax rate is **25%**.

**LIFO → FIFO (Parts a–c):**  
Harborline has used **LIFO for both book and tax** since inception (LIFO conformity). On **January 1, Year 4**, management changes to **FIFO for both book and tax**. Comparative LIFO vs FIFO ending inventory balances:

| Date | LIFO inventory | FIFO inventory | FIFO − LIFO gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dec 31, Year 1 | $88,000 | $100,000 | $12,000 |
| Dec 31, Year 2 | 95,000 | 118,000 | 23,000 |
| Dec 31, Year 3 | 102,000 | 142,000 | **40,000** |

Beginning retained earnings, January 1, Year 4, as previously reported under LIFO: **$380,000**.

Because LIFO conformity requires the tax method to change as well, prior taxes saved under LIFO must be repaid; the tax effect of the cumulative inventory increase is **Income Tax Payable** (not deferred tax).

**Error — capitalized ordinary repairs (Parts d–e):**  
On **January 1, Year 1**, Harborline paid **$28,000** cash for ordinary repairs and **erroneously debited Equipment**. Straight-line depreciation is **10% per year** with no residual. The **same 10% rate is used for tax**, and the **error carried over to the tax return**. The error is discovered on **January 1, Year 3** (after Year 1 and Year 2 statements were issued)—record the correcting entry as of that date (period-open adjusting / PPA entry for restatement of prior periods).

**Settlement (Part f):**  
On **June 30, Year 4**, Harborline remits cash equal to the full Income Tax Payable recorded for the LIFO → FIFO change (ignore installment-plan timing for this demo).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** of the LIFO/FIFO inventory gap at each year-end (build-up of pretax cumulative effect).  
b. Prepare the **January 1, Year 4 initial recognition JE** for LIFO → FIFO including tax effects (Income Tax Payable).  
c. Prepare the beginning **RE rollforward** for Year 4.  
d. Compute the pretax income effects of the repair error through January 1, Year 3, and prepare the **correcting JE** including tax effects.  
e. Prepare a brief **error-effect schedule** (Year 1, Year 2, cumulative, tax, net of tax).  
f. Prepare the **June 30, Year 4 settlement JE** for the LIFO-related tax payable.  
g. In 2–3 sentences, contrast the tax account for LIFO→FIFO (books = tax after change) with FIFO→average books-only (books ≠ tax after change).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Subsequent measurement schedule — LIFO/FIFO inventory gap (pretax cumulative effect build-up)**

| Measurement date | LIFO EI | FIFO EI | Pretax inventory understatement under LIFO (FIFO − LIFO) | Incremental pretax NI understatement that year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dec 31, Year 1 | $88,000 | $100,000 | **$12,000** | $12,000 |
| Dec 31, Year 2 | 95,000 | 118,000 | **23,000** | 11,000 |
| Dec 31, Year 3 | 102,000 | 142,000 | **40,000** | 17,000 |

Cumulative pretax effect at Jan 1, Year 4 = **$40,000** (matches Dec 31, Year 3 gap).  
Check incremental: \(12{,}000 + 11{,}000 + 17{,}000 = 40{,}000\).

**b. Initial recognition — LIFO → FIFO for book and tax (January 1, Year 4)**

Pretax inventory increase = **$40,000**  
Income tax payable = \( \$40{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$10{,}000} \)  
Net-of-tax credit to retained earnings = \( \$40{,}000 - \$10{,}000 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000} \)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 40,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 10,000 |
| Retained Earnings | | 30,000 |
| *Retrospective LIFO → FIFO; LIFO conformity forces tax change; repay prior LIFO tax savings* | | |

**Check:** Dr 40,000 = Cr \(10{,}000 + 30{,}000 = 40{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**c. Retained earnings rollforward (beginning Year 4)**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Retained earnings, Jan 1, Year 4, as previously reported (LIFO) | $380,000 |
| Cumulative effect of change to FIFO (net of $10,000 tax) | 30,000 |
| **Retained earnings, Jan 1, Year 4, as adjusted** | **$410,000** |

**d. Error correction — repairs capitalized (January 1, Year 3)**

Two years of depreciation have been recorded on the improperly capitalized amount (\(2 \times \$28{,}000 \times 10\% = \$5{,}600\)).

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Correct repair expense for Year 1 (never recorded as expense) | $28,000 |
| Depreciation incorrectly recorded in Year 1 ($28,000 × 10%) | (2,800) |
| **Year 1 net understatement of pretax expense (overstatement of pretax income)** | **$25,200** |
| Depreciation incorrectly recorded in Year 2 ($28,000 × 10%) — overstatement of Year 2 expense | (2,800) |

| **Prior period adjustment, net of tax ($22,400 × 75%)** | **$16,800** |

Because the error also appeared on the tax return and book = tax measurement, cash taxes were overpaid → **Income Tax Receivable**, not deferred tax. No DTA/DTL to reverse (book and tax depreciation were identical under the error).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 5,600 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 16,800 | |
| Income Tax Receivable | 5,600 | |
| Equipment | | 28,000 |
| *Correct Years 1–2 capitalization of ordinary repairs; error on books and tax return* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(5{,}600 + 16{,}800 + 5{,}600 = 28{,}000\) = Cr 28,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Multi-year error-effect schedule**

| Period | Pretax income effect of error (overstatement / (understatement)) | Tax @ 25% | Net-of-tax effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|

**f. Settlement — remit LIFO-change tax payable (June 30, Year 4)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Payable | 10,000 | |
| Cash | | 10,000 |
| *Settle tax payable arising from LIFO → FIFO method change* | | |

**Check:** Dr 10,000 = Cr 10,000. **Balanced.**

**g. LIFO payable vs books-only deferred tax**

- **LIFO → FIFO (book and tax both change):** After the entry, book and tax inventory bases align under FIFO → **no temporary difference** remains. The tax effect of prior LIFO savings is a **current Income Tax Payable** (cash taxes to repay).  
- **FIFO → average books-only:** Book inventory basis changes while tax stays FIFO → **temporary difference** remains → **Deferred Tax Asset**, remeasured each period-end until layers reverse.

**Key insight:** LIFO conformity converts the tax leg of a principle change from deferred tax into **Income Tax Payable**. Capitalized-repair errors that also hit the tax return create **Income Tax Receivable** and a net-of-tax **prior period adjustment**, often recorded as a **period-end correcting entry** when discovered during year-end close.

### Q4 — MC (classification of tax effects)
**LO:** LO 21-5  
**Concept:** Classification — DTA for books-only inventory principle change that lowers book inventory; Income Tax Payable when LIFO changes for book and tax; Income Tax Receivable when an error overstated income equally for book and tax  

**Question 1:**  
On January 1 of the current year, a company changes from FIFO to weighted-average for **financial reporting only** and continues FIFO for tax. The change **decreases** beginning inventory by $80,000. The enacted tax rate is 25%. Which tax account is credited or debited in the retrospective entry, and for how much?

- A) Debit Deferred Tax Asset $20,000  
- B) Credit Deferred Tax Liability $20,000  
- C) Debit Income Tax Receivable $20,000  
- D) Credit Income Tax Payable $20,000  

**Answer:** **A.** Inventory down for books while tax inventory stays higher creates a **deductible temporary difference** of $80,000 → **Deferred Tax Asset** \(80{,}000 \times 25\% = 20{,}000\). Cash taxes are unchanged at the change date, so receivable/payable is incorrect. A liability would apply if book inventory **increased** above the tax base.

**Question 2:**  
A company discovers at year-end that it understated depreciation by a total of $40,000 over two prior closed years for **both book and tax**. The tax rate is 25%. The period-end correcting entry includes which tax effect?

- A) Credit Deferred Tax Liability $10,000  
- B) Debit Deferred Tax Asset $10,000  
- C) Debit Income Tax Receivable $10,000  
- D) Credit Income Tax Payable $10,000  

**Answer:** **C.** Pretax income was overstated by $40,000 on both books and tax → taxes were **overpaid** → **Income Tax Receivable** of \(40{,}000 \times 25\% = 10{,}000\). No temporary difference remains after correcting both bases the same way, so deferred tax is not used. Payable would apply if taxes had been underpaid (income understated).
