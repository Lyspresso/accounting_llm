# Agent 71 — CORE DEMO — LO 11-6

**Chapter:** 11  
**LO title:** Account for property, plant, and equipment related costs after acquisition  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Ordinary repairs and maintenance: expense in the period incurred (do not capitalize)
- Improvements / replacements — **substitution method**: remove old component (AD + loss/gain, credit asset) then capitalize new component
- Improvements / replacements — **reduction of accumulated depreciation**: when expenditure extends useful life (quality not improved) and old component cost is unknown
- Additions (extensions/enlargements): capitalize and depreciate over the **shorter** of the addition’s own life or remaining life of the original asset
- Rearrangements: capitalize if material and future periods benefit; depreciate over periods benefited
- Subsequent measurement: revise depreciable base / remaining life after capitalized post-acquisition costs; period-end depreciation adjusting JE
- Classification / presentation: capital expenditure vs revenue expenditure; misclassification distorts current and future income
- Do **not** accrue planned major maintenance in advance (no obligation before work begins)

### Q1 — CORE — Initial recognition of subsequent PPE costs (repairs, addition, roof substitution, AD reduction, rearrangement)
**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** Initial recognition journal entries for ordinary repairs, building addition, component replacement (substitution), life-extending update (reduce AD), and rearrangement  
**Scenario:**  
**Northwind Fabrication Co.** owns a corporate plant building. At **January 1, Year 5**, the building’s original cost is **$2,400,000** and accumulated depreciation is **$960,000**. During Year 5, Northwind incurred the following **cash** expenditures related to the building and plant operations (all after the building was already in service):

| Item | Description | Cash cost |
|---|---|---:|
| a | Ordinary repairs and routine maintenance (lubrication, painting, minor parts) | $45,000 |
| b | Completed a building **extension** adding office and mezzanine space. Estimated useful life of the **addition** is **10 years**. Remaining useful life of the original building is **16 years**. Residual value of the addition is **$0**. | 620,000 |
| c | Removed the original roof (**cost $180,000**, **60%** depreciated) and replaced it with a solar-enhanced roof | 350,000 |
| d | Updated the electrical system. The update **extends the useful life** of the building but does **not** improve quality/productivity beyond original expectations. **Old electrical costs are not separately tracked.** | 95,000 |
| e | Rearranged factory equipment layout to improve production flow; benefits are expected over **4 years** and the amount is material | 55,000 |

Northwind capitalizes material post-acquisition expenditures that increase useful life or productivity, and expenses ordinary maintenance. Use the **substitution method** for item (c) and the **reduction of accumulated depreciation method** for item (d). Record rearrangements to **Equipment** when they relate to factory layout.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the journal entry for each of items (a) through (e).  
b. After recording (a)–(e), compute the **revised Building gross cost** and **revised Accumulated Depreciation—Building** (ignore Year 5 depreciation on the building itself for this part).  
c. Compute **Year 5 straight-line depreciation on the addition only** (item b), and prepare the **December 31, Year 5** adjusting entry for that depreciation (full year in service).  
d. Briefly classify each of (a)–(e) as **expense immediately** or **capitalize**, and name the method used when more than one replacement method exists.

**Answer key:**

**a. Journal entries**

**(a) Ordinary repair — expense in period incurred**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense | 45,000 | |
| Cash | | 45,000 |
| *Ordinary maintenance; keeps asset in operating condition without extending life* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 45,000.

**(b) Addition — capitalize Building**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Building | 620,000 | |
| Cash | | 620,000 |
| *Building extension; depreciate over shorter of 10 or 16 years = 10 years* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 620,000.

**(c) Replacement — substitution method**  
Old roof AD = $180,000 × 60% = **$108,000**  
Old roof book value = $180,000 − $108,000 = **$72,000** (loss on removal)

Remove old roof:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Building | 108,000 | |
| Loss on Asset Improvement | 72,000 | |
| Building | | 180,000 |
| *Derecognize replaced roof component* | | |

**Check:** 108,000 + 72,000 = 180,000.

Capitalize new roof:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Building | 350,000 | |
| Cash | | 350,000 |
| *Capitalize replacement roof* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 350,000.

**(d) Life extension; old cost unknown — reduce accumulated depreciation**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Building | 95,000 | |
| Cash | | 95,000 |
| *Extends useful life without quality upgrade; old component cost unavailable* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 95,000.

**(e) Rearrangement — capitalize (future benefit, material)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 55,000 | |
| Cash | | 55,000 |
| *Factory rearrangement benefits 4 future years; capitalize and depreciate over 4 years* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 55,000.

**b. Building accounts after (a)–(e) (before Year 5 building depreciation)**

| Building gross cost | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Building | $2,400,000 |
| + Addition (b) | 620,000 |
| − Old roof removed (c) | (180,000) |
| + New roof (c) | 350,000 |
| **Revised Building cost** | **$3,190,000** |

| Accumulated Depreciation—Building | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning AD | $960,000 |
| − AD removed with old roof (c) | (108,000) |
| − AD reduced for electrical update (d) | (95,000) |
| **Revised AD—Building** | **$757,000** |

(Item a expenses; item e hits Equipment, not Building.)

**c. Year 5 depreciation on the addition only**  
Depreciable base = $620,000 − $0 residual = **$620,000**  
Life = min(10, 16) = **10 years**  
Annual depreciation = $620,000 / 10 = **$62,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 62,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Building | | 62,000 |
| *Period-end adjusting JE: Year 5 SL depreciation on addition* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 62,000.

**d. Classification summary**

| Item | Treatment | Method / note |
|---|---|---|
| a | Expense immediately | Ordinary repair / maintenance |
| b | Capitalize | Addition; depreciate over shorter life (10 yrs) |
| c | Capitalize (after removing old) | **Substitution** (old carrying value known) |
| d | Capitalize via AD reduction | **Reduce AD** (life extended; old cost unknown) |
| e | Capitalize | Rearrangement with multi-period benefit |

**Key insight:** Post-acquisition costs that merely maintain original service potential are expensed. Costs that add capacity (addition), replace a major component (substitution or capitalize), extend life (sometimes reduce AD), or rearrange for multi-period benefit are capitalized and then depreciated over the periods benefited—not necessarily the remaining life of the host asset.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Twin of subsequent-cost recognition (all amounts changed)
**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: ordinary repair, addition, substitution replacement, AD reduction for life extension, rearrangement  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Alloy Works** has a plant building with original cost **$3,600,000** and accumulated depreciation **$1,440,000** at **January 1, Year 3**. During Year 3, Cascade paid cash for:

| Item | Description | Cash cost |
|---|---|---:|
| a | Ordinary repairs and routine upkeep | $68,000 |
| b | Warehouse **wing addition**. Useful life of addition **12 years**; remaining life of original building **20 years**; residual of addition **$0** | 840,000 |
| c | Replaced a major HVAC unit: original HVAC cost **$240,000**, **75%** depreciated; new HVAC cost as shown | 275,000 |
| d | Structural reinforcement that **extends useful life** of the building (no quality upgrade beyond original; old component costs **not known**) | 130,000 |
| e | Relocated and reconfigured the receiving bay (material; **5-year** benefit) | 90,000 |

Use **substitution** for (c) and **reduction of accumulated depreciation** for (d). Capitalize (e) to **Building**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Subsequent measurement schedule, period-end depreciation, component disposal, and classification
**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** After-acquisition improvement with substitution; revised depreciable base and multi-year SL schedule; period-end adjusting JE; classification of mixed subsequent costs  
**Scenario:**  
**Silverpine Robotics Inc.** purchased specialized production equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$160,000**. Estimated residual value **$10,000**; useful life **10 years**; straight-line depreciation; calendar year-end. Depreciation has been recorded correctly through **December 31, Year 4**.

On **January 1, Year 5**, Silverpine completes the following **cash** transactions related to this equipment:

1. **Ordinary maintenance** (cleaning, minor parts): **$6,500** — expense.  
2. **Major engine replacement** (substitution): original engine cost **$40,000** with related accumulated depreciation **$24,000**; new engine cost **$52,000**.  
3. After the engine replacement, management revises remaining useful life of the **entire equipment unit** to **8 years** from January 1, Year 5. Residual value remains **$10,000**.

**Additional classification items (Year 5, unrelated to the equipment above — for part e only):**

| Tag | Expenditure | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| W | $12,000 painting and lubrication of plant machines | Maintains operating condition only |
| X | $88,000 new fire-sprinkler system installed in existing building | Addition of a system; remaining building life 15 years; sprinkler life 20 years |
| Y | $25,000 re-routing of assembly line for multi-year efficiency gains | Material rearrangement |
| Z | $9,000 repair after forklift damage caused by operator negligence | Restores asset to pre-accident condition; no increase in service potential |

**Required:**  
a. Compute equipment cost, AD, and book value **immediately before** the January 1, Year 5 engine work.  
b. Prepare all January 1, Year 5 journal entries for items 1–2 (maintenance and engine substitution).  
c. Compute **revised** equipment cost, AD, and book value **after** the substitution. Compute annual SL depreciation for Year 5 onward.  
d. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** of depreciation expense and ending book value for **Years 5–7** (three years). Prepare the **December 31, Year 5** adjusting entry.  
e. For tags W–Z, state **capitalize or expense (or loss)** and the primary reason (one line each). For X, state the depreciable life to use.

**Answer key:**

**a. Pre-improvement balances (Jan 1, Year 5, before entries)**  
Annual depreciation Years 1–4 = ($160,000 − $10,000) / 10 = **$15,000** per year  
AD after 4 years = 4 × $15,000 = **$60,000**  
Book value = $160,000 − $60,000 = **$100,000**

**b. January 1, Year 5 entries**

Maintenance:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense | 6,500 | |
| Cash | | 6,500 |

**Check:** 6,500 = 6,500.

Remove old engine (disposal of component):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 24,000 | |
| Loss on Asset Improvement | 16,000 | |
| Equipment | | 40,000 |
| *BV of old engine = 40,000 − 24,000 = 16,000* | | |

**Check:** 24,000 + 16,000 = 40,000.

Capitalize new engine:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 52,000 | |
| Cash | | 52,000 |

**Check:** 52,000 = 52,000.

**c. Revised balances and new annual depreciation**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment cost: 160,000 − 40,000 + 52,000 | **$172,000** |
| AD: 60,000 − 24,000 | **$36,000** |
| Book value | **$136,000** |
| Residual | 10,000 |
| Depreciable base | **$126,000** |
| Remaining life | 8 years |
| **Annual SL depreciation (Year 5+)** | **$15,750** |

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule (straight-line)**

| Year | Beg. BV | Depreciation expense | End. AD (cumulative from post-sub) | End. BV |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 136,000 | 15,750 | 36,000 + 15,750 = 51,750 | 120,250 |
| 6 | 120,250 | 15,750 | 67,500 | 104,500 |
| 7 | 104,500 | 15,750 | 83,250 | 88,750 |

(End. BV = Cost $172,000 − End. AD; after Year 7 AD = 36,000 + 3×15,750 = 36,000 + 47,250 = 83,250; BV = 172,000 − 83,250 = 88,750.)

**December 31, Year 5 adjusting JE:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 15,750 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 15,750 |
| *Period-end depreciation after post-acquisition capitalization* | | |

**Check:** 15,750 = 15,750.

**Proof rollforward Year 5:** Beg BV 136,000 − Dep 15,750 = End BV **120,250**.

**e. Classification (W–Z)**

| Tag | Treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| W | **Expense** (Repair/Maintenance Expense) | Ordinary maintenance; no increase in life or productivity |
| X | **Capitalize** (Building or Building Improvements); depreciate over **15 years** | Addition of a system; shorter of sprinkler life 20 vs remaining building life 15 |
| Y | **Capitalize** (Equipment or Plant Rearrangement); depreciate over periods benefited | Material rearrangement with multi-period benefit |
| Z | **Loss** (or expense as loss from damage) | Accident/neglect; repair does not increase service potential above pre-mishap level |

**Key insight:** Substitution both **disposes** of the old component (loss = old BV) and **capitalizes** the new one. Subsequent depreciation uses the **revised book value** (and revised remaining life), so period-end adjusting entries change after the improvement. Classification errors (expensing a capital addition or capitalizing ordinary repairs) overstate/understate income across periods.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of post-acquisition PPE costs
**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** Classify subsequent expenditures (capitalize vs expense) and choose the correct improvement method  

**Question 1:**  
On June 30, a company paid **$200,000** cash to replace a building’s elevators. The old elevators cost **$120,000** and were **70%** depreciated. The elevators are a major component; old carrying amounts are known. Which journal-entry approach is **most appropriate** under the substitution method?

- A) Debit Repair Expense $200,000; credit Cash $200,000 only  
- B) Debit Building $200,000; credit Cash $200,000 only (leave old elevators on the books)  
- C) Remove the old elevators (debit Accumulated Depreciation $84,000, debit Loss $36,000, credit Building $120,000) and debit Building $200,000; credit Cash $200,000  
- D) Debit Accumulated Depreciation $200,000; credit Cash $200,000 only  

**Answer:** C.  
Old AD = $120,000 × 70% = $84,000; BV = $36,000 loss. Substitution removes the old component and capitalizes the new elevators. A is ordinary-repair treatment (incorrect for a major replacement). B is pure capitalization without removing the old component (used when old cost is significantly depreciated / not tracked—not the facts here). D is the reduction-of-AD method for life extension when quality is not improved and often when old cost is unknown—not substitution with known BV.

**Question 2:**  
Which of the following post-acquisition expenditures should be **expensed** in the period incurred (not capitalized)?

- A) Material factory rearrangement expected to benefit production for six years  
- B) Construction of an additional loading dock attached to an existing warehouse  
- C) Annual HVAC filter changes, oiling, and minor belt replacements that keep machines at original efficiency  
- D) Installation of a new high-capacity production line section that increases output quality above original estimates  

**Answer:** C.  
Ordinary repairs and maintenance that preserve original service potential are expensed. A, B, and D increase future service potential or capacity and are capitalized (then depreciated over periods benefited).
