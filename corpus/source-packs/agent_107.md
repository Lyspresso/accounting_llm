# Agent 107 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-2

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Account for common stock issuance, including par and no-par, cash and noncash, and issue costs  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Par-value cash issuance:** Credit Common Stock for shares × par; excess of proceeds over par → Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock
- **True no-par cash issuance:** Credit Common Stock for the **full** proceeds; no separate PIC excess account
- **Stated-value no-par issuance:** Accounted for like par stock (CS at stated value; remainder to PIC in excess of stated value)
- **Noncash issuance:** Measure at FV of stock **or** FV of noncash consideration, whichever is **more clearly evident**
- **Stock issue costs:** Reduce net cash and reduce PIC in excess of par; **not** an expense (excluded from net income)
- **Lump-sum (multiple securities):** Proportional (relative FV) when both FVs known; incremental when only one FV known
- **Subsequent schedule:** Paid-in capital / shares rollforward after successive issuances (equity not remeasured at FV after issuance)
- **Period-end presentation:** Authorized, issued, outstanding, par; legal capital = par (or stated/full proceeds for true no-par); **no** period-end remeasurement adjusting JE for issued common stock
- **Authorization:** No journal entry when shares are merely authorized in the charter

### Q1 — CORE — Par, no-par, noncash, issue costs, equity rollforward
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JEs for par cash, true no-par, stated-value no-par, noncash asset exchange, and cash with stock issue costs; paid-in capital rollforward schedule; period-end equity presentation (no remeasurement AJ)  
**Scenario:**  
**CedarForge Analytics Inc.** is organized on January 1, Year 1. Its corporate charter authorizes **500,000 shares** of **$2 par** common stock. No journal entry is made for authorization alone.

The following **separate** common-stock transactions occur in Year 1 (treat each required part as independent unless noted):

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Mar 1 | Issues **25,000** shares of **$2 par** common stock for cash at **$18** per share. |
| Apr 10 | *(Alternative structure — ignore par for this part only)* Issues **25,000** shares of **true no-par** common stock for cash at **$18** per share. |
| Apr 10 | *(Alternative structure — ignore par for this part only)* Issues **25,000** shares of **no-par, stated value $2** common stock for cash at **$18** per share. |
| Jun 15 | Issues **8,000** shares of **$2 par** common stock in exchange for specialized equipment. CedarForge common stock trades actively at **$19** per share on June 15. The equipment has **no** reliable stand-alone fair value. |
| Sep 1 | Issues **12,000** shares of **$2 par** common stock for cash at **$20** per share and incurs **$9,000** of stock issue costs (registration, underwriting, legal). Cash is received net of issue costs. |
| Dec 31 | Year-end reporting. No further equity transactions. Common stock is **not** remeasured to fair value after issuance. |

For the **rollforward and balance-sheet presentation** parts, assume CedarForge uses **$2 par** stock and records **only** the March 1, June 15, and September 1 transactions (not the April 10 alternatives).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **March 1** journal entry for the **par-value** cash issuance.  
b. Prepare the **April 10** journal entry assuming **true no-par** stock.  
c. Prepare the **April 10** journal entry assuming **no-par with $2 stated value**.  
d. Prepare the **June 15** journal entry for the **noncash** equipment issuance (state the measurement basis).  
e. Prepare the **September 1** journal entry for cash issuance **including stock issue costs**.  
f. Prepare a **paid-in capital and shares rollforward schedule** from March 1 through September 1 (par stock path only). Show ending Common Stock, PIC—CS, total paid-in capital, shares issued, and **legal capital**.  
g. **Period-end:** State whether a December 31 **adjusting** journal entry is required to remeasure issued common stock. Prepare the **stockholders’ equity presentation** of paid-in capital at December 31 (ignore retained earnings and AOCI).  

**Answer key:**  

**a. March 1 — par-value cash issuance (emphasis: initial recognition JE)**

Cash proceeds = 25,000 × $18 = **$450,000**  
Common Stock (par) = 25,000 × $2 = **$50,000**  
PIC in excess of par = $450,000 − $50,000 = **$400,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 450,000 | |
| Common Stock (25,000 × $2) | | 50,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 400,000 |
| *Record $2 par common stock issued for cash at $18* | | |

**Check:** Dr 450,000 = Cr 50,000 + 400,000. **Balanced.**

**b. April 10 — true no-par cash issuance**

Full proceeds credited to Common Stock; **no** separate PIC excess account.

Cash = 25,000 × $18 = **$450,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 450,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 450,000 |
| *Record true no-par common stock issued for cash* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 450,000. **Balanced.**

**c. April 10 — no-par with stated value $2 (same mechanics as par)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 450,000 | |
| Common Stock (25,000 × $2 stated value) | | 50,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Stated Value—Common Stock | | 400,000 |
| *Record stated-value no-par common stock issued for cash* | | |

**Check:** Dr 450,000 = Cr 50,000 + 400,000. **Balanced.**

**d. June 15 — noncash issuance (equipment)**

**Measurement basis:** Fair value of the **common stock is more clearly evident** ($19 actively traded) than the equipment; measure the exchange at stock FV.

Transaction amount = 8,000 × $19 = **$152,000**  
Common Stock = 8,000 × $2 = **$16,000**  
PIC excess = $152,000 − $16,000 = **$136,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 152,000 | |
| Common Stock (8,000 × $2) | | 16,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 136,000 |
| *Issue common stock for equipment; measure at FV of stock* | | |

**Check:** Dr 152,000 = Cr 16,000 + 136,000. **Balanced.**

**e. September 1 — cash issuance with stock issue costs**

Gross proceeds = 12,000 × $20 = **$240,000**  
Stock issue costs = **$9,000**  
Net cash = $240,000 − $9,000 = **$231,000**  
Common Stock = 12,000 × $2 = **$24,000**  
PIC excess (gross) = 12,000 × ($20 − $2) = **$216,000**  
PIC excess **after** issue costs = $216,000 − $9,000 = **$207,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash ((12,000 × $20) − $9,000) | 231,000 | |
| Common Stock (12,000 × $2) | | 24,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 207,000 |
| *Net proceeds; issue costs reduce PIC, not expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 231,000 = Cr 24,000 + 207,000. **Balanced.**  
Issue costs are **excluded from net income** (capital transaction per ASC 505-10-25-2).

**f. Subsequent measurement / paid-in capital rollforward schedule (par path)**

Issued common stock is **not** subsequently remeasured to market. The schedule rolls balances after each issuance.

| Date | Event | Shares issued (cum.) | Dr/Cr effect on CS | CS balance | PIC—CS balance | Total paid-in capital |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1 | Authorization only | 0 | — | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Mar 1 | Cash @ $18 | 25,000 | +50,000 | 50,000 | 400,000 | 450,000 |
| Jun 15 | Equipment @ stock FV $19 | 33,000 | +16,000 | 66,000 | 536,000 | 602,000 |
| Sep 1 | Cash @ $20, net of $9,000 issue costs | 45,000 | +24,000 | 90,000 | 743,000 | 833,000 |
| **12/31** | **Ending** | **45,000** | | **$90,000** | **$743,000** | **$833,000** |

**Legal capital** (par of issued shares) = 45,000 × $2 = **$90,000**.  
Shares outstanding = **45,000** (none reacquired).  
Total equity from these issuances = **$833,000** (assets increased by cash $450,000 + $231,000 and equipment $152,000 = $833,000).

**g. Period-end adjusting JE and equity presentation**

**No December 31 adjusting journal entry** is required to remeasure common stock or PIC to fair value. After initial recognition, paid-in capital remains at historical issuance amounts (unless later capital transactions such as treasury stock, retirement, or dividends—outside this LO’s issuance focus).

**Stockholders’ equity — paid-in capital section (December 31, Year 1):**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $2 par value; 500,000 shares authorized; 45,000 shares issued and outstanding | $90,000 |
| Additional paid-in capital (Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock) | 743,000 |
| **Total paid-in capital** | **$833,000** |

**Key insight:** For par stock, **Common Stock** is always credited at **par × shares issued**; the **premium** (and reductions for stock issue costs) flows through **PIC in excess of par**. True no-par puts the entire proceeds in Common Stock. Noncash issuances use the more clearly evident fair value. Issue costs never hit the income statement.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Issuance twin (all numbers changed)
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: par cash, true no-par, stated-value, noncash (asset FV more clearly evident), stock issue costs, rollforward, period-end equity  
**Scenario:**  
**NorthPine Robotics Corp.** authorizes **800,000** shares of **$1 par** common stock on January 1, Year 1 (no JE for authorization).

Independent alternatives and sequential par-path transactions:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Feb 5 | Issues **40,000** shares of **$1 par** common for cash at **$12.50** per share. |
| Feb 5 alt. | Same 40,000 shares as **true no-par** at $12.50 cash. |
| Feb 5 alt. | Same 40,000 shares as **no-par, stated value $1** at $12.50 cash. |
| May 20 | Issues **15,000** shares of **$1 par** common for a parcel of land. Land was recently **appraised at $210,000**. NorthPine stock is thinly traded; stock FV is **not** clearly evident. |
| Aug 12 | Issues **30,000** shares of **$1 par** common for cash at **$15** per share; stock issue costs **$12,000**. |
| Dec 31 | Year-end. Par-path transactions only: Feb 5, May 20, Aug 12. |

**Required:**  
a. Journal entry — Feb 5 **par** cash issuance.  
b. Journal entry — Feb 5 **true no-par**.  
c. Journal entry — Feb 5 **stated value $1**.  
d. Journal entry — May 20 **noncash land** (state measurement basis).  
e. Journal entry — Aug 12 with **issue costs**.  
f. **Rollforward schedule** (par path) of CS, PIC—CS, total paid-in capital, and shares; compute legal capital at year-end.  
g. Prepare **December 31 paid-in capital presentation**. Confirm whether a period-end FV adjusting JE is needed.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Feb 5 — par cash**

Cash = 40,000 × $12.50 = **$500,000**  
CS = 40,000 × $1 = **$40,000**  
PIC = $500,000 − $40,000 = **$460,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 500,000 | |
| Common Stock (40,000 × $1) | | 40,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 460,000 |

**Check:** 500,000 = 40,000 + 460,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Feb 5 — true no-par**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 500,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 500,000 |

**Check:** 500,000 = 500,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Feb 5 — stated value $1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 500,000 | |
| Common Stock (40,000 × $1) | | 40,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Stated Value—Common Stock | | 460,000 |

**Check:** 500,000 = 40,000 + 460,000. **Balanced.**

**d. May 20 — noncash land**

**Measurement basis:** Fair value of the **land is more clearly evident** (recent appraisal $210,000); stock FV not clearly evident. Measure at **$210,000**.

CS = 15,000 × $1 = **$15,000**  
PIC = $210,000 − $15,000 = **$195,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Land | 210,000 | |
| Common Stock (15,000 × $1) | | 15,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 195,000 |
| *Issue common stock for land; measure at FV of land* | | |

**Check:** 210,000 = 15,000 + 195,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Aug 12 — cash with issue costs**

Gross = 30,000 × $15 = **$450,000**  
Issue costs = **$12,000**  
Net cash = **$438,000**  
CS = 30,000 × $1 = **$30,000**  
PIC = 30,000 × ($15 − $1) − $12,000 = $420,000 − $12,000 = **$408,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash ((30,000 × $15) − $12,000) | 438,000 | |
| Common Stock (30,000 × $1) | | 30,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 408,000 |

**Check:** 438,000 = 30,000 + 408,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Rollforward (par path)**

| Date | Event | Cum. shares | CS bal. | PIC—CS bal. | Total paid-in |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Feb 5 | Cash @ $12.50 | 40,000 | $40,000 | $460,000 | $500,000 |
| May 20 | Land @ $210,000 | 55,000 | 55,000 | 655,000 | 710,000 |
| Aug 12 | Cash @ $15 net of $12,000 costs | 85,000 | 85,000 | 1,063,000 | 1,148,000 |
| **12/31** | **Ending** | **85,000** | **$85,000** | **$1,063,000** | **$1,148,000** |

**Legal capital** = 85,000 × $1 = **$85,000**.  
Asset check: cash $500,000 + $438,000 + land $210,000 = **$1,148,000**.

**g. Period-end**

**No** December 31 adjusting entry to remeasure equity to market.

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $1 par; 800,000 shares authorized; 85,000 shares issued and outstanding | $85,000 |
| Additional paid-in capital | 1,063,000 |
| **Total paid-in capital** | **$1,148,000** |

**Key insight:** Changing every input (shares, par, prices, costs, which side has clearer FV) still follows the same template: CS at par/stated (or full proceeds if true no-par); remainder and issue-cost reduction in PIC; noncash at the more reliable FV.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Lump-sum issuance (proportional vs incremental) and classification
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Lump-sum (combined) stock issuance — proportional allocation when both FVs known; incremental allocation when only one FV known; classification of allocated amounts to CS/PS and PIC; settlement of proceeds among classes  
**Scenario:**  
**Bayline Packaging Co.** completes a **combined (lump-sum) offering** on March 1, Year 1, receiving a single cash package for both common and preferred stock. Two independent cases (do not combine).

**Case A — Proportional (both fair values known)**  
Issues **2,000** shares of common stock, **$5 par**, and **1,000** shares of preferred stock, **$10 par**, for a **lump-sum cash price of $95,000**.  
At issuance, common stock sells separately at **$36** per share and preferred stock at **$28** per share.

**Case B — Incremental (only common FV known)**  
Issues **2,000** shares of common stock, **$5 par**, and **1,000** shares of preferred stock, **$10 par**, for a **lump-sum cash price of $100,000**.  
Common stock market price is **$38** per share; preferred stock has **no** reliable market price.

**Required:**  
a. **Case A:** Compute relative fair values, allocation percentages, allocated proceeds to common and preferred, and prepare the **March 1 journal entry**.  
b. **Case B:** Compute incremental allocation and prepare the **March 1 journal entry**.  
c. For Case A only, show how the **equity accounts** would be **classified/presented** immediately after issuance (paid-in capital lines).  
d. Explain in one sentence why stock issue costs (if any had been incurred on the lump-sum offering) would **not** appear as an expense.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Case A — proportional allocation**

| Security | Fair value | Weight |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common (2,000 × $36) | $72,000 | 72,000 / 100,000 = **72%** |
| Preferred (1,000 × $28) | 28,000 | 28,000 / 100,000 = **28%** |
| **Total FV** | **$100,000** | **100%** |

Allocated proceeds of **$95,000**:  
Common = $95,000 × 72% = **$68,400**  
Preferred = $95,000 × 28% = **$26,600**  
Check: $68,400 + $26,600 = **$95,000**.

Common Stock (par) = 2,000 × $5 = **$10,000**  
PIC—Common = $68,400 − $10,000 = **$58,400**  
Preferred Stock (par) = 1,000 × $10 = **$10,000**  
PIC—Preferred = $26,600 − $10,000 = **$16,600**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 95,000 | |
| Common Stock (2,000 × $5) | | 10,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 58,400 |
| Preferred Stock (1,000 × $10) | | 10,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Preferred Stock | | 16,600 |
| *Lump-sum issuance — proportional (relative FV) allocation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 95,000 = Cr 10,000 + 58,400 + 10,000 + 16,600 = 95,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Case B — incremental allocation**

Common allocation (known FV) = 2,000 × $38 = **$76,000**  
Preferred allocation (residual) = $100,000 − $76,000 = **$24,000**

Common Stock = 2,000 × $5 = **$10,000**  
PIC—Common = $76,000 − $10,000 = **$66,000**  
Preferred Stock = 1,000 × $10 = **$10,000**  
PIC—Preferred = $24,000 − $10,000 = **$14,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 100,000 | |
| Common Stock (2,000 × $5) | | 10,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 66,000 |
| Preferred Stock (1,000 × $10) | | 10,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Preferred Stock | | 14,000 |
| *Lump-sum issuance — incremental allocation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 100,000 = Cr 10,000 + 66,000 + 10,000 + 14,000 = 100,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Case A — classification / presentation immediately after issuance**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Preferred stock, $10 par, 1,000 shares issued and outstanding | $10,000 |
| Common stock, $5 par, 2,000 shares issued and outstanding | 10,000 |
| Additional paid-in capital (PIC—PS $16,600 + PIC—CS $58,400) | 75,000 |
| **Total paid-in capital** | **$95,000** |

(Often PIC accounts are aggregated as one “Additional paid-in capital” line on the face of the balance sheet.)

**d. Issue costs and income**  
Stock issue costs are reductions of paid-in capital (capital transactions in the entity’s own stock) and are **excluded from the determination of net income**.

**Key insight:** A lump-sum sale is a single **settlement of cash** that must be **allocated** to each class before applying the usual par/PIC split. Use **proportional** allocation when both market values are known; use **incremental** when only one class has a reliable FV.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Classification of stock issue costs — capital reduction, not expense  
**Question:**  
HarborVue Media Inc. issues 50,000 shares of $1 par common stock for $22 per share cash and pays $18,000 of underwriting and legal costs directly related to the issuance. How should the $18,000 of stock issue costs be accounted for?

- A) Debit Stock Issue Expense (or Selling Expense) $18,000 in the period of issuance  
- B) Capitalize as a deferred charge (asset) and amortize to expense over a reasonable period  
- C) Reduce Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock (net cash and net PIC); no effect on net income  
- D) Reduce Common Stock (par) first, then reduce PIC for any remainder  

**Answer:** **C.** Stock issue costs reduce net proceeds and are treated as a reduction of paid-in capital in excess of par. They are capital transactions and are excluded from net income. Common Stock remains at par × shares issued ($50,000); PIC absorbs both the gross premium and the issue-cost reduction. Options A and B incorrectly expense or defer costs related to equity issuance; D incorrectly reduces the par (legal capital) account for issue costs.

### Q5 — MC (measurement / method)
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Measurement basis for common stock issued for noncash consideration  
**Question:**  
On July 1, a company privately issues 10,000 shares of $2 par common stock in exchange for a patent. The stock is not publicly traded and has no reliable market price. A recent independent appraisal values the patent at $175,000. At what amount should the company credit **total** paid-in capital (Common Stock + PIC) for this issuance?

- A) $20,000 (par only; patent not recorded until amortized)  
- B) $175,000  
- C) $0 until the patent generates cash flows  
- D) An amount assigned arbitrarily by management without using the appraisal  

**Answer:** **B.** Noncash stock issuances are measured at the fair value of the stock **or** the noncash consideration, whichever is more clearly evident. Here the patent appraisal ($175,000) is more clearly evident, so the patent is debited and total equity credited for **$175,000** (CS $20,000 + PIC $155,000). Par alone is incomplete; waiting for cash flows or ignoring the appraisal is not GAAP.
