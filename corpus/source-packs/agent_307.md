# Agent 307 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-7

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Describe receivables disclosures and ratio analyses  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Receivable disclosures (ASC 310-10-50):** accounting policies for trade receivables; major categories; allowance balance; charge-off / past-due / nonaccrual policies; receivables serving as **collateral**; **allowance activity** (beginning, current-period provision, write-offs, recoveries, ending); estimation methodology and judgment factors; significant purchases/sales of receivables; fair value (excluding short-term trade AR due within one year)
- **Concentration of credit risk:** disclose when receivables share common economic risks or are concentrated with a major customer, industry, or region
- **Accounts receivable turnover:** Net sales ÷ Average net accounts receivable (gross AR less allowance)
- **Average days to collect receivables:** 365 ÷ Accounts receivable turnover
- **Initial recognition JEs:** credit sales; cash collections on account
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** Accounts Receivable rollforward; aging-based expected credit loss schedule; Allowance for Doubtful Accounts rollforward used for **note disclosure**
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** Bad Debt Expense / Allowance for Doubtful Accounts to bring the allowance to the net amount expected to be collected (CECL / allowance method)
- **Disposal / settlement JEs:** write-off of specific uncollectibles (Dr AFDA / Cr AR); recovery reinstate-and-collect
- **Balance sheet presentation:** Accounts receivable, **net** of allowance
- **Ratio interpretation:** year-over-year and peer comparison; effect of larger provisions and factoring (sale) on turnover and days
- **Not core for this pack:** fair value option election (Expanding Your Knowledge sidebar only)

### Q1 — CORE — Period-end CECL provision, allowance disclosure, AR ratios (emphasis: period_end_adjusting_JE)

The **period-end adjusting provision** of $45,990 is the amount that appears in the allowance **activity disclosure** and drives net AR used in the ratios.

**Key insight:** LO 8-7 pairs **qualitative disclosures** (policies, allowance activity, concentrations, collateral) with **quantitative ratio analysis** using **net** AR. The **period-end adjusting JE** is the bridge: it sets the ending allowance that appears on the BS, in the allowance rollforward note, and in the denominator of AR turnover.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Peer ratio comparison, period-end provision only, disclosure mapping, BDE/factoring effects
**LO:** LO 8-7

**Scenario:**  
**Summit Trail Outfitters** and **Prairie Forge Tools Inc.** are competitors. Selected information from recent annual reports ($ thousands omitted — treat amounts as whole dollars for computation):

| | Summit Trail | Summit Trail | Prairie Forge | Prairie Forge |

**Additional facts for Summit Trail at December 31, Year 3 (before the final CECL adjusting entry has been posted):**
- Accounts receivable, **gross**: **$875,000**
- Allowance for doubtful accounts, **unadjusted** (credit balance remaining after write-offs and recoveries): **$8,500**
- Management’s aging / CECL analysis indicates the **desired ending allowance** should be **$55,000** so that ending **net AR = $820,000** (matches the comparative table above).
- **$120,000** of Summit’s AR is pledged as collateral for a short-term borrowing.

(e) In 2–4 sentences each:  
  (1) What effect does a **significant increase in bad debt expense** (higher ending allowance, lower net AR, holding sales constant) have on AR turnover and on average days to collect?  
  (2) What effect does **factoring AR accounted for as a sale** (removing AR from the BS) have on AR turnover and on average days to collect?

**Answer key:**

**(a) Ratio computations**

| Company / Year | Net sales | Average net AR | AR turnover | Days to collect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Summit Trail Year 3 | $5,400,000 | \((740{,}000 + 820{,}000)/2 = 780{,}000\) | \(5{,}400{,}000/780{,}000 = \mathbf{6.92}\) | \(365/6.92 = \mathbf{52.75}\) |
| Summit Trail Year 2 | $5,100,000 | \((700{,}000 + 740{,}000)/2 = 720{,}000\) | \(5{,}100{,}000/720{,}000 = \mathbf{7.08}\) | \(365/7.08 = \mathbf{51.55}\) |
| Prairie Forge Year 3 | $9,200,000 | \((1{,}080{,}000 + 1{,}150{,}000)/2 = 1{,}115{,}000\) | \(9{,}200{,}000/1{,}115{,}000 = \mathbf{8.25}\) | \(365/8.25 = \mathbf{44.24}\) |
| Prairie Forge Year 2 | $8,700,000 | \((1{,}020{,}000 + 1{,}080{,}000)/2 = 1{,}050{,}000\) | \(8{,}700{,}000/1{,}050{,}000 = \mathbf{8.29}\) | \(365/8.29 = \mathbf{44.03}\) |

**(b) Comparative analysis**

- **Prairie Forge** collects faster in Year 3 (turnover **8.25** vs Summit’s **6.92**; days **44.24** vs **52.75**).  
- **Summit Trail:** slight **worsening** Year 2 → Year 3 (turnover 7.08 → 6.92; days 51.55 → 52.75).  
- **Prairie Forge:** essentially **stable / tiny decline** (8.29 → 8.25; days 44.03 → 44.24).  
Differences may reflect industry mix, credit terms, customer composition of receivables, or collection practices—not only “better” or “worse” management without further context.

**(c) Period-end adjusting JE — Summit Trail (emphasis)**

Provision = Desired ending AFDA − Unadjusted AFDA  
= \(55{,}000 - 8{,}500 = \mathbf{\$46{,}500}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bad Debt Expense | 46,500 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 46,500 |
| *Year-end provision to CECL estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 46,500 = Cr 46,500. **Balanced.**  
Ending AFDA = \(8{,}500 + 46{,}500 = \mathbf{\$55{,}000}\).  
Net AR = \(875{,}000 - 55{,}000 = \mathbf{\$820{,}000}\). **Matches** the Year 3 comparative table used in part (a).

**(d) Disclosure mapping — Summit Trail examples**

1. **Accounting policy** for trade receivables and for estimating the **allowance** (methodology, judgments, forecasts).  
2. **Allowance balance** and **activity** (beg, provision **$46,500**, write-offs, recoveries, end **$55,000**).  
3. **Charge-off / past-due / nonaccrual** policies.  
4. **Collateral:** **$120,000** of AR pledged for short-term borrowing.  
5. **Concentration of credit risk:** **35%** of ending gross AR with two outdoor-retail chains (common customer-channel risk).  
6. Major categories / significant purchases or sales of receivables if applicable.

**(e) Effects on ratios**

**(1) Significant increase in bad debt expense:**  
A larger provision **increases** the allowance and **decreases net AR**. Holding net sales constant, **average net AR falls**, so **AR turnover rises** and **average days to collect falls**. The ratios can look “stronger” even though credit quality worsened—users must read the **allowance activity** and bad debt expense disclosures, not turnover alone.

**(2) Factoring AR as a sale:**  
Sale accounting **removes** (or reduces) AR from the balance sheet. Average net AR declines; if sales remain on the income statement, **turnover increases** and **days to collect decrease**. The improvement may reflect **off-balance-sheet financing / accelerated cash**, not organic collection efficiency—hence disclosure of **significant sales of receivables**.

**Key insight:** Peer ratio tables (Demo 8-7 style) are pure LO 8-7 core work. The **period-end adjusting provision** is what makes the **net AR** and **allowance activity note** consistent. Ratio “improvements” from heavier provisions or factoring sales require careful interpretation using the disclosures.

### Q4 — MC — Concentration of credit risk disclosure
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Identify when a concentration of credit risk disclosure is required for receivables  
**Question:**  
Which of the following situations **most clearly** requires disclosure of a **concentration of credit risk** related to receivables?

- A) A company holds thousands of small consumer receivables diversified across many regions and industries, none individually material.  
- B) Approximately **40%** of a manufacturer’s trade receivables are from customers in the **same agricultural region**, so a drought or commodity price shock could impair many balances at once.  
- C) All trade receivables are due within 30 days and are therefore exempt from **all** footnote disclosures under ASC 310.  
- D) The company elects the fair value option for one long-term note receivable and records an unrealized gain in income.

**Answer:** **B.**  
A concentration exists when receivables from different counterparties share **common economic risks** (here, the same agricultural region) or when balances are concentrated with major customers. Diversified small balances (A) typically do **not** create a reportable concentration. Short-term maturity (C) does **not** eliminate ASC 310 disclosure requirements (policies, allowance activity, collateral, concentrations, etc.). Fair value option (D) is an Expanding Your Knowledge measurement election, not the concentration disclosure rule.

### Q5 — MC — AR turnover formula and net AR
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Correct computation of accounts receivable turnover and average days to collect  
**Question:**  
Net credit sales for the year are **$900,000**. Net accounts receivable were **$100,000** at the beginning of the year and **$140,000** at year-end. Which amounts are correct?

- A) Turnover **9.00**; average days to collect **40.56**  
- B) Turnover **7.50**; average days to collect **48.67**  
- C) Turnover **6.43**; average days to collect **56.77**  
- D) Turnover **9.00**; average days to collect **45.63** using year-end AR only  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Average net AR = \((100{,}000 + 140{,}000)/2 = 120{,}000\).
