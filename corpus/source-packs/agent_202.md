# Agent 202 — CORE DEMO — LO 11-6

**Chapter:** 11  
**LO title:** Account for property, plant, and equipment related costs after acquisition  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Ordinary repairs / maintenance:** expense in the period incurred (preserve original service potential only)
- **Improvements / replacements — substitution:** remove old component (debit AD and loss for old BV; credit asset cost), then capitalize new component
- **Improvements / replacements — capitalization:** add new cost to the asset when old component is significantly depreciated or old cost is not tracked as a separate component for removal
- **Improvements — reduction of accumulated depreciation:** when expenditure extends useful life without improving quality/productivity and old component cost is unknown
- **Additions:** capitalize extensions/enlargements; depreciate over the **shorter** of the addition’s own life or remaining life of the host asset
- **Rearrangements:** capitalize if material and future periods benefit; depreciate over periods benefited
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** revise gross cost, AD, book value, residual, and remaining life after capitalized post-acquisition costs; roll SL depreciation and ending BV forward period by period
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** depreciation expense on revised depreciable base
- **Component disposal JE (substitution):** derecognize replaced major component (loss = old book value)
- Do **not** accrue planned major maintenance in advance (no obligation until work begins)

### Q1 — CORE — Equipment life cycle: ordinary repair, engine substitution, full subsequent SL schedule
**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** Subsequent PPE costs (expense vs substitution capitalization); revised BV; multi-year straight-line subsequent measurement schedule; period-end depreciation; component disposal  
**Scenario:**  
**Trailhead Canister Co.** (calendar year-end) purchased specialized filling equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$240,000** cash. Estimated residual value **$20,000**; useful life **8 years**; straight-line depreciation. Depreciation is recorded each **December 31** and has been recorded correctly through **December 31, Year 3**.

On **January 1, Year 4**, Trailhead incurs the following **cash** costs related to this equipment:

1. **Ordinary maintenance** (cleaning, lubrication, minor parts): **$8,400**.  
2. **Major drive-motor replacement** treated under the **substitution method**: original motor cost **$48,000** with related accumulated depreciation equal to **75%** of that cost; new motor cost **$65,000**.  
3. Immediately after the motor replacement, management revises the **remaining useful life of the entire equipment unit** to **6 years** from January 1, Year 4. Residual value remains **$20,000**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE** for the equipment purchase.  
b. Compute equipment cost, accumulated depreciation, and book value **immediately before** the January 1, Year 4 subsequent-cost entries. Show annual depreciation for Years 1–3.  
c. Prepare all **January 1, Year 4 journal entries** for items 1–2 (maintenance and motor substitution, including removal of the old motor).  
d. Compute **revised** equipment cost, AD, and book value **after** the substitution. Compute annual SL depreciation for Year 4 onward.  
e. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for **Years 4 through 9** (full remaining life) showing beginning BV, depreciation expense, ending AD, and ending BV.  
f. Prepare the **December 31, Year 4** period-end adjusting JE for depreciation.

**Answer key:**

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 240,000 | |
| Cash | | 240,000 |
| *Acquire filling equipment at historical cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 240,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Pre-subsequent balances (Jan 1, Year 4, before entries)**

Annual depreciation Years 1–3 = \((\$240{,}000 - \$20{,}000) / 8 = \$27{,}500\) per year  
AD after 3 years = \(3 \times \$27{,}500 = \$82{,}500\)  
Book value = \(\$240{,}000 - \$82{,}500 = \$157{,}500\)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment cost | $240,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation | 82,500 |
| **Book value** | **$157,500** |

**c. January 1, Year 4 subsequent-cost JEs**

**(1) Ordinary repair — expense**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense | 8,400 | |
| Cash | | 8,400 |
| *Ordinary maintenance; keeps asset at original service potential* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 8,400. **Balanced.**

**(2) Substitution — remove old motor (component disposal)**  
Old motor AD = \(\$48{,}000 \times 75\% = \$36{,}000\)  
Old motor BV (loss) = \(\$48{,}000 - \$36{,}000 = \$12{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 36,000 | |
| Loss on Asset Improvement | 12,000 | |
| Equipment | | 48,000 |
| *Derecognize replaced motor component (loss = old BV)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 36,000 + 12,000 = 48,000 = Cr. **Balanced.**

**(2 continued) Capitalize new motor**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 65,000 | |
| Cash | | 65,000 |
| *Capitalize replacement motor* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 65,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Revised balances and new annual depreciation**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment cost: \(240{,}000 - 48{,}000 + 65{,}000\) | **$257,000** |
| AD: \(82{,}500 - 36{,}000\) | **$46,500** |
| Book value | **$210,500** |
| Residual | 20,000 |
| Depreciable base | **$190,500** |
| Remaining life | 6 years |
| **Annual SL depreciation (Year 4+)** | **$31,750** |

Computation: \(\$190{,}500 / 6 = \$31{,}750\).

**e. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — Years 4–9**

| Year | Beg. BV | Depreciation expense | Ending AD | Ending BV |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | $210,500 | $31,750 | $78,250 | $178,750 |
| 5 | 178,750 | 31,750 | 110,000 | 147,000 |
| 6 | 147,000 | 31,750 | 141,750 | 115,250 |
| 7 | 115,250 | 31,750 | 173,500 | 83,500 |
| 8 | 83,500 | 31,750 | 205,250 | 51,750 |
| 9 | 51,750 | 31,750 | 237,000 | **20,000** |
| **Totals** | | **$190,500** | | |

Ending AD = post-substitution AD \(\$46{,}500\) + cumulative depreciation after substitution.  
Ending BV = Cost \(\$257{,}000\) − Ending AD.  
**Schedule checks:**  
- Total depreciation Years 4–9 = \(6 \times 31{,}750 = 190{,}500\) = revised depreciable base  
- Ending BV at end of Year 9 = residual **$20,000**  
- Year 4 rollforward: \(210{,}500 - 31{,}750 = 178{,}750\)  
- Year 9 AD: \(46{,}500 + 190{,}500 = 237{,}000\); BV \(257{,}000 - 237{,}000 = 20{,}000\)

**f. December 31, Year 4 period-end adjusting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 31,750 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 31,750 |
| *Period-end SL depreciation on revised post-improvement base* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 31,750. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Ordinary repairs never enter the asset base. Under **substitution**, the old component is **disposed of** (loss = its book value) and the new component is capitalized, so both **gross cost and AD change**. All later periods use a **revised BV ÷ remaining life** schedule that must roll to residual—exam work often fails by keeping the pre-improvement annual depreciation.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Twin: repair, substitution, full remaining-life SL schedule
**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin of subsequent PPE costs with multi-year subsequent measurement schedule after component substitution  
**Scenario:**  
**Oakridge Precision Tools Inc.** (calendar year-end) purchased CNC equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$300,000** cash. Residual value **$30,000**; useful life **10 years**; straight-line. Depreciation recorded correctly through **December 31, Year 4**.

On **January 1, Year 5**, Oakridge pays cash for:

1. Ordinary maintenance: **$11,200**.  
2. Major spindle-assembly replacement (**substitution**): original spindle cost **$50,000**, **80%** depreciated; new spindle cost **$72,000**.  
3. Remaining useful life of the **entire unit** is revised to **7 years** from January 1, Year 5. Residual remains **$30,000**.

**Required:**  
a. January 1, Year 1 purchase JE.  
b. Cost, AD, and BV immediately before January 1, Year 5 subsequent entries.  
c. All January 1, Year 5 JEs (maintenance + substitution).  
d. Revised cost, AD, BV, and annual SL depreciation Year 5+.  
e. Subsequent measurement schedule for **Years 5–11** (full remaining life): beg. BV, dep. expense, end. AD, end. BV.  
f. December 31, Year 5 depreciation adjusting JE.

**Answer key:**

**a. January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 300,000 | |
| Cash | | 300,000 |

**Check:** 300,000 = 300,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Pre-subsequent balances**  
Annual dep Years 1–4 = \((\$300{,}000 - \$30{,}000) / 10 = \$27{,}000\)  
AD = \(4 \times \$27{,}000 = \$108{,}000\)  
BV = \(\$300{,}000 - \$108{,}000 = \$192{,}000\)

**c. January 1, Year 5 JEs**

Maintenance:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense | 11,200 | |
| Cash | | 11,200 |

**Check:** 11,200 = 11,200.

Remove old spindle:  
AD = \(\$50{,}000 \times 80\% = \$40{,}000\); BV/loss = \(\$10{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 40,000 | |
| Loss on Asset Improvement | 10,000 | |
| Equipment | | 50,000 |

**Check:** 40,000 + 10,000 = 50,000.

Capitalize new spindle:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 72,000 | |
| Cash | | 72,000 |

**Check:** 72,000 = 72,000.

**d. Revised balances and depreciation**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment cost: \(300{,}000 - 50{,}000 + 72{,}000\) | **$322,000** |
| AD: \(108{,}000 - 40{,}000\) | **$68,000** |
| Book value | **$254,000** |
| Residual | 30,000 |
| Depreciable base | **$224,000** |
| Remaining life | 7 years |
| **Annual SL depreciation (Year 5+)** | **$32,000** |

\(\$224{,}000 / 7 = \$32{,}000\).

**e. Subsequent measurement schedule (Years 5–11)**

| Year | Beg. BV | Depreciation expense | Ending AD | Ending BV |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | $254,000 | $32,000 | $100,000 | $222,000 |
| 6 | 222,000 | 32,000 | 132,000 | 190,000 |
| 7 | 190,000 | 32,000 | 164,000 | 158,000 |
| 8 | 158,000 | 32,000 | 196,000 | 126,000 |
| 9 | 126,000 | 32,000 | 228,000 | 94,000 |
| 10 | 94,000 | 32,000 | 260,000 | 62,000 |
| 11 | 62,000 | 32,000 | 292,000 | **30,000** |
| **Totals** | | **$224,000** | | |

**Schedule checks:**  
- \(7 \times 32{,}000 = 224{,}000\) (full revised depreciable base)  
- End Year 11 AD = \(68{,}000 + 224{,}000 = 292{,}000\); BV = \(322{,}000 - 292{,}000 = 30{,}000\) residual  
- Year 5: \(254{,}000 - 32{,}000 = 222{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 32,000 | |

**Key insight:** Changing dollars and lives scales the loss on component removal and the post-improvement annual depreciation, but the **pattern** is fixed: expense ordinary repairs; remove old BV under substitution; capitalize the new component; then **rebuild** the multi-period SL schedule from revised BV to residual.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Building subsequent costs (repairs, addition, roof substitution, AD reduction) + addition schedule
**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** Classify and record post-acquisition building costs; substitution disposal of old roof; reduce AD for life extension; addition life = shorter of two lives; multi-year subsequent measurement of the addition; period-end dep JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Fairhaven Dairy Processing** owns a plant building. At **January 1, Year 6**, the building’s original cost is **$2,100,000** and accumulated depreciation is **$900,000**. Remaining useful life of the original building is **15 years**. Residual value assigned to the **building structure as a whole** for depreciation of the original building after Year 5 improvements is ignored in this problem (treat residual of the **addition only** as **$0**). During Year 6, Fairhaven paid **cash** for:

| Item | Description | Cash cost |
|---|---|---:|
| a | Ordinary repairs and routine maintenance (painting, lubrication, minor parts) | $52,000 |
| b | Completed a **cold-storage wing addition**. Estimated useful life of the **addition** is **9 years**. Remaining useful life of the original building is **15 years**. Residual of addition **$0**. Full year of depreciation in Year 6. | 540,000 |
| c | Removed original roof (**cost $210,000**, **70%** depreciated) and replaced it with a solar-enhanced roof | 320,000 |

| 8 | 420,000 | 60,000 | 360,000 |

**Rollforward check:** \(540{,}000 - 3 \times 60{,}000 = 360{,}000\). Over full 9-year life, carrying amount rolls to **$0**.

**e. Classification summary**

| Item | Treatment | Method / note |
|---|---|---|
| a | Expense immediately | Ordinary repair / maintenance |
| b | Capitalize | Addition; depreciate over **shorter** life (9 yrs) |
| c | Capitalize after removing old | **Substitution** (old carrying amount known) |
| d | Capitalize via AD reduction | **Reduce AD** (life extended; old cost unknown) |
| e | Capitalize | Rearrangement with multi-period benefit |

**f. Rearrangement annual depreciation**  
\(\$75{,}000 / 5 = \$15{,}000\) per year.

**Key insight:** Post-acquisition costs that only **maintain** original service potential are expensed. Costs that **add capacity** (addition), **replace a major component** (substitution), **extend life** without a quality upgrade when old cost is unknown (reduce AD), or **rearrange** for multi-period benefit are capitalized. The addition’s depreciation life is the **shorter** of its own life and the host’s remaining life, and that amount feeds a clean multi-year carrying-amount schedule.

### Q4 — MC — Method choice and capitalize vs expense
**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** Classify subsequent expenditures and choose substitution vs reduce-AD vs pure capitalization  

**Question 1:**  
On March 1, a company paid **$275,000** cash to replace a building’s elevators (a major component). The old elevators cost **$160,000** and were **75%** depreciated. Old carrying amounts are known. Which approach is **most appropriate**?

- A) Debit Repair Expense $275,000; credit Cash $275,000 only  
- B) Debit Building $275,000; credit Cash $275,000 only (leave old elevators on the books)  
- C) Remove the old elevators (debit Accumulated Depreciation $120,000, debit Loss $40,000, credit Building $160,000) and debit Building $275,000; credit Cash $275,000  
- D) Debit Accumulated Depreciation $275,000; credit Cash $275,000 only  

**Answer:** C.  
Old AD = \(\$160{,}000 \times 75\% = \$120{,}000\); BV = \(\$40{,}000\) loss. **Substitution** removes the old component and capitalizes the new elevators when old carrying amounts are known. A is ordinary-repair treatment (wrong for a major replacement). B is pure capitalization without removing the old component (used when the old asset is significantly depreciated / not tracked—not these facts). D is the **reduction-of-AD** method for life extension when quality is not improved and often when old cost is unknown—not substitution with known BV.

**Question 2:**  
Which post-acquisition expenditure should be **expensed** in the period incurred (not capitalized as a plant asset)?

- A) Material factory rearrangement expected to benefit production for five years  
- B) Construction of an additional loading dock attached to an existing warehouse  
- C) Annual filter changes, oiling, and minor belt replacements that keep machines at original efficiency  
- D) Installation of a high-capacity line section that increases output quality above original estimates  

**Answer:** C.  
Ordinary repairs and maintenance that preserve original service potential are expensed. A, B, and D increase future service potential or capacity and are capitalized (then depreciated over periods benefited).
