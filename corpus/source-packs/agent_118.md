# Agent 118 — CORE DEMO — LO 20-4

**Chapter:** 20  
**LO title:** Compute earnings per share (EPS) with a simple capital structure  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Basic EPS formula:** net income available to common stockholders ÷ weighted-average common shares outstanding
- **Simple capital structure:** equity consists only of common stock; no instruments that could convert into common and dilute EPS
- **Initial recognition JE:** issuance of common stock that establishes shares outstanding for the EPS denominator
- **Subsequent measurement / WA schedule:** shares outstanding for the full year (constant shares) → weighted-average common shares
- **Period-end adjusting / closing JE:** close net income to retained earnings; EPS is a **presentation** measure (no separate “EPS” ledger account)
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** cash common dividends declare-and-pay (settlement of distributions) — **no** reduction of net income available to common for basic EPS
- **Net loss per share:** net loss ÷ weighted-average common shares (loss per share)
- **Classification / presentation:** simple capital structure reports **basic EPS only** on the face of the income statement (single EPS presentation)

### Q1 — CORE — Basic EPS with simple capital structure (initial stock issuance emphasis)
**LO:** LO 20-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for common stock issuance; weighted-average shares schedule (shares constant all year); basic EPS computation; period-end closing of net income; income-statement presentation of basic EPS  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverstone Fabrication Co.** is a calendar-year public company with a **simple capital structure** (only common stock outstanding; no preferred stock, convertible debt, options, warrants, or other potentially dilutive instruments).

On **January 1, Year 1**, Riverstone issues **250,000 shares** of **$2 par** common stock for **$15** cash per share. Those shares remain outstanding **the entire year**. Net income for Year 1 is **$875,000**. There are no share issuances, buybacks, stock dividends, or stock splits during the year. No noncontrolling interest exists.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition journal entry** for the common stock issuance. Show Dr = Cr.  
b. Prepare a **weighted-average common shares outstanding schedule** for Year 1.  
c. Compute **basic earnings per share** for Year 1. Show the numerator, denominator, and per-share amount.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end closing entry** that transfers net income to retained earnings (use Income Summary).  
e. Show how **net income** and **basic earnings per share** appear on the face of the Year 1 income statement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition of common stock issuance**

Cash proceeds = 250,000 × $15 = **$3,750,000**  
Common stock (par) = 250,000 × $2 = **$500,000**  
APIC—Common = $3,750,000 − $500,000 = **$3,250,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 3,750,000 | |
| Common Stock ($2 par) | | 500,000 |
| Additional Paid-in Capital—Common | | 3,250,000 |
| *Issue 250,000 shares of $2 par common at $15* | | |

**Check:** Dr = **3,750,000**; Cr = 500,000 + 3,250,000 = **3,750,000**. **Balanced.**

**b. Weighted-average common shares outstanding schedule (Year 1)**

| Period outstanding | Shares | Weight (months/12) | Weighted shares |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1 – Dec 31 (entire year) | 250,000 | 12/12 = 1.00 | 250,000 |
| **Weighted-average common shares outstanding** | | | **250,000** |

Because no shares were issued or reacquired during the year, weighted-average common shares equal shares outstanding all year.

**c. Basic EPS — Year 1**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net income available to common stockholders (numerator) | $875,000 |
| Weighted-average common shares outstanding (denominator) | 250,000 |
| **Basic EPS** | **$3.50** |

Computation:  
\[
\text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\$875{,}000}{250{,}000} = \mathbf{\$3.50}
\]

**d. December 31, Year 1 — period-end closing of net income**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Summary | 875,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | | 875,000 |
| *Close Year 1 net income to retained earnings* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **875,000**. **Balanced.**

*Note:* Basic EPS is computed from net income and weighted-average shares for **presentation**; there is no journal entry that “books” EPS itself.

**e. Financial statement presentation (simple capital structure)**

**Income Statement (partial) — Year ended December 31, Year 1**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Net income | $875,000 |
| Basic earnings per share | **$3.50** |

Only **basic** EPS is presented (single EPS amount) because the capital structure is **simple**.

**Key insight:** Under LO 20-4, **basic EPS** = net income available to common ÷ weighted-average common shares. When only common stock is outstanding all year and no dilutive instruments exist, the company has a **simple capital structure** and reports a **single** basic EPS figure on the face of the income statement. The stock-issuance JE sets the share count that feeds the denominator; closing NI to RE does not change the EPS formula.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Basic EPS simple capital structure twin
**LO:** LO 20-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: common stock issuance JE, constant-share weighted-average schedule, basic EPS, closing entry, and simple-structure presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Maple Harbor Packaging Inc.** has a **simple capital structure** (common stock only; no convertible or option-type instruments).

On **January 1, Year 1**, Maple Harbor issues **480,000 shares** of **$1 par** common stock for **$8** cash per share. All 480,000 shares remain outstanding **the entire year**. Net income for Year 1 is **$1,440,000**. No share activity occurs during the year. No noncontrolling interest.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1** common stock **initial recognition** journal entry.  
b. Prepare the **weighted-average common shares** schedule for Year 1.  
c. Compute **basic EPS** for Year 1.  
d. Prepare the **December 31** closing entry transferring net income to retained earnings.  
e. Present **net income** and **basic EPS** as they would appear on the Year 1 income statement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

Cash = 480,000 × $8 = **$3,840,000**  
Common stock (par) = 480,000 × $1 = **$480,000**  
APIC—Common = $3,840,000 − $480,000 = **$3,360,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 3,840,000 | |
| Common Stock ($1 par) | | 480,000 |
| Additional Paid-in Capital—Common | | 3,360,000 |
| *Issue 480,000 shares of $1 par common at $8* | | |

**Check:** Dr = **3,840,000**; Cr = 480,000 + 3,360,000 = **3,840,000**. **Balanced.**

**b. Weighted-average common shares schedule**

| Period outstanding | Shares | Weight | Weighted shares |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1 – Dec 31 | 480,000 | 12/12 | 480,000 |
| **Weighted-average common shares** | | | **480,000** |

**c. Basic EPS**

\[
\text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\$1{,}440{,}000}{480{,}000} = \mathbf{\$3.00}
\]

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net income available to common | $1,440,000 |
| Weighted-average common shares | 480,000 |
| **Basic EPS** | **$3.00** |

**d. December 31 — close net income**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Summary | 1,440,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | | 1,440,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **1,440,000**. **Balanced.**

**e. Income statement presentation (simple capital structure)**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Net income | $1,440,000 |
| Basic earnings per share | **$3.00** |

**Key insight:** Same LO 20-4 core path as Q1 with fully changed company, par, issue price, share count, and net income. When shares are constant all year, the weighted-average denominator equals year-end (and year-start) shares outstanding.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Net loss per share; common dividend settlement; classification/presentation
**LO:** LO 20-4  
**Concept:** Basic EPS when the company has a net loss; cash common dividend declaration and payment JEs (settlement) do not change net income available to common; simple capital structure presentation of loss per share  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakeside Components LLC** is a calendar-year public company with a **simple capital structure**. Throughout Year 1 it has **120,000 shares** of common stock outstanding (issued in a prior year; no Year 1 share issuances or buybacks). There are **no** preferred shares and **no** potentially dilutive securities.

For Year 1, Lakeside reports a **net loss of $180,000**. On **December 15, Year 1**, the board declares a **cash dividend of $0.20 per common share**, payable **December 28, Year 1**. The dividend is paid on the payment date.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **December 15 declaration** and **December 28 payment** journal entries for the common cash dividend. Show Dr = Cr on each entry.  
b. Compute **basic earnings (loss) per share** for Year 1. Explicitly state the numerator used and whether the common cash dividend affects the numerator.  
c. Prepare a brief **EPS computation schedule** (numerator, denominator, per-share).  
d. Show the **income statement presentation** of the net loss and basic EPS for Year 1.  
e. Explain, in one or two sentences, why Lakeside reports **only basic EPS** (not diluted EPS) on the face of the income statement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Common cash dividend — declaration and settlement**

Shares outstanding = **120,000**  
Cash dividend = 120,000 × $0.20 = **$24,000**

*December 15 — declaration*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (or Dividends Declared) | 24,000 | |
| Dividends Payable | | 24,000 |
| *Declare $0.20 cash dividend on 120,000 common shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **24,000**. **Balanced.**

*December 28 — payment (settlement)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Dividends Payable | 24,000 | |
| Cash | | 24,000 |
| *Pay common cash dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **24,000**. **Balanced.**

**b. Numerator for basic EPS (net loss case)**

Net loss for the year = **$(180,000)**  
Common cash dividends **do not** change net income available to common for EPS. EPS measures earnings (or loss) **per common share**; distributing a portion of retained earnings as a common dividend does not increase the loss attributable to common shares for the EPS calculation.

Numerator = **$(180,000)**  
Denominator (shares outstanding all year) = **120,000**

\[
\text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\$(180{,}000)}{120{,}000} = \mathbf{\$(1.50)}
\]

**c. EPS computation schedule**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net income (loss) available to common stockholders | $(180,000) |
| Weighted-average common shares outstanding | 120,000 |
| **Basic earnings (loss) per share** | **$(1.50)** |

**d. Income statement presentation**

**Income Statement (partial) — Year ended December 31, Year 1**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Net loss | $(180,000) |
| Basic earnings (loss) per share | **$(1.50)** |

**e. Classification / presentation reason**  
Lakeside has a **simple capital structure** (only common stock; no instruments that could convert into common and dilute EPS). Therefore a **single** EPS presentation—**basic** EPS (here, a loss per share)—is appropriate on the face of the income statement; diluted EPS is not required.

**Key insight:** A **net loss** is still divided by weighted-average common shares to produce a **loss per share**. **Common** cash dividends reduce retained earnings when declared but **do not** adjust the EPS numerator. (Preferred dividends would reduce income available to common—that refinement is developed further with other capital-structure complications; this item stays on the LO 20-4 simple-structure path.)

### Q4 — MC — Simple capital structure EPS presentation
**LO:** LO 20-4  
**Concept:** Classification of which EPS amount(s) appear on the face of the income statement when the capital structure is simple  
**Question:**  
**Oakridge Tools Inc.** has only common stock outstanding for the entire year. It has no convertible debt, convertible preferred stock, stock options, warrants, or other potentially dilutive instruments. Net income is $500,000 and weighted-average common shares outstanding are 100,000. Which earnings-per-share presentation is appropriate on the face of Oakridge’s income statement?

- A) Basic EPS of $5.00 only  
- B) Diluted EPS of $5.00 only  
- C) Both basic EPS of $5.00 and diluted EPS of $5.00 with equal prominence  
- D) No EPS disclosure is required because the company has no dilutive securities  

**Answer:** **A.** With a **simple capital structure**, the company presents **basic EPS only** (single EPS presentation) on the face of the income statement. Basic EPS = $500,000 ÷ 100,000 = **$5.00**. Diluted EPS is required when the structure is complex (potential common shares exist), not merely when basic and diluted would equal the same number by coincidence of having no dilutives. Absence of dilutives means diluted is not the required presentation form for a simple structure under the LO 20-4 core path.

### Q5 — MC — Basic EPS formula (numerator and denominator)
**LO:** LO 20-4  
**Concept:** Classification of the correct elementary basic EPS computation for a simple capital structure  
**Question:**  
**Pine Valley Outfitters Corp.** has a simple capital structure. Common shares outstanding for the entire year: **200,000**. Net income: **$640,000**. No preferred stock. What is basic earnings per share for the year, and which description of the computation is correct?

- A) Basic EPS = $3.20; net income ÷ weighted-average common shares outstanding  
- B) Basic EPS = $3.20; net income ÷ year-end common shares only if shares changed mid-year  
- C) Basic EPS = $6.40; net income ÷ (weighted-average common shares × ½)  
- D) Basic EPS = $3.20; cash dividends declared on common ÷ weighted-average common shares  

**Answer:** **A.**  
\[
\text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\$640{,}000}{200{,}000} = \mathbf{\$3.20}
\]  
Elementary basic EPS divides **net income available to common stockholders** by **weighted-average common shares outstanding**. With shares constant all year, the weighted average equals 200,000. Common cash dividends are not the numerator; there is no “half-year” factor when shares were outstanding the entire year.
