# Agent 108 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-3

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Account for reacquisition of common stock  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition (cost method):** debit Treasury Stock at acquisition cost; credit Cash; TS is contra equity, not an asset
- **Share status:** treasury shares remain **issued** but are not **outstanding**; direct retirement reclassifies shares to **unissued**
- **Reissuance above cost:** credit Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock for the excess of proceeds over cost
- **Reissuance below cost:** debit PIC—Treasury Stock only to the extent of its prior credit balance; any excess debit hits Retained Earnings (never a “gain/loss” on the income statement under the cost method core path)
- **Subsequent retirement of treasury stock:** remove Common Stock and original APIC at issue amounts; remove Treasury Stock at cost; balance with PIC—TS / PIC—Retired Stock / RE as needed
- **Direct retirement:** remove capital accounts at original issue amounts and credit Cash; excess paid → RE (or PIC—Retired Stock if credit balance exists); excess original issue over cash → credit PIC—Retired Stock
- **Multi-lot cost layers:** FIFO / LIFO / weighted average may be used to assign cost when reissuing or retiring treasury shares purchased at different prices
- **Presentation:** equity section shows CS (issued), APIC, RE, less Treasury Stock at cost; disclose authorized / issued / outstanding / treasury share counts

### Q1 — CORE — Treasury stock cost method: purchase, reissue above/below, presentation, retirement
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of treasury stock at cost; reissuance above and below cost; year-end equity presentation; subsequent retirement  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Harbor Corp.** has **50,000** shares of **$2** par common stock authorized. On January 1, Meridian issued **20,000** shares for cash at **$35** per share. Retained earnings before any treasury-related charges was **$180,000**. All cash transactions.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| June 1 | Reacquired **3,000** shares for the treasury at **$40** per share. |
| Sept 15 | Reissued **800** treasury shares at **$45** per share. |
| Nov 20 | Reissued **700** treasury shares at **$32** per share. |
| Dec 31 | Prepare the stockholders’ equity section (after all TS effects). |
| Mar 1 (next year) | Formally **retired** the remaining treasury shares (returned to unissued). |

**Required:**  
a. Journal entry to **initially recognize** the June 1 treasury purchase (cost method).  
b. Journal entry for the September 15 reissuance (sales price > cost).  
c. Journal entry for the November 20 reissuance (sales price < cost).  
d. Stockholders’ equity section at December 31 (show share counts and Treasury Stock deduction).  
e. Journal entry on March 1 of the next year to **retire** the remaining treasury shares.  
f. After retirement, state authorized, issued, outstanding, and treasury shares.

**Answer key:**  

**a. June 1 — initial recognition of treasury stock (cost method)**  
Cost = 3,000 × $40 = **$120,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 120,000 | |
| Cash | | 120,000 |
| *To reacquire 3,000 shares at cost for the treasury* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 120,000. Assets ↓ and equity ↓ by the same amount. TS is **contra equity**, not an asset.

**b. September 15 — reissue above cost**  
Cash = 800 × $45 = **$36,000**  
Treasury Stock removed = 800 × $40 = **$32,000**  
Excess = **$4,000** → Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 36,000 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 32,000 |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | | 4,000 |
| *Reissue 800 treasury shares above cost* | | |

**Check:** 36,000 = 32,000 + 4,000.

**c. November 20 — reissue below cost**  
Cash = 700 × $32 = **$22,400**  
Treasury Stock removed = 700 × $40 = **$28,000**  
Shortfall = **$5,600**  
PIC—Treasury Stock credit balance available = **$4,000** (from Sept 15)  
Debit PIC—TS **$4,000**; remaining **$1,600** → Retained Earnings

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 22,400 | |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | 4,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | 1,600 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 28,000 |
| *Reissue 700 treasury shares below cost; RE absorbs excess after PIC—TS exhausted* | | |

**Check:** 22,400 + 4,000 + 1,600 = 28,000.

**d. December 31 — equity presentation**  
Remaining treasury shares = 3,000 − 800 − 700 = **1,500**  
Treasury Stock balance = 1,500 × $40 = **$60,000**  
Common stock (issued) = 20,000 × $2 = **$40,000**  
PIC in excess of par = 20,000 × ($35 − $2) = **$660,000**  
RE = $180,000 − $1,600 = **$178,400**  
Outstanding = 20,000 − 1,500 = **18,500**

| Stockholders’ Equity | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $2 par, 50,000 shares authorized, 20,000 issued, 18,500 outstanding | $40,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common | 660,000 |
| Retained earnings | 178,400 |
| Less: Treasury stock, 1,500 shares (at cost) | (60,000) |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$818,400** |

**Check:** 40,000 + 660,000 + 178,400 − 60,000 = 818,400.

**e. March 1 next year — subsequent retirement of remaining treasury stock**  
Remove capital at **original issue** amounts; remove TS at **cost**.  
Common Stock = 1,500 × $2 = **$3,000**  
PIC—CS = 1,500 × $33 = **$49,500**  
Original issue total = **$52,500**  
Treasury Stock cost = **$60,000**  
Difference = **$7,500** → Retained Earnings (no remaining PIC—TS)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 3,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 49,500 | |
| Retained Earnings | 7,500 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 60,000 |
| *Retire 1,500 treasury shares; excess of cost over original issue to RE* | | |

**Check:** 3,000 + 49,500 + 7,500 = 60,000. Net equity unchanged by the retirement itself (TS contra removed as capital accounts removed); share status changes.

**f. Share counts after retirement**  
Authorized **50,000**; issued **18,500**; outstanding **18,500**; treasury **0** (retired shares return to unissued).

**Key insight:** Under the **cost method**, treasury purchases are recorded at cost as a **contra equity** account. Reissuance above cost builds PIC—Treasury Stock; reissuance below cost first uses that PIC, then **Retained Earnings**—never an income-statement gain or loss. Formal retirement removes original capital accounts and the TS balance.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Treasury stock full path with different facts
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — cost-method purchase, above- and below-cost reissuance, equity presentation, subsequent retirement  
**Scenario:**  
**Oakridge Ventures Inc.** has **40,000** shares of **$1** par common authorized. On January 1 it issued **12,000** shares at **$42** cash per share. Retained earnings before treasury-related charges: **$95,000**.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| May 10 | Purchased **2,400** shares for the treasury at **$48** per share. |
| Aug 5 | Sold **900** treasury shares at **$55** per share. |
| Oct 18 | Sold **600** treasury shares at **$35** per share. |
| Dec 31 | Equity section after all treasury effects. |
| Feb 14 (next year) | Retired all remaining treasury shares. |

**Required:**  
a. Journal entries for May 10 purchase, August 5 reissuance, and October 18 reissuance.  
b. Stockholders’ equity section at December 31.  
c. Journal entry to retire remaining treasury shares on February 14.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Journal entries**

*May 10 — initial recognition*  
Cost = 2,400 × $48 = **$115,200**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 115,200 | |
| Cash | | 115,200 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 115,200.

*August 5 — above cost*  
Cash = 900 × $55 = **$49,500**  
TS = 900 × $48 = **$43,200**  
PIC—TS = **$6,300**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 49,500 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 43,200 |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | | 6,300 |

**Check:** 49,500 = 43,200 + 6,300.

*October 18 — below cost*  
Cash = 600 × $35 = **$21,000**  
TS = 600 × $48 = **$28,800**  
Shortfall = **$7,800**  
Use PIC—TS **$6,300**; RE **$1,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 21,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | 6,300 | |
| Retained Earnings | 1,500 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 28,800 |

**Check:** 21,000 + 6,300 + 1,500 = 28,800.

**b. December 31 equity**  
Remaining TS shares = 2,400 − 900 − 600 = **900**  
TS balance = 900 × $48 = **$43,200**  
CS = 12,000 × $1 = **$12,000**  
PIC—CS = 12,000 × $41 = **$492,000**  
RE = 95,000 − 1,500 = **$93,500**  
Outstanding = 12,000 − 900 = **11,100**

| Stockholders’ Equity | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $1 par, 40,000 authorized, 12,000 issued, 11,100 outstanding | $12,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common | 492,000 |
| Retained earnings | 93,500 |
| Less: Treasury stock, 900 shares | (43,200) |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$554,300** |

**Check:** 12,000 + 492,000 + 93,500 − 43,200 = 554,300.

**c. February 14 — retire remaining 900 treasury shares**  
CS = 900 × $1 = **$900**  
PIC—CS = 900 × $41 = **$36,900**  
TS cost = **$43,200**  
RE plug = 43,200 − 900 − 36,900 = **$5,400**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 900 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 36,900 | |
| Retained Earnings | 5,400 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 43,200 |

**Check:** 900 + 36,900 + 5,400 = 43,200. After retirement: issued = outstanding = **11,100**; treasury = **0**.

**Key insight:** Same cost-method mechanics as Q1 with all new amounts: cost locks the TS debit; above-cost reissue credits PIC—TS; below-cost reissue exhausts PIC—TS then hits RE; retirement removes original capital + cost difference.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Direct retirement (above and below original issue price)
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Direct retirement of common stock — cash paid greater than or less than original issue proceeds; share reclassification to unissued  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Fabricators Co.** issued **8,000** shares of **$5** par common stock on January 1 at **$50** per share. On June 30, management decides **not** to hold treasury shares and instead **directly retires** **1,200** shares.

**Case A:** Cash paid to retire = **$55** per share.  
**Case B (alternative):** Cash paid to retire = **$42** per share.  
No prior balance exists in Paid-In Capital—Retired Stock in either case. Beginning RE (before retirement) = **$210,000**. Authorized = **25,000** shares.

**Required:**  
a. Journal entry for **Case A** (direct retirement when cash paid > original issue price).  
b. Journal entry for **Case B** (direct retirement when cash paid < original issue price).  
c. For Case A only: authorized, issued, outstanding, and unissued shares **immediately after** retirement.  
d. Briefly contrast Case A’s effect on total stockholders’ equity with holding the same 1,200 shares as treasury stock at $55 (cost method) **before** any reissuance or formal retirement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Case A — cash paid exceeds original issue**  
Original issue per share = $50 → par $5 + APIC $45.  
Cash = 1,200 × $55 = **$66,000**  
CS removed = 1,200 × $5 = **$6,000**  
PIC—CS removed = 1,200 × $45 = **$54,000**  
Excess paid = 66,000 − 60,000 = **$6,000** → Retained Earnings (no PIC—Retired Stock credit balance)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 6,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 54,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | 6,000 | |
| Cash | | 66,000 |
| *Direct retirement of 1,200 shares at $55; excess over original issue to RE* | | |

**Check:** 6,000 + 54,000 + 6,000 = 66,000.

**b. Case B — cash paid less than original issue**  
Cash = 1,200 × $42 = **$50,400**  
CS = **$6,000**; PIC—CS = **$54,000**  
Difference = 60,000 − 50,400 = **$9,600** → credit **Paid-In Capital—Retired Stock**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 6,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 54,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital—Retired Stock | | 9,600 |
| Cash | | 50,400 |
| *Direct retirement at $42; excess of original issue over cash to PIC—Retired Stock* | | |

**Check:** 6,000 + 54,000 = 9,600 + 50,400.

**c. Share status after Case A retirement**  

| Status | Shares |
|---|---:|
| Authorized | 25,000 |
| Issued | 8,000 − 1,200 = **6,800** |
| Outstanding | **6,800** |
| Treasury | **0** |
| Unissued | 25,000 − 6,800 = **18,200** |

**d. Contrast with treasury (cost method) at same $55 repurchase**  
Treasury path (before reissue/retirement): Dr Treasury Stock **$66,000**, Cr Cash **$66,000**. Issued remains **8,000**; outstanding falls to **6,800**; total equity still declines by **$66,000**, but capital stock and APIC accounts stay on the books and TS is a separate contra-equity line. Direct retirement **derecognizes** CS and APIC immediately and reclassifies shares to **unissued**.

**Key insight:** Direct retirement removes original capital accounts when cash is paid. Pay more than original issue → debit RE (or PIC—Retired Stock if available). Pay less → credit PIC—Retired Stock. Shares leave “issued” and return to the unissued pool.

### Q4 — CORE — Multi-lot treasury purchases: FIFO cost schedule and reissuance
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Subsequent cost assignment for multi-lot treasury shares (FIFO); reissuance JE; remaining TS carrying amount and period-end equity presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Bluepeak Analytics LLC** issued **15,000** shares of **$1** par common at **$20** per share on January 1. The company uses the **cost method** and assigns treasury cost under **FIFO** when lots are purchased at different prices. RE (no TS charges) = **$120,000**. Authorized = **60,000**.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Mar 1 | Purchased **2,000** shares for the treasury at **$22** per share. |
| Aug 1 | Purchased **1,500** shares for the treasury at **$25** per share. |
| Nov 15 | Reissued **2,500** treasury shares at **$27** per share (FIFO cost). |
| Dec 31 | Equity presentation after all treasury activity. |

**Required:**  
a. Journal entries for the March 1 and August 1 treasury purchases.  
b. **Treasury cost-layer schedule** after each purchase and after the November 15 reissuance (FIFO).  
c. Journal entry for the November 15 reissuance.  
d. Remaining Treasury Stock balance and December 31 stockholders’ equity section (including share counts).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition of each lot**

*March 1* — 2,000 × $22 = **$44,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 44,000 | |
| Cash | | 44,000 |

*August 1* — 1,500 × $25 = **$37,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 37,500 | |
| Cash | | 37,500 |

**Check:** Each entry balances. Aggregate TS after both purchases = 44,000 + 37,500 = **$81,500** (3,500 shares).

**b. FIFO treasury cost-layer schedule**

| Date | Event | Layer @ $22 | Layer @ $25 | Total shares | Total cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1 | Purchase | 2,000 ($44,000) | — | 2,000 | 44,000 |
| Aug 1 | Purchase | 2,000 ($44,000) | 1,500 ($37,500) | 3,500 | 81,500 |
| Nov 15 | Reissue 2,500 FIFO | (2,000) (−$44,000) | (500) (−$12,500) | 1,000 | 25,000 |
| **End** | | **0** | **1,000 ($25,000)** | **1,000** | **$25,000** |

FIFO cost removed on reissue = (2,000 × $22) + (500 × $25) = 44,000 + 12,500 = **$56,500**.

**c. November 15 reissuance**  
Cash = 2,500 × $27 = **$67,500**  
TS cost (FIFO) = **$56,500**  
PIC—TS = **$11,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 67,500 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 56,500 |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | | 11,000 |
| *Reissue 2,500 treasury shares using FIFO cost; proceeds > cost* | | |

**Check:** 67,500 = 56,500 + 11,000.

**d. Period-end presentation**  
Remaining TS = **1,000** shares × $25 = **$25,000**  
CS = 15,000 × $1 = **$15,000**  
PIC—CS = 15,000 × $19 = **$285,000**  
RE = **$120,000** (no below-cost charge)  
Outstanding = 15,000 − 1,000 = **14,000**  
(PIC—TS of $11,000 is part of paid-in capital)

| Stockholders’ Equity | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $1 par, 60,000 authorized, 15,000 issued, 14,000 outstanding | $15,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common | 285,000 |
| Paid-in capital—treasury stock | 11,000 |
| Retained earnings | 120,000 |
| Less: Treasury stock, 1,000 shares (at cost) | (25,000) |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$406,000** |

**Check:** 15,000 + 285,000 + 11,000 + 120,000 − 25,000 = 406,000.

**Key insight:** Multiple treasury lots require a **cost-flow assumption** (FIFO used here) to measure the cost removed on reissuance or retirement—analogous to inventory layers, but the “inventory” is a **contra-equity** balance.

### Q5A — MC — Classification of treasury stock on the balance sheet
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Classification/presentation — treasury stock is contra equity, not an asset  
**Question:**  
Under the **cost method**, how should the cost of shares reacquired and held as treasury stock be reported in the statement of financial position?

- A) As a current asset (investment in own shares)  
- B) As a noncurrent investment  
- C) As a deduction from total paid-in capital and retained earnings (contra equity)  
- D) As a liability until the shares are reissued or retired  

**Answer:** **C.** Treasury stock under the cost method is reported as a **deduction from stockholders’ equity** (contra equity). Reacquired shares are not an asset of the issuer and do not create a liability merely by being held in treasury. Voting/dividend rights are generally suspended while held as treasury.

### Q5B — MC — Below-cost reissuance after PIC—Treasury Stock exhausted
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Classification of the balancing debit when treasury stock is reissued below cost  
**Question:**  
Harborline Co. reissued treasury shares for cash less than the shares’ treasury cost. After fully eliminating the credit balance in Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock, a debit balance remains to complete the entry. That remaining debit should be recorded as:

- A) A realized loss on the income statement  
- B) A debit to Common Stock for the residual  
- C) A debit to Retained Earnings  
- D) A deferred charge (asset) amortized over remaining life  

**Answer:** **C.** After PIC—Treasury Stock is exhausted, any remaining difference on a below-cost reissuance is **debited to Retained Earnings**. The cost method does not recognize gains or losses from treasury transactions in net income.
