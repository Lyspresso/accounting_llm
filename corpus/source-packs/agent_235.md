# Agent 235 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-8

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Explain and demonstrate accounting for uncertainty in income tax decisions  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Two-step process (ASC 740):** (1) **Recognition** — more likely than not (>50%) the tax position will be sustained on technical merits (assume full examination knowledge; ignore detection risk); (2) **Measurement** — largest amount of tax benefit with **cumulative probability >50%** of being realized upon settlement
- **Initial recognition JE:** Income Tax Expense = Income Tax Payable + **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits (UTB)** when part (or all) of the filed benefit is not recognized for financial reporting
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period roll-forward of the UTB liability (beginning → additions/reductions for remeasurement → settlements → ending); Target-style disclosure categories when multiple positions exist
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** change in estimate of unrecognized amount as new information becomes available (prospective; not prior-period error restatement)
- **Settlement / resolution JE:** (a) full benefit lost, (b) full benefit realized, (c) partial realization matching prior measurement, (d) partial realization differing from prior measurement
- **Fails recognition threshold:** no step-two measurement of benefit; full tax effect of the position is recorded as UTB liability
- Classification / disclosure: UTB liability; note reconciliation of beginning to ending unrecognized tax benefits

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-period UTB subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis)
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits; initial recognition after two-step process; multi-year remeasurement (period-end adjusting JEs); settlement JE  
**Scenario:**  
**SilverOak Precision Inc.** (calendar-year) claims a **$240,000** research-classification deduction on its Year 1 tax return. Management concludes the position meets the **more-likely-than-not** recognition threshold based on technical merits (assuming the taxing authority examines the position with full knowledge of all relevant information). Taxable income for Year 1, **after** taking the full $240,000 deduction, is **$960,000**. The enacted tax rate is **25%** for all years. SilverOak has no temporary differences other than the uncertain tax position effects illustrated here.

**Year 1** management estimates of possible settlement outcomes:

| Possible estimated outcome (allowable tax deduction) | Individual probability | Cumulative probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $240,000 | 15% | 15% |
| 180,000 | 20% | 35% |
| 150,000 | 30% | 65% |
| 90,000 | 20% | 85% |
| 30,000 | 15% | 100% |

At **December 31, Year 2**, new information revises the outcome table (change in estimate):

| Possible estimated outcome (allowable tax deduction) | Individual probability | Cumulative probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $240,000 | 10% | 10% |
| 150,000 | 15% | 25% |
| 120,000 | 30% | 55% |
| 60,000 | 30% | 85% |
| 0 | 15% | 100% |

At **December 31, Year 3**, further new information improves the outlook:

| Possible estimated outcome (allowable tax deduction) | Individual probability | Cumulative probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $240,000 | 25% | 25% |
| 180,000 | 30% | 55% |
| 120,000 | 25% | 80% |
| 60,000 | 20% | 100% |

On **April 15, Year 4**, after negotiations, SilverOak and the taxing authority settle: **$165,000** of the original $240,000 deduction is sustained (**$75,000** disallowed). Any additional tax is currently payable. Ignore interest and penalties.

**Required:**  
a. Apply the **two-step** process at December 31, Year 1: does recognition apply? What **amount of deduction benefit** is measured for financial reporting?  
b. Compute **Income Tax Payable**, **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits**, and **Income Tax Expense** at December 31, Year 1. Prepare the **initial recognition journal entry**. Show Dr = Cr.  
c. Prepare a complete **subsequent measurement schedule** for the **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits** from December 31, Year 1 through April 15, Year 4 settlement. Show date, description, change, and ending balance after each event.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2** and **December 31, Year 3 period-end adjusting JEs** for remeasurement.  
e. Prepare the **April 15, Year 4 settlement journal entry**.  
f. In one or two sentences, explain why Year 1 tax expense is **not** restated when later remeasurements or settlement differ from the Year 1 estimate.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Two-step recognition and measurement (Year 1)**

- **Step 1 — Recognition:** Yes. Management concludes it is **more likely than not (>50%)** the position will be sustained on technical merits → proceed to measurement.  
- **Step 2 — Measurement:** Largest allowable deduction with **cumulative probability >50%** is **$150,000** (cumulative 65%).  
- Recognized benefit (deduction base) = **$150,000**  
- Unrecognized deduction base = $240,000 − $150,000 = **$90,000**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — initial recognition JE**

Income Tax Payable = \(960{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$240{,}000}\)  
Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits = \(90{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$22{,}500}\)  
Income Tax Expense = \(240{,}000 + 22{,}500 = \mathbf{\$262{,}500}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 262,500 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 240,000 |
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | | 22,500 |
| *Record Year 1 tax expense; UTB for unrecognized portion of uncertain deduction* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$262,500**. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits**

**Year 2 remeasurement:**  
Largest amount with cumulative probability >50% = **$120,000** (cumulative 55%).  
Unrecognized base = $240,000 − $120,000 = **$120,000**  
Required UTB = \(120{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$30{,}000}\)  
Increase = \(30{,}000 - 22{,}500 = \mathbf{\$7{,}500}\)

**Year 3 remeasurement:**  
Largest amount with cumulative probability >50% = **$180,000** (cumulative 55%).  
Unrecognized base = $240,000 − $180,000 = **$60,000**  
Required UTB = \(60{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\)  
Decrease = \(30{,}000 - 15{,}000 = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\)

| Date | Description | Change in UTB | Ending UTB liability |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 12/31/Y1 | Initial recognition (unrecognized base $90,000 × 25%) | +22,500 | **22,500** |
| 12/31/Y2 | Remeasurement — increase (worse estimate) | +7,500 | **30,000** |
| 12/31/Y3 | Remeasurement — decrease (improved estimate) | (15,000) | **15,000** |
| 4/15/Y4 | Settlement — reverse remaining UTB | (15,000) | **0** |

**Roll-forward check:** \(22{,}500 + 7{,}500 - 15{,}000 - 15{,}000 = \mathbf{\$0}\).

**d. Period-end adjusting JEs (remeasurement)**

*December 31, Year 2 — Increase UTB*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 7,500 | |
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | | 7,500 |
| *Remeasure UTB for new information; prospective change in estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$7,500**. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 3 — Decrease UTB*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 15,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 15,000 |
| *Remeasure UTB for improved outlook; reduce tax expense prospectively* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$15,000**. **Balanced.**

**e. April 15, Year 4 — settlement JE**

Deduction sustained = **$165,000**  
Deduction disallowed = $240,000 − $165,000 = **$75,000**  
Additional Income Tax Payable = \(75{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$18{,}750}\)  
Remove UTB liability of **$15,000**  
Difference increases Income Tax Expense: \(18{,}750 - 15{,}000 = \mathbf{\$3{,}750}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 15,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | 3,750 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 18,750 |
| *Settle uncertain tax position; reverse UTB; true-up tax expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 15,000 + 3,750 = Cr 18,750. **Balanced.**

**f. Change in estimate (not error)**  
Remeasurements and settlement differences arise from **new information** and are accounted for **prospectively** as changes in accounting estimate. Prior-period tax expense and financial statements are **not** restated.

**Key insight:** The **subsequent measurement schedule** is the exam core for multi-period uncertain tax positions: lock each period’s UTB from the cumulative-probability measurement table, roll the liability forward, and treat every later change (remeasurement or settlement) as a **prospective estimate update**—never a restatement of Year 1.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Twin UTB schedule with full-benefit settlement
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — subsequent measurement schedule for UTB; initial recognition after two-step process; period-end remeasurement; settlement when full benefit is realized  
**Scenario:**  
**QuarryBend Analytics Corp.** claims a **$96,000** deduction on its Year 1 return related to classification of certain project costs. Management determines the position is **more likely than not** to be sustained. Taxable income after the full deduction is **$480,000**. Tax rate is **25%**. No other temporary differences.

**Year 1** possible outcomes:

| Possible estimated outcome (allowable tax deduction) | Individual probability | Cumulative probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $96,000 | 25% | 25% |
| 72,000 | 20% | 45% |
| 60,000 | 25% | 70% |
| 36,000 | 20% | 90% |
| 12,000 | 10% | 100% |

At **December 31, Year 2**, new information revises the table so the largest amount with cumulative probability >50% is now **$72,000** (management documents cumulative probability of 58% at $72,000).  
On **August 20, Year 3**, QuarryBend **fully sustains** the entire $96,000 deduction after examination.

**Required:**  
a. Measure the Year 1 recognized deduction benefit and unrecognized base; compute tax payable, UTB liability, and tax expense.  
b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 initial recognition JE**.  
c. Prepare a **UTB liability subsequent measurement schedule** through settlement.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2 period-end remeasurement JE**.  
e. Prepare the **August 20, Year 3 settlement JE** (full benefit realized).  
f. Compute what **Income Tax Expense** would have been in Year 1 if the individual probability of the full $96,000 outcome had been **55%** (so cumulative probability at $96,000 exceeds 50%).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1 measurement**

Largest amount with cumulative probability >50% = **$60,000** (cumulative 70%).  
Unrecognized deduction base = $96,000 − $60,000 = **$36,000**  
Income Tax Payable = \(480{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}\)  
Liability for UTB = \(36{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$9{,}000}\)  
Income Tax Expense = \(120{,}000 + 9{,}000 = \mathbf{\$129{,}000}\)

**b. December 31, Year 1 — initial recognition JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 129,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 120,000 |
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | | 9,000 |
| *Record tax expense and UTB for unrecognized portion* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$129,000**. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — UTB liability**

Year 2 required liability: unrecognized base = $96,000 − $72,000 = **$24,000**; UTB = \(24{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\)

| Date | Description | Change in UTB | Ending UTB liability |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 12/31/Y1 | Initial recognition | +9,000 | **9,000** |
| 12/31/Y2 | Remeasurement (decrease) | (3,000) | **6,000** |
| 8/20/Y3 | Settlement — full benefit realized | (6,000) | **0** |

**Roll-forward check:** \(9{,}000 - 3{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$0}\).

**d. December 31, Year 2 — period-end remeasurement JE**

Decrease in UTB = \(9{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$3{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 3,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 3,000 |
| *Reduce UTB for improved measurement estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$3,000**. **Balanced.**

**e. August 20, Year 3 — settlement (full tax benefit realized)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 6,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 6,000 |
| *Favorable resolution; reverse remaining UTB; reduce tax expense (change in estimate)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$6,000**. **Balanced.**  
No Income Tax Payable is recorded because the full return position is sustained (no additional tax due).

**f. Counterfactual — full $96,000 has >50% likelihood**

If individual probability of the full $96,000 outcome is **55%**, the largest amount with cumulative probability >50% is **$96,000**.  
Unrecognized base = **$0**; UTB = **$0**  
Income Tax Expense = Income Tax Payable = **$120,000** only.

**Key insight:** The **cumulative** probability column—not the individual probability of the top outcome—drives measurement once recognition is met. An improving subsequent schedule reduces UTB and tax expense prospectively; full success settles UTB entirely against tax expense with **zero** additional payable.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — multi-position UTB roll-forward + fails MLTN + settlement outcomes
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule (disclosure-style multi-position roll-forward); initial recognition when position fails MLTN; settlement/resolution JEs for full loss, full sustain, and negotiated partial realization  
**Scenario:**  

**Part A — Multi-position UTB subsequent measurement schedule**  
**Redcedar Foundries Co.** begins Year 2 with a **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits of $18,000** composed of prior-year **Position P ($12,000)** and prior-year **Position R ($6,000)**. During Year 2 the following occur (tax rate **25%** throughout):

1. **Remeasurement of prior-year Position P:** new information increases the required UTB for Position P by **$4,500** (from $12,000 to $16,500).  
2. **Current-year Position Q:** Redcedar claims a **$80,000** deduction that meets the more-likely-than-not threshold. Cumulative-probability measurement supports recognizing benefit of a **$56,000** deduction (unrecognized base **$24,000**).  
3. **Settlement of prior-year Position R:** the taxing authority allows exactly the previously measured benefit, so Redcedar pays **$6,000** additional tax and removes that UTB with **no** income-statement effect.

**Part B — Fails recognition threshold**  
**TidalHarbor Goods Inc.** takes a **$48,000** deduction on its Year 1 tax return. Management assesses that the probability the position will be sustained on technical merits is **only 35%** (does **not** meet more-likely-than-not). Taxable income after taking the $48,000 deduction is **$360,000**. Tax rate is **25%**.

**Part C — Settlement outcomes (separate case; meets threshold previously)**  
**Ashgrove Components LLC** previously recorded a **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits of $9,000** at December 31, Year 1 related to a Year 1 uncertain deduction of **$120,000**. That liability reflected an unrecognized deduction base of **$36,000** at 25% (i.e., financial statements recognized benefit of an **$84,000** deduction). In December of Year 2, consider each resolution **separately**:

1. **Full tax benefit is lost** — none of the $120,000 deduction is sustained.  
2. **Full tax benefit is realized** — the entire $120,000 deduction is sustained.  
3. **Portion realized as previously measured** — taxing authority allows **$84,000** of the deduction.  
4. **Portion realized differently** — taxing authority allows only **$60,000** of the deduction.

**Required:**  
a. (Part A) Prepare the **Year 2 subsequent measurement schedule** (beginning UTB → additions for prior-year remeasurement → additions for current-year position → settlements → ending UTB). Also compute the UTB amount recorded for Position Q.  
b. (Part B) Explain why no benefit is measured under step two. Compute amounts and prepare TidalHarbor’s **December 31, Year 1 income tax JE**.  
c. (Part C) Prepare the **Year 2 settlement JE** for each of cases 1–4.  
d. For case 3 only, explain the **income statement effect** relative to cases 1, 2, and 4.  
e. State the **balance-sheet classification** of a material UTB liability expected to be negotiated over multi-year examinations and name one typical **disclosure** for the roll-forward.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Redcedar — Year 2 subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis)**

Position Q UTB = \(24{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\)

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Balance at beginning of Year 2 | **$18,000** |
| Additions for tax positions of prior years (Position P remeasurement) | **4,500** |
| Additions based on tax positions related to the current year (Position Q) | **6,000** |
| Settlements (Position R) | **(6,000)** |
| **Balance at end of Year 2** | **$22,500** |

**Roll-forward check:** \(18{,}000 + 4{,}500 + 6{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$22{,}500}\).  
Composition at year-end: Position P = \(12{,}000 + 4{,}500 = \$16{,}500\); Position Q = $6,000; Position R = $0.

**b. TidalHarbor — fails recognition threshold (initial recognition JE)**

- **Step 1:** Probability of sustainment = 35% **≤ 50%** → does **not** meet MLTN recognition threshold.  
- **Step 2 measurement is not applied** to recognize any financial-statement benefit of the position.  
- Full tax effect of the $48,000 deduction is treated as unrecognized.

Income Tax Payable (as filed) = \(360{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$90{,}000}\)  
Liability for UTB = \(48{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\)  
Income Tax Expense = \(90{,}000 + 12{,}000 = \mathbf{\$102{,}000}\)

(Equivalent view: tax expense as if the deduction is fully disallowed = \((360{,}000 + 48{,}000) \times 25\% = \$102{,}000\).)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 102,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 90,000 |
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | | 12,000 |
| *Position fails MLTN; no FS benefit recognized for the $48,000 deduction* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$102,000**. **Balanced.**

**c. Ashgrove — Year 2 settlement JEs**

**Case 1 — Full tax benefit lost**

Additional tax due on full $120,000 disallowance = \(120{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$30{,}000}\)  
Prior UTB accrual = **$9,000**  
Incremental Income Tax Expense in Year 2 = \(30{,}000 - 9{,}000 = \mathbf{\$21{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 9,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | 21,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 30,000 |
| *Fail to sustain tax position; settle UTB and accrue remaining tax* | | |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 + 21,000 = Cr 30,000. **Balanced.**

**Case 2 — Full tax benefit realized**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 9,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 9,000 |
| *Favorable resolution; reverse UTB; reduce tax expense prospectively* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$9,000**. **Balanced.**

**Case 3 — Portion realized as previously measured ($84,000 allowed)**

Additional tax due = unrecognized base \(36{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$9{,}000}\)  
Equals existing UTB → pure reclassification; **no** income-statement effect.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 9,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 9,000 |
| *Negotiated resolution matches prior measurement; reclass UTB to payable* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **$9,000**. **Balanced.**

**Case 4 — Portion realized differently ($60,000 allowed)**

Disallowed base = $120,000 − $60,000 = **$60,000**  
Additional tax due = \(60{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\)  
Prior UTB = **$9,000**  
Incremental Income Tax Expense = \(15{,}000 - 9{,}000 = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits | 9,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | 6,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 15,000 |
| *Settlement worse than prior measurement; true-up tax expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 + 6,000 = Cr 15,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Income statement effect — case 3 vs others**  
- Case 3: **$0** Year 2 income-statement effect (settlement equals prior estimate).  
- Case 1: **+$21,000** tax expense (worse than estimate).  
- Case 2: **−$9,000** tax expense (better than estimate; full reverse of UTB).  
- Case 4: **+$6,000** tax expense (somewhat worse than the $84,000 measured benefit).  
Resolution effects are **changes in accounting estimates**, not prior-period error corrections.

**e. Classification / disclosure**  
A material UTB liability related to multi-year examinations is typically presented as a **noncurrent liability** unless management expects settlement (and payment) within one year of the balance-sheet date. Notes typically include a **reconciliation** of beginning to ending unrecognized tax benefits (additions for current-year positions, additions/reductions for prior-year positions, settlements, and other changes).

**Key insight:** The disclosure-style **subsequent measurement schedule** aggregates multiple positions (current-year additions, prior-year remeasurements, settlements). If step 1 **fails**, the entire tax benefit is unrecognized. If step 1 **passed** and a UTB was accrued, settlement either reclasses to payable (estimate correct), increases tax expense (worse), or decreases tax expense (better).

### Q4 — MC (measurement method)
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Measurement method for a tax position that meets the more-likely-than-not recognition threshold  

**Question:**  
Windrow Biosystems Inc. meets the more-likely-than-not recognition threshold for an uncertain tax deduction of **$50,000**. Management’s cumulative probability table shows: **15%** chance of sustaining **$50,000**; **40%** chance of sustaining at least **$35,000**; **70%** chance of sustaining at least **$20,000**; **100%** chance of sustaining at least **$0**. The tax rate is **25%**. What amount of **Liability for Unrecognized Tax Benefits** should Windrow record related to this position?

- A) $0  
- B) $3,750  
- C) $7,500  
- D) $12,500  

**Answer:** **C.** Once recognition is met, measure the **largest** benefit with **cumulative** probability **>50%**. Cumulative probability at $35,000 is only **40%** (does not exceed 50%); cumulative probability at $20,000 is **70%**. Recognized deduction = **$20,000**; unrecognized base = $50,000 − $20,000 = **$30,000**; UTB = \(30{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$7{,}500}\).  
Option B ($3,750) is the common trap if students incorrectly treat the $35,000 row (40% cumulative) as meeting the >50% cumulative measurement rule (\(15{,}000 \times 25\%\)). Option D ($12,500) treats the position as if it failed recognition entirely (\(50{,}000 \times 25\%\)). Option A would require the full $50,000 to clear the cumulative >50% test.

### Q4b — MC (recognition threshold)
**LO:** LO 18-8  
**Concept:** Effect of failing the more-likely-than-not recognition threshold on initial measurement  

**Question:**  
Copperline Packaging Co. claims a **$28,000** uncertain deduction. Management is **only 42%** certain the position will be sustained on technical merits if examined. Taxable income after the deduction is **$400,000**; tax rate **25%**. Copperline accrued taxes payable based on the tax position taken. What **Income Tax Expense** should Copperline record for the year related to current taxes and this uncertain position (ignore other temporary differences)?

- A) $100,000  
- B) $100,700  
- C) $107,000  
- D) $93,000  

**Answer:** **C.** Recognition threshold is **not** met (42% ≯ 50%). No financial-statement benefit is recognized for the $28,000 deduction.  
Tax payable as filed = \(400{,}000 \times 25\% = \$100{,}000\)  
UTB = \(28{,}000 \times 25\% = \$7{,}000\)  
Income Tax Expense = \(100{,}000 + 7{,}000 = \mathbf{\$107{,}000}\)  
(Alternatively: taxable income without the deduction \(428{,}000 \times 25\% = \$107{,}000\).)  
A is tax payable only (incorrectly ignores UTB). B has no conceptual support. D is as if a larger benefit were recognized.
