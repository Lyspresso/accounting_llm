# Agent 393 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-8

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for internally developed software and cloud computing arrangements  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis angle:** disposal_maturity_or_settlement_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Software to be sold/leased (ASC 985-20):** expense as R&D until **technological feasibility**; capitalize product-master costs after feasibility until product release
- **Amortization of capitalized software to be sold:** begin when product is marketed; annual amount = **greater of** revenue method or straight-line
- **Revenue method:** (current product revenue ÷ total anticipated product revenue remaining, including current) × unamortized capitalized cost
- **Internal-use software (ASC 350-40):** preliminary → expense; application development → capitalize; postimplementation → expense; **training always expensed**
- **Internal-use amortization:** generally straight-line once substantially complete and ready for intended use (partial year when needed)
- **Cloud hosting arrangement:** software purchase only if customer has right to take possession **without significant penalty** **and** can run/host elsewhere; otherwise **service contract**
- **Service-contract cloud implementation costs:** capitalize development-stage implementation; amortize over hosting term (usually SL); expense subscription fees
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement (emphasis):** write off remaining unamortized software or cloud implementation costs on abandonment, sale of remaining rights, early termination of hosting, or end-of-life
- **Classification / presentation:** cloud implementation costs presented like hosting prepayments; amortization in same IS line as hosting fees

### Q1 — CORE — Software for sale: initial recognition, greater-of schedule, period-end amort, and disposal of remaining rights

### Q4 — MC — Classification of software / cloud costs and disposal presentation
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Classification — expense vs capitalize for software-for-sale, internal-use, and cloud service-contract costs; disposal of remaining intangible  

**Question 1:**  
Which cost is **capitalized** as an intangible asset under U.S. GAAP for software **developed to be sold** to customers?

- A) Coding and testing costs incurred **before** technological feasibility is established  
- B) Costs of producing product masters incurred **after** technological feasibility and **before** the product is available for general release  
- C) Customer training costs incurred after the software is released for sale  
- D) Marketing campaign costs in the first year the product is sold  

**Answer:** **B.** After technological feasibility and before release, costs to obtain product masters are capitalized (ASC 985-20). Pre-feasibility costs are R&D expense (A). Post-release training and marketing are operating expenses, not software capitalization (C, D).

**Question 2:**  
Lakeview Co. enters a four-year SaaS hosting arrangement. It **cannot** take possession of the software without significant penalty. It pays **$120,000** of implementation costs at inception and **$80,000** per year for access. After two full years of SL amortization of implementation costs, Lakeview terminates the arrangement early with no refund. What is the correct disposal entry amount for remaining implementation costs?

- A) Write off **$120,000** (full original implementation cost)  
- B) Write off **$60,000** (remaining unamortized implementation: \(120{,}000 - 2 \times 30{,}000\))  
- C) Capitalize the remaining two years of subscription fees as an intangible  
- D) Reclassify remaining implementation costs to R&D expense only  

**Answer:** **B.** Service-contract implementation costs are amortized over the **hosting term** (\(120{,}000 / 4 = 30{,}000\) per year). After two years, unamortized balance is **$60,000**, which is written off on early termination. Full original cost is not re-expensed (A). Future subscription fees that will not be paid are not capitalized (C). Remaining implementation is not reclassified as R&D (D).
