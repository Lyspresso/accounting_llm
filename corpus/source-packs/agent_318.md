**Chapter:** 9  
**LO title:** Perform inventory ratio analysis and interpretation  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Inventory turnover = Cost of goods sold ÷ Average inventory
- Average days in inventory = 365 ÷ Inventory turnover
- Gross profit percentage = Gross profit ÷ Net sales
- Average inventory = (Beginning inventory + Ending inventory) ÷ 2
- **Period-end adjusting JE (periodic):** close beginning inventory, purchases, freight-in, and purchase returns; establish ending inventory; residual COGS
- **Period-end adjusting JE (LIFO conversion):** increase LIFO reserve → debit COGS, credit inventory allowance
- **Initial recognition JE** of merchandise purchases and freight-in (periodic Purchases / Freight-in accounts)
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** sales revenue recognition; settlement of accounts payable; purchase returns
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** COGS computation bridge and multi-year / multi-basis ratio tables
- Method effects when prices rise: LIFO → higher COGS, lower inventory, **higher turnover**, lower days, lower GP% than FIFO
- Comparability: restate LIFO to FIFO using the **LIFO reserve** before peer ratio comparison
- Interpretation: higher turnover / lower days generally preferred; method differences ≠ physical flow differences

### Q1 — CORE — Ashwick Merchandising: period-end COGS adjusting JE, inventory bridge, multi-year ratios (period-end adjusting JE emphasis)
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting journal entry under the periodic system that establishes ending inventory and residual COGS; initial recognition of purchases/freight; AP settlement and sales; multi-year inventory turnover, days in inventory, and gross profit % with interpretation  
**Scenario:**  
**Ashwick Merchandising Co.** is a wholesale merchandiser using a **periodic inventory system**. Fiscal year ends **December 31**. Selected balances:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Inventory — Dec 31, Year 0 | $400,000 |
| Inventory — Dec 31, Year 1 | 440,000 |
| Net sales — Year 1 | 2,800,000 |
| Cost of goods sold — Year 1 | 1,680,000 |
| Net sales — Year 2 | 3,150,000 |

**Year 2 activity (chronological summary):**  
1. Purchased merchandise on account, **$1,950,000** (invoice gross).  
2. Paid freight-in of **$48,000** cash on Year 2 purchases (f.o.b. shipping point; inventoriable).  
3. Returned defective merchandise to suppliers; accounts payable reduced **$32,000** (purchase returns).  
4. Paid the remaining Year 2 accounts payable related to these purchases in cash.  
5. Sold merchandise on account for **$3,150,000** (periodic system: no COGS entry at sale date).  
6. Year-end physical count of ending inventory: **$465,000**.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the **initial recognition** journal entries for Year 2 purchases and freight-in (periodic accounts).  
(b) Prepare the journal entries for the **purchase return** and for **settlement** of the related accounts payable.  
(c) Prepare the journal entry for **Year 2 sales**.  
(d) Compute **Year 2 cost of goods sold** using the inventory bridge (subsequent measurement schedule). Prepare the **combined period-end adjusting journal entry** that (1) removes beginning inventory, (2) closes Purchases, Freight-in, and Purchase Returns and Allowances, (3) records ending inventory, and (4) records residual COGS. Prove that Dr = Cr.  
(e) Prepare a **ratio analysis schedule** for **Year 1 and Year 2** showing: (1) average inventory, (2) inventory turnover, (3) average days in inventory, (4) gross profit, and (5) gross profit percentage. Round turnover and days to **two** decimals; round percentages to **two** decimals. (Compute turnover to two decimals first, then divide 365 by that rounded turnover for days.)  
(f) Briefly **interpret** the Year 1 → Year 2 trend (efficiency and margin).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Initial recognition — Year 2 purchases and freight (periodic)**  
```
Dr Purchases ............................... 1,950,000
   Cr Accounts Payable .............................. 1,950,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 1,950,000)

```
Dr Freight-in ................................. 48,000
   Cr Cash ............................................. 48,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 48,000)

**(b) Purchase return and AP settlement**  
```
Dr Accounts Payable ........................... 32,000
   Cr Purchase Returns and Allowances .................. 32,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 32,000)

Net AP from these purchases = $1,950,000 − $32,000 = **$1,918,000**

```
Dr Accounts Payable ........................ 1,918,000
   Cr Cash ........................................... 1,918,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 1,918,000)

**(c) Sales (periodic — revenue only at sale)**  
```
Dr Accounts Receivable ..................... 3,150,000
   Cr Sales Revenue .................................. 3,150,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 3,150,000)

**(d) Year 2 COGS bridge and period-end adjusting JE**  

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory (Jan 1, Year 2) | $440,000 |
| + Purchases | 1,950,000 |
| + Freight-in | 48,000 |
| − Purchase returns | (32,000) |
| Goods available for sale | 2,406,000 |
| − Ending inventory (physical count) | (465,000) |
| **Cost of goods sold — Year 2** | **$1,941,000** |

Check: \(440{,}000 + 1{,}950{,}000 + 48{,}000 - 32{,}000 - 465{,}000 = 1{,}941{,}000\).

**Combined period-end adjusting JE:**  
```
Dr Inventory (ending) ........................ 465,000
Dr Purchase Returns and Allowances ............ 32,000
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ...................... 1,941,000
   Cr Inventory (beginning) ........................... 440,000
   Cr Purchases ..................................... 1,950,000
   Cr Freight-in ....................................... 48,000
```
Dr total = \(465{,}000 + 32{,}000 + 1{,}941{,}000 = \mathbf{2{,}438{,}000}\)  
Cr total = \(440{,}000 + 1{,}950{,}000 + 48{,}000 = \mathbf{2{,}438{,}000}\)  
(Dr = Cr = 2,438,000)

*Under the periodic system, COGS is a residual period-end amount: beginning inventory + net inventoriable purchases − ending inventory from the physical count.*

**(e) Ratio analysis schedule**  

| Year | Net sales | COGS | Gross profit | GP % | Average inventory | Inventory turnover | Avg days in inventory |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 | $2,800,000 | $1,680,000 | $1,120,000 | **40.00%** | \((400{,}000 + 440{,}000)/2 = 420{,}000\) | \(1{,}680{,}000 / 420{,}000 = \mathbf{4.00}\) | \(365 / 4.00 = \mathbf{91.25}\) days |
| Year 2 | $3,150,000 | $1,941,000 | $1,209,000 | **38.38%** | \((440{,}000 + 465{,}000)/2 = 452{,}500\) | \(1{,}941{,}000 / 452{,}500 = \mathbf{4.29}\) | \(365 / 4.29 = \mathbf{85.08}\) days |

Gross profit checks:  
- Year 1: \(2{,}800{,}000 - 1{,}680{,}000 = 1{,}120{,}000\); \(1{,}120{,}000 / 2{,}800{,}000 = 40.00\%\).  
- Year 2: \(3{,}150{,}000 - 1{,}941{,}000 = 1{,}209{,}000\); \(1{,}209{,}000 / 3{,}150{,}000 = 38.3810\ldots\% \rightarrow 38.38\%\).

Turnover checks:  
- Year 1: \(1{,}680{,}000 / 420{,}000 = 4.00\).  
- Year 2: \(1{,}941{,}000 / 452{,}500 = 4.28950\ldots \rightarrow 4.29\); \(365 / 4.29 = 85.0816\ldots \rightarrow 85.08\).

**(f) Interpretation**  
Inventory turnover **increased** from 4.00 to 4.29 and average days in inventory **decreased** from 91.25 to 85.08, indicating **improved inventory velocity**. Gross profit percentage **declined** from 40.00% to 38.38%, so the faster turns came with some **margin compression** (COGS grew faster than sales relative to the prior year). Analysts should investigate whether rising product costs, competitive pricing, or product mix drove the GP% decline while efficiency improved.

**Key insight:** Under the **periodic system**, the **period-end adjusting JE** is what produces the COGS amount that feeds inventory turnover and gross profit %—ratios depend entirely on that residual COGS and the physical-count ending inventory.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Crestmoor Industrial Supply: LIFO period-end adjusting JE, multi-basis ratio schedule, peer comparability
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Period-end LIFO conversion adjusting JE; subsequent measurement ratio schedule under LIFO vs FIFO (LIFO reserve restatement); method effects when prices rise; peer comparison after adjustment  
**Scenario:**  
**Crestmoor Industrial Supply Inc.** keeps **internal perpetual records on FIFO** and converts to **LIFO for external reporting** at year-end. Inventory acquisition costs have been **rising**. Selected data:

| Item | Dec 31, Year 2 | Dec 31, Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (LIFO, as reported) | $620,000 | $655,000 |
| LIFO reserve (FIFO inventory − LIFO inventory) | 88,000 | 118,000 |

| Item | Year 3 |
|---|---:|
| Net sales | $5,100,000 |
| Cost of goods sold (LIFO, as reported) | 3,480,000 |

**Peer:** **Oakridge Outfitters Inc.** uses **FIFO** and reports for Year 3: beginning inventory **$720,000**, ending inventory **$760,000**, COGS **$3,400,000**.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute the **increase in the LIFO reserve** for Year 3 and prepare the **period-end adjusting journal entry** that converts FIFO books to LIFO (debit COGS; credit inventory allowance / LIFO reserve).  
(b) Compute **FIFO inventory** at Dec 31, Year 2 and Dec 31, Year 3, and **FIFO COGS** for Year 3.  
(c) Prepare a **ratio comparison schedule** for Crestmoor under **LIFO (as reported)** and **FIFO (as if)** for Year 3: average inventory, inventory turnover, average days in inventory, gross profit, and gross profit percentage (same rounding as Q1).  
(d) Compute Oakridge’s Year 3 inventory turnover and average days in inventory.  
(e) **Interpret:** On an as-reported basis, how does Crestmoor’s turnover compare to Oakridge’s? After restating Crestmoor to FIFO, what changes? Why does method matter?

**Answer key:**

**(a) LIFO reserve increase and period-end adjusting JE**  
Increase in LIFO reserve = $118,000 − $88,000 = **$30,000**

```
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ......................... 30,000
   Cr Inventory Allowance (LIFO reserve) ............... 30,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 30,000)

*To increase the LIFO reserve (reduce inventory carrying amount from FIFO to LIFO) and recognize the additional LIFO COGS when prices rose.*

**(b) FIFO amounts**  
- FIFO inventory Dec 31, Year 2 = $620,000 + $88,000 = **$708,000**  
- FIFO inventory Dec 31, Year 3 = $655,000 + $118,000 = **$773,000**  
- FIFO COGS Year 3 = LIFO COGS − increase in LIFO reserve = $3,480,000 − $30,000 = **$3,450,000**

**(c) Crestmoor ratio schedule — LIFO vs FIFO (Year 3)**  

| Basis | COGS | Gross profit | GP % | Average inventory | Inventory turnover | Avg days |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **LIFO (as reported)** | $3,480,000 | $1,620,000 | **31.76%** | \((620{,}000 + 655{,}000)/2 = 637{,}500\) | \(3{,}480{,}000 / 637{,}500 = \mathbf{5.46}\) | \(365 / 5.46 = \mathbf{66.85}\) |
| **FIFO (as if)** | $3,450,000 | $1,650,000 | **32.35%** | \((708{,}000 + 773{,}000)/2 = 740{,}500\) | \(3{,}450{,}000 / 740{,}500 = \mathbf{4.66}\) | \(365 / 4.66 = \mathbf{78.33}\) |

Checks:  
- GP LIFO: \(5{,}100{,}000 - 3{,}480{,}000 = 1{,}620{,}000\); \(1{,}620{,}000 / 5{,}100{,}000 = 31.7647\ldots\% \rightarrow 31.76\%\).  
- GP FIFO: \(5{,}100{,}000 - 3{,}450{,}000 = 1{,}650{,}000\); \(1{,}650{,}000 / 5{,}100{,}000 = 32.3529\ldots\% \rightarrow 32.35\%\).  
- TO LIFO: \(3{,}480{,}000 / 637{,}500 = 5.45882\ldots \rightarrow 5.46\); days \(365 / 5.46 = 66.8498\ldots \rightarrow 66.85\).  
- TO FIFO: \(3{,}450{,}000 / 740{,}500 = 4.65901\ldots \rightarrow 4.66\); days \(365 / 4.66 = 78.3262\ldots \rightarrow 78.33\).

**(d) Oakridge (FIFO peer)**  
Average inventory = \((720{,}000 + 760{,}000)/2 = 740{,}000\)  
Inventory turnover = \(3{,}400{,}000 / 740{,}000 = 4.59459\ldots = \mathbf{4.59}\)  
Average days = \(365 / 4.59 = \mathbf{79.52}\) days  

**(e) Interpretation**  
On **as-reported** bases, Crestmoor’s LIFO turnover (**5.46**) looks much stronger than Oakridge’s FIFO turnover (**4.59**). That gap is largely **method-driven**: with rising prices, LIFO reports **higher COGS** and **lower average inventory**, both of which **inflate turnover** (and shrink days) even when physical flow is similar. After restating Crestmoor to FIFO, turnover is **4.66** vs Oakridge **4.59**—nearly comparable velocity. Users should adjust LIFO companies to FIFO (using the LIFO reserve) **before** ranking inventory efficiency.

**Key insight (Demo 9-9):** Ratios differ across methods even when physical flow is identical. Consistency over time and common-basis restatement across companies are essential for interpretation. The period-end LIFO conversion JE is what moves COGS and inventory from FIFO books to LIFO-reported amounts.

### Q4 — MC — Method effect on inventory turnover (rising prices)
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Classification of cost-flow method effects on inventory turnover when acquisition costs are rising  
**Question:**  
Assuming **rising** inventory purchase prices and identical physical inventory activity, which cost-flow assumption typically produces the **highest inventory turnover** ratio?

- A) FIFO  
- B) Weighted-average cost  
- C) LIFO  
- D) Specific identification (always highest regardless of price trend)

**Answer:** **C.** With rising prices, LIFO assigns higher (more recent) costs to COGS and lower (older) costs to ending inventory. Higher COGS and lower average inventory both increase inventory turnover relative to FIFO; average cost typically falls between them. Specific identification depends on which units are sold, not a systematic “always highest” result.

### Q5 — MC — Computing inventory turnover and days
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Correct computation of inventory turnover and average days in inventory  
**Question:**  
**Tidewell Goods Co.** reports cost of goods sold of **$912,000**, beginning inventory of **$142,000**, and ending inventory of **$178,000**. What are inventory turnover and average days in inventory (use 365 days; round turnover to two decimals and days to two decimals using the rounded turnover)?

- A) Turnover 5.70; days 64.04  
- B) Turnover 5.12; days 71.29  
- C) Turnover 6.42; days 56.85  
- D) Turnover 5.70; days 64.00  

**Answer:** **A.** Average inventory = \((142{,}000 + 178{,}000)/2 = 160{,}000\). Inventory turnover = \(912{,}000 / 160{,}000 = 5.70\). Average days = \(365 / 5.70 = 64.035\ldots = 64.04\) days.  
(B uses ending inventory only as the denominator; C uses beginning only; D mis-rounds days.)
