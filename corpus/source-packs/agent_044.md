# Agent 44 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-6

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Account for the sale of receivables and use as collateral for borrowing  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Secured borrowing (assignment of AR as collateral): initial recognition of loan proceeds, finance fee, and note payable
- Subsequent measurement: monthly interest on unpaid loan balance; remittance of customer collections applied first to interest then principal
- Period-end collection, sales-adjustment, and remittance journal entries while AR remains on the books
- Settlement/payoff of the remaining note payable balance
- Sale (factoring) of receivables **without recourse**: derecognize AR, finance fee as loss, factor holdback as receivable
- Sale (factoring) of receivables **with recourse**: same structure plus recourse liability; loss includes estimated recourse cost
- Settlement of factor holdback and recourse obligation
- Discounting a note receivable (sale): accrue interest to discount date; compute proceeds on maturity value; record gain/loss
- Classification: sale vs secured borrowing (control criteria); presentation when AR is collateral

### Q1 — CORE — Copperline Trading: assignment of AR as collateral (full lifecycle)
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of secured borrowing; subsequent interest and remittance schedule; settlement of note payable  
**Scenario:**  
**Copperline Trading Inc.** finances operations by assigning accounts receivable as collateral for a short-term loan from Northline Finance.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Date of assignment | **January 1, Year 1** |
| Accounts receivable assigned (face) | **$125,000** |
| Loan advance | **80%** of assigned AR |
| Flat finance charge | **$2,000** (deducted from proceeds) |
| Interest | **12%** per year on the **unpaid loan (note) balance**, payable when cash is remitted |
| Collection agreement | Copperline continues to collect from customers and remits collections to Northline as payments on the loan |
| Accounting | Gross method for cash discounts; assigned AR remains on Copperline’s books |

**Events:**
1. **January 1, Year 1** — Receive loan proceeds (net of finance charge).  
2. **January 31, Year 1** — Collect **$55,000** cash on **$60,000** of the assigned accounts after **$4,000** sales returns (previously recorded as a refund liability) and **$1,000** sales discounts. Remit the **$55,000** to Northline.  
3. **February** — Write off **$3,000** of assigned accounts as uncollectible (against the allowance). Collect an additional **$40,000** cash on assigned accounts.  
4. **February 28, Year 1** — Pay the **remaining loan balance in full**, including February interest.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1** journal entry to record receipt of loan proceeds (initial recognition).  
b. Prepare the **January 31** entries to record collection on account and remittance to the finance company.  
c. Prepare a **note payable amortization / roll-forward schedule** for January–February (beginning balance, interest, cash paid, principal reduction, ending balance).  
d. Prepare the February collection/write-off entry and the **February 28** loan payoff entry.  
e. Compute the **total cost of borrowing** (finance charge + interest).

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition of secured borrowing**  
Note payable face = \(0.80 \times \$125{,}000 = \mathbf{\$100{,}000}\)  
Cash proceeds = \(\$100{,}000 - \$2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$98{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 98,000 | |
| Finance Expense | 2,000 | |
| Note Payable | | 100,000 |
| *To record loan proceeds at 80% of assigned AR less flat finance charge; AR remains on books* | | |

**Check:** Dr 98,000 + 2,000 = Cr 100,000. Balanced.  
(Receivables are **not** derecognized — this is a **borrowing**, not a sale.)

**b. January 31 — collection and remittance**  

*Collection on assigned accounts:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 55,000 | |
| Sales Discount | 1,000 | |
| Refund Liability | 4,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 60,000 |
| *Cash $55,000 on $60,000 face after $1,000 discounts and $4,000 returns* | | |

**Check:** 55,000 + 1,000 + 4,000 = 60,000. Balanced.

*Remittance to finance company (interest first, remainder to principal):*  
Interest expense = \(\$100{,}000 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$1{,}000}\)  
Principal reduction = \(\$55{,}000 - \$1{,}000 = \mathbf{\$54{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 54,000 | |
| Interest Expense | 1,000 | |
| Cash | | 55,000 |
| *Apply January remittance to interest then principal* | | |

**Check:** 54,000 + 1,000 = 55,000. Balanced.  
Ending note payable after January = \(100{,}000 - 54{,}000 = \mathbf{\$46{,}000}\).

**c. Note payable schedule**

| Date | Beg. NP | Interest (12% × 1/12) | Cash paid | Principal reduction | End. NP |
|------|--------:|----------------------:|----------:|--------------------:|--------:|
| Jan 1 (loan recorded) | — | — | — | — | 100,000 |
| Jan 31 | 100,000 | 1,000 | 55,000 | 54,000 | 46,000 |
| Feb 28 (payoff) | 46,000 | 460 | 46,460 | 46,000 | **0** |

Interest Feb: \(46{,}000 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$460}\).

**d. February collection/write-off and loan settlement**  

*Collection and write-off (AR still on Copperline’s books):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 40,000 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | 3,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 43,000 |
| *Collect $40,000 and write off $3,000 of assigned receivables* | | |

**Check:** 40,000 + 3,000 = 43,000. Balanced.

*February 28 — payoff of remaining loan:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 46,000 | |
| Interest Expense | 460 | |
| Cash | | 46,460 |
| *Settle remaining principal plus February interest* | | |

**Check:** 46,000 + 460 = 46,460. Balanced.

**e. Total cost of borrowing**  
Finance charge **$2,000** + January interest **$1,000** + February interest **$460** = **$3,460**.  
Total cash paid to lender for principal and interest after proceeds: remittances \(55{,}000 + 46{,}460 = 101{,}460\); principal repaid \(100{,}000\); interest \(1{,}460\); plus initial finance fee embedded in reduced proceeds.

**Key insight:** When AR is used as **collateral**, the receivables stay on the borrower’s books and a **liability** is recognized. Collections and bad debts continue to be recorded by the borrower; cash remitted services interest then principal until the note is settled.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Summit Ridge: assignment of AR as collateral
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition of secured borrowing; monthly interest; full settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Ridge Supply LLC** assigns accounts receivable to Cascade Credit Corp. as collateral.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Date | **May 1, Year 1** |
| AR assigned | **$240,000** |
| Advance rate | **75%** of assigned AR |
| Finance fee | **1%** of the **accounts receivable assigned** (deducted from proceeds) |
| Interest | **12%** per year on unpaid note balance |
| May 31 | Collect and remit **$100,000** cash from customers on assigned accounts (no returns or discounts this month for simplicity) |
| June 30 | Pay remaining loan balance in full (including June interest). No further customer remittance is applied in June before the separate payoff check. |

**Required:**  
a. May 1 journal entry for loan proceeds.  
b. May 31 entry to record remittance of $100,000 (interest + principal).  
c. Note payable roll-forward through June 30 payoff; June 30 payoff entry.  
d. Total finance fee + interest cost.

**Answer key:**  

**a. May 1 — initial recognition**  
Note payable = \(0.75 \times \$240{,}000 = \mathbf{\$180{,}000}\)  
Finance expense = \(0.01 \times \$240{,}000 = \mathbf{\$2{,}400}\)  
Cash = \(180{,}000 - 2{,}400 = \mathbf{\$177{,}600}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 177,600 | |
| Finance Expense | 2,400 | |
| Note Payable | | 180,000 |

**Check:** 177,600 + 2,400 = 180,000. Balanced.

**b. May 31 — remittance**  
Interest = \(180{,}000 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$1{,}800}\)  
Principal reduction = \(100{,}000 - 1{,}800 = \mathbf{\$98{,}200}\)  
Ending NP = \(180{,}000 - 98{,}200 = \mathbf{\$81{,}800}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 98,200 | |
| Interest Expense | 1,800 | |
| Cash | | 100,000 |

**Check:** 98,200 + 1,800 = 100,000. Balanced.

**c. Schedule and June 30 payoff**

| Date | Beg. NP | Interest | Cash paid | Principal red. | End. NP |
|------|--------:|---------:|----------:|---------------:|--------:|
| May 1 | — | — | — | — | 180,000 |
| May 31 | 180,000 | 1,800 | 100,000 | 98,200 | 81,800 |
| Jun 30 | 81,800 | 818 | 82,618 | 81,800 | 0 |

June interest = \(81{,}800 \times 0.12 \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$818}\).  
Cash to pay off = \(81{,}800 + 818 = \mathbf{\$82{,}618}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 81,800 | |
| Interest Expense | 818 | |
| Cash | | 82,618 |

**Check:** 81,800 + 818 = 82,618. Balanced.

**d. Total cost**  
Finance fee **$2,400** + interest \(1{,}800 + 818 = 2{,}618\) = **$5,018**.

**Key insight:** Same structure as Q1 with different advance rate, fee base (percent of AR assigned), and remittance amounts — recompute proceeds, monthly interest on **remaining** principal, and final settlement.

### Q3 — CORE — Harbor & Key: factoring without/with recourse; holdback and recourse settlement; note discounting
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Sale of receivables without and with recourse (initial JEs); settlement of holdback and recourse; discounting a note receivable  
**Scenario:**  
**Harbor & Key Merchants Co.** is evaluating three separate cash-acceleration alternatives on **March 1, Year 1**. Treat each as an independent case.

**Case A — Sale without recourse**  
Factors **$400,000** of accounts receivable **without recourse**. Factor charges a **10%** finance fee and retains **8%** of face AR for potential sales adjustments. Arrangement qualifies as a **sale**.

**Case B — Sale with recourse**  
Factors the same **$400,000** **with recourse**. Factor charges a **5%** finance fee and retains **10%** of face AR for sales adjustments. Harbor estimates a **recourse liability** of **$5,000** (no prior allowance for these accounts has been recorded). Arrangement qualifies as a **sale**.

**Case C — Subsequent settlement (extends Case A)**  
On **April 20**, actual sales returns/allowances on the factored accounts are **$22,000**. The factor remits the remaining holdback in cash.

**Case D — Discounting a note receivable (sale)**  
On **April 1, Year 1**, Harbor accepted a **$60,000, 6%, one-year** note receivable (interest due at maturity) from a customer. On **July 1, Year 1**, Harbor discounts the note **with recourse** to State Metro Bank. The bank charges **9%** on the **maturity value** for the **9 months** remaining to maturity. The discounting **qualifies as a sale**.

**Required:**  
a. Case A: seller’s March 1 journal entry.  
b. Case B: seller’s March 1 journal entry.  
c. Case C: April 20 entry to settle the receivable from factor.  
d. Case D: (i) July 1 entry to accrue interest to the discount date; (ii) computation of bank proceeds; (iii) entry to record the discounting.  
e. Briefly contrast balance-sheet effects of Case A vs a secured borrowing of the same AR.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Case A — sale without recourse (March 1)**  
Finance fee (loss) = \(0.10 \times \$400{,}000 = \mathbf{\$40{,}000}\)  
Holdback (Receivable from Factor) = \(0.08 \times \$400{,}000 = \mathbf{\$32{,}000}\)  
Cash = \(400{,}000 - 32{,}000 - 40{,}000 = \mathbf{\$328{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 328,000 | |
| Receivable from Factor | 32,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Receivables | 40,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 400,000 |
| *Derecognize AR sold without recourse; holdback for sales adjustments* | | |

**Check:** 328,000 + 32,000 + 40,000 = 400,000. Balanced.

**b. Case B — sale with recourse (March 1)**  
Finance fee = \(0.05 \times \$400{,}000 = \mathbf{\$20{,}000}\)  
Holdback = \(0.10 \times \$400{,}000 = \mathbf{\$40{,}000}\)  
Cash = \(400{,}000 - 40{,}000 - 20{,}000 = \mathbf{\$340{,}000}\)  
Loss on sale = finance fee + estimated recourse = \(20{,}000 + 5{,}000 = \mathbf{\$25{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 340,000 | |
| Receivable from Factor | 40,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Receivables | 25,000 | |
| Recourse Liability | | 5,000 |
| Accounts Receivable | | 400,000 |
| *Sale with recourse: loss includes finance fee and estimated recourse obligation* | | |

**Check:** 340,000 + 40,000 + 25,000 = 5,000 + 400,000 = 405,000. Balanced.

**c. Case C — settle factor holdback (April 20)**  
Holdback remaining after actual adjustments: \(32{,}000 - 22{,}000 = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\) cash from factor.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 10,000 | |
| Sales Returns and Allowances | 22,000 | |
| Receivable from Factor | | 32,000 |
| *Actual sales adjustments charged against holdback; remainder remitted* | | |

**Check:** 10,000 + 22,000 = 32,000. Balanced.

**d. Case D — discounting the note (July 1)**  

*(i) Accrue interest April 1 → July 1 (3 months):*  
\(\$60{,}000 \times 0.06 \times 3/12 = \mathbf{\$900}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Receivable | 900 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 900 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 900.

*(ii) Proceeds computation:*

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|-------:|
| Principal | 60,000 |
| Interest to maturity (\(60{,}000 \times 0.06 \times 12/12\)) | 3,600 |
| **Maturity value** | **63,600** |
| Bank discount (\(63{,}600 \times 0.09 \times 9/12\)) | 4,293 |
| **Proceeds to Harbor** | **59,307** |

*(iii) Record discounting as a sale:*  
Carrying amount of note + accrued interest = \(60{,}000 + 900 = \mathbf{\$60{,}900}\)  
Loss = \(60{,}900 - 59{,}307 = \mathbf{\$1{,}593}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 59,307 | |
| Loss on Sale of Note Receivable | 1,593 | |
| Note Receivable | | 60,000 |
| Interest Receivable | | 900 |
| *Remove note and accrued interest; difference vs proceeds is loss* | | |

**Check:** 59,307 + 1,593 = 60,000 + 900 = 60,900. Balanced.

**e. Presentation contrast**  
- **Case A (sale):** AR is **derecognized**; cash and receivable-from-factor recognized; loss on income statement; **no** loan liability.  
- **Secured borrowing of same AR:** AR **remains** an asset (often disclosed as assigned/pledged); cash and **note payable** recognized; finance fee and subsequent interest are borrowing costs, not a “loss on sale.”

**Key insight:** Without recourse, the factor’s fee is the entire seller loss (plus any later sales adjustments against the holdback). With recourse, estimated uncollectibles become a **recourse liability** added to the loss. Discounting a note is parallel: accrue interest to the transfer date, price the bank fee on **maturity value** for the remaining term, and remove the note at carrying amount.

### Q4 — MC — Sale vs secured borrowing classification
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Classification of a receivables arrangement as a sale vs secured borrowing  
**Question:**  
On June 30, **Pinecroft Distributors** transfers a portfolio of trade receivables to a finance company and receives cash. Which set of facts most strongly supports accounting for the transfer as a **sale** (rather than a secured borrowing)?

- A) Pinecroft pledges the receivables, continues to collect from customers, remits collections to the lender, and remains fully obligated to repay a note payable regardless of collections.  
- B) Pinecroft surrenders control: the receivables are isolated from Pinecroft, the finance company may pledge or exchange the receivables, and Pinecroft has no obligation to repurchase the receivables; a separate estimated recourse obligation, if any, is recognized as a liability.  
- C) Pinecroft assigns specific receivables under a promissory note that allows the lender to seek payment from receivable proceeds if Pinecroft defaults, but title and customer billing remain with Pinecroft.  
- D) The finance company withholds a holdback for sales adjustments and charges a monthly interest rate on an unpaid “loan” balance that Pinecroft must repay even if customers never pay.

**Answer:** B.  
Under ASC 860, a transfer is a **sale** only if the transferor **surrenders control** (isolation, buyer’s right to pledge/exchange, no effective repurchase obligation). A recourse obligation **by itself** does not preclude sale accounting. A, C, and D describe **secured borrowing / assignment / pledge** patterns: receivables remain the transferor’s assets and a liability is recognized for the financing.

### Q5 — MC — Presentation and disclosure of collateralized receivables
**LO:** LO 8-6  
**Concept:** Classification / presentation when receivables secure borrowing vs when sold  
**Question:**  
**Elm Street Appliances** uses **$90,000** of accounts receivable as **collateral** for a bank loan (assignment). At year-end the unpaid note is **$40,000** and **$55,000** of the assigned receivables remain uncollected. How should Elm Street present this arrangement?

- A) Remove the $90,000 of receivables from the balance sheet and report only the $40,000 note payable.  
- B) Continue to report the receivables (including remaining assigned amounts) as assets, report the $40,000 as a liability, and **disclose** that receivables serve as collateral for borrowings.  
- C) Reclassify the entire $55,000 remaining assigned AR as a reduction of the note payable (net presentation only).  
- D) Report a loss on sale equal to the difference between face AR and cash proceeds, and eliminate both AR and the note.

**Answer:** B.  
In a **secured borrowing**, the entity **retains** the receivables and records a **liability**. GAAP requires disclosure of receivables that serve as collateral. Netting AR against the note or treating the arrangement as a sale would misstate assets, liabilities, and income.
