# Agent 268 — CORE DEMO — LO 14-5

**Chapter:** 14  
**LO title:** Account for equity securities following the equity method  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** Dr Investment in Investee Stock; Cr Cash at cost when significant influence exists (presumptively 20%–50% voting stock; rebuttable)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** roll Investment = cost + share of investee NI − share of dividends − excess depreciation/amortization on depreciable basis differences (goodwill/land not amortized)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** (1) Dr Investment; Cr Investment Income for ownership % × investee NI; (2) Dr Investment Income; Cr Investment for excess depreciation on FV > BV of depreciable assets; (3) dividends reduce the Investment (not dividend revenue)
- **Ignore fair value:** unlike FV-NI, period-end market price changes are **not** recorded under the equity method (unless impairment under LO 14-6)
- **Disposal JE:** update Investment through disposal date (prorate NI and excess dep; include dividends during holding period), then remove carrying amount vs cash; difference = gain/loss
- **Classification / presentation:** typically noncurrent long-term investment; SCF — acquisition/sale usually investing; under indirect method, subtract (equity income − dividends received) from NI in operating section
- **Number-variant twin:** same mechanics, all different company names and amounts

### Q1 — CORE — Equity method with basis difference: initial recognition, period-end adjusting JEs, and investment schedule (emphasis)
**LO:** LO 14-5  
**Concept:** Period-end equity-method adjusting entries (share of NI, excess depreciation on basis difference, dividends as return of investment) and investment account rollforward  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Harbor Holdings** (calendar-year) acquires a long-term equity interest in **Meridian Forge Co.** on **January 1, Year 1**. Cascade can exercise **significant influence** and applies the **equity method**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase price (Jan 1, Year 1) | **$240,000** |
| Ownership interest | **30%** of Meridian’s voting common stock |
| Meridian stockholders’ equity (book value of net assets) at acquisition | **$700,000** |
| Fair value of Meridian’s net assets exceeds book value solely due to undervalued **equipment** | Equipment undervalued by **$40,000** total on Meridian’s books |
| Remaining useful life of the undervalued equipment | **8 years**, straight-line, no residual |
| Remainder of Cascade’s cost over share of fair value of net assets | Attributable to **goodwill** (not amortized) |
| Meridian net income for Year 1 | **$80,000** |
| Meridian cash dividends declared and paid Dec 31, Year 1 | **$20,000** |
| Fair value of Cascade’s 30% interest at Dec 31, Year 1 | **$255,000** |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry. Briefly support why the equity method applies.  
b. Allocate the **$240,000** cost among share of book value, depreciable basis difference, and goodwill.  
c. Record all **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting** journal entries under the equity method (share of NI, excess depreciation, dividends). State whether any fair-value adjustment is recorded.  
d. Prepare the **subsequent measurement (Investment) schedule** for Year 1 and compute the Dec 31 carrying amount.  
e. Compute **net equity-method income** recognized in Year 1 (investment income after excess depreciation).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1 — Purchase equity-method investment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Meridian Forge Stock | 240,000 | |
| Cash | | 240,000 |
| *Acquisition at cost; significant influence → equity method* | | |

**Check:** Dr 240,000 = Cr 240,000. **Balanced.**

**Why equity method:** 30% voting interest is within the 20%–50% range that **presumes significant influence** (rebuttable). Cascade does not control Meridian (would require consolidation), so equity method is the core measurement model.

**b. Cost allocation**

| Component | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Share of Meridian book value (stockholders’ equity) | \(0.30 \times \$700{,}000\) | **210,000** |
| Share of equipment undervaluation (depreciable basis difference) | \(0.30 \times \$40{,}000\) | **12,000** |
| Goodwill (plug) | \(240{,}000 - 210{,}000 - 12{,}000\) | **18,000** |
| **Total cost** | | **240,000** |

**Annual excess depreciation (equipment only):** \( \$12{,}000 \div 8 = \mathbf{\$1{,}500}\) per year.  
Goodwill: **not amortized**.

**c. Period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis)**

*December 31, Year 1 — Share of investee net income*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Meridian Forge Stock | 24,000 | |
| Investment Income | | 24,000 |
| *\(0.30 \times \$80{,}000\)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 24,000 = Cr 24,000. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 1 — Excess depreciation on depreciable basis difference*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment Income | 1,500 | |
| Investment in Meridian Forge Stock | | 1,500 |
| *\( \$12{,}000 \div 8 \)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,500 = Cr 1,500. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 1 — Investor’s share of dividends (return of investment, not revenue)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 6,000 | |
| Investment in Meridian Forge Stock | | 6,000 |
| *\(0.30 \times \$20{,}000\)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,000 = Cr 6,000. **Balanced.**

**Fair value at Dec 31 ($255,000):** **No entry.** Under the equity method, unrealized changes in fair value are ignored (contrast with FV-NI).

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule — Investment in Meridian Forge**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| January 1, Year 1 cost | 240,000 |
| + Share of Meridian NI | 24,000 |
| − Excess depreciation (equipment basis difference) | (1,500) |
| − Share of dividends | (6,000) |
| **December 31, Year 1 carrying amount** | **256,500** |

**Roll-forward check:** \(240{,}000 + 24{,}000 - 1{,}500 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$256{,}500}\). ✓  
(Note: market value $255,000 is **not** the carrying amount.)

**e. Net equity-method income (Year 1)**  
\( \$24{,}000 - \$1{,}500 = \mathbf{\$22{,}500} \).

**Key insight:** Period-end equity-method adjusting entries **increase** the investment for the investor’s share of earnings and **decrease** it for excess depreciation on depreciable FV–BV differences and for dividends. Dividends are **not** dividend revenue when significant influence exists. Fair value is ignored unless impaired.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Equity method with basis difference (all numbers changed)
**LO:** LO 14-5  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: initial recognition; period-end equity-method adjustments (NI share, excess dep, dividends); investment schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**Northwind Analytics LLC** (calendar-year) purchases **25%** of the voting common stock of **Solstice Components Inc.** on **January 1, Year 1** for cash and obtains significant influence. Equity method applies.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase price | **$180,000** |
| Ownership | **25%** |
| Solstice stockholders’ equity at acquisition | **$600,000** |
| Depreciable plant assets undervalued on Solstice’s books (total undervaluation) | **$48,000** |
| Remaining life of those plant assets | **6 years**, SL, no residual |
| Excess of cost over share of FV of identifiable net assets | **Goodwill** (not amortized) |
| Solstice Year 1 net income | **$96,000** |
| Solstice cash dividends declared and paid Dec 31, Year 1 | **$24,000** |
| Fair value of Northwind’s 25% interest at Dec 31 | **$200,000** |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1** purchase entry.  
b. Allocate cost to book-value share, depreciable basis difference, and goodwill; compute **annual excess depreciation**.  
c. Record **all December 31 period-end** equity-method entries (NI, excess dep, dividends). Confirm treatment of the $200,000 fair value.  
d. Prepare the Year 1 **Investment schedule** and ending carrying amount.  
e. Compute net equity-method income for Year 1.  
f. Under the **indirect method** SCF, what net adjustment related to this investment appears in the operating section for Year 1 (sign and amount)? Briefly explain.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Solstice Components Stock | 180,000 | |
| Cash | | 180,000 |

**Check:** Dr 180,000 = Cr 180,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Cost allocation and excess depreciation**

| Component | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Share of book value | \(0.25 \times \$600{,}000\) | **150,000** |
| Share of plant undervaluation | \(0.25 \times \$48{,}000\) | **12,000** |
| Goodwill | \(180{,}000 - 150{,}000 - 12{,}000\) | **18,000** |
| **Total cost** | | **180,000** |

**Annual excess depreciation:** \( \$12{,}000 \div 6 = \mathbf{\$2{,}000}\).

**c. Period-end adjusting JEs**

*Share of NI — \(0.25 \times \$96{,}000 = \$24{,}000\)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Solstice Components Stock | 24,000 | |
| Investment Income | | 24,000 |

**Check:** Dr 24,000 = Cr 24,000. **Balanced.**

*Excess depreciation — $2,000*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment Income | 2,000 | |
| Investment in Solstice Components Stock | | 2,000 |

**Check:** Dr 2,000 = Cr 2,000. **Balanced.**

*Dividends — \(0.25 \times \$24{,}000 = \$6{,}000\)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 6,000 | |
| Investment in Solstice Components Stock | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr 6,000 = Cr 6,000. **Balanced.**

**Fair value $200,000:** **No fair-value adjustment** under the equity method.

**d. Investment schedule**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cost, January 1 | 180,000 |
| + Share of NI | 24,000 |
| − Excess depreciation | (2,000) |
| − Share of dividends | (6,000) |
| **Carrying amount, December 31** | **196,000** |

**Check:** \(180{,}000 + 24{,}000 - 2{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$196{,}000}\). ✓

**e. Net equity-method income**  
\( \$24{,}000 - \$2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$22{,}000} \).

**f. SCF operating (indirect method)**  
Equity-method income in NI (net of excess dep) = **$22,000**; cash dividends received = **$6,000**.  
Adjustment: **subtract $16,000** (\(22{,}000 - 6{,}000\)) from net income in the operating section so that operating cash reflects only cash dividends received. Acquisition cash outflow of $180,000 is **investing**.

**Key insight:** Same equity-method engine as Q1 with new numbers: period-end entries always hit the Investment account for earnings, excess dep, and dividends; FV is ignored; SCF strips noncash equity income down to cash dividends.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Partial-period equity method and disposal (sale)
**LO:** LO 14-5  
**Concept:** Update equity-method carrying amount through disposal date (prorate share of NI and excess dep; dividends during holding period), then record disposal JE and classify gain/loss; balance-sheet / SCF presentation notes  
**Scenario:**  
**Lumen Peak Capital** has accounted for its investment in **Alder Ridge Media** under the **equity method** since acquisition. Lumen owns **35%** of Alder’s voting stock and has significant influence.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Carrying amount of Investment in Alder Ridge, January 1, Year 2 | **$280,000** |
| Alder net income for the **nine months ended September 30, Year 2** (through disposal date) | **$90,000** |
| Alder cash dividends declared and paid **March 20, Year 2** (while Lumen still held the shares) | **$40,000** total by Alder |
| Annual excess depreciation on Lumen’s depreciable basis difference (full year) | **$2,800** |
| On **October 1, Year 2**, Lumen sells its **entire** Alder investment for cash | **$305,000** |
| After the sale, Lumen has no remaining interest in Alder | — |

Assume excess depreciation and share of earnings are recognized through the disposal date on a time-proportion basis (9/12 of the annual excess depreciation). Ignore taxes.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **period-end / pre-disposal adjusting** entries on (or effective as of) **September 30 / October 1, Year 2** for: (1) share of Alder’s NI for the nine months held, (2) excess depreciation for nine months, and (3) Lumen’s share of the March dividends.  
b. Compute the **carrying amount immediately before sale**.  
c. Record the **October 1, Year 2 disposal** journal entry.  
d. **Classification / presentation:** (1) Before sale, is the investment typically current or noncurrent? (2) How is the $305,000 cash proceeds classified on the statement of cash flows (assuming the investment was held for strategic long-term influence, not short-term trading)? (3) Does the gain on sale affect OCI or net income?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Pre-disposal equity-method updates**

*(1) Share of NI for nine months held — \(0.35 \times \$90{,}000 = \$31{,}500\)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Alder Ridge Media Stock | 31,500 | |
| Investment Income | | 31,500 |

**Check:** Dr 31,500 = Cr 31,500. **Balanced.**

*(2) Excess depreciation for 9/12 year — \( \$2{,}800 \times 9/12 = \$2{,}100 \)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment Income | 2,100 | |
| Investment in Alder Ridge Media Stock | | 2,100 |

**Check:** Dr 2,100 = Cr 2,100. **Balanced.**

*(3) Share of March dividends — \(0.35 \times \$40{,}000 = \$14{,}000\)*  
*(Recorded March 20, Year 2; shown here as part of the holding-period updates)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 14,000 | |
| Investment in Alder Ridge Media Stock | | 14,000 |

**Check:** Dr 14,000 = Cr 14,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Carrying amount immediately before sale**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| January 1, Year 2 carrying amount | 280,000 |
| + Share of NI (9 months) | 31,500 |
| − Excess depreciation (9/12) | (2,100) |
| − Share of dividends | (14,000) |
| **Carrying amount at disposal** | **295,400** |

**Check:** \(280{,}000 + 31{,}500 - 2{,}100 - 14{,}000 = \mathbf{\$295{,}400}\). ✓

**c. Disposal JE**

*October 1, Year 2 — Sell entire equity-method investment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 305,000 | |
| Investment in Alder Ridge Media Stock | | 295,400 |
| Gain on Sale of Equity-Method Investment | | 9,600 |
| *Gain = \(305{,}000 - 295{,}400\)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 305,000 = Cr 295,400 + 9,600. **Balanced.**

**d. Classification / presentation**  
1. **Noncurrent** (long-term strategic equity-method affiliate) until the sale is imminent / completed.  
2. Cash proceeds **$305,000** → **investing inflow** (not operating; investment was not held for short-term trading profits).  
3. Gain of **$9,600** is recognized in **net income** (not OCI). Equity-method investments are not marked through OCI.

**Key insight:** Before disposal, bring the Investment account current through the sale date (prorated NI and excess dep; dividends during the holding period only). The disposal entry removes that updated carrying amount against cash; any difference is a gain or loss in earnings.

### Q4 — MC — Equity method vs FV-NI classification (method choice)
**LO:** LO 14-5  
**Concept:** Classification of measurement method — when equity method applies vs FV-NI; period-end dividend treatment  

**Question 1:**  
On January 1, **Harborline Industries** acquires **22%** of the voting common stock of **Pinecrest Outfitters** for cash and can exercise significant influence over Pinecrest’s operating and financial policies. Harborline intends to hold the investment indefinitely. Which measurement model does Harborline apply, and how does it record its share of Pinecrest’s Year 1 cash dividend?

- A) FV-NI; debit Cash, credit Dividend Revenue  
- B) Equity method; debit Cash, credit Dividend Revenue  
- C) Equity method; debit Cash, credit Investment in Pinecrest Stock  
- D) Consolidation; no separate Investment account is reported  

**Answer:** **C.** Ownership of 22% with significant influence → **equity method** (presumption at ≥20%, supported here by facts). Under the equity method, dividends are a **return of investment** (reduce the Investment account), not dividend revenue. Consolidation requires **control** (typically >50%). FV-NI applies when influence is **insignificant**.

---

**Question 2:**  
**Summit Vale Corp.** uses the equity method for a 40% investee. At year-end the fair value of the investment exceeds its equity-method carrying amount by $15,000. Summit Vale has not elected the fair value option. What period-end entry, if any, is required solely for that fair-value difference?

- A) Debit Fair Value Adjustment $15,000; credit Unrealized Gain—Income $15,000  
- B) Debit Investment $15,000; credit Unrealized Gain—OCI $15,000  
- C) Debit Investment $15,000; credit Investment Income $15,000  
- D) No entry for the fair-value difference  

**Answer:** **D.** Under the equity method **without** the fair value option, changes in fair value are **ignored** (unless impairment applies under LO 14-6). Options A–C confuse equity method with FV-NI or with share-of-earnings entries.
