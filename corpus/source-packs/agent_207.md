# Agent 207 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-10

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Record acquisition and depletion of natural resources  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Depletion cost base:** capitalize acquisition, exploration (as applicable), development, and PV of restoration (ARO); residual (salvage) land value **reduces** the base
- **Initial recognition JE:** debit Natural Resource (Mine/Quarry/Timberland); credit Cash and Asset Retirement Obligation
- **Units-of-production depletion:** rate = depletion base ÷ estimated recoverable units; period depletion = rate × units **extracted** (not sold)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-year rollforward of extraction, depletion, accumulated depletion, carrying amount, inventory tons/dollars, and COGS on units sold
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** Dr Inventory / Cr Natural Resource (or Accumulated Depletion); Dr COGS / Cr Inventory for units sold
- **Prospective change in estimate:** revise unit rate as remaining carrying amount (net of residual) ÷ revised remaining recoverable units
- **Disposal / settlement:** sell residual land (and undepleted resource if any); settle ARO at actual restoration cash
- **Classification:** natural resource within PPE (gross less accum. depletion); extracted inventory is a **current** asset; movable multi-use equipment is separate PPE (not in depletion base)
- **Oil & gas exploration methods (core Demo 12-10B):** successful-efforts vs full-cost — method choice changes the capitalized base

### Q1 — CORE — Zinc mine: initial recognition, multi-year depletion schedule, period-end JEs, residual land & ARO closeout

**Scenario:**  
**Silverpine Mining LLC** (calendar year) acquires and opens a zinc mine on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|

| PV of legally required restoration (ARO) | **$300,000** |
| Estimated residual land value after zinc removed | **$300,000** |
| Estimated recoverable zinc ore | **2,100,000 tons** |
| Movable haul trucks (alternative uses on other sites) | **$420,000** cash; **6-year** SL life; **$0** residual; full-year convention |

Silverpine credits **Mine Property** directly for depletion (no Accumulated Depletion account). Haul trucks are **PPE**, depreciated separately — **not** in the depletion base. Ignore ARO accretion (Ch. 11). Inventory of extracted ore is carried at unit depletion cost (FIFO not needed — constant rate until estimates change; none here until fully depleted).

On **December 31, Year 5**, after Year-5 depletion and COGS:  
- Sell residual land for **$285,000 cash**.  
- Settle the ARO by paying **$310,000 cash** for restoration.

**Required:**

b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** period-end entries for (1) depletion of ore extracted, (2) cost of goods sold for ore sold, and (3) depreciation of haul trucks.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–5 showing: tons extracted, depletion (→ Inventory), accumulated depletion, carrying amount of Mine Property, ending inventory tons, and ending inventory dollars. Confirm total tons extracted = 2,100,000 and residual carrying amount of Mine Property before land sale = $300,000.  
d. Compute **COGS** for Years 2–5.  
e. Prepare the **December 31, Year 5 disposal / settlement** entries for (1) sale of residual land and (2) settlement of the ARO.  
f. Briefly state Year-1 year-end **classification** of Mine Property, Inventory—Zinc Ore, haul trucks, and ARO.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

- Undepleted resource after Y4: \((2{,}100{,}000 - 1{,}750{,}000) \times 2 = 350{,}000 \times 2 = 700{,}000\); + residual \(300{,}000\) = carrying amount **$1,000,000**. ✓

**d. COGS Years 2–5**  
- Year 2: \(460{,}000 \times 2.00 = \mathbf{\$920{,}000}\)  
- Year 3: \(490{,}000 \times 2.00 = \mathbf{\$980{,}000}\)  
- Year 4: \(420{,}000 \times 2.00 = \mathbf{\$840{,}000}\)  
- Year 5: \(350{,}000 \times 2.00 = \mathbf{\$700{,}000}\)  
- Sum of all COGS (Y1–Y5) = \(760{+}920{+}980{+}840{+}700 = \mathbf{\$4{,}200{,}000}\) = depletion base (all resource cost flows through inventory to COGS; residual stays until land sale).

|---|---:|---:|

| *Sell residual land; remove remaining mine carrying amount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 285,000 + 15,000 = Cr 300,000. **Balanced.**

*Settle ARO* (book value of ARO = $300,000; cash paid $310,000)  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Asset Retirement Obligation | 300,000 | |
| Loss on Settlement of ARO | 10,000 | |
| Cash | | 310,000 |
| *Pay restoration costs; difference is loss* | | |

**Check:** Dr 300,000 + 10,000 = Cr 310,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Classification (Dec 31, Year 1)**  
- **Mine Property** carrying amount **$3,700,000** — noncurrent PPE / natural resources (cost $4,500,000 less $800,000 depletion credited directly).  
- **Inventory—Zinc Ore** **$40,000** — **current** asset.  
- **Equipment—Haul Trucks** gross $420,000 less Accum. Deprec. $70,000 = **$350,000** net — noncurrent PPE (separate from natural resource).  
- **ARO** **$300,000** — typically noncurrent liability.  
- Depletion of sold tons is in **COGS**; truck depreciation is operating expense (or inventoriable product cost if manufacturing).

**Key insight:** At initial recognition, all costs that create the right to extract (acquisition, successful exploration, development, PV of restoration) are capitalized; residual land is **excluded from the depletion base**. The **subsequent measurement schedule** is pure units-of-production: each period move cost Mine Property → Inventory on **extraction**, then Inventory → COGS on **sales**. Movable multi-use equipment stays outside the base. At life’s end, residual land is disposed of and the ARO is settled in cash.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Coal mine twin (schedule-focused)

| PV of restoration obligation (ARO) | **$500,000** |

| Estimated recoverable coal | **4,000,000 tons** |

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Prospective change in recoverable units; revised schedule
**LO:** LO 12-10  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement of natural resource after a **prospective** change in estimated recoverable units; revised unit rate from remaining carrying amount ÷ revised remaining units; period-end depletion and COGS under old and new rates  
**Scenario:**  
**Redrock Minerals Inc.** places a mineral property in service on **January 1, Year 1** with the following facts (already recorded — no ARO in this problem):

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Capitalized Mineral Property cost (Jan 1, Year 1) | **$5,000,000** |
| Residual value | **$0** |
| Original estimated recoverable units | **1,000,000 tons** |
| Original unit rate | **$5.00 per ton** |
| Year 1: tons extracted / sold | **200,000 / 180,000** |
| Year 2: tons extracted / sold | **200,000 / 210,000** |

On **January 1, Year 3**, before any Year-3 extraction, engineers revise the estimate of **remaining** recoverable tons from **600,000** to **750,000**. No change in residual ($0). Changes in estimate are accounted for **prospectively**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Year 3: tons extracted / sold | **250,000 / 240,000** |
| Year 4: tons extracted / sold | **250,000 / 260,000** |
| Year 5: tons extracted / sold | **250,000 / 260,000** |

Redrock credits **Mineral Property** directly for depletion.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare Year 1 and Year 2 **period-end** depletion and COGS journal entries (use the original $5.00 rate). Show carrying amount of Mineral Property and ending inventory at Dec 31, Year 2.  
b. Compute the **revised unit depletion rate** effective January 1, Year 3.  
c. Prepare a **Years 1–5 subsequent measurement schedule** (extraction, depletion, accum. depletion, carrying amount, EI tons, EI $) reflecting the prospective rate change.  
d. Prepare Year 3 period-end depletion and COGS entries under the revised rate.  
e. Prove that total depletion over the life equals the original $5,000,000 capitalized cost.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Years 1–2 at $5.00/ton**

*Year 1*  
Depletion = \(200{,}000 \times 5 = \mathbf{\$1{,}000{,}000}\)  
COGS = \(180{,}000 \times 5 = \mathbf{\$900{,}000}\)  
EI = \(20{,}000 \times 5 = \mathbf{\$100{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Minerals | 1,000,000 | |
| Mineral Property | | 1,000,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,000,000.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 900,000 | |
| Inventory—Minerals | | 900,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 900,000.

Carrying amount end Y1: \(5{,}000{,}000 - 1{,}000{,}000 = \mathbf{\$4{,}000{,}000}\).

*Year 2*  
Depletion = \(200{,}000 \times 5 = \mathbf{\$1{,}000{,}000}\)  
COGS = \(210{,}000 \times 5 = \mathbf{\$1{,}050{,}000}\)  
EI tons: \(20{,}000 + 200{,}000 - 210{,}000 = \mathbf{10{,}000}\); EI $ = \(10{,}000 \times 5 = \mathbf{\$50{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Minerals | 1,000,000 | |
| Mineral Property | | 1,000,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,000,000.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 1,050,000 | |
| Inventory—Minerals | | 1,050,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,050,000.  
(Note: COGS can exceed current-period depletion when beginning inventory is sold — schedule tracks the flow.)

Carrying amount end Y2: \(4{,}000{,}000 - 1{,}000{,}000 = \mathbf{\$3{,}000{,}000}\).  
Accumulated depletion end Y2: **$2,000,000**. Remaining units under **old** estimate: 600,000.

**b. Revised rate (prospective, Jan 1, Year 3)**

\[
\text{Revised unit rate} = \frac{\text{Remaining carrying amount}}{\text{Revised remaining recoverable units}} = \frac{\$3{,}000{,}000}{750{,}000} = \mathbf{\$4.00 \text{ per ton}}
\]

No restatement of Years 1–2. Ending inventory at Dec 31, Year 2 remains at **historical** unit cost of $5.00 ($50,000); only **future** extractions use $4.00. (When Y2 EI is sold in Y3, COGS for those tons uses $5.00; new extractions use $4.00.)

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule (prospective change at start of Y3)**

| Year | Rate | Tons extracted | Depletion | Accum. depletion | Carrying amt | EI (tons) | EI ($) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | — | — | — | 0 | 5,000,000 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | $5.00 | 200,000 | 1,000,000 | 1,000,000 | 4,000,000 | 20,000 | 100,000 |
| 2 | $5.00 | 200,000 | 1,000,000 | 2,000,000 | 3,000,000 | 10,000 | 50,000 |
| 3 | $4.00 | 250,000 | 1,000,000 | 3,000,000 | 2,000,000 | 20,000 | 80,000 |
| 4 | $4.00 | 250,000 | 1,000,000 | 4,000,000 | 1,000,000 | 10,000 | 40,000 |
| 5 | $4.00 | 250,000 | 1,000,000 | 5,000,000 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**Y3 inventory dollars (mixed cost layers, FIFO):**  
- Beg. inv.: 10,000 @ $5.00 = $50,000  
- Additions: 250,000 @ $4.00 = $1,000,000  
- Sold 240,000: first 10,000 @ $5 = $50,000 + 230,000 @ $4 = $920,000 → **COGS $970,000**  
- EI: 20,000 @ $4.00 = **$80,000**

**Y4 inventory:**  
- Beg.: 20,000 @ $4 = $80,000  
- Add: 250,000 @ $4 = $1,000,000  
- Sold 260,000 @ $4 = **COGS $1,040,000**  
- EI: 10,000 @ $4 = **$40,000**

**Y5 inventory:**  
- Beg.: 10,000 @ $4 = $40,000  
- Add: 250,000 @ $4 = $1,000,000  
- Sold 260,000 @ $4 = **COGS $1,040,000**  
- EI: **0**

**Resource check:** Revised remaining at Y3 start = 750,000. Extracted Y3–Y5 = \(250{,}000 + 250{,}000 + 250{,}000 = 750{,}000\). Mineral Property carrying amount end Y5 = **$0**. Inventory also fully sold.

**d. Year 3 period-end entries (revised rate on extraction; FIFO for COGS)**

*Depletion*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Minerals | 1,000,000 | |
| Mineral Property | | 1,000,000 |
| *250,000 × $4.00* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,000,000.

*COGS (FIFO)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 970,000 | |
| Inventory—Minerals | | 970,000 |
| *10,000 × $5 + 230,000 × $4* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 970,000.

**e. Life total depletion**  
\(1{,}000{,}000 + 1{,}000{,}000 + 1{,}000{,}000 + 1{,}000{,}000 + 1{,}000{,}000 = \mathbf{\$5{,}000{,}000}\) = original capitalized cost. ✓  
(Change in estimate changes **timing** of depletion per ton, not total cost to be allocated when residual is zero.)

**Key insight:** Estimate changes for natural resources are **prospective**. Recompute rate as **remaining book value (net of residual) ÷ revised remaining recoverable units**. Past depletion is not restated; the subsequent measurement schedule shows the kink at the revision date. Inventory layers may mix old and new unit costs.

### Q4 — MC (method / classification)
**LO:** LO 12-10  
**Concept:** Successful-efforts vs full-cost — amount capitalized to oil and gas properties  
**Question:**  
**Northfork Energy Corp.** drills **five** exploratory wells at **$1,200,000** cash each (total outlay **$6,000,000**). **One** well is successful; **four** are dry. Estimated fair value of reserves in the successful well is **$15,000,000**. Under the **successful-efforts** method, what amount is capitalized to Oil and Gas Properties for these wells?

- A) $0  
- B) $1,200,000  
- C) $4,800,000  
- D) $6,000,000  

**Answer:** **B.** Successful-efforts capitalizes only the successful well (**$1,200,000**); dry-hole costs of \(4 \times 1{,}200{,}000 = \$4{,}800{,}000\) are expensed as exploration expense. Full-cost would capitalize all **$6,000,000** because \(6{,}000{,}000 < 15{,}000{,}000\) reserve value.  
**Check:** classification/method choice (Demo 12-10B path), not a multi-line JE pack.

### Q5 — MC (depletion base vs separate PPE)
**LO:** LO 12-10  
**Concept:** Which costs enter the depletion base vs separate PPE (movable multi-use equipment)  
**Question:**  
On development of a silver mine, which cost is **least** likely to be included in the **depletion base** of the natural resource?

- A) Cost of mineral rights (acquisition)  
- B) Cost of a permanent mineshaft with no alternative use  
- C) Present value of legally required site restoration  
- D) Cost of movable haul trucks that will be reassigned to other mines after this deposit is exhausted  

**Answer:** **D.** Movable assets with **alternative uses** are recorded as **PPE** and depreciated over their own useful lives; they are **not** added to the natural-resource depletion base. A–C are classic components of the depletion cost base (acquisition, development without alternative use, restoration PV). Residual value would **reduce** the base but is not asked here.  
**Check:** cost classification for depletion base.
