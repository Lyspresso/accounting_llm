# Agent 98 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-5

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Describe accounting for subsequent events and contingencies including litigation, warranties, and other contingencies  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** accrue loss contingency when **probable** and **reasonably estimable** (Dr loss/expense, Cr liability or contra-asset)
- Range of loss: accrue **best estimate** within range; if no amount is better, accrue the **minimum** and disclose exposure above minimum
- **Assurance-type warranty:** estimate and fully recognize warranty expense in year of sale; liability reduced when actual costs incurred
- **Service-type warranty:** cash to deferred revenue at sale; recognize revenue (typically SL) over coverage; expense costs as incurred
- **Subsequent measurement / liability schedule:** warranty liability roll-forward (beg + accruals − actual costs = end)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** year-end warranty accrual; year-end litigation accrual using all information available through FS issuance date
- **Settlement JE:** remove estimated liability; difference vs cash paid is additional loss (or gain) as change in estimate
- **Recognized subsequent events:** conditions existing at BS date → adjust Year 1 accounts (e.g., post-year-end lawsuit settlement of a pre-year-end accident)
- **Nonrecognized subsequent events:** new conditions after BS date → disclose if material/misleading otherwise (no Year 1 accrual)
- Gain contingencies: **never accrue**; may disclose carefully
- Classification: remote → generally neither accrue nor disclose (except certain guarantees); reasonably possible → disclose; probable + estimable → accrue

### Q1 — CORE — Litigation initial recognition, settlement, range, and assurance warranty life cycle
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of probable/estimable litigation loss; settlement JE; range-minimum accrual; assurance warranty period-end accrual and multi-period liability schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverton Fabrication Inc.** (calendar year) issues its Year 1 financial statements on **March 5, Year 2**. Ignore income taxes.

**Litigation A — estimate then settle after FS issued**  
- Industrial accident and lawsuit both occurred in **Year 1**.  
- At December 31, Year 1, counsel concludes a loss is **probable** and the **best estimate** of loss is **$175,000** (plaintiff seeks $400,000).  
- On **April 18, Year 2** (after FS issuance), Riverton settles for **$190,000** cash.

**Litigation B — range with no better estimate**  
- Separate Year 1 environmental claim; loss is **probable**.  
- Estimated range of loss: **$60,000 to $220,000**; **no amount within the range is a better estimate** than any other.

**Assurance-type warranty**  
- Year 1 cash product sales: **$2,400,000** (ignore COGS). Selling price includes a 2-year assurance warranty against manufacturing defects.  
- Experience: warranty costs ≈ **1.25% of sales**.  
- Actual warranty repair costs: **$11,000** in Year 1; **$20,500** in Year 2 (no new warranty accrual for Year 2 sales in this problem).  
- Riverton accrues estimated warranty cost at **year-end** and records actual costs when incurred.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** journal entry for **initial recognition** of Litigation A.  
b. Prepare the **April 18, Year 2 settlement** journal entry for Litigation A.  
c. For Litigation B: compute the amount to **accrue** at December 31, Year 1; prepare the JE; state what (if anything) is **disclosed** for exposure above the accrual.  
d. Prepare all **Year 1** journal entries for sales and the assurance warranty (sale, year-end accrual, actual costs).  
e. Prepare a **warranty liability roll-forward schedule** for Year 1 and Year 2 (related only to Year 1 sales), and the **Year 2** journal entry for actual warranty costs.  
f. Briefly classify Litigation A under ASC 450 if instead the loss were only **reasonably possible** (not probable) at year-end—accrue, disclose only, or neither?

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition — Litigation A (probable + reasonably estimable)**

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrue loss contingency*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation Settlement | 175,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 175,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 175,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Settlement after FS issuance (change in estimate)**

Cash paid $190,000 − liability $175,000 = **additional loss $15,000**.

*April 18, Year 2 — Settle litigation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated Litigation Liability | 175,000 | |
| Loss on Litigation Settlement | 15,000 | |
| Cash | | 190,000 |

**Check:** Dr 175,000 + 15,000 = Cr 190,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Range — no better estimate → accrue minimum**

Accrue **minimum** of range = **$60,000**.  
Disclose nature of contingency and **possible additional loss** of up to **$160,000** ($220,000 − $60,000), or state that estimate of additional loss is not possible if that were the case (here the excess range is known).

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrue minimum of range*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Contingency (Environmental) | 60,000 | |
| Estimated Contingency Liability | | 60,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 60,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Year 1 sales and assurance warranty**

Estimated warranty cost = $2,400,000 × 1.25% = **$30,000**.

*Year 1 — Product sales (assurance warranty included in price)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 2,400,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 2,400,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 2,400,000.

*December 31, Year 1 — Period-end warranty accrual (initial recognition of warranty liability)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 30,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 30,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 30,000.

*Year 1 — Actual warranty costs incurred*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 11,000 | |
| Cash (and other resources) | | 11,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 11,000.

Ending warranty liability, Dec 31 Year 1: $30,000 − $11,000 = **$19,000**.

**e. Warranty liability schedule and Year 2 settlement of remaining obligation**

| Year | Beginning liability | + Accrual | − Actual costs | Ending liability |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 30,000 | 11,000 | 19,000 |
| 2 | 19,000 | 0 | 20,500 | 0* |

\*Carrying amount before excess: $19,000; cash costs $20,500 → **additional warranty expense $1,500** (change in estimate). Ending liability after entry = **$0**.

*Year 2 — Actual warranty costs (exhaust liability; excess to expense)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 19,000 | |
| Warranty Expense | 1,500 | |
| Cash (and other resources) | | 20,500 |

**Check:** Dr 19,000 + 1,500 = Cr 20,500. **Balanced.**

**Roll-forward check:** Total costs related to Year 1 sales = $11,000 + $20,500 = **$31,500**; total expense over two years = $30,000 + $1,500 = **$31,500**.

**f. Reasonably possible (not probable)**  
**Disclose only** — no accrual. Notes should describe the nature of the contingency and an estimate of loss (or range), or state that an estimate cannot be made.

**Key insight:** **Initial recognition** of a loss contingency requires both **probable** outcome and a **reasonable estimate**. The first JE establishes the liability; later settlement or revised experience is a **change in estimate** (extra loss or gain), not a prior-period restatement. Assurance warranties are accrued fully in the **sale year** even though cash outflows span later periods.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Litigation, range, and assurance warranty twin
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: initial litigation accrual, settlement with gain, range-minimum accrual, assurance warranty schedule and settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**SummitForge Appliances LLC** (calendar year) issues Year 1 financial statements on **February 28, Year 2**.

**Litigation A**  
- Suit filed in Year 1 for a Year 1 product injury. Loss is **probable**; best estimate at Dec 31, Year 1: **$420,000**.  
- Settled **May 12, Year 2** for **$395,000** cash (after FS issued).

**Litigation B — range**  
- Probable loss; range **$90,000–$350,000**; **no better estimate** within the range.

**Assurance warranty**  
- Year 1 cash sales: **$6,800,000** (ignore COGS).  
- Estimated warranty costs: **0.80% of sales**.  
- Actual warranty costs: Year 1 **$22,000**; Year 2 **$35,000** (no Year 2 sales accrual in this problem).

**Required:**  
a. December 31, Year 1 **initial recognition** JE for Litigation A.  
b. May 12, Year 2 **settlement** JE for Litigation A.  
c. Accrual amount and JE for Litigation B; disclosure of excess.  
d. Year 1 warranty sale, accrual, and actual-cost JEs; ending liability.  
e. Warranty liability schedule (Years 1–2) and Year 2 actual-cost JE.

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition — Litigation A**

*December 31, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation Settlement | 420,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 420,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 420,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Settlement with gain (cash < accrued liability)**

Gain = $420,000 − $395,000 = **$25,000**.

*May 12, Year 2*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated Litigation Liability | 420,000 | |
| Gain on Litigation Settlement | | 25,000 |
| Cash | | 395,000 |

**Check:** Dr 420,000 = Cr 25,000 + 395,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Range — accrue minimum $90,000**

Disclose possible additional loss up to **$260,000** ($350,000 − $90,000).

*December 31, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Contingency | 90,000 | |
| Estimated Contingency Liability | | 90,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 90,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Year 1 warranty**

Estimated warranty = $6,800,000 × 0.80% = **$54,400**.

*Sale*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 6,800,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 6,800,000 |

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrual*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 54,400 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 54,400 |

*Year 1 actual costs*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 22,000 | |
| Cash (and other resources) | | 22,000 |

Ending liability Year 1: $54,400 − $22,000 = **$32,400**.

All three entries balance (Dr = Cr).

**e. Schedule and Year 2**

| Year | Beginning | + Accrual | − Costs | Ending |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 54,400 | 22,000 | 32,400 |
| 2 | 32,400 | 0 | 35,000 | 0* |

\*Excess cost over remaining liability: $35,000 − $32,400 = **$2,600** additional expense.

*Year 2 — Actual costs*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 32,400 | |
| Warranty Expense | 2,600 | |
| Cash (and other resources) | | 35,000 |

**Check:** Dr 32,400 + 2,600 = Cr 35,000. **Balanced.**

Total expense for Year 1 sales: $54,400 + $2,600 = **$57,000** = $22,000 + $35,000 total cash costs.

**Key insight:** Same decision tree and JE patterns as Q1 with all amounts changed. Settlement **below** the accrued amount produces a **gain** in the settlement period (change in estimate), not a restatement of the prior-year accrual.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Recognized vs nonrecognized subsequent events; service-type warranty; gain contingency
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Recognized subsequent event adjusts Year 1; nonrecognized subsequent event is disclosure only; service-type warranty deferred revenue; gain contingency never accrued  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakewood Precision Optics** has a December 31 year-end. Year 1 financial statements are issued **March 1, Year 2**.

**Case 1 — Recognized subsequent event (litigation)**  
- Vehicle accident involving a company truck occurred **November 12, Year 1**. Lawsuit filed December Year 1.  
- On **February 8, Year 2**, before FS issuance, parties settle for **$125,000** cash (paid February 8).

**Case 2 — Nonrecognized subsequent event**  
- On **January 20, Year 2**, a warehouse fire (new event after year-end) destroys uninsured inventory with carrying amount **$210,000**. Fire is unrelated to any Year 1 condition.

**Case 3 — Service-type (extended) warranty**  
- January 1, Year 1: sells equipment for cash **$48,000** (stand-alone selling price) and **60** separately priced 2-year extended warranties at **$90** each.  
- Actual costs of servicing extended warranties in Year 1: **$1,100**.  
- Extended warranty revenue is recognized **straight-line** over 2 years.

**Case 4 — Gain contingency**  
- Counsel believes it is **highly probable** Lakewood will **win** a countersuit and receive **$200,000**, expected in Year 2 Q2.

**Required:**  
a. Case 1: Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** adjusting JE and the **February 8, Year 2** settlement JE. Explain why Year 1 is adjusted.  
b. Case 2: What is recorded in **Year 1** financial statements (entry and/or disclosure)? What entry is recorded when the fire occurs in Year 2?  
c. Case 3: Prepare January 1 sale entry, Year 1 actual service-cost entry, and December 31 Year 1 revenue recognition entry.  
d. Case 4: December 31, Year 1 accounting for the gain contingency.  
e. Presentation/classification: For Case 1, is the $125,000 liability at Dec 31, Year 1 typically **current** or **noncurrent**? Why?

**Answer key:**

**a. Recognized subsequent event**

The accident and related conditions **existed at the balance sheet date**. Settlement before FS issuance provides evidence of the Year 1 loss amount → **recognize** in Year 1.

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrue using subsequent settlement evidence*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation Settlement | 125,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 125,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 125,000. **Balanced.**

*February 8, Year 2 — Pay settlement*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated Litigation Liability | 125,000 | |
| Cash | | 125,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 125,000. **Balanced.**  
(No additional Year 2 loss—amount already recognized in Year 1.)

**b. Nonrecognized subsequent event**

**Year 1:** **No accrual** for the fire (condition did **not** exist at Dec 31, Year 1). **Disclose** in Year 1 notes if needed to keep FS from being misleading: nature of event and estimate of financial effect ($210,000 inventory loss), or statement that estimate cannot be made.

*January 20, Year 2 — Record loss when fire occurs (Year 2)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss from Fire (Inventory) | 210,000 | |
| Inventory | | 210,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 210,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Service-type warranty (not a loss contingency)**

Cash for warranties = 60 × $90 = **$5,400**.  
Annual warranty revenue (SL, 2 years) = $5,400 / 2 = **$2,700**.

*January 1, Year 1 — Sale of equipment and extended warranties*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 53,400 | |
| Deferred Warranty Revenue | | 5,400 |
| Sales Revenue | | 48,000 |

**Check:** Dr 53,400 = Cr 5,400 + 48,000. **Balanced.**  
($48,000 + $5,400 = $53,400.)

*Year 1 — Actual costs of servicing (expense as incurred)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 1,100 | |
| Cash (and other resources) | | 1,100 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,100.

*December 31, Year 1 — Recognize SL warranty revenue*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Warranty Revenue | 2,700 | |
| Warranty Revenue | | 2,700 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 2,700.

Deferred warranty revenue ending balance: $5,400 − $2,700 = **$2,700** (typically **current** for the next 12 months of the remaining 1-year term).

**d. Gain contingency**  
**No journal entry** on December 31, Year 1. Contingent gains are **never accrued**. May **disclose** carefully without implying the gain is certain to be realized.

**e. Classification**  
**Current liability** — settlement/payment occurred (or is expected) within one year of the balance sheet date; here payment is already known in the subsequent-event window and is short-term.

**Key insight:** Distinguish **when the underlying condition arose**. Pre-balance-sheet conditions clarified after year-end → **recognized** subsequent events (adjust accounts). Post-balance-sheet new events → **nonrecognized** (disclose if material). Service-type warranties are **deferred revenue** performance obligations, not ASC 450 loss contingencies; assurance-type warranties are the loss-contingency path.

### Q4 — MC (classification)
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Classification of loss contingencies (accrue vs disclose) and subsequent-event type  

**Question 1:**  
At December 31, Year 1, Northvale Co. is a defendant in a lawsuit for an accident that occurred in Year 1. Management concludes a loss is **reasonably possible** and estimates the loss at **$80,000**. Financial statements are issued February 20, Year 2. What is the correct Year 1 treatment?

- A) Accrue a liability and loss of $80,000  
- B) Disclose the contingency; do **not** accrue  
- C) Neither accrue nor disclose  
- D) Accrue $80,000 only if settled before FS issuance  

**Answer:** **B.** Accrual requires **probable** and reasonably estimable. Reasonably possible → **disclosure only**, not accrual. Settlement before issuance would matter for measurement **if** the loss were already in the probable category / conditions existed—but “reasonably possible” alone does not support accrual.  
**Concept label:** classification_presentation_or_disclosure

---

**Question 2:**  
On January 15, Year 2 (after the Dec 31, Year 1 balance sheet date but before Year 1 FS issuance), Pinecroft Co. issues $5 million of bonds. The bond issuance is unrelated to any Year 1 contingency. Correct Year 1 reporting?

- A) Recognize bonds payable on the Dec 31, Year 1 balance sheet  
- B) Recognize a Year 1 loss equal to issuance costs  
- C) Do not recognize in Year 1 amounts; **disclose** as a nonrecognized subsequent event if needed to keep FS from being misleading  
- D) Reclassify Year 1 long-term debt to current for the full $5 million  

**Answer:** **C.** Bond issuance after year-end is a classic **nonrecognized subsequent event** (new condition). No Year 1 recognition of the bonds; **disclose** nature and estimated financial effect if material to users.  
**Concept label:** classification_presentation_or_disclosure / nonrecognized subsequent event
