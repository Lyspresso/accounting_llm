# Agent 213 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-6

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Account for errors in reporting property, plant, and equipment  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE (wrong vs correct):** ordinary repairs must be expensed; capital improvements must be capitalized to Equipment (not expensed)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** year-by-year comparison of depreciation (and related expenses) **as recorded** vs **as correct**; cumulative AD and retained-earnings misstatement
- **Prior period adjustment JE:** after financial statements are released, correct Equipment / Accumulated Depreciation against **Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment**; restate prior periods
- **Same-period correction:** reverse the error through current-period accounts if discovered before books close / FS release (no PPA)
- **Period-end adjusting JE after correction:** resume correct straight-line depreciation on the corrected carrying amount and remaining life
- **Disposal JE after error correction:** remove cost and **corrected** AD; gain/loss = proceeds vs corrected book value
- **Classification:** PPE reporting **errors** → retrospective restatement + PPA; **changes in estimate** → prospective only (no PPA)
- **Number-variant twin:** same skill path with all amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Wrong service life: multi-year dep schedule, PPA, post-correction dep, disposal
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Depreciation system error (useful life entered wrong); subsequent measurement schedule of incorrect vs correct depreciation; prior period adjustment; period-end adjusting JE after correction; disposal using corrected book value  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Processors Inc.** (calendar year-end) purchased production equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$540,000** cash. Management estimated residual value of **$30,000** and a useful life of **12 years**. Cedarline uses **straight-line** depreciation. When the asset was entered into the depreciation system, the service life was inadvertently entered as **6 years** instead of **12 years**.  

The error is detected on **January 1, Year 4**, after financial statements for Years 1–3 have been released. The error is **material**. **Ignore income taxes.**

Cedarline records correct depreciation for Years 4 and 5. On **January 1, Year 6**, it sells the equipment for **$315,000** cash.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry for the equipment purchase.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement comparison schedule** for Years 1–3 showing incorrect depreciation (6-year life), correct depreciation (12-year life), excess depreciation each year, and cumulative amounts at January 1, Year 4. State the misstatements in Accumulated Depreciation and Retained Earnings immediately before correction.  
c. Prepare the **January 1, Year 4 prior period adjustment** journal entry.  
d. Compute post-correction carrying amount, remaining life, and annual depreciation. Prepare the **December 31, Year 4 period-end adjusting** entry for depreciation.  
e. Roll Accumulated Depreciation and book value through Year 5; prepare the **January 1, Year 6 disposal** journal entry (sale for $315,000). Prove the gain or loss.  
f. **Classification:** Explain in 2–3 sentences why this is an **accounting error** (retrospective / PPA) rather than a **change in estimate** (prospective only).

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 540,000 | |
| Cash | | 540,000 |
| *Record equipment at cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 540,000 = Cr 540,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — Years 1–3**

Incorrect annual depreciation (life entered as 6 years):

\[
\frac{\$540{,}000 - \$30{,}000}{6} = \$85{,}000
\]

Correct annual depreciation (intended 12-year life):

\[
\frac{\$540{,}000 - \$30{,}000}{12} = \$42{,}500
\]

Excess depreciation per year = \$85,000 − \$42,500 = **\$42,500**

| Year | Dep recorded (wrong life) | Correct dep | Excess dep | Ending AD (as recorded) | Ending AD (correct) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 85,000 | 42,500 | 42,500 | 85,000 | 42,500 |
| 2 | 85,000 | 42,500 | 42,500 | 170,000 | 85,000 |
| 3 | 85,000 | 42,500 | 42,500 | 255,000 | 127,500 |
| **Cumulative at 1/1/Y4** | **255,000** | **127,500** | **127,500** | **255,000** | **127,500** |

*Illustrative period-end entry each of Years 1–3 (as incorrectly recorded):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 85,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 85,000 |

**Check:** Dr 85,000 = Cr 85,000 each year.

Misstatements at January 1, Year 4 **before** correction:
- Accumulated Depreciation **overstated \$127,500**
- Retained earnings **understated \$127,500** (three years of excess expense)
- Carrying amount **understated \$127,500** (\$540,000 − \$255,000 = \$285,000 recorded vs correct \$412,500)

**c. Prior period adjustment — January 1, Year 4**

*January 1, Year 4 — To correct depreciation error (wrong service life entered)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 127,500 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 127,500 |
| *Reduce overstated AD; restore beginning RE for prior over-depreciation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 127,500 = Cr 127,500. **Balanced.**

Correct AD after entry = \$255,000 − \$127,500 = **\$127,500** (= 3 × \$42,500).  
Correct carrying amount = \$540,000 − \$127,500 = **\$412,500**.

**d. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 4 (after correction)**

Remaining useful life from January 1, Year 4 = 12 − 3 = **9 years**  
Remaining depreciable base = \$412,500 − \$30,000 = **\$382,500**  
Annual depreciation (Y4 onward) = \$382,500 / 9 = **\$42,500** (matches original correct annual amount)

*December 31, Year 4 — Depreciation after correction*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 42,500 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 42,500 |

**Check:** Dr 42,500 = Cr 42,500. **Balanced.**

AD at December 31, Year 4 = \$127,500 + \$42,500 = **\$170,000**  
Book value = \$540,000 − \$170,000 = **\$370,000**

**e. Roll-forward through Year 5 and disposal — January 1, Year 6**

Year 5 depreciation (same \$42,500):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 42,500 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 42,500 |

**Check:** Dr 42,500 = Cr 42,500. **Balanced.**

| Date | AD balance | Book value (\$540,000 − AD) |
|---|---:|---:|
| After PPA, 1/1/Y4 | 127,500 | 412,500 |
| 12/31/Y4 | 170,000 | 370,000 |
| 12/31/Y5 (= 1/1/Y6 before sale) | 212,500 | 327,500 |

Sale proceeds \$315,000 − book value \$327,500 = **Loss \$12,500**

*January 1, Year 6 — Sale of equipment*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 315,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 212,500 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 12,500 | |
| Equipment | | 540,000 |

**Check:** Dr 315,000 + 212,500 + 12,500 = **540,000** = Cr 540,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Classification — error vs change in estimate**  
Entering **6 years instead of the intended 12** is a **mistake in applying facts already known** when the asset was placed in service—an **accounting error**, not a revision based on new information. **Errors** require **retrospective restatement** of prior periods and a **PPA to beginning Retained Earnings** (plus correction of Accumulated Depreciation). A **change in estimate** (e.g., revising remaining life because of new usage data) is applied **prospectively only**—no PPA and no restatement of prior depreciation.

**Key insight:** Wrong service life **over-depreciates** the asset → **debit AD, credit RE—PPA** for the cumulative excess. Subsequent measurement after correction uses the **corrected BV and remaining life**. Disposal gain/loss must use the **corrected** book value; skipping the PPA misstates the disposal result.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — multi-year improper capitalization of ordinary repairs
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: ordinary repairs improperly capitalized; multi-year subsequent measurement schedule of depreciation on the error; prior period adjustment removing cost, AD, and net RE overstatement  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Alloy Works** paid **$72,000** cash on **January 1, Year 1** for **ordinary repairs** on plant equipment and **incorrectly debited Equipment**. Summit depreciates the misposted cost at **10% per year** (straight-line; no residual allocated to this amount). The error is not discovered until **January 1, Year 3**, after Year 1 and Year 2 financial statements have been issued. The error is **material**. **Ignore income taxes.**

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **incorrect initial recognition** entry (January 1, Year 1) and the **correct** entry that should have been recorded.  
b. Prepare a **two-year subsequent measurement schedule** of depreciation recorded on the \$72,000 error (Year 1 and Year 2), with cumulative AD at December 31, Year 2. Show each year’s period-end depreciation JE amounts.  
c. Prepare a **cumulative income / RE effect schedule** through December 31, Year 2 (repair expense never recorded vs excess depreciation).  
d. Prepare the **January 1, Year 3 prior period adjustment** journal entry. Prove Dr = Cr and that net assets fall by the RE adjustment.  
e. Briefly state **presentation / restatement** of the correction.  
f. **Alternate discovery timing:** Assume the error is instead discovered on **December 28, Year 1**, before Year 1 books are closed and before FS release, and that Year-1 depreciation on the \$72,000 has already been posted. Prepare the Year 1 correcting entries (no PPA).

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition — wrong vs correct**

*Incorrect (as recorded):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 72,000 | |
| Cash | | 72,000 |
| *Ordinary repairs improperly capitalized* | | |

*Correct (should have been):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense (or Maintenance Expense) | 72,000 | |
| Cash | | 72,000 |
| *Ordinary repairs expensed as incurred* | | |

**Check:** both entries Dr = Cr = 72,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (depreciation on the error)**

Annual depreciation on improper capitalization = \$72,000 × 10% = **\$7,200**

| Year | Beginning “cost” of error | Depreciation recorded | Ending AD on error |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 72,000 | 7,200 | 7,200 |
| 2 | 72,000 | 7,200 | 14,400 |

Cumulative AD related to error at December 31, Year 2 = **\$14,400**

*Period-end entry each of Years 1 and 2 (as recorded on the error):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 7,200 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 7,200 |

**Check:** Dr 7,200 = Cr 7,200 each year. **Balanced.**

**c. Cumulative income / RE effect through Year 2**

| Component | Amount | Direction |
|---|---:|---|
| Repair expense never recorded | $72,000 | Expense **understated** → income/RE **overstated** |
| Depreciation recorded in error (2 × \$7,200) | 14,400 | Expense **overstated** → income/RE **understated** |
| **Net understatement of expense (= overstatement of RE)** | **$57,600** | |

Balance-sheet carryforward at January 1, Year 3 (before correction):

| Account | Misstatement |
|---|---|
| Equipment | Overstated **$72,000** |
| Accumulated Depreciation | Overstated **$14,400** |
| Net assets | Overstated **$57,600** |
| Retained earnings | Overstated **$57,600** |

**d. Prior period adjustment — January 1, Year 3**

*January 1, Year 3 — Correct multi-year improper capitalization of ordinary repairs*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 14,400 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 57,600 | |
| Equipment | | 72,000 |
| *Remove cost and related AD; reduce beginning RE for net prior-period overstatement of income* | | |

**Check:** Dr 14,400 + 57,600 = **72,000** = Cr 72,000. **Balanced.**

After the entry: Equipment −72,000 and AD −14,400 remove the improper gross and contra; RE reduced by 57,600. Net assets and equity each fall by **\$57,600**.

**e. Presentation / restatement**  
Debit beginning **Retained Earnings** (PPA) for **\$57,600**; **restate** Years 1–2 statements so ordinary repairs are expensed in Year 1 and the related \$7,200 of depreciation is removed from each year. Year 3 income is **not** charged for the cumulative prior error.

**f. Same-period discovery (before FS release) — December 28, Year 1**  
Use current-period accounts (no PPA): reverse improper capitalization and reverse related depreciation; effect is Year-1 repair expense of \$72,000 and zero of the spurious depreciation.

*To reverse improper capitalization*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense | 72,000 | |
| Equipment | | 72,000 |

*To reverse depreciation recorded on the error*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 7,200 | |
| Depreciation Expense | | 7,200 |

**Check:** each entry Dr = Cr. **Balanced.** Net Year-1 expense after correction = \$72,000 repair expense (and \$0 of the spurious \$7,200 dep).

**Key insight:** Capitalizing ordinary repairs **overstates assets and income** in the year of the outlay, partially offset by **extra depreciation** in that and later years. After statements are out, correct **Equipment, Accumulated Depreciation, and beginning RE**—never dump the cumulative misstatement into current-period expense. The multi-year schedule shows RE overstatement = **cost − cumulative AD on the error**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — capital improvement expensed as repairs (inverse error)
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Capital improvement incorrectly expensed (initial recognition error); subsequent measurement schedule of missing depreciation; prior period adjustment to capitalize cost, catch up AD, and restore RE; period-end adjusting JE after correction  
**Scenario:**  
**Lumen Forge LLC** (calendar year-end) paid **$96,000** cash on **January 1, Year 1** for a **capital improvement** (major motor-system upgrade) that extends the service potential of existing equipment. The improvement should have been capitalized to **Equipment** and depreciated **straight-line over the remaining 8-year useful life** with **zero residual value**. The bookkeeper **incorrectly debited Repair Expense** for the full amount and took **no depreciation** related to the improvement.  

The error is discovered on **January 1, Year 3**, after Year 1 and Year 2 financial statements have been released. The error is **material**. **Ignore income taxes.**

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **incorrect initial recognition** entry and the **correct** entry that should have been recorded on January 1, Year 1.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–2 comparing (i) repair expense, (ii) depreciation expense related to the improvement, and (iii) net effect on pretax income each year and **cumulatively**.  
c. Prepare the **January 1, Year 3 prior period adjustment** journal entry that places the improvement on the books at correct cost and AD and adjusts Retained Earnings.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3 period-end adjusting** entry for depreciation on the corrected improvement.  
e. **Classification / presentation:** State how prior periods are treated and why the RE adjustment equals cost minus catch-up AD (not the full \$96,000).

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition — wrong vs correct**

*Incorrect (as recorded):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense | 96,000 | |
| Cash | | 96,000 |
| *Capital improvement improperly expensed* | | |

*Correct (should have been):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 96,000 | |
| Cash | | 96,000 |
| *Capitalize capital improvement* | | |

**Check:** both entries Dr = Cr = 96,000. **Balanced.**

Correct annual depreciation on the improvement:

\[
\frac{\$96{,}000 - \$0}{8} = \$12{,}000
\]

**b. Subsequent measurement / error-impact schedule (emphasis)**

| Year | As recorded | Correct | Net effect of error on pretax income |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Year 1** | Repair exp. \$96,000; dep. \$0 | Repair exp. \$0; dep. \$12,000 | Expense **overstated** by \$96,000 − \$12,000 = **\$84,000** → income/RE **understated \$84,000** |
| **Year 2** | Repair exp. \$0; dep. \$0 | Repair exp. \$0; dep. \$12,000 | Expense **understated \$12,000** → income/RE **overstated \$12,000** |
| **Cumulative at 1/1/Y3** | — | — | RE **understated by \$72,000** (= \$84,000 − \$12,000) |

Schedule of correct AD that should exist:

| Year | Correct depreciation | Correct cumulative AD |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 12,000 | 12,000 |
| 2 | 12,000 | 24,000 |

Proof of RE understatement:  
Asset missing from books (gross) \$96,000 − missing AD \$24,000 = **net assets understated \$72,000** → equity understated \$72,000.

**c. Prior period adjustment — January 1, Year 3**

*January 1, Year 3 — To correct capital improvement improperly expensed*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 96,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 24,000 |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 72,000 |
| *Capitalize cost; catch up two years of AD; restore beginning RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 96,000 = Cr 24,000 + 72,000 = **96,000**. **Balanced.**

After the entry: Equipment includes the \$96,000 improvement; AD includes \$24,000 of catch-up; carrying amount = **\$72,000**; RE increased by \$72,000.

**d. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 3**

Remaining life from January 1, Year 3 = 8 − 2 = **6 years**  
Remaining depreciable base = \$72,000 − \$0 = **\$72,000**  
Annual depreciation = \$72,000 / 6 = **\$12,000** (same as original correct annual amount)

*December 31, Year 3 — Depreciation on corrected improvement*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 12,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 12,000 |

**Check:** Dr 12,000 = Cr 12,000. **Balanced.**

AD related to improvement at December 31, Year 3 = \$24,000 + \$12,000 = **\$36,000**  
Book value of improvement = \$96,000 − \$36,000 = **\$60,000**

**e. Classification / presentation**  
- Material error after FS release → **prior period adjustment** to beginning RE and **restatement** of Years 1–2 (capitalize the improvement in Y1; record \$12,000 depreciation in Y1 and Y2).  
- The RE credit is **\$72,000**, not \$96,000, because two years of **correct depreciation would have reduced** prior income by \$24,000; only the **net** prior misstatement of income is restored through RE.  
- Year 3 does **not** re-expense the \$96,000 as repairs; it only records **current** depreciation of \$12,000.

**Key insight:** Expensing a capital improvement is the **inverse** of Demo 12-6 (capitalizing repairs). The subsequent measurement schedule must track **both** the missing asset and the **missing depreciation**. PPA puts Equipment and catch-up AD on the books and credits RE for **cost − cumulative correct AD**.

### Q4 — MC (classification / measurement)
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Classification of PPE error correction path; measurement of net PPA when depreciation was double-recorded and partly omitted  

**Question 4A:**  
On January 1, Year 1, **MesaRidge Fabrication** paid \$40,000 for ordinary repairs and incorrectly debited Equipment. It recorded \$4,000 of depreciation on that amount in Year 1. The error is discovered in **Year 2 after** Year 1 financial statements were issued. Which treatment is **correct** (ignore taxes)?

- A) Debit Repair Expense \$40,000 in Year 2 and credit Equipment \$40,000; leave Accumulated Depreciation unchanged.  
- B) Debit Accumulated Depreciation \$4,000, debit Retained Earnings—PPA \$36,000, credit Equipment \$40,000; restate prior-period statements.  
- C) Debit Retained Earnings—PPA \$40,000 and credit Equipment \$40,000 only (do not touch Accumulated Depreciation).  
- D) Treat the correction prospectively like a change in depreciation estimate—no restatement and no PPA.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Net prior overstatement of income = \$40,000 − \$4,000 = **\$36,000**. Remove the gross asset and related AD against **beginning RE** (PPA) and **restate** prior periods. A dumps a prior error into current expense after FS release. C omits the AD reversal and uses the wrong RE amount. D confuses **errors** (retrospective) with **changes in estimate** (prospective).

**Question 4B:**  
In Year 1, **Prairie Bench Co.** (1) recorded annual equipment depreciation of **\$61,000 twice**, and (2) failed to depreciate equipment purchased **December 1, Year 1** (cost **\$360,000**, 6-year useful life, zero residual, straight-line). Errors are discovered in Year 2 after Year 1 statements were issued. Ignoring taxes, the January 1, Year 2 prior period adjustment **increases or decreases retained earnings** by how much?

- A) \$66,000 increase  
- B) \$56,000 increase  
- C) \$56,000 decrease  
- D) \$66,000 decrease  

**Answer:** **B. \$56,000 increase.**  

Double-recorded depreciation: expense overstated \$61,000 → RE **understated** \$61,000 → **credit RE \$61,000** (and debit AD \$61,000).  

Omitted depreciation: Dec 1–Dec 31 = **1/12** year  

\[
\frac{\$360{,}000}{6} \times \frac{1}{12} = \$5{,}000
\]  

Expense understated \$5,000 → RE **overstated** \$5,000 → **debit RE \$5,000** (and credit AD \$5,000).  

**Net credit to Retained Earnings—PPA** = \$61,000 − \$5,000 = **\$56,000 increase**.

Combined correcting entry (net):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 56,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 56,000 |

**Check:** Dr 56,000 = Cr 56,000. **Balanced.**
