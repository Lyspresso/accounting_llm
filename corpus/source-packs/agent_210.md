# Agent 210 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-3

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Calculate depreciation using group and composite depreciation methods  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Group vs composite:** group = homogeneous (similar) assets; composite = heterogeneous (dissimilar) assets; calculations and journal entries are the same
- **Composite (group) annual depreciation rate** = Total annual straight-line depreciation ÷ Total acquisition cost (round rate to three decimals when required)
- **Composite (group) useful life** = Total depreciable cost ÷ Total annual straight-line depreciation
- **Composite (group) annual depreciation** = Rate × Total acquisition cost (control-account approach)
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize composite/group assets at total acquisition cost
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** Dr Depreciation Expense / Cr Accumulated Depreciation (single AD control account)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** after non-material addition/disposal, apply **old rate** × **new** total acquisition cost; roll forward cost and AD
- **Disposal JE:** no gain or loss recognized; cash + AD plug = original cost removed from asset control account
- Classification/presentation: PPE at cost less one AD control account; method disclosed; G/L absorbed in AD

### Q1 — CORE — Composite schedule, multi-year subsequent measurement, initial recognition, dep, disposal
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Composite depreciation schedule; composite rate and useful life; initial recognition JE; subsequent measurement schedule (rate × revised cost); period-end composite dep JE; disposal with AD plug (no G/L)  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Precision Works LLC** (calendar year) places the following **dissimilar** plant assets into a single **composite** early in **Year 1** (full-year depreciation in Year 1). All assets were purchased for cash.

| Asset class | Quantity | Unit acquisition cost | Unit residual value | Unit useful life (years) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CNC lathes | 4 | $90,000 | $9,000 | 9 |
| Heat-treat ovens | 6 | $30,000 | $0 | 5 |
| Ultrasonic cleaners | 8 | $15,000 | $0 | 4 |
| Mobile tool carts | 20 | $4,500 | $0 | 5 |

On **February 28, Year 2**, Cascade sells **one heat-treat oven** for **$14,000** cash. Management judges the disposal **not material** to the makeup of the composite (continue the original rate).

**Required:**  
a. Complete a **Year 1 composite depreciation schedule** (total acquisition cost, total residual, total annual SL depreciation by class and in total).  
b. Compute the **composite annual depreciation rate** (three decimal places), **composite useful life** (two decimal places), and **Year 1 composite annual depreciation**.  
c. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry for the composite purchase (cash).  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entry for composite depreciation.  
e. Prepare the **February 28, Year 2 disposal** journal entry for the one heat-treat oven.  
f. Build a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–2 (beginning cost, disposals, ending cost, depreciation = old rate × ending cost, AD roll-forward, ending carrying amount).  
g. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2** composite depreciation entry.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1 composite depreciation schedule**

| Asset class | Qty | Unit cost | Unit residual | Life | Total acquisition cost | Total residual | Total annual SL depreciation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CNC lathes | 4 | $90,000 | $9,000 | 9 | $360,000 | $36,000 | ($360,000 − $36,000) / 9 = **$36,000** |
| Heat-treat ovens | 6 | 30,000 | 0 | 5 | 180,000 | 0 | $180,000 / 5 = **36,000** |
| Ultrasonic cleaners | 8 | 15,000 | 0 | 4 | 120,000 | 0 | $120,000 / 4 = **30,000** |
| Mobile tool carts | 20 | 4,500 | 0 | 5 | 90,000 | 0 | $90,000 / 5 = **18,000** |
| **Totals** | | | | | **$750,000** | **$36,000** | **$120,000** |

Total depreciable cost = $750,000 − $36,000 = **$714,000**

**b. Rate, life, and Year 1 depreciation**

\[
\text{Composite annual depreciation rate} = \frac{\$120{,}000}{\$750{,}000} = \mathbf{0.160}
\]

\[
\text{Composite useful life} = \frac{\$714{,}000}{\$120{,}000} = \mathbf{5.95\ \text{years}}
\]

\[
\text{Composite annual depreciation (Year 1)} = 0.160 \times \$750{,}000 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}
\]

(Composite life is **not** a simple average of unit lives; it weights classes by contribution to total depreciable cost.)

**c. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (composite purchase)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Machinery and Equipment (composite control) | 750,000 | |
| Cash | | 750,000 |
| *Capitalize heterogeneous plant assets in one composite control account at total acquisition cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 750,000. **Balanced.**

**d. December 31, Year 1 — period-end composite depreciation**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Machinery and Equipment | 120,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Machinery and Equipment | | 120,000 |
| *Composite annual depreciation = rate × total acquisition cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 120,000. **Balanced.**

**e. February 28, Year 2 — disposal of one heat-treat oven (no G/L)**

Original cost of one oven = **$30,000**  
Cash proceeds = **$14,000**  
Accumulated depreciation (plug) = $30,000 − $14,000 = **$16,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 14,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Machinery and Equipment | 16,000 | |
| Machinery and Equipment (composite control) | | 30,000 |
| *Remove asset cost; difference vs cash plugged to AD — no gain or loss recognized* | | |

**Check:** Dr 14,000 + 16,000 = Cr 30,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Subsequent measurement schedule (Years 1–2)**

Original rate retained (disposal not material). Depreciation each year = **0.160 × ending-of-year total acquisition cost** after that year’s non-material changes.

| Item | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning total acquisition cost | $750,000 | $750,000 |
| Disposals (original cost) | 0 | (30,000) |
| **Ending total acquisition cost** | **750,000** | **720,000** |
| Composite rate (unchanged) | 0.160 | 0.160 |
| **Depreciation expense** | **120,000** | **115,200** |
| Beginning AD | 0 | 120,000 |
| + Depreciation | 120,000 | 115,200 |
| − AD removed on disposal (plug) | 0 | (16,000) |
| **Ending AD** | **120,000** | **219,200** |
| **Ending carrying amount** (cost − AD) | **630,000** | **500,800** |

Year 2 depreciation check: \(0.160 \times \$720{,}000 = \$115{,}200\).

**g. December 31, Year 2 — composite depreciation**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Machinery and Equipment | 115,200 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Machinery and Equipment | | 115,200 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 115,200. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Composite methods replace unit-by-unit tracking with a **rate × cost** control-account system. **Initial recognition** is at total cost; the **subsequent measurement schedule** applies the same rate to the **revised** cost after non-material disposals; **disposals** never produce a separate G/L — AD is the balancing plug.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Composite twin (different company and numbers)
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: composite schedule, rate/life, initial recognition JE, Year-1 dep, disposal with AD plug, subsequent measurement (old rate × new cost), Year-2 dep  
**Scenario:**  
**Mesa River Packaging Co.** (calendar year) combines the following **heterogeneous** production assets into a composite at the start of **Year 1** (full-year depreciation). Purchased for cash.

| Asset class | Quantity | Unit acquisition cost | Unit residual value | Unit useful life (years) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Form-fill sealers | 4 | $55,000 | $5,000 | 10 |
| Label applicators | 5 | $40,000 | $0 | 5 |
| Case packers | 6 | $20,000 | $0 | 4 |
| Pallet wrappers | 10 | $6,000 | $0 | 5 |

On **September 12, Year 2**, Mesa River sells **one label applicator** for **$17,000** cash. The disposal is **not material**; continue the original composite rate.

**Required:**  
a. Complete the composite schedule (totals and annual SL depreciation by class).  
b. Compute composite rate (three decimals), composite useful life (two decimals), and Year 1 depreciation.  
c. Journal entries: (1) **initial recognition** of the composite, (2) **Year 1** depreciation, (3) **disposal** of the label applicator, (4) **Year 2** depreciation.  
d. Show a brief **subsequent measurement** cost/dep roll-forward for Years 1–2.  
e. Show Dr = Cr for every entry.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Composite schedule**

| Asset class | Qty | Total acquisition cost | Total residual | Total annual SL depreciation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Form-fill sealers | 4 | $220,000 | $20,000 | ($220,000 − $20,000) / 10 = **$20,000** |
| Label applicators | 5 | 200,000 | 0 | $200,000 / 5 = **40,000** |
| Case packers | 6 | 120,000 | 0 | $120,000 / 4 = **30,000** |
| Pallet wrappers | 10 | 60,000 | 0 | $60,000 / 5 = **12,000** |
| **Totals** | | **$600,000** | **$20,000** | **$102,000** |

Total depreciable cost = $600,000 − $20,000 = **$580,000**

**b. Rate, life, Year 1 depreciation**

\[
\text{Rate} = \frac{\$102{,}000}{\$600{,}000} = \mathbf{0.170}
\]

\[
\text{Composite useful life} = \frac{\$580{,}000}{\$102{,}000} = \mathbf{5.69\ \text{years}}
\]

\[
\text{Year 1 composite depreciation} = 0.170 \times \$600{,}000 = \mathbf{\$102{,}000}
\]

**c–e. Journal entries (all balanced) and subsequent measurement**

*(1) January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment (composite control) | 600,000 | |
| Cash | | 600,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 600,000.

*(2) December 31, Year 1 — period-end depreciation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Equipment | 102,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 102,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 102,000.

*(3) September 12, Year 2 — dispose of one label applicator (cost $40,000; cash $17,000; AD plug $23,000)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 17,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 23,000 | |
| Equipment (composite control) | | 40,000 |

**Check:** Dr 17,000 + 23,000 = Cr 40,000. **No gain/loss.**

*(4) December 31, Year 2 — composite depreciation on revised cost*

Revised cost = $600,000 − $40,000 = **$560,000**  
Depreciation = 0.170 × $560,000 = **$95,200**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Equipment | 95,200 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 95,200 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 95,200.

**Subsequent measurement roll-forward**

| Item | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ending total acquisition cost | $600,000 | $560,000 |
| Rate | 0.170 | 0.170 |
| Depreciation expense | $102,000 | $95,200 |
| Ending AD (after disposal plug in Y2) | $102,000 | $102,000 − $23,000 + $95,200 = **$174,200** |
| Ending carrying amount | $498,000 | $560,000 − $174,200 = **$385,800** |

**Key insight:** Changing every input (costs, lives, residual, disposal proceeds) still follows the same pipeline: **build schedule → rate/life → capitalize total cost → dep = rate × cost → dispose with AD plug → reapply old rate to new cost on the subsequent measurement schedule**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Group depreciation (homogeneous fleet): schedule, dep JE, multi-unit disposal
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Group depreciation for similar assets; group rate and life; period-end adjusting JE; multi-unit disposal with AD plug; subsequent group measurement (old rate × revised cost)  
**Scenario:**  
**Prairie Express Courier LLC** acquires a **homogeneous fleet** of **18 identical cargo vans** on **January 1, Year 1** for cash (full-year depreciation).

| Fact | Amount |
|---|---|
| Unit cost | $36,000 |
| Unit residual value | $3,600 |
| Estimated useful life | 6 years |
| Quantity | 18 vans |
| Depreciation system | **Group** (similar assets) |

On **May 18, Year 2**, Prairie Express sells **2 vans** for **$20,000 cash each** ($40,000 total). The retirement is **not material** to the group’s overall composition; retain the original group rate.

**Required:**  
a. Compute total acquisition cost, total residual, total depreciable cost, **annual group depreciation**, **group depreciation rate** (three decimals), and **group useful life**.  
b. Prepare the **initial recognition** JE and the **December 31, Year 1** group depreciation JE.  
c. Prepare the **May 18, Year 2** multi-unit disposal JE.  
d. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–2 and record **Year 2** group depreciation.  
e. Explain in one sentence how **group** differs from **composite** (classification concept).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Group measures**

| Item | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Total acquisition cost | 18 × $36,000 | **$648,000** |
| Total residual | 18 × $3,600 | **$64,800** |
| Total depreciable cost | $648,000 − $64,800 | **$583,200** |
| Annual group depreciation | $583,200 / 6 | **$97,200** |
| Group depreciation rate | $97,200 / $648,000 | **0.150** |
| Group useful life | $583,200 / $97,200 | **6.00 years** |

(For identical assets, group life equals each unit’s useful life.)

**b. Initial recognition and Year 1 depreciation**

*January 1, Year 1 — purchase fleet*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Delivery Equipment (group control) | 648,000 | |
| Cash | | 648,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 648,000.

*December 31, Year 1 — group depreciation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Delivery Equipment | 97,200 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Equipment | | 97,200 |
| *0.150 × $648,000 = $97,200* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 97,200.

**c. May 18, Year 2 — dispose of 2 vans**

Cost removed = 2 × $36,000 = **$72,000**  
Cash = 2 × $20,000 = **$40,000**  
AD plug = $72,000 − $40,000 = **$32,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 40,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Equipment | 32,000 | |
| Delivery Equipment (group control) | | 72,000 |
| *No gain or loss; difference absorbed in AD* | | |

**Check:** Dr 40,000 + 32,000 = Cr 72,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule and Year 2 depreciation**

| Item | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning total acquisition cost | $648,000 | $648,000 |
| Disposals (original cost) | 0 | (72,000) |
| **Ending total acquisition cost** | **648,000** | **576,000** |
| Group rate (unchanged) | 0.150 | 0.150 |
| **Depreciation expense** | **97,200** | **86,400** |
| Beginning AD | 0 | 97,200 |
| + Depreciation | 97,200 | 86,400 |
| − AD removed on disposal | 0 | (32,000) |
| **Ending AD** | **97,200** | **151,600** |
| **Ending carrying amount** | **550,800** | **424,400** |

Year 2 depreciation: \(0.150 \times \$576{,}000 = \$86{,}400\).

*December 31, Year 2*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Delivery Equipment | 86,400 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Equipment | | 86,400 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 86,400.

**e. Group vs composite**  
**Group** depreciation combines **homogeneous/similar** assets (e.g., a fleet of like cargo vans); **composite** depreciation combines **heterogeneous/dissimilar** assets (e.g., lathes, ovens, cleaners, and carts) — **computations and JEs are otherwise identical**.

**Key insight:** Group method is the practical fleet application of the same control-account logic: average rate on total cost, subsequent measurement continues at the **old rate on revised cost**, and **retirements never trigger G/L** — only cost removal and an AD plug.

### Q4 — MC — Classification / method treatment (composite disposal)
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Classification of disposal accounting under group/composite methods (no separate G/L)  
**Question:**  
Under the **composite (or group) depreciation method**, when an individual asset is sold before the end of the composite life for cash less than its original cost, the company typically:

- A) Recognizes a loss equal to original cost minus cash proceeds.  
- B) Recognizes a gain or loss equal to cash proceeds minus the asset’s carrying amount computed with unit-level accumulated depreciation.  
- C) Debits Cash for proceeds, credits the asset control account for original cost, and plugs **Accumulated Depreciation** for the difference (no gain or loss).  
- D) Leaves the asset cost on the books and continues depreciating it until the composite life ends.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Group/composite methods maintain cost by class/control account and a single AD control account. On disposal, remove original cost, record cash, and balance the entry through AD so that **unrecorded gains and losses offset within AD over time**. Unit-level book value and separate G/L are not used.

### Q5 — MC — Subsequent measurement after non-material disposal
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement after non-material disposal — retain original rate × revised total acquisition cost  
**Question:**  
A company uses composite depreciation. The original composite annual depreciation rate is **0.160** based on total acquisition cost of **$750,000**. During Year 2, one asset with original cost **$30,000** is sold; management concludes the change is **not material**. Year 2 composite depreciation expense should be:

- A) \(0.160 \times \$750{,}000 = \$120{,}000\) (ignore the disposal for depreciation).  
- B) \(0.160 \times (\$750{,}000 - \$30{,}000) = \$115{,}200\) (old rate × revised total acquisition cost).  
- C) Recomputed from scratch using only remaining assets’ individual lives (new rate required).  
- D) Zero, because any disposal requires reclassification to held-for-sale for the entire composite.

**Answer:** **B.**  
When changes are **not material**, the **original rate is maintained** and applied to the **new balance in the composite asset control account** after the addition or deletion. Material makeup changes can require a new rate; that is not the case here.
