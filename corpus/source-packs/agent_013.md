# Agent 13 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-3

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for Bonds Issued at Face Value  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Initial recognition of bonds when stated rate equals market rate (proceeds = face)
- Subsequent measurement: interest expense equals cash interest each period (no discount/premium amortization)
- Interest payment schedule / carrying-amount rollforward for par bonds
- Period-end adjusting entry when fiscal year-end is not an interest payment date
- Derecognition (settlement) of bonds payable at maturity
- Balance-sheet classification and disclosure for bonds issued at face
- Number-variant twin of the core lifecycle

### Q1 — CORE — Full lifecycle: issuance, interest, schedule, maturity
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE + subsequent interest + maturity settlement for bonds issued at face  
**Scenario:** On January 1 of Year 1, **Cedar Ridge Manufacturing Co.** authorizes and issues **$400,000** of **6%** interest-bearing bonds. The stated rate and market rate are equal, so the bonds sell at face value. The bonds mature in **five years** on December 31 of Year 5 and pay cash interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. Cedar Ridge’s fiscal year ends December 31.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the journal entry for issuance on January 1 of Year 1.  
(b) Record the first interest payment on June 30 of Year 1.  
(c) Prepare a subsequent-measurement schedule for the first **four** semiannual periods (columns: Period ending; Cash interest; Interest expense; Amortization; Carrying amount end of period). Explain why amortization is zero.  
(d) Compute total interest expense over the full bond term and reconcile it to total cash interest paid.  
(e) Record the entry on December 31 of Year 5 to derecognize the bonds at maturity. Assume the final interest payment on that date has already been recorded.

**Answer key:**

Semiannual cash interest = \(400{,}000 \times 6\% \times 6/12 = \$12{,}000\).  
Number of semiannual periods = \(5 \times 2 = 10\).  
Because stated rate = market rate, PV at issuance = face = **$400,000**. Carrying amount remains **$400,000** until maturity.

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — Issuance at face value**
```
Dr Cash .............................. 400,000
   Cr Bonds Payable .............................. 400,000
```
Dr 400,000 = Cr 400,000 ✓

**(b) June 30, Year 1 — Interest payment**
```
Dr Interest Expense ..................  12,000
   Cr Cash .......................................  12,000
```
Dr 12,000 = Cr 12,000 ✓  
(Same entry each semiannual period for five years.)

**(c) Subsequent-measurement schedule (first four periods)**

| Period ending | Cash interest | Interest expense | Amortization | Carrying amount (end) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Issuance 1/1/Y1 | — | — | — | $400,000 |
| 6/30/Y1 | $12,000 | $12,000 | $0 | $400,000 |
| 12/31/Y1 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 400,000 |
| 6/30/Y2 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 400,000 |
| 12/31/Y2 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 400,000 |

Amortization is **zero** because there is no discount or premium when bonds are issued at face. Interest expense equals cash interest every period.

**(d) Total interest over term**  
Total cash interest = \(12{,}000 \times 10 = \$120{,}000\).  
Total interest expense = \$120{,}000 (same; no amort of discount/premium).  
Reconciliation: Total interest expense = Total cash interest payments over bond term when bonds are issued at face.

**(e) December 31, Year 5 — Derecognition at maturity**
```
Dr Bonds Payable ..................... 400,000
   Cr Cash ....................................... 400,000
```
Dr 400,000 = Cr 400,000 ✓

**Key insight:** When stated rate = market rate, bonds are recognized at face, carrying amount never changes, interest expense equals cash interest each period, and maturity simply pays face (Bonds Payable) in cash.

### Q2 — CORE number variant
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — par-bond issuance, annual interest, maturity  
**Scenario:** On January 1 of Year 1, **Northwind Logistics Inc.** authorizes and issues **$250,000** of **4%** bonds at face value (stated rate equals market rate of 4%). The bonds mature in **eight years** on December 31 of Year 8. Cash interest is paid **annually** on December 31. Northwind’s fiscal year ends December 31.

**Required:**  
(a) Record issuance on January 1 of Year 1.  
(b) Record the interest payment on December 31 of Year 1.  
(c) State the carrying amount of the bonds on December 31 of Year 1, Year 4, and Year 7 (immediately after any interest entry on those dates).  
(d) Compute total interest expense over the eight-year term.  
(e) Record the maturity settlement on December 31 of Year 8, **including** the final interest payment in a compound entry (or two separate entries).

**Answer key:**

Annual cash interest = \(250{,}000 \times 4\% = \$10{,}000\).  
Carrying amount stays at face for the entire term.

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — Issuance**
```
Dr Cash .............................. 250,000
   Cr Bonds Payable .............................. 250,000
```
Dr 250,000 = Cr 250,000 ✓

**(b) December 31, Year 1 — Interest**
```
Dr Interest Expense ..................  10,000
   Cr Cash .......................................  10,000
```
Dr 10,000 = Cr 10,000 ✓

**(c) Carrying amounts**  
Year 1 end: **$250,000**  
Year 4 end: **$250,000**  
Year 7 end: **$250,000**  
(Par bonds: no amortization; carrying amount = face throughout.)

**(d) Total interest expense**  
\(10{,}000 \times 8 = \$80{,}000\) = total cash interest paid over the term.

**(e) December 31, Year 8 — Final interest + maturity**

Compound form:
```
Dr Interest Expense ..................  10,000
Dr Bonds Payable ..................... 250,000
   Cr Cash ....................................... 260,000
```
Dr 260,000 = Cr 260,000 ✓

(Alternatively, two entries: Dr Interest Expense 10,000 / Cr Cash 10,000; and Dr Bonds Payable 250,000 / Cr Cash 250,000.)

**Key insight:** Changing face, rate, term, and payment frequency does not change the LO 16-3 pattern: Dr Cash / Cr Bonds Payable at face; interest expense = cash interest; settle face at maturity.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — period-end adjusting JE + classification
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Year-end interest accrual for par bonds (interest date ≠ fiscal year-end) and BS classification near maturity  
**Scenario:** On January 1 of Year 1, **Summit Ridge Utilities** issues **$240,000** of **8%**, **five-year** bonds at face value (market rate also 8%). Cash interest is payable **semiannually on July 1 and January 1**. Summit Ridge’s fiscal year ends **December 31**. The bonds mature on January 1 of Year 6 (same date as an interest payment).

**Required:**  
(a) Record issuance on January 1 of Year 1.  
(b) Record the interest payment on July 1 of Year 1.  
(c) Record the **adjusting entry** on December 31 of Year 1.  
(d) Record the interest payment on January 1 of Year 2 (no reversing entry was made).  
(e) On December 31 of Year 5 (one day before maturity on January 1 of Year 6), how should the bonds and any related interest be classified on the balance sheet? State account names and amounts.  
(f) Record the entries on January 1 of Year 6 for the final interest payment and principal settlement (assume the Dec 31 Year 5 accrual was recorded and not reversed).

**Answer key:**

Semiannual cash interest = \(240{,}000 \times 8\% \times 6/12 = \$9{,}600\).

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — Issuance at face**
```
Dr Cash .............................. 240,000
   Cr Bonds Payable .............................. 240,000
```
Dr 240,000 = Cr 240,000 ✓

**(b) July 1, Year 1 — Interest payment**
```
Dr Interest Expense ..................   9,600
   Cr Cash .......................................   9,600
```
Dr 9,600 = Cr 9,600 ✓

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — Adjusting entry (6 months accrued)**  
Interest accrued July 1–Dec 31: \(240{,}000 \times 8\% \times 6/12 = \$9{,}600\).
```
Dr Interest Expense ..................   9,600
   Cr Interest Payable ...........................   9,600
```
Dr 9,600 = Cr 9,600 ✓

**(d) January 1, Year 2 — Interest payment (clear payable)**
```
Dr Interest Payable ..................   9,600
   Cr Cash .......................................   9,600
```
Dr 9,600 = Cr 9,600 ✓  
(No additional Interest Expense on Jan 1; the full six months was recognized in Year 1 via the adjusting entry.)

**(e) Classification on December 31 of Year 5**  
- **Interest Payable** (current liability): **$9,600** (accrued July 1–Dec 31 Year 5).  
- **Bonds Payable** (or Current portion of long-term debt): **$240,000** — **entirety current**, because principal is due within one year (January 1 of Year 6).  
- No Discount or Premium accounts (issued at face).  
- Carrying amount = face = **$240,000**.

**(f) January 1, Year 6 — Final interest + maturity**
```
Dr Interest Payable ..................   9,600
   Cr Cash .......................................   9,600
```
Dr 9,600 = Cr 9,600 ✓

```
Dr Bonds Payable ..................... 240,000
   Cr Cash ....................................... 240,000
```
Dr 240,000 = Cr 240,000 ✓

**Key insight:** For par bonds, period-end adjusting entries accrue Interest Payable (expense still equals the time-proportion of stated interest). Near maturity, the full face is current; there is no unamortized discount/premium to reclassify.

### Q4 — MC — Recognition criterion / presentation
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Classification of when bonds are recognized at face value  
**Question:** Under the core bond measurement model (issuer’s books), bonds are recognized at **face value** with no discount or premium when:

- A) The bonds are secured by specific collateral, regardless of market yields  
- B) The stated (coupon) rate equals the market (yield) rate at issuance so that proceeds equal face  
- C) Cash interest is paid annually rather than semiannually  
- D) The bonds are callable at the issuer’s option before maturity  

**Answer:** **B.** Bonds sell (and are recorded) at face when investors accept the stated rate as the market measure of risk at issuance—i.e., stated rate = market rate, so PV of principal and interest cash flows equals face. Security, payment frequency, and call features do not by themselves cause face-value recognition.

### Q5 — CORE — Sold at face between interest dates (initial recognition variant)
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for par bonds sold between interest payment dates + first interest payment  
**Scenario:** **Lakeshore Packing Corp.** authorized **$600,000** of **6%**, 10-year bonds dated January 1 of Year 1. Interest is payable semiannually on **June 30 and December 31**. The market rate on the sale date equals the stated rate. The bonds are sold **at face (par) on March 1 of Year 1** (two months after the interest date). Lakeshore’s fiscal year ends December 31.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute cash received on March 1 and record the issuance entry.  
(b) Record the first interest payment on June 30 of Year 1.  
(c) Briefly explain how much Interest Expense is recognized for Year 1 related to these bonds (from March 1 through December 31), and show the supporting total.

**Answer key:**

Semiannual cash interest = \(600{,}000 \times 6\% \times 6/12 = \$18{,}000\).  
Accrued interest collected from buyers for Jan 1–Feb 28 (2 months) = \(600{,}000 \times 6\% \times 2/12 = \$6{,}000\).

**(a) March 1, Year 1 — Sold at par between interest dates**  
Cash received = face + accrued interest = \(600{,}000 + 6{,}000 = \$606{,}000\).
```
Dr Cash .............................. 606,000
   Cr Bonds Payable .............................. 600,000
   Cr Interest Payable ...........................   6,000
```
Dr 606,000 = Cr 606,000 ✓  
(Bonds Payable still recorded at face; buyers prepaid two months of interest.)

**(b) June 30, Year 1 — First interest payment**  
Interest expense for March–June (4 months outstanding) = \(600{,}000 \times 6\% \times 4/12 = \$12{,}000\).
```
Dr Interest Payable ..................   6,000
Dr Interest Expense ..................  12,000
   Cr Cash .......................................  18,000
```
Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000 ✓  
(Investors receive a full six-month coupon, but only four months is expense to Lakeshore.)

**(c) Year 1 Interest Expense (March 1–Dec 31 = 10 months)**  
\(600{,}000 \times 6\% \times 10/12 = \$30{,}000\).  

Check via entries:  
- June 30: $12,000 expense  
- Dec 31: full semi coupon all expense (bonds outstanding all six months) = $18,000  
- Total = \(12{,}000 + 18{,}000 = \$30{,}000\) ✓

**Key insight:** Even when sold between interest dates, par bonds are still recognized at face in Bonds Payable; accrued interest is a liability (Interest Payable), not a premium, and first-period expense covers only the months the bonds are outstanding.
