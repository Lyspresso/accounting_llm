# Agent 162 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-6

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Explain how a change in tax rates impacts deferred taxes  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** establish deferred tax liability (or asset) using **enacted** rates that will apply in the year(s) of reversal; current tax payable uses the **current-year** enacted rate
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** match each future taxable/deductible amount to the **enacted rate in the year of reversal**; multi-rate phase-in produces a layered deferred tax balance; roll DTL/DTA as differences reverse
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** remeasure existing DTA/DTL when a new rate is **enacted** (not merely anticipated); the entire remeasurement hits **income tax expense** in the period of enactment
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JE:** as temporary differences reverse, draw down DTA/DTL (offset to tax expense / payable); rate-change remeasurement is separate from ordinary reversal
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** deferred taxes noncurrent; rate-change effect is part of **income tax expense from continuing operations** in the enactment period; statutory-to-effective rate reconciliation often discloses the rate-change component
- **Number-variant twin:** same multi-rate DTL path with all company facts and amounts changed
- **Direction of income effect:** rate **decrease** → DTL down (tax benefit / lower expense); DTA down (tax detriment / higher expense)

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-rate enacted schedule: DTL origin, rollforward, full reversal, presentation
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Match multi-year taxable temporary differences to phased-in enacted rates; initial DTL + current tax JE; subsequent measurement schedule; period-end reversal JEs; balance-sheet classification  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Instruments Co.** (calendar year-end) reports **taxable income of $80,000** for the year ended **December 31, Year 1**. In Year 1, Cedarline originates a **taxable temporary difference of $24,000** because the **GAAP carrying amount of equipment exceeds tax basis** by $24,000. The temporary difference will reverse as **future taxable amounts** of:

| Year of reversal | Future taxable amount |
|---|---:|
| Year 2 | $9,000 |
| Year 3 | $9,000 |
| Year 4 | $6,000 |
| **Total** | **$24,000** |

**Enacted tax rates** (a new tax law was signed on **December 15, Year 1**):

| Period | Enacted rate |
|---|---:|
| Year 1 (current) | **30%** |
| Year 2 | **28%** |
| Year 3 | **25%** |
| Year 4 | **22%** |

Beginning balances in all deferred tax accounts on January 1, Year 1 are **zero**. No other temporary or permanent differences exist in Years 1–4. **Year 2 taxable income = $90,000**; **Year 3 taxable income = $100,000**; **Year 4 taxable income = $70,000**. Each year’s current tax is computed at that year’s enacted rate. No new temporary differences originate after Year 1.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **schedule to compute the deferred tax liability** at **December 31, Year 1** (match each reversal year to its enacted rate).  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 1 income tax journal entry** (current + deferred).  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement / DTL rollforward schedule** for Years 1–4 showing: beginning DTL, originating/(reversing) temporary amounts by year, ending cumulative taxable TD, ending DTL by remaining rate layers, and the period change in DTL.  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 2** and **December 31, Year 3** period-end income tax entries.  
e. Record the **December 31, Year 4 settlement / final reversal** income tax entry (DTL to zero).  
f. State how the **December 31, Year 1 DTL** is **classified** on the balance sheet and where the **Year 1 effect of multi-rate measurement** appears in the financial statements / rate reconciliation.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Schedule — Deferred tax liability, December 31, Year 1**

Future enacted rates are matched to taxable amounts in the years of reversal:

| | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Reversal of GAAP vs tax bases | $9,000 | $9,000 | $6,000 | $24,000 |
| Tax rate in effect in year of reversal | × 28% | × 25% | × 22% | |
| **Deferred tax liability (layer)** | **$2,520** | **$2,250** | **$1,320** | **$6,090** |

**DTL ending balance, Dec. 31, Year 1 = $6,090.**  
(Do **not** use a single flat 30% current rate for deferred taxes when future rates differ and are enacted.)

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Income tax JE (initial recognition of DTL + current tax)**

Current tax payable = $80,000 × 30% = **$24,000**  
Deferred tax expense = increase in DTL = **$6,090**  
Income tax expense = $24,000 + $6,090 = **$30,090**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 30,090 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | | 6,090 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 24,000 |
| *Current tax at 30%; DTL at multi-rate enacted future rates* | | |

**Check:** Dr 30,090 = Cr 6,090 + 24,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — DTL rollforward (emphasis)**

Cumulative taxable temporary difference (TD) and layered DTL:

| Date | Beg. cum. TD | Originating /(Reversing) TD | End. cum. TD | Beg. DTL | End. DTL computation | End. DTL | Δ DTL Dr/(Cr) to books |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Dec 31 Y1 | $0 | +$24,000 | $24,000 | $0 | 9k×28% + 9k×25% + 6k×22% | $6,090 | (6,090) |
| Dec 31 Y2 | 24,000 | (9,000) | 15,000 | 6,090 | 9k×25% + 6k×22% = 2,250 + 1,320 | 3,570 | 2,520 |
| Dec 31 Y3 | 15,000 | (9,000) | 6,000 | 3,570 | 6k×22% | 1,320 | 2,250 |
| Dec 31 Y4 | 6,000 | (6,000) | 0 | 1,320 | none remaining | 0 | 1,320 |

**Layer checks:**  
- Y2 Δ DTL debit = $9,000 × 28% = **$2,520** (reversal of the Year 2 layer).  
- Y3 Δ DTL debit = $9,000 × 25% = **$2,250**.  
- Y4 Δ DTL debit = $6,000 × 22% = **$1,320**.  
- Sum of debits over Y2–Y4 = **$6,090** = original DTL. **Schedule OK.**

**d. Period-end adjusting / tax provision JEs — Years 2 and 3**

**December 31, Year 2**  
Income tax payable = $90,000 × 28% = **$25,200**  
DTL decrease (debit) = **$2,520**  
Income tax expense = $25,200 − $2,520 = **$22,680**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 22,680 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 2,520 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 25,200 |
| *Current tax at 28%; reverse Y2 DTL layer* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,680 + 2,520 = 25,200. **Balanced.**

**December 31, Year 3**  
Income tax payable = $100,000 × 25% = **$25,000**  
DTL decrease = **$2,250**  
Income tax expense = $25,000 − $2,250 = **$22,750**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 22,750 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 2,250 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 25,000 |
| *Current tax at 25%; reverse Y3 DTL layer* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,750 + 2,250 = 25,000. **Balanced.**

**e. December 31, Year 4 — Settlement / final maturity of temporary difference**

Income tax payable = $70,000 × 22% = **$15,400**  
DTL decrease (final) = **$1,320**  
Income tax expense = $15,400 − $1,320 = **$14,080**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 14,080 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 1,320 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 15,400 |
| *Final reverse of taxable TD; DTL to zero* | | |

**Check:** Dr 14,080 + 1,320 = 15,400. **Balanced.**  
Ending DTL = **$0**; cumulative TD = **$0**.

**f. Classification / presentation / disclosure**

- **Balance sheet (Dec. 31, Year 1):** Deferred tax liability **$6,090** is classified as **noncurrent** (ASC 740 noncurrent presentation of deferred tax balances).  
- **Income statement:** Year 1 **income tax expense $30,090** includes current tax of $24,000 and deferred tax expense of $6,090. The multi-rate deferred component (vs. applying a flat 30% to $24,000 TD = $7,200) is recognized **in the period rates are enacted / applied to measurement**, not when differences reverse.  
- **Rate reconciliation / disclosure:** Temporary differences reversing at rates other than the current statutory rate cause effective tax rate to differ from the current statutory rate; companies disclose significant components of income tax expense (current vs deferred) and often explain rate-change / multi-rate effects in the statutory-to-effective reconciliation.

**Key insight:** Deferred taxes are measured at the **enacted rates expected when temporary differences reverse**. A phase-in of rates requires a **layer schedule**. Remeasurement and multi-rate measurement affect **income tax expense in the measurement period**; later years simply reverse the premeasured layers as the differences reverse.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Multi-rate DTL path with fully changed facts
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — same multi-rate deferred tax liability origin, schedule, rollforward, and settlement path with all amounts and rates changed  
**Scenario:**  
**Northpine Analytics LLC** (calendar year-end) has **taxable income of $150,000** for **Year 1**. It originates a **taxable temporary difference of $40,000** (book basis of depreciable assets > tax basis). Future taxable amounts on reversal:

| Year of reversal | Future taxable amount |
|---|---:|
| Year 2 | $16,000 |
| Year 3 | $14,000 |
| Year 4 | $10,000 |
| **Total** | **$40,000** |

**Enacted rates** (new law enacted late in Year 1):

| Period | Enacted rate |
|---|---:|
| Year 1 | **35%** |
| Year 2 | **32%** |
| Year 3 | **28%** |
| Year 4 | **24%** |

Beginning deferred tax balances = **$0**. No other differences. Taxable income: **Year 2 $175,000**; **Year 3 $120,000**; **Year 4 $95,000**. Current tax each year uses that year’s enacted rate. No new temporary differences after Year 1.

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **December 31, Year 1 DTL** by rate layer.  
b. Record the **Year 1 income tax JE**.  
c. Prepare the **DTL subsequent measurement rollforward** for Years 1–4.  
d. Record the **Year 2, Year 3, and Year 4** income tax journal entries.

**Answer key:**  

**a. DTL schedule — December 31, Year 1**

| | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Future taxable amount | $16,000 | $14,000 | $10,000 | $40,000 |
| Enacted rate | × 32% | × 28% | × 24% | |
| **DTL layer** | **$5,120** | **$3,920** | **$2,400** | **$11,440** |

**DTL, Dec. 31, Year 1 = $11,440.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Income tax JE**

Current tax payable = $150,000 × 35% = **$52,500**  
Deferred tax expense = **$11,440**  
Income tax expense = $52,500 + $11,440 = **$63,940**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 63,940 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | | 11,440 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 52,500 |
| *Current 35%; multi-rate DTL $11,440* | | |

**Check:** Dr 63,940 = Cr 11,440 + 52,500. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement — DTL rollforward**

| Date | End. cum. TD | End. DTL computation | End. DTL | Period Δ DTL Dr/(Cr) |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Dec 31 Y1 | $40,000 | 16k×32% + 14k×28% + 10k×24% | $11,440 | (11,440) |
| Dec 31 Y2 | 24,000 | 14k×28% + 10k×24% = 3,920 + 2,400 | 6,320 | 5,120 |
| Dec 31 Y3 | 10,000 | 10k×24% | 2,400 | 3,920 |
| Dec 31 Y4 | 0 | — | 0 | 2,400 |

**Checks:** Y2 debit $16,000 × 32% = $5,120; Y3 $14,000 × 28% = $3,920; Y4 $10,000 × 24% = $2,400; sum = $11,440. **OK.**

**d. Years 2–4 income tax JEs**

**Year 2:** Payable = $175,000 × 32% = **$56,000**; DTL debit **$5,120**; Expense = $56,000 − $5,120 = **$50,880**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 50,880 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 5,120 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 56,000 |

**Check:** 50,880 + 5,120 = 56,000. **Balanced.**

**Year 3:** Payable = $120,000 × 28% = **$33,600**; DTL debit **$3,920**; Expense = **$29,680**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 29,680 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 3,920 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 33,600 |

**Check:** 29,680 + 3,920 = 33,600. **Balanced.**

**Year 4 (settlement):** Payable = $95,000 × 24% = **$22,800**; DTL debit **$2,400**; Expense = **$20,400**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 20,400 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 2,400 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 22,800 |

**Check:** 20,400 + 2,400 = 22,800. **Balanced.** DTL ends at **$0**.

**Key insight:** Same LO 18-6 mechanics as Q1 with different base, rates, and layers—always **schedule by year of reversal × enacted rate**, then roll layers off as differences reverse.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Rate change remeasurement of existing DTA and DTL; then settlement
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Period-end remeasurement of existing deferred tax asset and liability when a new rate is enacted; income-statement direction of the adjustment; Year-2 settlement when temporary differences reverse  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakeshore Packaging Inc.** (calendar year-end) has the following **cumulative temporary differences** already on its books at **December 31, Year 1**, measured at the **prior enacted rate of 35%** (balances before considering a newly enacted rate change):

| Item | Cumulative temporary difference | Nature | Existing deferred tax balance (at 35%) |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Warranty accrual (liability) | $40,000 | Deductible | DTA **$14,000** |
| Equipment (book basis > tax basis) | $60,000 | Taxable | DTL **$21,000** |

On **December 31, Year 1**, a new corporate tax rate of **21%** is **enacted**, effective for **Year 2 and thereafter**. Both temporary differences are expected to reverse entirely in **Year 2** (warranty paid; depreciation timing catches up).  

**Year 1 taxable income = $200,000.** The **Year 1 current tax rate remains 35%** (the new 21% rate is not yet effective for current taxes). No valuation allowance is required. Ignore state taxes.

In **Year 2**, taxable income before considering the reversals’ cash/tax effects is managed such that **taxable income for the year is $180,000** after all deductible and taxable reversals are reflected in the tax return (i.e., use $180,000 as given taxable income). The Year 2 enacted rate is **21%**. At the end of Year 2, both temporary differences are **fully reversed** (ending DTA = DTL = $0).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare **schedules** of the **required ending DTA and DTL** at December 31, Year 1 after the rate change, and compute the **adjustment** to each account.  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 1 combined income tax journal entry** (current tax at 35% **and** remeasurement of DTA/DTL to 21%). Show T-account bridges for DTA and DTL.  
c. Explain the **net effect of the rate change** on Year 1 income tax expense (direction and amount).  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 2** income tax entry when the temporary differences **settle/reverse** and deferred balances go to zero.  
e. Briefly state **classification** of the net deferred tax position at December 31, Year 1 and that rate-change effects are recognized **only upon enactment**.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Ending deferred tax balances after rate change (Year 1)**

| Account | Cumulative TD | New enacted rate (Y2 reverse) | Required ending balance | Existing balance | Adjustment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Deferred tax asset | $40,000 deductible | 21% | **$8,400** | $14,000 | **Credit DTA $5,600** |
| Deferred tax liability | $60,000 taxable | 21% | **$12,600** | $21,000 | **Debit DTL $8,400** |

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Combined income tax JE**

Current tax payable = $200,000 × 35% = **$70,000**  

Net deferred tax effect of remeasurement:  
- Write-down of DTA **$5,600** → **increases** tax expense  
- Write-down of DTL **$8,400** → **decreases** tax expense  
- Net deferred tax **benefit** = $8,400 − $5,600 = **$2,800**  

Income tax expense = current $70,000 − net deferred benefit $2,800 = **$67,200**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 67,200 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 8,400 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 5,600 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 70,000 |
| *Current tax at 35%; remeasure DTA/DTL to 21% enacted future rate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 67,200 + 8,400 = 75,600; Cr 5,600 + 70,000 = 75,600. **Balanced.**

**T-account bridges:**

```
Deferred Tax Asset              Deferred Tax Liability
-------------------             ----------------------
Beg  14,000 |  5,600 Adj        Adj  8,400 | 21,000 Beg
            |                   |          |
End   8,400 |                   |   12,600 End
```

**c. Net income-statement effect of the rate decrease**

- Lower future rate **reduces the value of the DTA** → like losing an asset → **higher** tax expense (+$5,600).  
- Lower future rate **reduces the DTL** → like reducing a liability → **lower** tax expense (−$8,400).  
- **Net:** tax expense **lower by $2,800** because the DTL reduction exceeds the DTA reduction ($60,000 taxable TD > $40,000 deductible TD).  
- Effect recognized **entirely in Year 1** (period of **enactment**), not when differences reverse.

**d. December 31, Year 2 — Settlement / reverse temporary differences**

At Year 2 rates, beginning deferred balances (after Y1 remeasurement) are DTA $8,400 and DTL $12,600. Both reverse fully:

Income tax payable = $180,000 × 21% = **$37,800**  

Reversal of DTA: **credit DTA $8,400** (use of deferred tax benefit increases tax expense)  
Reversal of DTL: **debit DTL $12,600** (settlement of deferred tax expense decreases tax expense)  
Net deferred tax **benefit** from reversals = $12,600 − $8,400 = **$4,200**  

Income tax expense = $37,800 − $4,200 = **$33,600**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 33,600 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 12,600 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 8,400 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 37,800 |
| *Current tax at 21%; settle DTA and DTL as differences reverse* | | |

**Check:** Dr 33,600 + 12,600 = 46,200; Cr 8,400 + 37,800 = 46,200. **Balanced.**  
Ending DTA = **$0**; ending DTL = **$0**.

**e. Classification / enactment rule**

- At Dec. 31, Year 1: DTA $8,400 and DTL $12,600 are both **noncurrent**; often presented **net** as a **noncurrent deferred tax liability of $4,200** if same tax-paying component / jurisdiction (ASC 740 netting rules).  
- **Do not** adjust deferred taxes for an **anticipated** rate change—only when the rate is **enacted into law**. Remeasurement effect is recognized in **income tax expense** (continuing operations) in the **enactment period** (ASC 740-10-25-47; 740-10-35-4).

**Key insight:** A tax rate **decrease** hurts DTAs and helps DTLs. Both adjustments hit **current-period tax expense** at **enactment**. Later, when differences reverse, you reverse the **already-remeasured** balances—you do **not** re-apply the old rate.

### Q4 — MC — Timing and measurement of enacted rate changes
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Recognize deferred tax remeasurement only when a rate change is enacted; apply rates of the reversal years  
**Question:**  
On November 1, Year 1, the legislature **passes and the president signs** a law reducing the corporate tax rate from 30% to 22%, **effective January 1, Year 2**. On December 1, Year 1, a company’s board discusses a **possible** further cut to 18% that analysts expect to pass in Year 2, but no further law has been enacted. The company has a taxable temporary difference of $100,000 expected to reverse entirely in Year 2. At what amount should the **deferred tax liability** be reported at **December 31, Year 1**, and when is the remeasurement (if any from the 30% → 22% change) recognized?

- A) DTL $30,000; remeasure when the temporary difference reverses in Year 2  
- B) DTL $22,000; remeasure in Year 1 (period of enactment of the 22% rate); ignore the anticipated 18% rate  
- C) DTL $18,000; remeasure in Year 1 using the expected future rate of 18%  
- D) DTL $22,000; remeasure only on the January 1, Year 2 effective date of the new rate  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are measured using **enacted** rates applicable when differences reverse. The 22% rate is **enacted** in Year 1 for Year 2 reversals → DTL = $100,000 × 22% = **$22,000**. The effect of the change from any prior 30% measurement is recognized in **income tax expense in Year 1** (date of **enactment**), not on the effective date and not when the difference reverses. The **anticipated** 18% rate is **not** used until enacted.

### Q5 — MC — Income effect of a rate decrease on existing DTA vs DTL
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Direction of income tax expense when enacted rates fall for existing deferred balances  
**Question:**  
Immediately before a newly enacted rate decrease (from 40% to 25%, effective next year), a company holds only: (1) a deferred tax **asset** of $40,000 from a deductible temporary difference, and (2) a deferred tax **liability** of $12,000 from a taxable temporary difference. Both differences reverse next year. Ignoring current tax, what is the **net effect of remeasurement** on income tax expense in the **period of enactment**?

- A) Increase tax expense by $28,000  
- B) Decrease tax expense by $10,500  
- C) Increase tax expense by $10,500  
- D) Decrease tax expense by $15,000  

**Answer:** **C.**  

Supporting math:  
- Deductible TD underlying DTA = $40,000 ÷ 40% = **$100,000** → new DTA = $100,000 × 25% = **$25,000** → DTA write-down **$15,000** (increases expense).  
- Taxable TD underlying DTL = $12,000 ÷ 40% = **$30,000** → new DTL = $30,000 × 25% = **$7,500** → DTL write-down **$4,500** (decreases expense).  
- **Net increase** in tax expense = $15,000 − $4,500 = **$10,500**.
