# Agent 63 — CORE DEMO — LO 10-7

**Chapter:** 10  
**LO title:** Explain accounting treatment of inventory errors  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Correcting entry when inventory error is discovered **in the year of the error** (same-period fix to Inventory and COGS)
- **Counterbalancing** ending-inventory errors: effects reverse in the next year via beginning inventory → COGS
- Multi-year **error impact schedule** (BI, purchases, EI, COGS, pretax income — over/understated)
- Prior-period adjustment (Retained Earnings—PPA and Inventory) when error is found **before** self-correction
- **No journal entry** after self-correction; still restate comparative financial statements
- Noncounterbalancing inventory errors (e.g., purchase misclassified as equipment) require ongoing PPA until fully corrected
- Classification/presentation: restatement of all periods presented; PPA to opening retained earnings for periods before those presented
- Effect analysis for combined purchase/inventory cutoff errors (periodic system)

### Q1 — CORE — Counterbalancing ending-inventory overstatement: discovery in Year 1, 2, or 3
**LO:** LO 10-7  
**Concept:** Initial correcting JE (same year); PPA before self-correction; no entry after self-correction; two-year impact schedule  
**Scenario:**  
Meridian Outdoor Supply Co. takes a physical inventory each December 31 and adjusts perpetual records to the count. Management later learns that the **December 31, Year 1** physical count **overstated ending inventory by $18,000**. Purchases and sales were correctly recorded in both years. The **December 31, Year 2** ending inventory is **correct**. Ignore income taxes. Meridian uses a perpetual system that is closed to COGS at year-end for analysis purposes (periodic-style COGS rollforward below reflects the same error effects).

Amounts **as recorded with the error** (before any correction):

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | $45,000 | $72,000 (overstated) |
| Net purchases | 210,000 | 225,000 |
| Ending inventory | 72,000 (overstated) | 58,000 (correct) |
| Net sales | 280,000 | 320,000 |
| Other expenses | 40,000 | 42,000 |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare a two-year schedule showing **as recorded**, **correct**, and **error effect** (over/understated amount and direction) for: ending inventory, cost of goods sold, and pretax income.  
b. Assume the error is discovered **in Year 1** (before Year 1 statements are issued). Record the **correcting journal entry** and state the financial-statement impact on prior periods.  
c. Assume the error is discovered **early in Year 2** (before books self-correct and before Year 2 statements are issued). Record the **January 1, Year 2** correcting entry and describe restatement.  
d. Assume the error is discovered **in Year 3** (after Year 2 has closed). Record any Year 3 journal entry and describe restatement requirements.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Impact schedule**

Correct Year 1 ending inventory = $72,000 − $18,000 = **$54,000**.  
Correct Year 2 beginning inventory = **$54,000**.

| Item | Year 1 as recorded | Year 1 correct | Year 1 effect | Year 2 as recorded | Year 2 correct | Year 2 effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| Ending inventory | 72,000 | 54,000 | **Over $18,000** | 58,000 | 58,000 | None |
| COGS | 45,000 + 210,000 − 72,000 = **183,000** | 45,000 + 210,000 − 54,000 = **201,000** | **Under $18,000** | 72,000 + 225,000 − 58,000 = **239,000** | 54,000 + 225,000 − 58,000 = **221,000** | **Over $18,000** |
| Pretax income | 280,000 − 183,000 − 40,000 = **57,000** | 280,000 − 201,000 − 40,000 = **39,000** | **Over $18,000** | 320,000 − 239,000 − 42,000 = **39,000** | 320,000 − 221,000 − 42,000 = **57,000** | **Under $18,000** |

**Check:** Year 1 pretax overstatement $18,000 + Year 2 pretax understatement $18,000 = **$0** net over two years (counterbalancing). Cumulative pretax income over two years is correct at $57,000 + $39,000 = $96,000 as recorded and as correct.

**b. Discovery in Year 1 (same accounting period) — initial recognition correcting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 18,000 | |
| Inventory | | 18,000 |
| *To correct overstated Year 1 physical inventory count* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 18,000.  
**FS impact:** No prior-period restatement (error fixed in the year it occurred). Year 1 statements report correct inventory, COGS, and income.

**c. Discovery in Year 2 before self-correction — prior period adjustment**

At January 1, Year 2, Inventory and beginning Retained Earnings are still overstated by $18,000 (Year 1 NI was overstated).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 18,000 | |
| Inventory | | 18,000 |
| *To correct Year 1 inventory overstatement discovered in Year 2 before self-correction* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 18,000.  
**FS impact:** Restate **all** comparative financial statements presented (correct Year 1 EI, COGS, NI, RE). Year 2 operations then proceed from correct BI = $54,000.

**d. Discovery in Year 3 after self-correction**

In Year 2, overstated BI caused COGS overstatement of $18,000 and pretax income understatement of $18,000, **offsetting** the Year 1 income overstatement. By the start of Year 3, Inventory is correct and Retained Earnings is correct **in total**.

**Journal entry in Year 3:** **None** (counterbalancing error has self-corrected).  
**FS impact:** Still **restate** all comparative periods presented (e.g., show correct Year 1 and Year 2 amounts if both years appear) so users see proper year-by-year income; do not book a current-period PPA once books have already self-corrected.

**Key insight:** Overstated ending inventory understates COGS and overstates pretax income in the error year; the reverse hits the next year through beginning inventory. Same-year discovery: Dr COGS / Cr Inventory. Discovery before self-correction: Dr RE—PPA / Cr Inventory. After self-correction: no JE, but restate presented statements.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Ending inventory understatement: twin schedule and correcting entries
**LO:** LO 10-7  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — understated EI (opposite direction); same-period JE vs PPA vs no entry after self-correction  
**Scenario:**  
Cedar Ridge Trading LLC discovers that its **December 31, Year 1** ending inventory was **understated by $12,500** due to omitted warehouse locations. Purchases were correct both years. **Year 2 ending inventory is correct**. Ignore taxes.

Amounts **as recorded with the error**:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | $32,000 | $41,000 (understated) |
| Net purchases | 148,000 | 160,000 |
| Ending inventory | 41,000 (understated) | 48,000 (correct) |
| Net sales | 240,000 | 275,000 |
| Other expenses | 35,000 | 38,000 |

**Required:**  
a. Complete the two-year correct vs as-recorded analysis for EI, COGS, and pretax income.  
b. Correcting entry if discovered **December 31, Year 1**.  
c. Correcting entry if discovered **January 1, Year 2**.  
d. Entry (if any) if discovered **January 1, Year 3**.  
e. Briefly state whether the error is counterbalancing and the two-year net effect on pretax income.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Analysis**  
Correct Year 1 EI = $41,000 + $12,500 = **$53,500**.

| Item | Year 1 recorded | Year 1 correct | Effect | Year 2 recorded | Year 2 correct | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| EI | 41,000 | 53,500 | **Under $12,500** | 48,000 | 48,000 | None |
| COGS | 32k+148k−41k = **139,000** | 32k+148k−53.5k = **126,500** | **Over $12,500** | 41k+160k−48k = **153,000** | 53.5k+160k−48k = **165,500** | **Under $12,500** |
| Pretax | 240k−139k−35k = **66,000** | 240k−126.5k−35k = **78,500** | **Under $12,500** | 275k−153k−38k = **84,000** | 275k−165.5k−38k = **71,500** | **Over $12,500** |

**Math checks:**  
Y1 COGS over 12,500 → pretax under 12,500.  
Y2 COGS under 12,500 → pretax over 12,500.  
Two-year pretax total: recorded 66,000 + 84,000 = **150,000**; correct 78,500 + 71,500 = **150,000**. Net two-year error = **$0**.

**b. Discovered Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 12,500 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 12,500 |
| *To correct understated Year 1 ending inventory* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 12,500.

**c. Discovered January 1, Year 2 (before self-correction)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 12,500 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 12,500 |
| *To restore inventory and correct understated Year 1 NI via PPA* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 12,500. Restate comparative statements for Year 1.

**d. Discovered January 1, Year 3**  
**No journal entry** — error self-corrected through Year 2 COGS/income. Restate comparative FS if prior years are presented.

**e.** Counterbalancing **yes**. Net pretax effect over Year 1 + Year 2 = **zero**; individual years remain misstated if not restated.

**Key insight:** Understated EI is the mirror image of overstatement: COGS is overstated and income understated in Year 1; Year 2 reverses. Correcting JE directions flip (Dr Inventory / Cr COGS or Cr RE—PPA).

### Q3 — CORE — Alternate angles: multi-error effect table, dual-year EI errors, and noncounterbalancing misclassification
**LO:** LO 10-7  
**Concept:** Period-end effect analysis; PPA when only the latest EI error remains on the books; noncounterbalancing purchase-as-equipment error with correcting JEs  
**Scenario:**  
Part A and B use **Harborline Wholesale Inc.** (periodic inventory system). Part C uses **Summit Gear Works**. Ignore income taxes throughout.

**Part A — Effect analysis (classification of error impacts)**  
For each **separate** error below, indicate the effect on **current-year COGS**, **current-year pretax income**, and **current-year ending inventory**: overstated (O), understated (U), or no effect (NE).

| # | Error |
|---|---|
| A1 | Ending inventory overstated (count error only; purchases correct) |
| A2 | Ending inventory understated (count error only; purchases correct) |
| A3 | Ending inventory and purchases both overstated by the same amount (goods in transit recorded and counted incorrectly) |
| A4 | Ending inventory and purchases both understated by the same amount |
| A5 | Beginning inventory overstated |
| A6 | Beginning inventory understated |

**Part B — Two consecutive EI overstatements (PPA only for uncorrected balance)**  
In early Year 3, Harborline discovers that ending inventory was overstated by **$15,000** at December 31, Year 1 and by **$10,000** at December 31, Year 2. Purchases were correct each year. Year 2 books have already closed; Year 3 has just begun.

**Required (Part B):** Prepare the **January 1, Year 3** correcting journal entry (if any). Explain why the Year 1 amount does or does not appear in the entry.

**Part C — Noncounterbalancing error**  
On July 1, Year 1, Summit Gear Works purchased inventory costing **$40,000** on account but incorrectly debited **Equipment**. The company depreciates equipment straight-line over **10 years**, residual value zero, half-year convention in Year 1 (**$2,000** depreciation recorded in Year 1). The goods remained in inventory at December 31, Year 1 (none sold). The error is discovered on **January 5, Year 2**, before Year 2 statements. Ignore taxes.

**Required (Part C):**  
1. State whether this error is counterbalancing or noncounterbalancing and why.  
2. Prepare the **January 5, Year 2** correcting entry (or entries) to fix inventory, equipment, accumulated depreciation / depreciation expense effects on RE, and accounts payable if still misstated. Assume Accounts Payable was correctly credited for $40,000 when the purchase was recorded (only the debit was wrong).  
3. State presentation/disclosure treatment for prior-period amounts.

**Answer key:**  

**Part A**

| # | Current COGS | Current pretax income | Current EI |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | U | O | O |
| A2 | O | U | U |
| A3 | NE (purchases↑ and EI↑ offset in COGS) | NE | O |
| A4 | NE | NE | U |
| A5 | O | U | NE (EI is independent of BI error for the current count) |
| A6 | U | O | NE |

**Logic notes:**  
- A1/A2: COGS = BI + Purch − EI → EI error alone moves COGS opposite to EI and income same direction as EI.  
- A3: Purch over + EI over by same amount → COGS unchanged; BS shows inventory (and typically AP if credit purchase) overstated.  
- A4: Symmetric to A3.  
- A5/A6: BI error flows fully into COGS; year-end physical EI is not caused by BI error.

**Part B — January 1, Year 3 correcting entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 10,000 | |
| Inventory | | 10,000 |
| *To remove remaining Year 2 EI overstatement still on the books* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,000.

**Why not $15,000 or $25,000?**  
The Year 1 overstatement of $15,000 **self-corrected in Year 2** (Year 2 BI overstated → Year 2 COGS overstated → Year 2 NI understated by $15,000). By January 1, Year 3, only the **Year 2** EI overstatement of **$10,000** still overstates Inventory and Retained Earnings. Restate comparative statements for both prior years as presented.

**Part C — Noncounterbalancing misclassification**

**1.** **Noncounterbalancing.** Misrecording a purchase as equipment does **not** reverse within two consecutive periods solely through the inventory equation. Equipment remains on the books and is depreciated over 10 years; inventory, COGS (when sold), and depreciation remain wrong until a prior-period correction is recorded.

**2. Correcting entry January 5, Year 2**  
Remove equipment cost and related depreciation already closed to RE; place cost in Inventory (still on hand at end of Year 1).

Depreciation taken in Year 1 that should not have been taken = **$2,000** (overstated expense → understated RE).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 40,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 2,000 | |
| Equipment | | 40,000 |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 2,000 |
| *To reclassify Year 1 purchase from equipment to inventory and reverse improper Year 1 depreciation closed to RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 40,000 + 2,000 = 42,000; Cr 40,000 + 2,000 = 42,000. Balanced.

(If Year 1 depreciation had not yet been closed, credit Depreciation Expense instead of RE—PPA for the $2,000; here discovery is in Year 2 so RE—PPA is appropriate.)

**3. Presentation:** Restate all financial statements presented. Cumulative effect of periods before those presented adjusts **opening retained earnings**. Disclose nature of error and quantitative effects on inventory, equipment (net), and income as applicable.

**Key insight:** Counterbalancing EI count errors self-correct in two years; only the latest uncorrected EI error needs a PPA at the discovery date after later years have closed. Misclassifying purchases as long-lived assets is noncounterbalancing and needs a full balance-sheet reclassification plus RE adjustment for cumulative income effects (e.g., excess depreciation).

### Q4 — MC — Error type and correcting-entry timing
**LO:** LO 10-7  
**Concept:** Classification of counterbalancing vs noncounterbalancing errors; when a PPA journal entry is required  

**Question 1:**  
On December 31, Year 1, Pine & Stone Retail overstated ending inventory by $7,000 (purchases correct). The error is discovered on March 1, Year **3**, after Year 2 books have closed and Year 2 ending inventory was correct. Ignoring taxes, which statement is best?

- A) Debit Retained Earnings—PPA $7,000 and credit Inventory $7,000 on March 1, Year 3.  
- B) Debit Inventory $7,000 and credit Retained Earnings—PPA $7,000 on March 1, Year 3.  
- C) No correcting journal entry is required in Year 3 because the error is counterbalancing and has self-corrected; comparative statements should still be restated.  
- D) Debit Cost of Goods Sold $7,000 and credit Inventory $7,000 in Year 3 as a current-period correction.

**Answer:** **C.** Year 1 income was overstated $7,000 and Year 2 income understated $7,000, so RE and inventory are correct by Year 3. After self-correction, no JE; restatement of presented prior periods is still required. A/B would double-correct. D treats a prior error as current COGS and is wrong after self-correction.

---

**Question 2:**  
Which of the following inventory-related errors is **noncounterbalancing** (does not self-correct within two consecutive periods solely through BI/EI)?

- A) Ending inventory understated due to a math error in the physical count; next year’s count is correct.  
- B) Ending inventory overstated because consigned-in goods were included; next year’s count is correct.  
- C) Goods purchased FOB shipping point were neither recorded as a purchase nor included in EI in Year 1; both purchase and inventory are recorded correctly when received in Year 2.  
- D) A merchandise purchase was debited to Equipment and is being depreciated over several years; the goods were sold in the following year.

**Answer:** **D.** Equipment misclassification and multi-year depreciation do not reverse through the two-period inventory rollforward alone; a PPA (and reclassification) is required. A and B are classic counterbalancing EI errors. C is the special case where **omitting both** the purchase and EI leaves **COGS and income unaffected** in Year 1 (and next-year COGS/EI effects depend on correct recording when goods arrive); it is not the multi-year noncounterbalancing pattern illustrated by purchase-as-equipment.
