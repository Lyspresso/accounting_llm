# Agent 176 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-7

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Describe receivables disclosures and ratio analyses  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Receivable disclosures (ASC 310-10-50):** accounting policies for trade receivables; major categories; **allowance balance**; charge-off / past-due policies; receivables used as **collateral**; **allowance activity** (beg, provision, write-offs, recoveries, end); estimation methodology and judgment factors; significant purchases/sales of receivables; fair value (excluding short-term trade AR)
- **Concentration of credit risk:** disclosure when receivables share common economic risks or are concentrated with a major customer / industry / region
- **Accounts receivable turnover:** Net sales ÷ Average net accounts receivable (AR less allowance)
- **Average days to collect receivables:** 365 ÷ Accounts receivable turnover
- **Initial recognition JEs:** credit sales; recovery reinstate; cash collections
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period **Accounts Receivable** and **Allowance for Doubtful Accounts** rollforwards used both for measurement and for **note disclosure** of allowance activity
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** provision (Bad Debt Expense) to set allowance to desired ending balance; BS presentation of gross AR, allowance, and net AR
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** write-off of specific uncollectible accounts (Dr AFDA / Cr AR); recovery settlement (reinstate then collect)
- **Ratio analysis:** interpret year-over-year changes in turnover and collection days (efficiency of credit/collection policies and collectibility trends)
- **Not core for this pack:** fair value option election (Expanding Your Knowledge sidebar only)

### Q1 — CORE — Allowance activity disclosure schedule, BS presentation, write-off/recovery, AR turnover & days
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of credit sales and collections; subsequent measurement schedules (AR and AFDA rollforwards for disclosure); period-end CECL provision JE; write-off and recovery settlement JEs; required gross/net AR and allowance-activity disclosures; AR turnover and average days to collect with year-over-year analysis  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Marine Outfitters Inc.** is a calendar-year merchandiser. Management estimates expected credit losses under the **CECL allowance method** and prepares year-end note disclosures required under ASC 310-10-50.

**Balances at January 1, Year 2:**
- Accounts receivable (gross): **$420,000**
- Allowance for doubtful accounts: **$18,900** credit  
- Net accounts receivable: **$401,100**

**Activity during Year 2:**
1. Credit sales: **$2,640,000** (all trade receivables)
2. Cash collections on account (excluding recoveries): **$2,485,000**
3. Accounts written off as uncollectible: **$22,400**
4. Recoveries of accounts previously written off: **$3,100** (each recovery is fully reinstated and then collected in cash)

**December 31, Year 2 aging** of ending gross receivables (historical loss rates adjusted for current conditions and reasonable and supportable forecasts):

| Age category | Receivable balance | Expected credit loss rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Not past due (current) | $320,000 | 1.5% |
| 1–30 days past due | 125,000 | 4.0% |
| 31–60 days past due | 72,000 | 15.0% |
| Over 60 days past due | 35,600 | 40.0% |
| **Total** | **$552,600** | |

**Comparative information for ratio analysis:**
- Year 1 net credit sales: **$2,380,000**
- Net accounts receivable, December 31, Year 0: **$365,000**
- Net accounts receivable, December 31, Year 1 (= Jan 1, Year 2 net): **$401,100**

**Additional disclosure facts at December 31, Year 2:**
- One customer, **Northbay Fleet Services**, represents **$154,728** of gross AR (**28%** of the ending AR balance).
- Harborline’s policy: trade receivables past due more than 90 days are placed on nonaccrual status for interest (if any); write-offs occur when collection is no longer reasonably expected after exhausting normal collection procedures.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **initial recognition** summary JE for Year 2 credit sales and the summary JE for cash collections (excluding recoveries).  
(b) Record the **write-off (settlement)** JE for uncollectible accounts and the **recovery settlement** JEs.  
(c) Prepare **subsequent measurement schedules** for Year 2: (1) Accounts Receivable rollforward; (2) Allowance for Doubtful Accounts rollforward to the **unadjusted** year-end balance; (3) aging schedule computing the **desired ending allowance**.  
(d) Record the **December 31, Year 2 period-end adjusting JE** for bad debt expense. Prepare the **balance sheet disclosure** of gross AR, allowance, and net AR.  
(e) Prepare the **note disclosure schedule of activity in the allowance for doubtful accounts** for Year 2 (beginning balance, provision, write-offs, recoveries, ending balance). List at least **four other** ASC 310-style receivable disclosures Harborline should consider (beyond the allowance rollforward and the face of the BS).  
(f) Compute **(1) accounts receivable turnover** and **(2) average days to collect receivables** for Year 2 and Year 1. Briefly analyze the year-over-year change and note the concentration-of-credit-risk disclosure implication of the Northbay balance.

**Check:** \(18{,}900 + 35{,}240 + 3{,}100 - 22{,}400 = 34{,}840\).

**Other receivable disclosures to consider (examples):**  
1. Accounting policies for trade receivables and for estimating the allowance (CECL methodology, risk pools, judgments, forecasts).  
2. Policy for charging off uncollectibles, determining past-due status, and nonaccrual treatment.  
3. **Concentration of credit risk** (e.g., Northbay Fleet Services ≈ 28% of AR).  
4. Receivables serving as **collateral** for borrowings (if any).  
5. Major categories of receivables if not shown separately on the face of the BS; significant purchases or sales of receivables.

**(f) Ratio analysis**

**Year 2**  
Average net AR = \((401{,}100 + 517{,}760) / 2 = 459{,}430\)  
AR turnover = \(2{,}640{,}000 / 459{,}430 = \mathbf{5.75}\) times  
Average days to collect = \(365 / 5.75 = \mathbf{63.5}\) days  
(or \(365 \times 459{,}430 / 2{,}640{,}000 = 63.5\) days)

**Year 1**  
Average net AR = \((365{,}000 + 401{,}100) / 2 = 383{,}050\)  
AR turnover = \(2{,}380{,}000 / 383{,}050 = \mathbf{6.21}\) times  
Average days to collect = \(365 / 6.21 = \mathbf{58.8}\) days  

**Analysis:** Turnover **fell** from 6.21 to 5.75 and days outstanding **rose** from about 59 to 64 days, indicating slower collections and/or more liberal credit terms relative to Year 1. Users should also evaluate the **concentration of credit risk** with Northbay (28% of AR): adverse changes in that customer’s economic condition could disproportionately affect collectibility and future allowance provisions.

**Key insight:** LO 8-7 pairs **qualitative disclosures** (policies, allowance activity, concentrations, collateral) with **quantitative ratio analysis** using **net** AR. The AFDA rollforward is both a subsequent-measurement control and a required note schedule; write-offs settle specific AR against the allowance without re-hitting income when the loss was already provided.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Collateral & concentration disclosures; period-end provision only; settlement write-off/recovery; multi-year ratios
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE and AFDA subsequent measurement/disclosure schedule; concentration of credit risk and collateral disclosures; settlement write-off and recovery; comparative AR turnover and days analysis; effect of higher provisions on ratio interpretation  
**Scenario:**  
**Solstice Kitchen Equipment Co.** (calendar year-end) is preparing Year 4 financial statement notes and ratio analytics for the audit committee.

**Selected Year 4 data:**
- Accounts receivable (gross), Dec 31, Year 4: **$960,000**
- Allowance for doubtful accounts, **beginning of Year 4:** **$36,000** credit  
- Write-offs during Year 4 (already recorded): **$41,200**  
- Recoveries during Year 4 (already recorded — reinstate + collect): **$5,600**  
- Management’s CECL estimate of **desired** ending AFDA (from pooling and aging): **$48,000**
- Net credit sales, Year 4: **$5,760,000**
- Net AR, Dec 31, Year 3: **$720,000**

**Disclosure facts at Dec 31, Year 4:**
- **$210,000** of trade receivables are **pledged as collateral** under a short-term borrowing arrangement with Summit State Bank (note payable outstanding **$175,000**).
- Trade receivables from customers in the **foodservice renovation** sector: **$384,000** (**40%** of gross AR). Those customers’ ability to pay is subject to common construction-cycle economic risks.
- Largest single customer balance: **$124,800** (**13%** of gross AR).

**Subsequent events / settlement in Year 5 (for settlement angle):**
- **January 22, Year 5:** Write off **$11,200** of a specific account (customer closed operations).
- **March 5, Year 5:** Unexpected recovery of **$2,800** of that written-off balance (reinstate and collect).

**Comparative ratio inputs:**
- Year 3 net credit sales: **$5,200,000**
- Net AR, Dec 31, Year 2: **$680,000**
- Net AR, Dec 31, Year 3: **$720,000**

**Required:**  
(a) Compute the **unadjusted** AFDA balance just before the Year 4 provision. Record the **December 31, Year 4 period-end adjusting JE**. Report ending **gross AR, AFDA, and net AR**.  
(b) Prepare the **Year 4 subsequent measurement / disclosure schedule** of activity in the allowance (beginning, provision, recoveries, write-offs, ending).  
(c) Draft concise **note-style disclosures** for (1) receivables pledged as collateral and (2) concentration of credit risk.  
(d) Record the **January 22 write-off** and **March 5 recovery** settlement JEs. Show that net AR is **unchanged** by the write-off alone (using post–Year 4 adjusted balances, before the recovery).  
(e) Compute AR turnover and average days to collect for Year 4 and Year 3. Explain how a **significant increase in bad debt expense** (relative to a stable sales base) would tend to affect (1) turnover and (2) days to collect if it increases the ending allowance and reduces ending net AR.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Unadjusted AFDA and period-end adjusting JE**

Unadjusted AFDA (before provision) = \(36{,}000 - 41{,}200 + 5{,}600 = \mathbf{\$400}\) credit.  
Provision = \(48{,}000 - 400 = \mathbf{\$47{,}600}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bad Debt Expense | 47,600 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 47,600 |
| *Adjust AFDA to CECL desired balance* | | |

**Check:** Dr 47,600 = Cr 47,600. **Balanced.**  
Ending AFDA = **$48,000** credit.  
Net AR = \(960{,}000 - 48{,}000 = \mathbf{\$912{,}000}\).

| Caption | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Accounts receivable (gross) | $960,000 |
| Allowance for doubtful accounts | (48,000) |
| **Accounts receivable, net** | **$912,000** |

**(b) Subsequent measurement / allowance activity disclosure schedule — Year 4**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Balance, beginning of year | $36,000 |
| Current-period provision | 47,600 |
| Recoveries of amounts previously charged off | 5,600 |
| Direct write-downs charged against the allowance | (41,200) |
| **Balance, end of year** | **$48,000** |

**Check:** \(36{,}000 + 47{,}600 + 5{,}600 - 41{,}200 = 48{,}000\).

**(c) Note-style disclosures**

**(1) Collateral:** At December 31, Year 4, trade accounts receivable of approximately **$210,000** were pledged as collateral under a short-term borrowing arrangement with Summit State Bank; related notes payable outstanding were **$175,000**.

**(2) Concentration of credit risk:** The Company has a concentration of credit risk arising from trade receivables from customers in the foodservice renovation sector, which comprised approximately **$384,000 (40%)** of gross accounts receivable at December 31, Year 4. Ability to collect those balances is subject to common economic conditions in that sector. The largest single customer balance was approximately **$124,800 (13%)** of gross accounts receivable.

**(d) Settlement JEs (Year 5) and net AR check**

*January 22, Year 5 — write-off*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | 11,200 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 11,200 |
| *Write off uncollectible account* | | |

**Check:** Dr 11,200 = Cr 11,200. **Balanced.**

Immediately **before** write-off: Gross AR \(960{,}000\); AFDA \(48{,}000\); **Net \(912{,}000\)**.  
Immediately **after** write-off: Gross AR \(948{,}800\); AFDA \(36{,}800\); **Net \(912{,}000\)**.  
Write-off alone does **not** change net AR or income (loss already provided).

*March 5, Year 5 — recovery*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 2,800 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 2,800 |
| *Reinstate recovered portion* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,800 = Cr 2,800. **Balanced.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 2,800 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 2,800 |
| *Collect recovered amount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,800 = Cr 2,800. **Balanced.**

**(e) Ratio analysis and BDE sensitivity**

**Year 4**  
Average net AR = \((720{,}000 + 912{,}000) / 2 = 816{,}000\)  
AR turnover = \(5{,}760{,}000 / 816{,}000 = \mathbf{7.06}\) times  
Average days = \(365 / 7.06 = \mathbf{51.7}\) days  

**Year 3**  
Average net AR = \((680{,}000 + 720{,}000) / 2 = 700{,}000\)  
AR turnover = \(5{,}200{,}000 / 700{,}000 = \mathbf{7.43}\) times  
Average days = \(365 / 7.43 = \mathbf{49.1}\) days  

**Analysis:** Turnover edged down and days edged up slightly—collections are still relatively strong but not quite as efficient as Year 3.

**Effect of a significant increase in bad debt expense (holding sales constant):** A larger provision increases ending AFDA and **decreases ending net AR**, which **decreases average net AR**. With sales unchanged, **turnover rises** and **average days to collect falls**—even though credit quality worsened. Users must not interpret a mechanical ratio improvement from a higher allowance as better collection performance; combine ratios with the **allowance activity disclosure**, aging quality, write-off trends, and concentration notes.

**Key insight:** LO 8-7 is not only “compute two ratios.” Core competence includes **what must be disclosed** (allowance activity, collateral, concentrations, policies) and **how ratios can mislead** if read without the subsequent-measurement (allowance) schedule.

### Q4 — MC — Required disclosure vs ratio interpretation
**LO:** LO 8-7  
**Concept:** Classification of required receivables disclosures and correct interpretation of accounts receivable turnover  

**Question 1:**  
Under ASC 310-style receivables disclosure guidance emphasized in LO 8-7, which item is a company **most clearly required** to disclose regarding the allowance for doubtful accounts?

- A) Only the ending allowance balance, with no reconciliation of activity  
- B) Beginning and ending allowance balances, current-period provisions, write-offs charged to the allowance, and recoveries of amounts previously charged off  
- C) Fair value of all trade receivables due within one year, updated each quarter  
- D) The name and taxpayer ID of every customer whose balance exceeds 5% of AR  

**Answer:** **B.** ASC 310-10-50 requires activity in the allowance for each period (beginning balance, provisions, direct write-downs, recoveries, ending balance), not merely an ending total. Fair value disclosure generally excludes short-term trade receivables; individual customer TIDs are not a standard allowance disclosure.

**Question 2:**  
Accounts receivable turnover is correctly computed as:

- A) Average gross accounts receivable ÷ Net sales  
- B) Net sales ÷ Ending gross accounts receivable  
- C) Net sales ÷ Average net accounts receivable (AR less allowance)  
- D) 365 ÷ Average net accounts receivable  

**Answer:** **C.** Turnover = Net sales / Average **net** accounts receivable. Average days to collect = 365 / turnover (not 365 / average AR alone).
