# Agent 132 — CORE DEMO — LO 14-1

**Chapter:** 14  
**LO title:** Account for debt securities measured at amortized cost  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **HTM classification:** positive intent **and** ability to hold debt security to maturity → measure at **amortized cost**; ignore unrealized fair value changes on the face of the statements
- **Initial recognition JE:** Investment in HTM Securities at acquisition cost (par, discount, or premium, net); brokerage/transfer costs added to cost
- **Purchase price:** PV of contractual principal + interest discounted at the **market (effective) rate** at acquisition
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** effective-interest amortization table — cash interest, interest revenue (CV × effective rate), discount/premium amortization, rolling amortized cost
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** cash (or interest receivable) + amortize discount (Dr Investment) or premium (Cr Investment); credit Interest Revenue
- **Maturity / settlement JE:** collect face; derecognize Investment (CV = face after full amortization)
- **Sale before maturity (rare for HTM):** remove CV; record cash; gain/loss in **net income**; early sale without ASC-allowed circumstances may taint HTM portfolio
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** report Investment in HTM at amortized cost; interest revenue in NI; disclose aggregate fair value and unrecognized holding G/L in notes; no FVA account for pure HTM

### Q1 — CORE — HTM discount: purchase, effective-interest schedule, period-end JEs, maturity, presentation
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** HTM debt at discount — initial recognition JE; full subsequent measurement (amortization) schedule; period-end interest JEs; maturity settlement; classification/presentation/disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarhaven Industrial Corp.** is a calendar-year company. On **January 1, 2025**, Cedarhaven purchases **$200,000** face of **Meridian Forge 4%** bonds. The bonds mature on **December 31, 2027** (3 years) and pay interest **semiannually** on **June 30** and **December 31**. Cedarhaven has the **positive intent and ability** to hold the bonds to maturity and classifies them as **held-to-maturity (HTM)**. The bonds yield a **6%** market (effective) annual rate at acquisition. Cedarhaven uses the **effective interest method** and records investments **net** (no separate discount account). Fair value of the bonds on December 31, 2025 is **$191,200** (for disclosure only).

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **January 1, 2025 purchase price** (round to nearest dollar). Classify the investment and prepare the **initial recognition JE**.  
b. Prepare the complete **effective-interest amortization schedule** for the life of the bonds (all six semiannual periods). Show for each period: cash interest, interest revenue, discount amortization, and ending amortized cost. Force the final period so amortized cost equals face.  
c. Prepare the **June 30, 2025** and **December 31, 2025** journal entries for interest revenue and discount amortization.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, 2027 maturity (settlement) JE** after the final interest entry has been recorded.  
e. **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** How is the investment reported on the December 31, 2025 balance sheet and 2025 income statement? What fair-value information is disclosed (and what is **not** recorded)?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Purchase price, classification, and initial recognition**

Semiannual effective rate = 6% ÷ 2 = **3%**  
Semiannual periods = 3 × 2 = **6**  
Semiannual cash interest = $200,000 × 4% × 6/12 = **$4,000**

\[
\begin{align*}
\text{PV of interest} &= \$4{,}000 \times \text{PVAF}(3\%,6) = \$4{,}000 \times 5.41719144 = \$21{,}668.77 \\
\text{PV of principal} &= \$200{,}000 \times \text{PVF}(3\%,6) = \$200{,}000 \times 0.83748426 = \$167{,}496.85 \\
\text{Purchase price} &= \$189{,}165.62 \rightarrow \mathbf{\$189{,}166}\ \text{(nearest dollar)}
\end{align*}
\]

Discount = $200,000 − $189,166 = **$10,834**  
**Classification:** **HTM** — positive intent and ability to hold to maturity → **amortized cost**; ignore fair value changes in the accounts.

*January 1, 2025 — initial recognition*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in HTM Securities—Meridian Bonds | 189,166 | |
| Cash | | 189,166 |
| *Purchase $200,000 face 4% Meridian bonds to yield 6%; HTM at amortized cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 189,166 = Cr 189,166. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (effective interest — discount)**  
Interest revenue each period = beginning amortized cost × **3%** (rounded to nearest dollar).  
Discount amortization = Interest revenue − Cash interest.  
Ending amortized cost = Beginning + Discount amortization.  
**Period 6** plugs so ending cost = face $200,000.

| Date | Cash interest (2% × face) | Interest revenue (3% × beg. CV) | Discount amort. | Amortized cost (end) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, 2025 | — | — | — | **189,166** |
| Jun 30, 2025 | 4,000 | 5,675 | 1,675 | 190,841 |
| Dec 31, 2025 | 4,000 | 5,725 | 1,725 | 192,566 |
| Jun 30, 2026 | 4,000 | 5,777 | 1,777 | 194,343 |
| Dec 31, 2026 | 4,000 | 5,830 | 1,830 | 196,173 |
| Jun 30, 2027 | 4,000 | 5,885 | 1,885 | 198,058 |
| Dec 31, 2027 | 4,000 | 5,942 | 1,942 | **200,000** |
| **Totals** | **24,000** | **34,834** | **10,834** | |

**Rollforward checks:**  
- Discount amortized: $10,834 = purchase discount.  
- Total interest revenue − total cash = total discount amort.: $34,834 − $24,000 = $10,834.  
- Ending CV at maturity = face. **Schedule rolls forward.**

Supporting interest-revenue math (selected):  
- Jun 30, 2025: $189,166 × 0.03 = $5,674.98 → **$5,675**  
- Dec 31, 2025: $190,841 × 0.03 = $5,725.23 → **$5,725**  
- Dec 31, 2027 (plug): amort = $200,000 − $198,058 = **$1,942**; interest rev = $4,000 + $1,942 = **$5,942**

**c. Period-end interest and amortization JEs (Year 2025)**

*June 30, 2025*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 4,000 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Meridian Bonds | 1,675 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 5,675 |
| *Semiannual cash interest + discount amortization (effective interest)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 + 1,675 = Cr 5,675. **Balanced.**  
CV after entry = $189,166 + $1,675 = **$190,841**.

*December 31, 2025*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 4,000 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Meridian Bonds | 1,725 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 5,725 |
| *Semiannual cash interest + discount amortization* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 + 1,725 = Cr 5,725. **Balanced.**  
CV at Dec 31, 2025 = $190,841 + $1,725 = **$192,566**.  
2025 interest revenue in NI = $5,675 + $5,725 = **$11,400**.

**d. Maturity / settlement JE (December 31, 2027)**  
After the final interest entry, amortized cost = face = **$200,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 200,000 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Meridian Bonds | | 200,000 |
| *Collect face at maturity; derecognize HTM investment* | | |

**Check:** Dr 200,000 = Cr 200,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification / presentation / disclosure (Dec 31, 2025)**

| Statement | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Balance sheet** | Investment in HTM securities (Meridian bonds) at **amortized cost $192,566** (noncurrent unless maturity within one year of BS date — here remaining term is 2 years → noncurrent) |
| **Income statement** | Interest revenue **$11,400** in net income (other revenue) |
| **Fair value** | **Not** adjusted on the books. **Disclose** in notes: amortized cost $192,566; fair value $191,200; unrecognized holding **loss** $1,366 ($192,566 − $191,200) |
| **No FVA account** | HTM at amortized cost does **not** use Fair Value Adjustment / unrealized G/L in AOCI or NI for ordinary FV changes |

**Key insight:** Under LO 14-1, HTM debt is carried at **amortized cost**. The **effective-interest schedule** is the control document: cash is fixed by the coupon; interest revenue = CV × yield; the difference amortizes discount and walks CV to face by maturity. Fair value is a **footnote**, not a period-end adjusting entry.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — HTM discount twin (all numbers changed)
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — HTM purchase at discount; full effective-interest subsequent measurement schedule; Year-1 period-end JEs; maturity settlement

**Northvale Logistics Inc.** (calendar year) purchases on **January 1, 2026** a **$250,000** face **Portage Utilities 3%** bond issue maturing **December 31, 2029** (4 years). Interest is paid **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. Northvale has the positive intent and ability to hold to maturity (**HTM**). Market yield at purchase is **5%** annual. Effective interest method; net investment account. Fair value at December 31, 2026 is **$235,000** (disclosure only).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — HTM premium schedule, period-end JE, early sale (settlement), presentation
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** HTM purchased at a premium — subsequent measurement schedule; period-end premium amortization JE; sale before maturity with realized G/L in NI; classification implications of early sale  
**Scenario:**  
On **January 1, 2025**, **Solstice Beverage Co.** purchases **$180,000** face of **Redwood Partners 7%** bonds maturing **December 31, 2027**. Interest is paid **semiannually** (June 30 / December 31). Solstice classifies the bonds as **HTM** (positive intent and ability). Market yield at purchase is **5%** annual. Effective interest method; net investment recording.

**Alternate event:** On **January 1, 2027** (immediately after the December 31, 2026 interest entry), Solstice sells **all** of the bonds for **$183,200** cash because of a **significant deterioration in the issuer’s creditworthiness** (an ASC 320 circumstance that does **not** taint the remaining HTM portfolio).

**Required:**  
a. Compute the purchase price (nearest dollar) and record the **January 1, 2025** purchase JE.  
b. Prepare the **full effective-interest amortization schedule** (premium).  
c. Record the **June 30, 2025** interest/premium amortization JE only (illustrative period-end adjusting pattern).  
d. Using the schedule, determine amortized cost on **January 1, 2027** and record the **sale (disposal) JE**.  
e. **Classification / presentation:** (i) How would the investment appear on the June 30, 2025 balance sheet and what interest revenue appears for the six months ended June 30, 2025? (ii) Does this early sale, given the stated reason, call into question Solstice’s intent to hold **other** HTM securities?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Purchase price and initial recognition**

Semiannual effective rate = 5% ÷ 2 = **2.5%**  
Periods = 3 × 2 = **6**  
Cash interest = $180,000 × 7% × 6/12 = **$6,300**

\[
\begin{align*}
\text{Purchase price} &= \$6{,}300 \times \text{PVAF}(2.5\%,6) + \$180{,}000 \times \text{PVF}(2.5\%,6) \\
&= \$34{,}728.29 + \$155{,}186.34 = \$189{,}914.63 \rightarrow \mathbf{\$189{,}915}
\end{align*}
\]

Premium = $189,915 − $180,000 = **$9,915**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in HTM Securities—Redwood Bonds | 189,915 | |
| Cash | | 189,915 |
| *Purchase $180,000 face 7% Redwood bonds to yield 5%; HTM at premium* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 189,915. **Balanced.**

**b. Effective-interest amortization schedule (premium)**  
Interest revenue = beg. CV × 2.5% (nearest dollar).  
Premium amortization = Cash interest − Interest revenue.  
Ending CV = Beginning − Premium amortization.  
Period 6 plugs to face.

| Date | Cash interest (3.5% × face) | Interest revenue (2.5% × beg. CV) | Premium amort. | Amortized cost (end) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, 2025 | — | — | — | **189,915** |
| Jun 30, 2025 | 6,300 | 4,748 | 1,552 | 188,363 |
| Dec 31, 2025 | 6,300 | 4,709 | 1,591 | 186,772 |
| Jun 30, 2026 | 6,300 | 4,669 | 1,631 | 185,141 |
| Dec 31, 2026 | 6,300 | 4,629 | 1,671 | 183,470 |
| Jun 30, 2027 | 6,300 | 4,587 | 1,713 | 181,757 |
| Dec 31, 2027 | 6,300 | 4,543 | 1,757 | **180,000** |
| **Totals** | **37,800** | **27,885** | **9,915** | |

**Checks:** Premium amort. total $9,915 = initial premium; cash − interest rev. = premium amort. ($37,800 − $27,885 = $9,915); final CV = face.  
Period 6 plug: amort = $181,757 − $180,000 = **$1,757**; interest rev = $6,300 − $1,757 = **$4,543**.

**c. June 30, 2025 period-end JE (premium)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 6,300 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Redwood Bonds | | 1,552 |
| Interest Revenue | | 4,748 |
| *Cash interest less premium amortization → interest revenue* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,300 = Cr 1,552 + 4,748. **Balanced.**  
CV after entry = $189,915 − $1,552 = **$188,363**.

**d. Sale before maturity (January 1, 2027)**  
After Dec 31, 2026 entry, amortized cost = **$183,470**.  
Cash proceeds = **$183,200**.  
**Loss** = $183,470 − $183,200 = **$270** (realized in net income).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 183,200 | |
| Loss on Sale of Investment | 270 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Redwood Bonds | | 183,470 |
| *Sell HTM bonds before maturity due to issuer credit deterioration; remove CV; loss in NI* | | |

**Check:** Dr 183,200 + 270 = Cr 183,470. **Balanced.**

**e. Presentation and HTM intent**

**(i) June 30, 2025**  
- Balance sheet: Investment in HTM securities **$188,363** (amortized cost).  
- Income (six months): Interest revenue **$4,748**. No unrealized FV entry.

**(ii) Early sale and tainting**  
Sale due to **significant deterioration in the issuer’s creditworthiness** is an ASC 320-10-25-6 circumstance that **does not** call into question the entity’s intent to hold **other** HTM securities to maturity. (A pattern of unjustified early sales **would** risk reclassification of the HTM portfolio to trading or AFS.)

**Key insight:** Premium HTM reverses the discount pattern: cash **exceeds** interest revenue; amortization **reduces** CV toward face. Disposal before maturity compares **cash to amortized cost** (not original cost or face) and puts the realized G/L in **net income**.

### Q4 — MC — When amortized cost (HTM) applies
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** Classification — positive intent and ability required to measure debt securities at amortized cost (HTM)  
**Question:**  
Which investment is appropriately accounted for at **amortized cost** under U.S. GAAP (ignoring the fair value option)?

- A) Corporate bonds the investor plans to sell within 90 days to fund working capital  
- B) Municipal bonds the investor has the **positive intent and ability** to hold until contractual maturity  
- C) Equity securities representing a 5% voting interest with no significant influence  
- D) Corporate bonds the investor may sell if market yields fall 50 basis points, even though maturity is five years away  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Debt securities are measured at amortized cost only when classified **held-to-maturity**, which requires **positive intent and ability** to hold to maturity (B). Near-term sale intent → trading (FV-NI) (A). Equity securities are not HTM amortized-cost debt (C). Contingent intent to sell if rates move means intent is uncertain → not HTM (D); amortized cost would be inappropriate.
