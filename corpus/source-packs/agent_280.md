# Agent 280 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-8

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for conversion of debt into equity  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** convertible bonds recorded **entirely as debt** (no proceeds allocated to the conversion feature / equity)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** amortize discount or premium (straight-line in this pack) over the contractual life
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** update **interest expense** and **discount/premium amortization** at each reporting date (and to the conversion date if conversion is not on an interest date)
- **Conversion (settlement into equity) JE:** derecognize bonds payable and remaining unamortized premium/discount; credit **Common Stock** (par × shares) and **APIC** for the residual **carrying amount**; **no gain or loss** in net income; market price of stock is a distractor
- **Induced conversion:** recognize **Debt Conversion Expense** equal to the fair value of **excess** consideration offered; equity still measured from debt carrying amount under original conversion terms
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** convertible debt is a liability until converted; post-conversion, carrying amount is equity (CS + APIC); inducement cost is an expense (not debt extinguishment G/L under induced-conversion criteria)
- **Number-variant twin:** same LO path with all different amounts

**Method note (core exam path):** ASC 470-20 — convertible debt is accounted for **in its entirety as a liability** at issuance. On conversion under the instrument’s terms, the **carrying amount** (face ± unamortized premium/discount, reduced by any cash paid under the instrument) is reclassified to equity; **no G/L**. Always **update amortization and interest through the conversion date** before the conversion entry (period-end / conversion-date adjusting JE).

### Q1 — CORE — Convertible bonds at premium: issuance, period-end interest/amort, schedule, partial conversion

**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of convertible debt as straight debt; subsequent SL premium amortization schedule; **period-end adjusting JE** for interest and amortization (emphasis); partial conversion into equity at book value (no G/L); presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Forge Inc.** (calendar year-end) raises capital with **convertible bonds**. Company policy and U.S. GAAP treat the issue as **straight debt** at issuance (no separate equity for the conversion feature). Premium is amortized **straight-line**. Interest is paid **annually on December 31**.

**Facts:**
1. On **January 1, Year 1**, Meridian issues **$400,000** face of **6%**, **10-year** convertible bonds for **$424,000** cash. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **20 shares** of Meridian **$5 par** common stock.
2. Cash interest is paid each **December 31**. Straight-line amortization of the premium is used.
3. On **December 31, Year 3**, after the year-end interest payment and amortization entry, holders of **50%** of the bonds exercise conversion. Meridian common stock is trading at **$48 per share** on the conversion date (market price is given as a distractor only).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE**.  
b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** for cash interest and premium amortization (emphasis).  
c. Prepare a **premium amortization schedule** for Years 1–3 showing beginning unamortized premium, amortization, ending unamortized premium, and **carrying amount** at each December 31.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** conversion JE for the **50%** converted (after the Year 3 period-end interest/amort entry).  
e. State the **Year 3 balance-sheet presentation** of any remaining convertible bonds and the **equity accounts** credited on conversion. Explain whether a gain or loss is recognized and whether the **$48** market price enters the conversion entry.

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — Initial recognition (entire proceeds as debt)**  
Premium = \(424{,}000 - 400{,}000 = \$24{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 424,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | | 24,000 |
| Bonds Payable | | 400,000 |
| *Issue $400,000 face 6% 10-year convertible bonds at premium; no equity for conversion feature* | | |

**Check:** Dr 424,000 = Cr 24,000 + 400,000 = 424,000. **Balanced.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Period-end adjusting JE (cash interest + SL amort) — emphasis**  
Annual cash interest = \(400{,}000 \times 0.06 = \$24{,}000\)  
Annual SL premium amortization = \(24{,}000 / 10 = \$2{,}400\)  
Interest expense = \(24{,}000 - 2{,}400 = \$21{,}600\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 21,600 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 2,400 | |
| Cash | | 24,000 |
| *Period-end: pay annual coupon; amortize 1/10 of premium (reduces interest expense)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 21,600 + 2,400 = 24,000 = Cr 24,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement — premium amortization schedule (Years 1–3)**

| Date | Cash interest | Premium amort. | Interest expense | Ending unamort. premium | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Y1 (issue) | — | — | — | $24,000 | $424,000 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | $24,000 | $2,400 | $21,600 | 21,600 | 421,600 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 24,000 | 2,400 | 21,600 | 19,200 | 419,200 |
| Dec 31, Y3 | 24,000 | 2,400 | 21,600 | 16,800 | 416,800 |

**Roll-forward check:** \(24{,}000 - 3 \times 2{,}400 = 16{,}800\); carrying \(400{,}000 + 16{,}800 = 416{,}800\). ✓

*(Same compound period-end JE as part b each December 31 for Years 2 and 3 before conversion.)*

**d. December 31, Year 3 — Conversion of 50% of bonds (book-value method; no G/L)**  
Converted face = \(400{,}000 \times 50\% = \$200{,}000\)  
Unamortized premium allocated = \(16{,}800 \times 50\% = \$8{,}400\)  
Carrying amount converted = \(200{,}000 + 8{,}400 = \$208{,}400\)  
Shares issued = \((200{,}000 / 1{,}000) \times 20 = 4{,}000\) shares  
Common stock (par) = \(4{,}000 \times \$5 = \$20{,}000\)  
APIC = \(208{,}400 - 20{,}000 = \$188{,}400\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 200,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 8,400 | |
| Common Stock ($5 par) | | 20,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 188,400 |
| *Convert 50% of convertible bonds; equity = carrying amount; no G/L; ignore $48 market price* | | |

**Check:** Dr 200,000 + 8,400 = 208,400; Cr 20,000 + 188,400 = 208,400. **Balanced.**

**Remaining after conversion:** Bonds Payable **$200,000**; unamortized premium **$8,400**; carrying amount **$208,400**.

**e. Classification / presentation / disclosure**

**Balance sheet after conversion (Dec 31, Year 3)**  
- **Liabilities — long-term:** Convertible bonds payable \(200{,}000 + 8{,}400 = **\$208{,}400**\) (or face $200,000 plus unamortized premium $8,400).  
- **Equity:** Common stock **+$20,000**; APIC—Common **+$188,400** (total equity increase = carrying amount converted **$208,400**).

**Income statement:** **No gain or loss** on conversion under the terms of the instrument. The **$48** market price is **not** used to measure equity or to recognize G/L. Period-end interest expense of **$21,600** (full year on $400,000 before conversion on an interest date after the amort entry) is recognized as usual.

**Key insight:** Convertible debt is booked like **ordinary debt** at issuance. At each **period-end**, update interest and amortization. On conversion, **replace the carrying amount of debt with equity** (par + APIC)—**never** mark to the stock’s market price and **never** record conversion G/L under the contractual conversion privilege.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Convertible bonds at discount: issuance, period-end amort, full conversion

**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin—initial recognition of convertible debt at discount; SL amortization schedule; period-end interest adjusting JE; full conversion at book value (no G/L)  
**Scenario:**  
**Cobalt Ridge Systems LLC** (calendar year-end) issues convertible bonds recorded **entirely as a liability**. Discount is amortized **straight-line**. Interest is paid **annually on December 31**.

**Facts:**
1. On **January 1, Year 1**, Cobalt Ridge issues **$250,000** face of **8%**, **5-year** convertible bonds for **$240,000** cash. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **12 shares** of Cobalt Ridge **$2 par** common stock.
2. Cash interest is paid each **December 31**. Straight-line amortization of the discount is used.
3. On **December 31, Year 2**, after the period-end interest and amortization entry, **all** bondholders convert. Common stock is trading at **$55 per share** (distractor).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE**.  
b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** (cash interest + discount amortization).  
c. Prepare a **discount amortization schedule** for Years 1–2 (through conversion date).  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2 conversion JE** (full conversion).  
e. Briefly state why the **$55** market price does not affect the conversion entry amounts.

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — Initial recognition**  
Discount = \(250{,}000 - 240{,}000 = \$10{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 240,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 10,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 250,000 |
| *Issue convertible bonds at discount; entire issue as debt* | | |

**Check:** Dr 240,000 + 10,000 = 250,000 = Cr 250,000. **Balanced.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis)**  
Cash interest = \(250{,}000 \times 0.08 = \$20{,}000\)  
SL discount amortization = \(10{,}000 / 5 = \$2{,}000\)  
Interest expense = \(20{,}000 + 2{,}000 = \$22{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 22,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 2,000 |
| Cash | | 20,000 |
| *Period-end: pay coupon; amortize 1/5 of discount (increases interest expense)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,000 = Cr 2,000 + 20,000 = 22,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Discount amortization schedule (Years 1–2)**

| Date | Cash interest | Discount amort. | Interest expense | Ending unamort. discount | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Y1 (issue) | — | — | — | $10,000 | $240,000 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | $20,000 | $2,000 | $22,000 | 8,000 | 242,000 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 20,000 | 2,000 | 22,000 | 6,000 | 244,000 |

**Roll-forward check:** \(10{,}000 - 2 \times 2{,}000 = 6{,}000\); carrying \(250{,}000 - 6{,}000 = 244{,}000\). ✓

**d. December 31, Year 2 — Full conversion (book value)**  
Unamortized discount = **$6,000**  
Carrying amount = **$244,000**  
Shares issued = \((250{,}000 / 1{,}000) \times 12 = 3{,}000\) shares  
Common stock (par) = \(3{,}000 \times \$2 = \$6{,}000\)  
APIC = \(244{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \$238{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 250,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 6,000 |
| Common Stock ($2 par) | | 6,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 238,000 |
| *Full conversion; equity = carrying amount $244,000; no G/L* | | |

**Check:** Dr 250,000 = Cr 6,000 + 6,000 + 238,000 = 250,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Market price**  
The **$55** trading price is **irrelevant** for the issuer’s conversion entry. Under ASC 470-20, equity is measured by the **carrying amount of the debt** (face less unamortized discount), not by the fair value of shares issued. Using market value would improperly introduce a gain or loss, which is **not** recognized on contractual conversion.

**Key insight:** Number-variant twin of Q1 with a **discount** instead of a premium: period-end amort **increases** interest expense; conversion still reclassifies **carrying amount** to CS + APIC with **Dr Bonds Payable** and **Cr Discount** for remaining unamortized discount.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Mid-period conversion (update interest/amort first) + induced conversion

**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Period-end-style adjusting JE to the conversion date (accrue interest + amortize premium between interest dates); conversion settlement JE; **induced conversion** expense; classification of inducement vs extinguishment  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Logistics Corp.** (calendar year-end) has outstanding convertible bonds. Interest is paid **semiannually on June 30 and December 31**. Premium is amortized **straight-line by period**. On a date **between interest payment dates**, bondholders convert—so Harborline must first record a **conversion-date adjusting entry** (same mechanics as a period-end interest adjusting JE). Separately, management later uses a cash inducement on a remaining issue.

**Facts — Part A (mid-period conversion):**  
1. On **January 1, Year 1**, Harborline issued **$300,000** face of **6%**, **10-year** convertible bonds for **$306,000**. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **15 shares** of Harborline **$10 par** common stock.  
2. Semiannual cash interest = \(300{,}000 \times 0.06 \times 6/12 = \$9{,}000\).  
3. Straight-line premium amortization per **semiannual** period = \(6{,}000 / 20 = \$300\).  
4. All required entries through **June 30, Year 3** have been recorded. On **October 1, Year 3**, **all** bondholders convert under the original terms. Stock trades at **$36** per share (distractor). Harborline **pays cash** for interest accrued from July 1 through October 1 on the conversion date.

**Facts — Part B (induced conversion on a different issue):**  
5. **Sierra Peak Holdings** has **$180,000** face of **7%** convertible bonds outstanding **at par** (no unamortized premium or discount). Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **10 shares** of **$1 par** common stock.  
6. To induce prompt conversion, Sierra Peak offers **$40 cash per bond** if holders convert within a limited window that qualifies for **induced conversion** accounting. **All** holders accept. Market price of stock on the acceptance date is **$28** per share (distractor for the stock component; use for measuring the inducement only as needed per ASC 470-20 cash inducement = cash paid as excess consideration).

**Required:**  
a. Compute unamortized premium at **June 30, Year 3** and at **October 1, Year 3**.  
b. Prepare the **October 1, Year 3 conversion-date adjusting JE** (accrue 3 months interest and amortize 3 months of premium) — emphasis.  
c. Prepare the **October 1, Year 3 conversion JE** and the **cash payment of accrued interest**.  
d. For Sierra Peak (Part B), prepare the **induced conversion JE**.  
e. Classification: how is the cash inducement reported, and why is induced conversion **not** treated like a typical debt extinguishment gain/loss for the inducement?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Unamortized premium**  
Periods completed by June 30, Year 3: from Jan 1 Y1 through June 30 Y3 = **5 years × 2 = 10** semiannual periods.  
Premium amortized through June 30 Y3 = \(10 \times 300 = \$3{,}000\)  
Unamortized premium June 30 Y3 = \(6{,}000 - 3{,}000 = \$3{,}000\)  
3 months (July–Sept) = half of one semiannual period → amort = \(300 \times 3/6 = \$150\)  
Unamortized premium at conversion Oct 1 Y3 = \(3{,}000 - 150 = \$2{,}850\)

**b. October 1, Year 3 — Conversion-date adjusting JE (period-end style) — emphasis**  
Accrued cash interest (3 months) = \(300{,}000 \times 0.06 \times 3/12 = \$4{,}500\)  
Premium amort (3 months) = **$150**  
Interest expense = \(4{,}500 - 150 = \$4{,}350\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 4,350 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 150 | |
| Interest Payable | | 4,500 |
| *Update interest and amortization to conversion date (like a period-end adjusting JE)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,350 + 150 = 4,500 = Cr 4,500. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount of bonds at conversion = \(300{,}000 + 2{,}850 = \$302{,}850\).

**c. October 1, Year 3 — Conversion JE + settle accrued interest**  
Shares = \((300{,}000 / 1{,}000) \times 15 = 4{,}500\) shares  
Common stock (par) = \(4{,}500 \times \$10 = \$45{,}000\)  
APIC = \(302{,}850 - 45{,}000 = \$257{,}850\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 300,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 2,850 | |
| Common Stock ($10 par) | | 45,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 257,850 |
| *Convert all bonds; equity = carrying amount; no G/L; ignore $36 market price* | | |

**Check:** Dr 300,000 + 2,850 = 302,850; Cr 45,000 + 257,850 = 302,850. **Balanced.**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 4,500 | |
| Cash | | 4,500 |
| *Pay interest accrued through conversion date* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,500 = Cr 4,500. **Balanced.**

**d. Sierra Peak — Induced conversion JE**  
Number of bonds = \(180{,}000 / 1{,}000 = 180\)  
Cash inducement = \(180 \times \$40 = \$7{,}200\) → **Debt Conversion Expense**  
Shares under original terms = \(180 \times 10 = 1{,}800\)  
Common stock (par) = \(1{,}800 \times \$1 = \$1{,}800\)  
APIC = \(180{,}000 - 1{,}800 = \$178{,}200\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 180,000 | |
| Debt Conversion Expense | 7,200 | |
| Common Stock ($1 par) | | 1,800 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 178,200 |
| Cash | | 7,200 |
| *Induced conversion: expense = FV of excess cash inducement; equity still from debt carrying amount under original terms* | | |

**Check:** Dr 180,000 + 7,200 = 187,200; Cr 1,800 + 178,200 + 7,200 = 187,200. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification / presentation of inducement**  
- **Debt Conversion Expense $7,200** is recognized in **net income** (expense for the fair value of consideration transferred **in excess of** securities/consideration issuable under the **original** conversion privileges).  
- Equity is still measured from the **carrying amount of the debt** for the stock issued under original terms—not remeasured to the stock’s market price for a conversion G/L.  
- When the limited-time inducement criteria are met, the cash sweetener is **conversion expense**, not an early-extinguishment gain/loss computation that compares reacquisition price to net carrying amount of debt as a pure extinguishment.

**Key insight:** If conversion is **not** on an interest date, the **first** entry is a **period-end-style adjusting JE** (interest + amort through the conversion date). Only then is debt derecognized into equity. For **induced** conversion, **expense the excess consideration**; do not invent a conversion gain/loss on the debt carrying amount.

### Q4 — MC — APIC on partial conversion (book value; market price distractor)

**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Classification/measurement—APIC recognized on conversion equals carrying amount of debt converted minus par of shares issued  
**Question:**  
On January 1 of Year 1, **Pinnacle Wire Co.** sells **$360,000** of convertible bonds for **$330,000**. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **6 shares** of Pinnacle **$2 par** common stock. On December 31 of Year 2, after updating amortization, holders convert **50%** of the bonds when the **total** unamortized discount on the entire issue is **$24,000**. Common stock is trading at **$85** per share. What amount of **Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock** is recognized upon conversion?

- A) $168,000  
- B) $165,600  
- C) $162,000  
- D) $180,000  

**Answer:** **B. $165,600.**  
Converted face = \(360{,}000 \times 50\% = \$180{,}000\)  
Unamortized discount allocated = \(24{,}000 \times 50\% = \$12{,}000\)  
Carrying amount converted = \(180{,}000 - 12{,}000 = \$168{,}000\)  
Shares issued = \((180{,}000 / 1{,}000) \times 6 = 1{,}080\) shares  
Common stock at par = \(1{,}080 \times \$2 = \$2{,}400\)  
APIC = \(168{,}000 - 2{,}400 = \$165{,}600\)  
(A is carrying amount before subtracting par; C ignores remaining discount; D uses face with no discount and wrong par treatment; **$85** market price is a distractor and is not used.)

### Q5 — MC — Issuance of convertible debt: conversion feature classification

**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Classification—convertible debt recorded entirely as a liability; no proceeds allocated to equity for the embedded conversion feature (unless separate derivative/premium guidance applies)  
**Question:**  
**Lumen Bridge Corp.** issues **$200,000** of convertible bonds for **$206,000**. Management estimates that similar **nonconvertible** bonds would have sold for **$198,000**, implying **$8,000** of proceeds are “attributable” to the conversion feature. Under the core ASC 470-20 model emphasized in LO 16-8 (no separate embedded derivative), how should Lumen Bridge record the issuance?

- A) Credit Bonds Payable $200,000, Premium $6,000; also credit Paid-In Capital—Conversion Feature $8,000 and debit Discount $8,000  
- B) Credit Bonds Payable $200,000 and Premium on Bonds Payable $6,000; **no** equity for the conversion feature  
- C) Credit Bonds Payable $198,000 and Paid-In Capital—Conversion Feature $8,000  
- D) Credit Bonds Payable $200,000 and Paid-In Capital—Conversion Feature $6,000  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Convertible debt with an inseparable conversion feature is recorded **in its entirety as a liability**. Proceeds of **$206,000** → Bonds Payable **$200,000** + Premium **$6,000**. No objective basis is used to peel equity from the hybrid under the general LO 16-8 / Demo 16-8 model (contrast: bonds with **detachable** warrants do allocate). A and C improperly bifurcate equity; D ignores the premium and misclassifies.
