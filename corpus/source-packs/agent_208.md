# Agent 208 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-11

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Depreciation under GAAP and under the Internal Revenue Code are different  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize PPE at historical cost (same cost basis starts both book and tax records)
- **MACRS (tax) vs GAAP (book):** statutory recovery percentages by property class; residual value **ignored** for tax; half-year convention built into MACRS rates; class life ≠ useful life
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** full multi-year MACRS tax depreciation schedule with tax-basis roll-forward side by side with GAAP straight-line schedule
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** only **GAAP** depreciation is recorded on the books (tax MACRS is a tax-return / workpaper computation)
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** remove asset and accumulated depreciation at **book** carrying amount; book gain/loss ≠ tax gain/loss because tax basis differs after accelerated MACRS
- **Classification:** MACRS is for **tax** only; totals differ by residual; life-cycle pretax net effect converges at sale (cost − proceeds); deferred taxes deferred to Ch. 18

### Q1 — CORE — Full subsequent measurement: MACRS (5-year) vs GAAP SL schedules
**LO:** LO 12-11  
**Concept:** Initial recognition at cost; full multi-year MACRS tax subsequent measurement schedule (rates × cost, tax-basis roll-forward) vs GAAP straight-line schedule; Year-1 and Year-2 period-end book adjusting JEs; total book vs tax comparison  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverton Precision Fabricators Inc.** (calendar year-end) places a specialized fabrication cell in service on **January 1, Year 1**. Management elects **out of** bonus depreciation and uses the **MACRS** tables for the tax deduction. For financial reporting, Riverton uses **straight-line** depreciation over the asset’s useful life.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase price (cost basis) | **$90,000** |
| MACRS property class (tax) | **Five-year** property |
| Useful life (GAAP) | **8 years** |
| Residual / salvage value (GAAP) | **$10,000** |
| Book depreciation method | Straight-line |
| Bonus depreciation | Not used |

**MACRS five-year recovery percentages (given):**  
Year 1: 20.00% · Year 2: 32.00% · Year 3: 19.20% · Year 4: 11.52% · Year 5: 11.52% · Year 6: 5.76%

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1** journal entry for **initial recognition** of the equipment (cash purchase).  
b. Compute **annual GAAP straight-line** depreciation. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 2** adjusting entries for book depreciation.  
c. Prepare a complete **MACRS tax subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–6: beginning tax basis, MACRS rate, tax depreciation, ending tax basis. Confirm total tax depreciation and ending tax basis of zero.  
d. Prepare a side-by-side **subsequent measurement comparison** of tax depreciation vs book depreciation for Years 1–8 (book continues through Year 8; tax is complete after Year 6). Total both columns and the excess (tax − book) column.  
e. Briefly explain **why** total tax depreciation over the MACRS recovery period differs from total book depreciation, and whether MACRS recovery percentages are used for **GAAP** financial statements.

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition (same cost starts book and tax)**

*January 1, Year 1 — Purchase equipment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—Fabrication Cell | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 90,000 |
| *Capitalize historical cost; cost basis for book and tax* | | |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**

**b. GAAP depreciation and period-end adjusting JEs**

\[
\text{Annual SL depreciation} = \frac{\$90{,}000 - \$10{,}000}{8} = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}
\]

*December 31, Year 1 — Book depreciation (GAAP only)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 10,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Fabrication Cell | | 10,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,000. **Balanced.**  
Book carrying amount end of Year 1 = $90,000 − $10,000 = **$80,000**.

*December 31, Year 2 — Book depreciation (GAAP only)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 10,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Fabrication Cell | | 10,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,000. **Balanced.**  
Book carrying amount end of Year 2 = $90,000 − $20,000 = **$70,000**.

*(Year-1 MACRS tax depreciation of $18,000 and Year-2 MACRS of $28,800 are computed on the tax return / tax workpapers — they are **not** the book depreciation entries.)*

**c. MACRS tax subsequent measurement schedule (five-year class; residual ignored) — emphasis**

Cost × statutory rate. Tax basis end = beginning tax basis − current MACRS depreciation. Beginning tax basis Year 1 = **$90,000**.

| Year | Tax basis, beginning | MACRS rate | MACRS depreciation | Tax basis, end |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 90,000 | 20.00% | **18,000** | 72,000 |
| 2 | 72,000 | 32.00% | **28,800** | 43,200 |
| 3 | 43,200 | 19.20% | **17,280** | 25,920 |
| 4 | 25,920 | 11.52% | **10,368** | 15,552 |
| 5 | 15,552 | 11.52% | **10,368** | 5,184 |
| 6 | 5,184 | 5.76% | **5,184** | **0** |
| **Total** | | **100.00%** | **90,000** | |

**Schedule / roll-forward checks:**  
- $90,000 × 0.20 = $18,000; × 0.32 = $28,800; × 0.192 = $17,280; × 0.1152 = $10,368; × 0.1152 = $10,368; × 0.0576 = $5,184  
- Sum: 18,000 + 28,800 + 17,280 + 10,368 + 10,368 + 5,184 = **$90,000**  
- Ending tax basis after Year 6 = **$0** (full cost recovered for tax; residual ignored)

**d. Side-by-side subsequent measurement: tax (MACRS) vs book (SL)**

| Year | MACRS tax depreciation | Book SL depreciation | Excess of tax over book |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 18,000 | 10,000 | 8,000 |
| 2 | 28,800 | 10,000 | 18,800 |
| 3 | 17,280 | 10,000 | 7,280 |
| 4 | 10,368 | 10,000 | 368 |
| 5 | 10,368 | 10,000 | 368 |
| 6 | 5,184 | 10,000 | (4,816) |
| 7 | 0 | 10,000 | (10,000) |
| 8 | 0 | 10,000 | (10,000) |
| **Total** | **90,000** | **80,000** | **10,000** |

**e. Why totals differ; GAAP use**  
- Under **MACRS**, residual value is **ignored**, so the full **$90,000** cost is recovered for tax over the statutory class recovery period (half-year convention embedded in the rates).  
- Under **GAAP**, depreciable base is cost minus residual: **$80,000**; residual remains on the books until disposal.  
- The **$10,000** excess of total tax depreciation over total book depreciation equals the GAAP residual value.  
- **MACRS rates are not used for GAAP** financial reporting. ASC 360 requires systematic allocation of cost (less residual) over useful life. Recovery periods / percentages that do not fall within a reasonable range of useful life shall not be used as depreciation expense for financial reporting (ASC 360-10-35-9). Deferred tax accounting for temporary book–tax differences is covered in Chapter 18.

**Key insight:** **Initial recognition** capitalizes the same historical cost for book and tax. Divergence is entirely a **subsequent measurement** story: statutory MACRS (tax schedule) vs useful-life / residual straight-line (GAAP schedule). Only the **GAAP** amount is journalized as Depreciation Expense each period.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Three-year MACRS twin schedules
**LO:** LO 12-11  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: initial PPE recognition; full MACRS three-year tax subsequent measurement schedule vs book SL; multi-year period-end book JEs; tax basis vs book carrying amount after Year 2  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Valley Diagnostics LLC** (calendar year) purchases specialized small diagnostic tooling on **January 1, Year 1** for cash. The tooling is **three-year** MACRS property for tax. Summit Valley does **not** use bonus depreciation. Book policy: straight-line over useful life to residual.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash cost | **$40,000** |
| MACRS class (tax) | **Three-year** property |
| Useful life (GAAP) | **5 years** |
| Residual value (GAAP) | **$4,000** |
| Book method | Straight-line |

**MACRS three-year recovery percentages (given):**  
Year 1: 33.33% · Year 2: 44.45% · Year 3: 14.81% · Year 4: 7.41%

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **initial recognition** journal entry on January 1, Year 1.  
b. Compute annual book depreciation. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 2** book adjusting JEs.  
c. Prepare the **full MACRS tax subsequent measurement schedule** (Years 1–4) with tax-basis roll-forward. Round MACRS amounts to the **nearest dollar** (apply rate × cost; ensure total tax depreciation equals cost).  
d. Prepare a comparison table of tax vs book depreciation for Years 1–5 and total both columns.  
e. State the **tax basis** and **book carrying amount** (cost − accum. dep.) at **December 31, Year 2** (after Year-2 depreciation for both systems).

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—Diagnostic Tooling | 40,000 | |
| Cash | | 40,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 40,000. **Balanced.**

\[
\text{Annual SL} = \frac{\$40{,}000 - \$4{,}000}{5} = \mathbf{\$7{,}200}
\]

*December 31, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 7,200 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Diagnostic Tooling | | 7,200 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 7,200. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 2*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 7,200 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Diagnostic Tooling | | 7,200 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 7,200. **Balanced.**  
Accumulated depreciation after 2 years = $14,400.

**c. MACRS three-year tax subsequent measurement schedule**

| Year | Beginning tax basis | Rate | MACRS depreciation | Ending tax basis |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 40,000 | 33.33% | **13,332** | 26,668 |
| 2 | 26,668 | 44.45% | **17,780** | 8,888 |
| 3 | 8,888 | 14.81% | **5,924** | 2,964 |
| 4 | 2,964 | 7.41% | **2,964** | **0** |
| **Total** | | **100.00%** | **40,000** | |

Computations (rate × **cost**, not × remaining basis):  
- Y1: $40,000 × 0.3333 = **$13,332**  
- Y2: $40,000 × 0.4445 = **$17,780**  
- Y3: $40,000 × 0.1481 = **$5,924**  
- Y4: $40,000 × 0.0741 = **$2,964**  

**Total check:** 13,332 + 17,780 + 5,924 + 2,964 = **40,000**. Ending tax basis **$0**.

**d. Tax vs book comparison**

| Year | MACRS tax | Book SL | Tax − Book |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 13,332 | 7,200 | 6,132 |
| 2 | 17,780 | 7,200 | 10,580 |
| 3 | 5,924 | 7,200 | (1,276) |
| 4 | 2,964 | 7,200 | (4,236) |
| 5 | 0 | 7,200 | (7,200) |
| **Total** | **40,000** | **36,000** | **4,000** |

Difference in totals = GAAP residual **$4,000**.

**e. December 31, Year 2 positions**  
- **Tax basis:** $40,000 − $13,332 − $17,780 = **$8,888**  
- **Book carrying amount:** $40,000 − ($7,200 × 2) = **$25,600**  

Tax basis is **much lower** early because MACRS accelerates deductions and ignores residual. The **subsequent measurement schedules** diverge sharply even though both started from the same $40,000 cost.

**Key insight:** Changing the class (3-year vs 5-year) and cost changes the **speed** of tax recovery, but the structural contrast remains: MACRS recovers **100% of cost** for tax; GAAP leaves residual on the books and spreads depreciable base over **useful life**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Multi-year bases, disposal JE, book vs tax gain/loss
**LO:** LO 12-11  
**Concept:** Subsequent book and tax bases after multi-year depreciation schedules; period-end book adjusting JE in year of sale; disposal JE; book vs tax gain/loss classification and life-to-date pretax reconciliation  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakeshore Packaging Group** (calendar year) acquired a light delivery truck on **January 1, Year 1** for **$120,000** cash. The truck is **five-year** MACRS property for tax (no bonus depreciation). For books: useful life **6 years**, residual **$12,000**, straight-line.

On **December 31, Year 3**, after recording Year-3 book depreciation, Lakeshore sells the truck for **$55,000** cash.

**MACRS five-year rates:** 20.00%, 32.00%, 19.20%, 11.52%, 11.52%, 5.76%

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1** purchase (initial recognition) JE.  
b. Compute **annual book** depreciation. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** book depreciation adjusting JE (year of sale, full year).  
c. Prepare a **tax subsequent measurement / basis roll-forward** for Years 1–3 under MACRS and state tax basis at the sale date.  
d. Compute **book carrying amount** at the sale date and the **book** gain or loss. Prepare the **disposal** journal entry.  
e. Compute the **tax** gain or loss on sale. Briefly reconcile why book and tax gains/losses differ, and show that **net life-to-date pretax effect** (depreciation ± gain/loss) is the same for book and tax through the sale.  
f. **Presentation:** On the Year-3 financial statements, which gain/loss amount is reported—book or tax? Where does MACRS depreciation appear?

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—Delivery Truck | 120,000 | |
| Cash | | 120,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 120,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Book depreciation; Year-3 adjusting JE**

\[
\text{Annual SL} = \frac{\$120{,}000 - \$12{,}000}{6} = \mathbf{\$18{,}000}
\]

*December 31, Year 3 — Book depreciation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 18,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Truck | | 18,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 18,000. **Balanced.**

Accumulated depreciation after 3 years: $18,000 × 3 = **$54,000**.

**c. MACRS tax subsequent measurement / basis roll-forward (Years 1–3)**

| Year | MACRS rate | Tax depreciation | Cumulative tax dep. | Tax basis, end |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 20.00% | 24,000 | 24,000 | 96,000 |
| 2 | 32.00% | 38,400 | 62,400 | 57,600 |
| 3 | 19.20% | 23,040 | **85,440** | **34,560** |

Tax basis at sale (Dec 31, Year 3) = **$34,560**.

**d. Book carrying amount, book gain/loss, disposal JE**

Book carrying amount = $120,000 − $54,000 = **$66,000**  
Cash proceeds = **$55,000**  
**Book loss** = $66,000 − $55,000 = **$11,000**

*December 31, Year 3 — Dispose of truck*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 55,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Truck | 54,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 11,000 | |
| Equipment—Delivery Truck | | 120,000 |

**Check:** Dr 55,000 + 54,000 + 11,000 = 120,000 = Cr 120,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Tax gain/loss and life-to-date reconciliation**

Tax gain = proceeds − tax basis = $55,000 − $34,560 = **$20,440** tax gain  
(Books reported an **$11,000 loss** — different because tax basis is lower after accelerated MACRS.)

**Net effect on pretax income / taxable income through sale date:**

| Item | Tax | Book |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cumulative depreciation (Y1–Y3) | 85,440 | 54,000 |
| Gain (loss) on sale | 20,440 gain | (11,000) loss |
| **Net reduction of income** | 85,440 − 20,440 = **65,000** | 54,000 + 11,000 = **65,000** |

Net reduction equals cost − proceeds: $120,000 − $55,000 = **$65,000**. Timing (and classification between depreciation vs gain/loss) differs; **lifetime net economic effect converges**.

**f. Presentation / classification**  
- Financial statements report the **book** loss of **$11,000** (and book depreciation of **$18,000** in Year 3).  
- **MACRS depreciation and the tax gain** appear on the **tax return** (and in deferred tax / tax footnote analysis), not as the primary book depreciation amount.  
- Temporary differences between book and tax bases give rise to deferred tax accounting (Chapter 18); they are **not** corrected by recording MACRS on the GAAP books.

**Key insight:** At **disposal**, book gain/loss uses **book carrying amount** from the GAAP subsequent measurement schedule; tax gain/loss uses **tax basis** from the MACRS schedule. Accelerated MACRS typically produces a **lower tax basis** early on, so a sale can show a **tax gain** even when books show a **loss**.

### Q4 — MC — MACRS vs GAAP classification
**LO:** LO 12-11  
**Concept:** Classification of MACRS as tax-only system; residual ignored; statutory rates vs GAAP useful life and residual  
**Question:**  
Which statement best describes depreciation under the **Internal Revenue Code (MACRS)** relative to **U.S. GAAP**?

- A) MACRS recovery percentages must be used for both the tax return and GAAP financial statements so book and tax depreciation always equal.  
- B) MACRS assigns assets to statutory property classes, applies prescribed recovery percentages (with a half-year convention built into the rates), and **ignores estimated residual value**; GAAP allocates cost less residual over useful life and does **not** use MACRS rates as book depreciation.  
- C) Under MACRS, residual value reduces the depreciable tax base exactly as under GAAP straight-line.  
- D) Total depreciation over an asset’s life is always equal under MACRS and GAAP because both methods depreciate to residual value.

**Answer:** **B.**  
MACRS is a **tax** system: class lives, statutory rates, residual ignored, half-year convention embedded. GAAP uses useful life and residual (expense recognition principle). A is false (MACRS is not GAAP depreciation). C is false (MACRS ignores residual). D is false (totals differ by residual when residual > 0; even when residual is zero, the **pattern** of periodic amounts still differs).
