# Agent 68 — CORE DEMO — LO 11-3

**Chapter:** 11  
**LO title:** Account for acquisition of property, plant, and equipment through debt and equity issuances  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Initial measurement of PPE acquired with a **noninterest-bearing note** when asset fair value is **not** determinable (PV of note at market rate)
- **Discount on Note Payable** as a contra liability; effective-interest amortization over the note term
- Multi-period **carrying-amount schedule** for the net note liability
- Period-end adjusting entries for interest expense (discount amortization)
- Settlement / maturity payment of the note
- Initial measurement of PPE acquired by issuing **equity** (fair value of stock vs fair value of asset — more clearly evident)
- Initial measurement when **asset fair value is known** and market rate is unknown (impute rate from FV and face)
- Classification / presentation: Discount on NP (contra liability), Common Stock at par, APIC, PPE at cost

### Q1 — CORE — Noninterest-bearing note (FV of asset unknown): initial recognition, schedule, period-end interest, maturity
**LO:** LO 11-3  
**Concept:** PPE acquired with noninterest-bearing note — record at PV of debt when asset FV not determinable; amortize discount with effective interest through maturity  
**Scenario:**  
Ironvale Fabrication Co. acquires specialized CNC equipment on **January 1, Year 1**. The equipment is custom-built for Ironvale’s production line and has **no reliable stand-alone fair value**. Payment terms:

- Cash down payment: **$20,000** on January 1, Year 1  
- Noninterest-bearing note with **face $80,000**, due in a **single lump sum on December 31, Year 3** (three years; no stated interest)  
- Prevailing market interest rate for similar risk and duration: **10%**  
- Fiscal year-end: **December 31**  
- Ironvale amortizes Discount on Note Payable using the **effective interest method**  
- **Ignore depreciation** of the equipment for this problem (focus on acquisition cost and the note)

**Required:**  
a. Compute the present value of the note and the initial cost of the equipment. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1** acquisition journal entry.  
b. Prepare a **three-year effective-interest amortization schedule** for the note (beginning carrying amount, interest expense, ending carrying amount).  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 2** adjusting entries for interest.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** entries to record Year 3 interest and payment of the note at maturity.  
e. Prove that total interest expense over the three years equals the original discount.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial measurement**  
\[
PV = \frac{\$80{,}000}{(1.10)^3} = \frac{\$80{,}000}{1.331} = \$60{,}105.18
\]  
Discount on Note Payable = $80,000 − $60,105.18 = **$19,894.82**  
Equipment cost = cash + PV of note = $20,000 + $60,105.18 = **$80,105.18**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 80,105.18 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | 19,894.82 | |
| Cash | | 20,000.00 |
| Note Payable | | 80,000.00 |
| *To record specialized equipment: cash down payment + PV of noninterest-bearing note at 10% market rate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 80,105.18 + 19,894.82 = **100,000.00**; Cr 20,000.00 + 80,000.00 = **100,000.00**. Balanced.  
Net note liability at acquisition = 80,000 − 19,894.82 = **$60,105.18**.

**b. Effective-interest schedule (market rate 10%)**

| Year | Beg. carrying amount (net NP) | Interest expense (10%) | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 60,105.18 | 6,010.52 | 66,115.70 |
| 2 | 66,115.70 | 6,611.57 | 72,727.27 |
| 3 | 72,727.27 | 7,272.73 | 80,000.00 |

Computations:  
- Year 1: $60,105.18 × 0.10 = **$6,010.52**  
- Year 2: $66,115.70 × 0.10 = **$6,611.57**  
- Year 3: $72,727.27 × 0.10 = **$7,272.73** (plugs carrying amount to face $80,000)

**c. Period-end adjusting entries (Years 1–2)**

December 31, Year 1:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 6,010.52 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | | 6,010.52 |
| *Year 1 effective interest on noninterest-bearing note* | | |

December 31, Year 2:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 6,611.57 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | | 6,611.57 |

**Check each:** Dr = Cr.

**d. December 31, Year 3 — interest and maturity settlement**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 7,272.73 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | | 7,272.73 |
| *Year 3 effective interest; discount now fully amortized* | | |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 80,000.00 | |
| Cash | | 80,000.00 |
| *Pay face of note at maturity* | | |

After Year 3 interest, Discount balance = 19,894.82 − 6,010.52 − 6,611.57 − 7,272.73 = **$0**. Net liability before payment = $80,000.

**e. Proof**  
Total interest expense = 6,010.52 + 6,611.57 + 7,272.73 = **$19,894.82** = original discount.  
Total cash outlay for equipment = 20,000 + 80,000 = **$100,000**, of which $80,105.18 is asset cost and $19,894.82 is interest over the financing period.

**Key insight:** When PPE is bought with a noninterest-bearing (or below-market) note and asset FV is not clearly evident, capitalize the **present value of future payments** at the market rate—not the face of the note. The excess of face over PV is **discount (interest)**, not part of PPE cost.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Noninterest-bearing note (FV unknown): acquisition through maturity
**LO:** LO 11-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — PPE via noninterest note; PV measurement, effective-interest schedule, settlement  
**Scenario:**  
Northline Presses LLC acquires a high-speed packaging press on **January 1, Year 1**. Fair value of the press is **not determinable**. Terms:

- Cash down payment: **$15,000**  
- Noninterest-bearing note face **$54,000**, due in a **single payment on December 31, Year 2** (two years)  
- Market rate for similar debt: **12%**  
- Year-end December 31; effective interest method; **ignore depreciation**

**Required:**  
a. Compute PV of the note, equipment cost, and the January 1, Year 1 journal entry.  
b. Prepare the two-year amortization schedule.  
c. Prepare December 31, Year 1 interest entry and December 31, Year 2 interest + payment entries.  
d. State the carrying amount of the net note liability at December 31, Year 1 (after adjusting entry).

**Answer key:**  

**a.**  
\[
PV = \frac{\$54{,}000}{(1.12)^2} = \frac{\$54{,}000}{1.2544} = \$43{,}048.47
\]  
Discount = $54,000 − $43,048.47 = **$10,951.53**  
Equipment = $15,000 + $43,048.47 = **$58,048.47**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 58,048.47 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | 10,951.53 | |
| Cash | | 15,000.00 |
| Note Payable | | 54,000.00 |

**Check:** Dr 58,048.47 + 10,951.53 = **69,000.00**; Cr 15,000 + 54,000 = **69,000.00**. Balanced.

**b. Schedule**

| Year | Beg. carrying amount | Interest (12%) | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 43,048.47 | 5,165.82 | 48,214.29 |
| 2 | 48,214.29 | 5,785.71 | 54,000.00 |

Year 1: $43,048.47 × 0.12 = **$5,165.82**  
Year 2: $48,214.29 × 0.12 = **$5,785.71** (plugs to face)

**c. Journal entries**

Dec 31, Year 1:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 5,165.82 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | | 5,165.82 |

Dec 31, Year 2:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 5,785.71 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | | 5,785.71 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 54,000.00 | |
| Cash | | 54,000.00 |

**d.** Net note liability at Dec 31, Year 1 = $43,048.47 + $5,165.82 = **$48,214.29**  
(or $54,000 − remaining discount $5,785.71 = $48,214.29).

**Key insight:** Changing face, rate, and term changes the PV and each year’s interest, but the measurement rule is unchanged: **PPE = cash + PV of note**; amortize discount with effective interest until carrying amount equals face at maturity.

### Q3 — CORE — Equity issuance; asset FV known (imputed rate); classification/presentation
**LO:** LO 11-3  
**Concept:** PPE acquired with common stock (more clearly evident FV); noninterest note when asset FV known; balance-sheet classification of discount and equity  
**Scenario:**  

**Part A — Equity issuance (securities actively traded)**  
Riverbend Materials Inc. purchases used conveyor equipment on **March 1**. The used equipment has **no verifiable market price**. In full payment, Riverbend issues **4,000 shares** of its **$2 par** common stock. The stock is **publicly traded** and closes at **$22 per share** on the exchange date. Riverbend has millions of shares outstanding (the issuance is not large enough to affect market price).

**Part B — Noninterest note; fair value of asset determinable**  
On **January 1, Year 1**, Summit Ore Logistics acquires a standard forklift fleet with a **clearly evident cash-selling price (fair value) of $42,000**. Summit issues a **noninterest-bearing note** with face **$50,000** due in **two years**. A market rate for similar notes is **not readily available**. Year-end is December 31. Ignore depreciation.

**Part C — Mixed consideration (stock + cash)**  
On **June 15**, Harborline Packaging issues **3,500 shares** of **$1 par** common stock (actively traded at **$48.50** per share) **plus $30,000 cash** for a packaging line. Fair value of the packaging line is not more reliably measurable than the stock. Record the acquisition.

**Required:**  
a. Part A: Prepare the March 1 journal entry to record the conveyor acquisition.  
b. Part B: Compute the imputed effective interest rate, the initial equipment and discount amounts, the January 1, Year 1 entry, Year 1 interest, and Year 2 interest + payment.  
c. Part C: Prepare the June 15 acquisition entry.  
d. Classification / presentation: For Part A and Part B at acquisition, identify how each of the following appears on the balance sheet: Equipment; Note Payable; Discount on Note Payable; Common Stock; Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par. Also state whether Discount on Note Payable is presented gross or net of the related note.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Part A — equity (FV of stock more clearly evident)**  
Equipment cost = 4,000 × $22 = **$88,000**  
Common Stock = 4,000 × $2 = **$8,000**  
APIC = $88,000 − $8,000 = **$80,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 88,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 8,000 |
| Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 80,000 |
| *Record equipment at fair value of shares issued ($22 × 4,000); credit CS at par, remainder to APIC* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 88,000.

**b. Part B — asset FV known; impute rate**  
Equipment is recorded at its fair value **$42,000**.  
Discount = $50,000 − $42,000 = **$8,000**.  
Imputed effective rate \(r\):  
\[
\$42{,}000 \times (1+r)^2 = \$50{,}000 \quad \Rightarrow \quad r = \sqrt{\frac{50{,}000}{42{,}000}} - 1 \approx 9.108945\%
\]

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 42,000.00 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | 8,000.00 | |
| Note Payable | | 50,000.00 |

**Check:** Dr 42,000 + 8,000 = Cr 50,000.

Effective-interest schedule:

| Year | Beg. carrying amount | Interest ≈ 9.108945% | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 42,000.00 | 3,825.76 | 45,825.76 |
| 2 | 45,825.76 | 4,174.24 | 50,000.00 |

Year 1: $42,000 × 0.09108945 ≈ **$3,825.76**  
Year 2: $50,000 − $45,825.76 = **$4,174.24** (equals $45,825.76 × r; total interest = $8,000)

Dec 31, Year 1:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 3,825.76 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | | 3,825.76 |

Dec 31, Year 2:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 4,174.24 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | | 4,174.24 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Note Payable | 50,000.00 | |
| Cash | | 50,000.00 |

**c. Part C — stock + cash**  
FV of stock = 3,500 × $48.50 = **$169,750**  
Equipment = $169,750 + $30,000 = **$199,750**  
Common Stock = 3,500 × $1 = **$3,500**  
APIC = $169,750 − $3,500 = **$166,250**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 199,750 | |
| Cash | | 30,000 |
| Common Stock | | 3,500 |
| Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 166,250 |

**Check:** Dr 199,750 = Cr 30,000 + 3,500 + 166,250 = 199,750.

**d. Classification / presentation**

| Item | Classification / presentation |
|---|---|
| Equipment | Noncurrent asset (PPE) at **historical cost** ($88,000 Part A; $42,000 Part B; $199,750 Part C) |
| Note Payable | Liability at **face**; current if due within one year, otherwise noncurrent (Part B: noncurrent at acquisition; reclassify to current in Year 2) |
| Discount on Note Payable | **Contra liability** deducted from Note Payable so **net liability = carrying amount** (e.g., Part B at acquisition: NP $50,000 − Discount $8,000 = net $42,000). Not an asset. |
| Common Stock | Equity — common stock (legal capital) at **par** of shares issued |
| Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par | Equity — additional paid-in capital for excess of stock FV over par |

**Key insight:** Cost principle for noncash PPE acquisitions uses the **more clearly evident** of (1) FV of consideration given or (2) FV of asset received. For debt, that is often PV of payments or asset FV; for equity, often market price of shares. The difference between note face and initial net liability is **interest**, not PPE cost.

### Q4 — MC — Measurement basis for debt vs equity PPE acquisitions
**LO:** LO 11-3  
**Concept:** Classification of measurement basis when acquiring PPE with notes or stock  

**Question 1:**  
Oakridge Dairy purchases equipment by issuing a **two-year noninterest-bearing note**. The equipment’s fair value is **not** determinable. The note’s face is $30,000; the market rate for similar debt is 9%. At what amount should Oakridge initially record the **equipment**?

- A) $30,000 (face of the note)  
- B) Present value of $30,000 discounted for two years at 9%  
- C) $30,000 plus two years of interest at 9% on the face  
- D) Zero until the note is paid, then $30,000  

**Answer:** **B**. When asset FV is not clearly evident, measure PPE at the **present value of the future cash payments** using the prevailing market rate. Face is recorded as Note Payable with a **discount** for the difference; the discount is interest cost over time, not part of equipment cost. A records face (overstates PPE and omits interest). C compounds face incorrectly and does not use PV. D violates the cost principle at acquisition.

**Question 2:**  
Pinecroft Logistics issues **1,000 shares** of its **$5 par** common stock for a delivery van. The stock is actively traded at **$40** per share. Management’s internal estimate of the van’s value is **$37,000**. At what amounts should Pinecroft debit **Equipment** and credit **Common Stock**?

- A) Equipment $37,000; Common Stock $5,000  
- B) Equipment $40,000; Common Stock $40,000  
- C) Equipment $40,000; Common Stock $5,000  
- D) Equipment $37,000; Common Stock $37,000  

**Answer:** **C**. Fair value of **publicly traded stock** ($40 × 1,000 = **$40,000**) is more clearly evident than management’s estimate of the van. Equipment is debited for **$40,000**. Common Stock is credited only for **par** ($5 × 1,000 = **$5,000**); the $35,000 excess is **Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par**. A uses the less reliable asset estimate for cost. B credits CS for full proceeds instead of par. D uses the estimate and ignores par structure.
