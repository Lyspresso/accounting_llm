# Agent 64 — CORE DEMO — LO 10-8

**Chapter:** 10  
**LO title:** Estimate ending inventory using the average cost and conventional retail methods  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Average cost retail method: include **both** net markups and net markdowns in goods available for sale at retail when computing the cost-to-retail ratio (estimates inventory **at average cost**)
- Conventional retail method: **exclude** net markdowns from the cost-ratio denominator (still deduct them to arrive at ending inventory at retail) → lower cost ratio ≈ **lower of average cost or market**
- Three-step process: (1) ending inventory at retail, (2) cost ratio, (3) EI at retail × cost ratio = EI at cost
- Net markups = additional markups − markup cancellations; net markdowns = markdowns − markdown cancellations
- Complicating factors: freight-in (cost only), purchase returns (cost and retail), abnormal casualty (remove cost and retail before ratio), sales returns, employee discounts (after ratio, retail only), normal spoilage (after ratio, retail only)
- Initial recognition JEs for purchases, freight-in, and purchase returns that feed the retail schedule
- Period-end adjusting/closing JEs under the periodic system that set ending inventory and cost of goods sold to the retail-method estimates
- Balance sheet presentation of estimated ending inventory; disclosure of inventory estimation method
- Number-variant twin of the average cost retail schedule with fully recomputed amounts

### Q1 — CORE — Rivermist Outfitters: average cost retail method (initial recognition emphasis)
**LO:** LO 10-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of purchases/freight/returns; average cost retail inventory schedule; period-end COGS and inventory adjusting entries; comparison to conventional retail  
**Scenario:**  
**Rivermist Outfitters Co.** is a specialty outdoor apparel retailer that uses the **retail inventory method** for interim and year-end inventory estimation. The company maintains a **periodic** inventory system and tracks inventory at both cost and retail. Fiscal year ends **December 31, Year 5**. Selected data:

| Item | At cost | At retail |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory, Jan 1, Year 5 | $48,000 | $80,000 |
| Purchases during Year 5 | 320,000 | 400,000 |
| Freight-in | 12,000 | — |
| Purchase returns | (20,000) | (30,000) |
| Additional markups | — | 35,000 |
| Markup cancellations | — | 5,000 |
| Markdowns | — | 40,000 |
| Markdown cancellations | — | 10,000 |
| Sales revenue (gross) | — | 410,000 |
| Sales returns (goods restored to inventory for resale) | — | 20,000 |

Rivermist uses the **average cost retail method** for financial reporting of ending inventory. Management also wants a side computation under the **conventional retail method** for comparison.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare **initial recognition** journal entries during Year 5 for: (1) purchases on account, (2) freight-in paid in cash, and (3) purchase returns for credit.  
(b) Prepare the journal entry to record **net sales** for the year (gross sales and sales returns). Assume all sales and returns are for cash for simplicity.  
(c) Prepare the complete **average cost retail method** schedule: goods available for sale, estimated ending inventory at retail, cost-to-retail ratio, and estimated ending inventory at cost. Show computation of net markups and net markdowns.  
(d) Compute **cost of goods sold** under the average cost retail method.  
(e) Prepare the **period-end adjusting/closing** journal entry (periodic system) to establish ending inventory and cost of goods sold for Year 5 under the average cost retail estimate.  
(f) Recompute estimated ending inventory at cost under the **conventional retail method**. Explain in one sentence why it differs from (c).  
(g) Show the December 31, Year 5 **balance sheet presentation** of inventory under the average cost retail method and identify the **method disclosure** Rivermist should make.

**(a) Initial recognition — purchases, freight, returns**

(Dr = Cr = 320,000)


**(b) Sales (settlement of merchandise through sale)**

```
   Cr Sales Revenue .................................... 410,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 410,000)

```

```
(Dr = Cr = 20,000)

**(c) Average cost retail schedule**

| | Cost | Retail |

Estimated ending inventory at cost:

**(d) Cost of goods sold — average cost retail**  
\[
\text{COGS} = \$360{,}000 - \$48{,}000 = \mathbf{\$312{,}000}

**(f) Conventional retail comparison**

|---|---:|---:|

| Net markups | — | 30,000 |

| Less: Net sales | | (390,000) |
| Less: Net markdowns | | (30,000) |

Cost ratio (conventional): \(360{,}000 / 480{,}000 = \mathbf{0.75}\).  
EI at cost: \(60{,}000 \times 0.75 = \mathbf{\$45{,}000}\).

**Why different:** Conventional **excludes net markdowns from the cost-ratio denominator**, producing a lower cost ratio (0.75 vs 0.80) and a lower inventory estimate that **approximates lower of average cost or market**; average cost retail **includes** net markdowns in the ratio and estimates inventory **at cost**.

**(g) Presentation and disclosure**  

| | Dec 31, Year 5 |
|---|---:|
| Inventory (at average cost, retail method) | **$48,000** |

**Key insight:** Purchases, freight-in, and returns are **initially recognized** at cost (and tracked at retail); the average cost retail schedule then converts ending inventory at retail to cost by a ratio that **includes net markdowns**. The period-end entry must put that estimated cost on the balance sheet and charge the residual of goods available to COGS.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Pinehaven Home Goods: conventional retail with complications; casualty settlement

**Scenario:**  
**Pinehaven Home Goods LLC** estimates year-end inventory using the **conventional retail method**. Year 7 information (amounts below do **not** yet reflect the six complicating items):

| (e) Discounts to employees | — | 10,000 |

The destroyed inventory (item c) had been included in purchases; the loss is abnormal and is not expected in normal operations.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the journal entry to record the **abnormal casualty loss** at cost (settlement/write-off of destroyed inventory).  
(b) Prepare the complete **conventional retail method** schedule including all complicating factors; compute the cost ratio and estimated ending inventory at cost.  
(c) Compute cost of goods sold for Year 7 under the conventional estimate (goods available at cost after casualty removal, less estimated ending inventory).  
(d) Prepare the period-end entry that sets ending inventory to the conventional retail estimate and records COGS (close purchase-related accounts; beginning inventory $90,000; purchases $550,000; freight-in $20,000; purchase returns $15,000; treat the casualty loss as already removed from inventory via part (a) so do not re-close the $45,000 cost in this entry).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Abnormal casualty — disposal/settlement of inventory**  
```
Dr Loss from Casualty (or Fire Loss) ........ 45,000
   Cr Purchases ........................................ 45,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 45,000)

*(Abnormal casualty is removed from goods available at **both** cost and retail **before** the cost ratio so it does not distort the ratio—as if the goods had never been available for sale. Crediting Purchases reduces net purchases cost from $550,000 to $505,000; with freight and returns, post-casualty cost in goods available remains $600,000 as in the schedule.)*
**(b) Conventional retail schedule with complications**

| | Cost | Retail |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | $90,000 | $160,000 |
| Purchases | 550,000 | 920,000 |
| Freight-in (a) | 20,000 | — |
| Purchase returns (b) | (15,000) | (25,000) |
| Net markups | — | 20,000 |
| Abnormal casualty loss (c) | (45,000) | (75,000) |
| **Goods available for sale (for cost ratio)** | **$600,000** | **$1,000,000** |
| Less: Net sales \((\$740{,}000 - \$20{,}000)\) (d) | | (720,000) |
| Less: Employee discounts (e) | | (10,000) |
| Less: Normal spoilage (f) | | (15,000) |
| Less: Net markdowns | | (55,000) |
| **Estimated ending inventory at retail** | | **$200,000** |

Cost-to-retail ratio (conventional — markdowns **excluded** from ratio base):  
\[
\frac{\$600{,}000}{\$1{,}000{,}000} = \mathbf{0.60}
\]

Estimated ending inventory at cost:  
\[
\$200{,}000 \times 0.60 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}
\]

Placement summary of complications:  
- Freight-in → **cost only**, before ratio  
- Purchase returns → **cost and retail**, before ratio  
- Abnormal casualty → **cost and retail**, before ratio  
- Sales returns → reduce sales (net sales), after ratio base  
- Employee discounts → **retail only**, after ratio  
- Normal spoilage → **retail only**, after ratio  
- Net markdowns (conventional) → **not** in ratio denominator; deducted to get EI at retail

Goods available at cost for merchandise sold/held (after casualty already removed): **$600,000**.  
\[
\text{COGS} = \$600{,}000 - \$120{,}000 = \mathbf{\$480{,}000}
\]  
*(Casualty loss $45,000 is reported separately as a loss, not as COGS.)*

**(d) Period-end inventory/COGS entry**  
After part (a), Purchases has a net credit-balance residual of \(550{,}000 - 45{,}000 = 505{,}000\) still to close; post-casualty goods available at cost = **$600,000** (BI $90,000 + net purchases after casualty $505,000 + freight $20,000 − returns $15,000). Assign to EI **$120,000** and COGS **$480,000**:

```
Dr Inventory (ending) ....................... 120,000
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ....................... 480,000
Dr Purchase Returns and Allowances .......... 15,000
   Cr Inventory (beginning) ........................... 90,000
   Cr Purchases ....................................... 505,000
   Cr Freight-in ...................................... 20,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 615,000)

Check: \(90{,}000 + 505{,}000 + 20{,}000 - 15{,}000 = 600{,}000 = 120{,}000 + 480{,}000\). Casualty loss remains **$45,000** (from part a), reported **separately from COGS**.**(e) Measurement, presentation, disclosure**  
- Conventional retail **approximates lower of average cost or market** (markdowns lower the cost ratio).  
- Balance sheet: **Inventory $120,000** (current asset).  
- Disclose use of the **conventional retail inventory method** (or “retail method approximating LCM”) and significant estimates (markdowns, spoilage, casualty treatment).

**Key insight:** Protect the **integrity of the cost ratio** by putting freight, returns, and abnormal casualty **above** the ratio line correctly; put employee discounts, normal spoilage, and (under conventional) markdowns **below** the ratio line when measuring ending inventory at retail. Average cost would have included the $55,000 net markdowns in the ratio denominator instead.

### Q4 — MC — Cost ratio treatment of net markdowns
**LO:** LO 10-8  
**Concept:** Distinguish average cost retail vs conventional retail treatment of net markdowns in the cost-to-retail ratio  
**Question:**  
Under the **average cost retail method**, how are **net markdowns** treated when computing the cost-to-retail ratio and ending inventory?

- A) Excluded from goods available for sale at retail in the cost ratio and also excluded when computing ending inventory at retail  
- B) Included in goods available for sale at retail when computing the cost ratio (and still reflected in ending inventory at retail)  
- C) Added to goods available for sale at **cost** only  
- D) Deducted only after the cost ratio is applied, identical to the conventional method’s ratio treatment  

**Answer:** **B.** Under average cost retail, both net markups and net markdowns enter goods available for sale at retail for the cost ratio, so the ratio estimates **average cost**. Conventional retail excludes net markdowns from the ratio denominator (while still deducting them to arrive at EI at retail), which lowers the ratio and approximates LCM. A is wrong because markdowns still affect EI at retail. C confuses cost-column items (e.g., freight-in). D describes conventional ratio treatment, not average cost.

### Q5 — MC — Measurement objective of conventional retail
**LO:** LO 10-8  
**Concept:** Conventional retail approximates lower of average cost or market  
**Question:**  
Compared with the average cost retail method, the **conventional retail method** typically reports a **lower** ending inventory amount primarily because it:

- A) Excludes net markups from goods available for sale at retail  
- B) Excludes net markdowns from the cost-ratio denominator, producing a lower cost-to-retail ratio that approximates lower of average cost or market  
- C) Values ending inventory at pure replacement cost without using a cost ratio  
- D) Capitalizes employee discounts into ending inventory at cost  

**Answer:** **B.** Excluding markdowns from the ratio base increases the retail denominator relative to average cost retail, lowers the cost ratio, and yields a lower EI estimate ≈ LCM. Net markups are included under both methods (A false). A cost ratio is still applied (C false). Employee discounts are deducted at retail after the ratio, not capitalized (D false).
