# Agent 228 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-4

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Account for short-term debt and classify debt on the balance sheet  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** interest-bearing short-term notes at face (stated = market); noninterest-bearing notes at PV with **Discount on Notes Payable** to face
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period interest accrual schedule (interest-bearing) and **straight-line discount amortization / carrying-amount roll-forward** (noninterest-bearing)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** accrue Interest Payable, or amortize discount to Interest Expense
- **Maturity / settlement JE:** remove Notes Payable (and Interest Payable or remaining discount); pay face + cash interest or face only on zero-coupon form
- **Balance sheet classification:** current maturities of LT debt; callable / covenant-violation debt; short-term debt expected to be refinanced (intent + ability); debt extinguished from noncurrent assets
- Note net of unamortized discount = **carrying amount** presented as a current liability when due within one year

### Q1 — CORE — Noninterest-bearing note: discount amortization schedule (emphasis), accrue, settle
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for SL discount amortization and carrying amount of a short-term noninterest-bearing note; initial recognition at PV; period-end adjusting JE; maturity settlement JE; net current presentation  
**Scenario:**  
On **September 1, Year 1**, **Solstice Trailers Inc.** (calendar-year company) signs a **one-year, noninterest-bearing** note and receives cash equal to the note’s present value. The company amortizes the discount using the **straight-line method**. Fiscal year-end is **December 31**. Year 1 financial statements are issued **March 10, Year 2**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Face (maturity) amount of note | **$106,000** |
| Cash proceeds (present value) | **$100,000** |
| Discount at issuance (face − PV) | **$6,000** |
| Stated rate | **0%** (noninterest-bearing) |
| Market rate implicit in PV | **6%** (given; PV supports face) |
| Term | **12 months** (matures **August 31, Year 2**) |
| Discount amortization | Straight-line over **12** months |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **September 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry.  
b. Prepare a complete **subsequent measurement schedule** for the note term showing, by period: months outstanding, discount amortized (interest expense), ending unamortized discount, and ending **carrying amount** (face − unamortized discount). Include the issuance row.  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting** entry.  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet** presentation of the note (net) and Year 1 interest expense.  
e. Record the **August 31, Year 2 maturity / settlement** journal entry.  
f. Prove that total interest expense over the term equals the original discount.

**Answer key:**

**a. September 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (noninterest-bearing)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 100,000 | |
| Discount on Notes Payable | 6,000 | |
| Notes Payable | | 106,000 |
| *Record face; discount = face − PV; cash at present value* | | |

**Check:** Dr 100,000 + 6,000 = **106,000**; Cr = **106,000**. **Balanced.**  
Initial carrying amount = $106,000 − $6,000 = **$100,000**.

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — SL discount amortization**

Monthly amortization = $6,000 ÷ 12 = **$500**.

| Period | Months | Discount amortized (Int. exp.) | Ending unamortized discount | Ending carrying amount (face − unamort. disc.) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| At issuance (Sep 1, Y1) | — | — | 6,000 | **100,000** |
| Sep 1 – Dec 31, Year 1 | 4 | **2,000** | 4,000 | **102,000** |
| Jan 1 – Aug 31, Year 2 | 8 | **4,000** | 0 | **106,000** |
| **Total term** | **12** | **6,000** | — | — |

**Roll-forward check:** Beginning CA + total interest expense over term = face at maturity: $100,000 + $6,000 = **$106,000**.  
Unamortized discount at Dec 31, Y1: $6,000 − $2,000 = **$4,000**; CA = $106,000 − $4,000 = **$102,000**.

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 2,000 | |
| Discount on Notes Payable ($500 × 4) | | 2,000 |
| *Amortize 4 months of discount (Sep–Dec)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **2,000**. **Balanced.**

**d. December 31, Year 1 — classification / presentation**

| Balance sheet (excerpt) — Dec 31, Year 1 | Amount |
|---|---:|
| **Current liabilities** | |
| Notes payable, net ($106,000 − $4,000 unamortized discount) | **$102,000** |
| **Income statement (excerpt) — Year 1** | |
| Interest expense | **$(2,000)** |

The entire note is **current** (due within one year of the balance sheet date). Discount is a **contra** to Notes Payable; report the **net** carrying amount.

**e. August 31, Year 2 — maturity / settlement JE**

Remaining discount amortization (8 months) = $500 × 8 = **$4,000** = Year 2 interest expense.  
Cash paid = face **$106,000** (no separate cash interest on a noninterest-bearing note).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 106,000 | |
| Interest Expense | 4,000 | |
| Cash | | 106,000 |
| Discount on Notes Payable | | 4,000 |
| *Settle face; amortize remaining discount to interest expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 106,000 + 4,000 = **110,000**; Cr 106,000 + 4,000 = **110,000**. **Balanced.**  
After this entry, Notes Payable and Discount balances are zero.

**f. Interest expense total over term**  
Year 1 $2,000 + Year 2 $4,000 = **$6,000** = original discount. Economic borrowing cost equals face − cash proceeds.

**Key insight:** For a **noninterest-bearing** short-term note, the core subsequent measurement deliverable is the **discount amortization schedule**: cash interest is zero, but **interest expense** still arises as the discount is amortized. Carrying amount **rolls up** from PV toward face. At maturity, remove face Notes Payable and any remaining discount, and pay face cash.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Noninterest-bearing note twin (schedule emphasis)
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: subsequent measurement (SL discount) schedule; initial recognition at PV; period-end amortization JE; maturity settlement; net current presentation  
**Scenario:**  
On **May 1, Year 1**, **Pinnacle Drift Outfitters** (calendar year) signs a **one-year, noninterest-bearing** note, receives cash equal to present value, and amortizes the discount **straight-line**. Year-end is **December 31**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Face (maturity) amount | **$127,200** |
| Cash proceeds (PV) | **$120,000** |
| Discount at issuance | **$7,200** |
| Term | **12 months** (matures **April 30, Year 2**) |
| Amortization | Straight-line over **12** months |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Required:**  
a. Record **May 1, Year 1 initial recognition**.  
b. Prepare the **subsequent measurement schedule** (issuance row; May–Dec Year 1; Jan–Apr Year 2; totals) with ending unamortized discount and carrying amount.  
c. Record **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entry.  
d. State Dec 31, Year 1 **notes payable, net** and Year 1 interest expense.  
e. Record **April 30, Year 2 settlement**.  
f. Reconcile total interest expense to the original discount.

**Answer key:**

**a. May 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 120,000 | |
| Discount on Notes Payable | 7,200 | |
| Notes Payable | | 127,200 |

**Check:** Dr 120,000 + 7,200 = **127,200**; Cr = **127,200**. **Balanced.**  
Initial CA = **$120,000**.

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis)**

Monthly amortization = $7,200 ÷ 12 = **$600**.

| Period | Months | Discount amortized (Int. exp.) | Ending unamortized discount | Ending carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| At issuance (May 1, Y1) | — | — | 7,200 | **120,000** |
| May 1 – Dec 31, Year 1 | 8 | **4,800** | 2,400 | **124,800** |
| Jan 1 – Apr 30, Year 2 | 4 | **2,400** | 0 | **127,200** |
| **Total term** | **12** | **7,200** | — | — |

**Roll-forward:** $120,000 + $7,200 = **$127,200** face at maturity.  
Dec 31 Y1: unamortized discount $7,200 − $4,800 = **$2,400**; CA = $127,200 − $2,400 = **$124,800**.

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 4,800 | |
| Discount on Notes Payable ($600 × 8) | | 4,800 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **4,800**. **Balanced.**

**d. Presentation — December 31, Year 1**  
- Notes payable, net (current): **$124,800**  
- Year 1 interest expense: **$4,800**

**e. April 30, Year 2 — maturity / settlement**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 127,200 | |
| Interest Expense | 2,400 | |
| Cash | | 127,200 |
| Discount on Notes Payable | | 2,400 |

**Check:** Dr 127,200 + 2,400 = **129,600**; Cr 127,200 + 2,400 = **129,600**. **Balanced.**

**f. Total interest**  
$4,800 + $2,400 = **$7,200** = original discount.

**Key insight:** Same measurement path as Q1 with different face, PV, term split, and monthly amort: **issue at PV with discount → roll carrying amount on the amortization schedule → YE amortize → settle face and remaining discount**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Interest-bearing note cycle + balance sheet classification of debt
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of interest-bearing short-term note; subsequent interest accrual schedule; period-end Interest Payable JE; maturity settlement; multi-scenario balance sheet classification (current maturities, callable debt, refinancing, noncurrent-asset settlement)  
**Scenario — Part A (interest-bearing note):**  
On **October 1, Year 1**, **Lumen Harbor Distillers** borrows cash on a **15-month, interest-bearing** note. Cash interest is due **in full at maturity** with principal. Stated rate equals market. Fiscal year-end is **December 31**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Cash proceeds / face | **$240,000** |
| Stated (and market) annual rate | **9%** |
| Issuance | October 1, Year 1 |
| Maturity | **December 31, Year 2** (15 months) |
| Interest pattern | All cash interest paid at maturity with principal |

**Scenario — Part B (classification only; ignore interest payable):**  
**Quarry Bend Fabrication** prepares its **December 31, Year 10** balance sheet. Year 10 financial statements will be issued on **March 5, Year 11**. For each **independent** scenario, determine amounts classified as **current** vs **noncurrent**.

| # | Debt scenario at December 31, Year 10 |
|---|---|
| 1 | $720,000 installment note; principal installments of **$144,000** due each December 31 for the next five years (next installment due Dec 31, Year 11). |
| 2 | $250,000 note due in **six years**. Debt covenant requires current ratio ≥ **1.40**. At Dec 31 Year 10, current ratio is **1.15** (CA $460,000 / CL $400,000, with the note still treated as long-term in that ratio). Creditor has **not** waived call rights; cure within the contractual grace period is **not** probable before statements are issued. |
| 3 | Same facts as #2, except Quarry Bend obtains a written **waiver** (before statements are issued) of the bank’s right to demand repayment for more than one year from Dec 31, Year 10. |
| 4 | $75,000 short-term note due **February 20, Year 11**. Before statements are issued, Quarry Bend **issues long-term debt** for $75,000 and uses those proceeds to refinance (intent **and** ability demonstrated by actual post-balance-sheet long-term issuance). |
| 5 | $75,000 short-term note due February 20, Year 11. On February 8, Year 11 the company **pays the note with operating cash**, then on February 25 issues a long-term note for $75,000 (before Year 10 statements are issued). |
| 6 | $180,000 principal due December 31, Year 11 that will be paid entirely from a **noncurrent bond sinking fund** (not from current assets). |

**Required:**  
**Part A**  
a. Record **October 1, Year 1 initial recognition**.  
b. Prepare the **subsequent measurement (interest) schedule** for Year 1 months, Year 2 months, and total term interest.  
c. Record **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entry.  
d. Show Dec 31, Year 1 **current liability** amounts related to the note.  
e. Record **December 31, Year 2 maturity / settlement** (principal + full cash interest).  

**Part B**  
f. Complete a table of **current** vs **noncurrent** amounts for scenarios 1–6 with brief support.

**Answer key:**

**Part A**

**a. October 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (interest-bearing; stated = market)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 240,000 | |
| Notes Payable | | 240,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **240,000**. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — interest over the term**

Total cash interest at maturity: $240,000 × 0.09 × 15/12 = **$27,000**.

| Period | Months | Computation | Interest expense |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Oct 1 – Dec 31, Year 1 | 3 | $240,000 × 0.09 × 3/12 | **$5,400** |
| Jan 1 – Dec 31, Year 2 | 12 | $240,000 × 0.09 × 12/12 | **$21,600** |
| **Total (term)** | **15** | $240,000 × 0.09 × 15/12 | **$27,000** |

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 5,400 | |
| Interest Payable | | 5,400 |
| *Accrue 3 months’ interest* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **5,400**. **Balanced.**

**d. December 31, Year 1 — current liabilities presentation**

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Notes payable | **$240,000** |
| Interest payable | **$5,400** |

Both are **current** (settlement within one year of the balance sheet date requires current resources). Year 1 interest expense = **$5,400**.

**e. December 31, Year 2 — maturity / settlement**

Cash paid = $240,000 + $27,000 = **$267,000**.  
Remove Interest Payable from Year 1 (**$5,400**); recognize Year 2 interest (**$21,600**).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 240,000 | |
| Interest Payable | 5,400 | |
| Interest Expense | 21,600 | |
| Cash | | 267,000 |

**Check:** Dr 240,000 + 5,400 + 21,600 = **267,000**; Cr = **267,000**. **Balanced.**  
Interest Payable roll-forward: $5,400 + $21,600 expense = $27,000 cash interest paid.

**Part B — f. Classification table**

| # | Current | Noncurrent | Support |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | **$144,000** | **$576,000** | Current maturity of LT debt = next installment; remainder $720,000 − $144,000 is noncurrent. |
| 2 | **$250,000** | **$0** | Covenant violation makes debt **callable**; no waiver and cure not probable → classify **entire** obligation as **current**. |
| 3 | **$0** | **$250,000** | Creditor **waived** demand rights for more than one year → avoid current classification; remains **noncurrent**. |
| 4 | **$0** | **$75,000** | Intent **and** ability to refinance on a long-term basis demonstrated by **actual post-BS long-term issuance** before statements issued → **noncurrent**. |
| 5 | **$75,000** | **$0** | Note was **extinguished with operating cash** before long-term financing was obtained → remains **current** (refinancing criteria not met for the cash-settled obligation). |
| 6 | **$0** | **$180,000** | Settlement from a **noncurrent** sinking fund (not current assets) → report as **noncurrent**. |

**Updated current ratio for scenario 2 (illustrative):** CA $460,000 / CL ($400,000 + $250,000) = $460,000 / $650,000 ≈ **0.71** (deteriorates when callable debt is reclassified current).

**Key insight:** Interest-bearing short-term notes use an **interest accrual schedule** and **Interest Payable**, not a discount account. Classification is **not** automatic: current maturities, **callable** covenant violations, and **refinancing** (intent + ability, without first using operating cash) can move debt between current and noncurrent.

### Q4 — MC — Short-term debt expected to be refinanced
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Balance sheet classification of short-term debt expected to be refinanced (intent and ability)  
**Question:**  
On December 31, Year 1, **Ironwood Components** has a **$400,000** note due March 15, Year 2. Management **intends** to refinance the note on a long-term basis. Which fact pattern allows Ironwood to classify the **entire** $400,000 as a **noncurrent** liability on the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet? (Year 1 statements are issued April 20, Year 2.)

- A) Management’s board minutes document intent to refinance; no agreement or post-balance-sheet financing has been obtained by the statement issuance date.  
- B) On February 1, Year 2, Ironwood **pays** the note with cash from operations, then on March 1, Year 2 issues $400,000 of 10-year bonds.  
- C) On February 10, Year 2 (before statements are issued), Ironwood issues **$400,000 of long-term bonds** and uses the proceeds to refinance the short-term note.  
- D) Ironwood enters a cancelable line of credit with a lender that may refuse funding at its discretion, with no other refinancing completed before statements are issued.

**Answer:** **C.**  
To exclude short-term debt from current liabilities, the entity must have **intent** to refinance on a long-term basis **and** demonstrate **ability** before financial statements are issued—by actually issuing long-term debt or equity, or by a **qualifying noncancelable** financing agreement with a viable lender. Option C meets the **post-balance-sheet issuance** demonstration. A is intent only. B extinguishes the note with **current resources** first, so the December 31 obligation remains current. D’s cancelable/discretionary facility does not demonstrate ability.

### Q5 — MC — Callable long-term debt / covenant violation
**LO:** LO 15-4  
**Concept:** Classification of long-term debt that is callable because of a covenant violation  
**Question:**  
At December 31, Year 5, **Silvercreek Logistics** has a **$900,000** note due in **eight years**. A debt covenant requires a minimum current ratio of **1.25**. At year-end the current ratio is **1.05**, making the note **callable** by the lender. Silvercreek has a **45-day grace period** to cure. Which outcome correctly classifies the $900,000 on the December 31, Year 5 balance sheet?

- A) Noncurrent, because the contractual maturity is eight years away regardless of covenant status.  
- B) Current, if the lender has **not** waived the right to demand repayment for more than one year **and** it is **not** probable that Silvercreek will cure the violation within the grace period before statements are issued.  
- C) Noncurrent, solely because interest is paid annually rather than monthly.  
- D) Split 50/50 between current and noncurrent when a grace period exists.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Long-term obligations that are (or will become) **callable** because of a covenant violation at the balance sheet date are classified as **current** unless (1) the creditor has **waived** (or lost) the right to demand repayment for more than one year from the balance sheet date, or (2) for debt with a **grace period**, it is **probable** the violation will be cured within that period so the debt does not become callable. Contractual long maturity alone (A) does not control when the creditor can demand payment. Interest payment pattern (C) is irrelevant. There is no automatic 50/50 split (D).
