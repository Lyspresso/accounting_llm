# Agent 171 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-2

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Account for sales and collections on account including the impact of cash discounts  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE (gross method):** Dr Accounts Receivable / Cr Sales Revenue at full invoice (gross); simultaneous COGS when inventory is transferred
- **Initial recognition JE (net method):** Dr Accounts Receivable / Cr Sales Revenue at gross less available cash discount
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** customer-level AR subsidiary / rollforward tracking sales, discount-period collections, and late settlements until AR = 0
- **Settlement within discount period (gross):** Dr Cash + Dr Sales Discount (contra-revenue) / Cr AR
- **Settlement after discount period (gross):** Dr Cash / Cr AR for full remaining gross
- **Settlement within discount period (net):** Dr Cash / Cr AR at the net carrying amount (no separate discount entry)
- **Settlement after discount period (net):** Dr Cash (gross remaining) / Cr Sales Discount Forfeited + Cr AR (net remaining)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** gross method reduces AR and revenue for discounts expected on open receivables; net method increases AR and revenue for discounts expected to be forfeited
- **Presentation:** gross method reports Sales − Sales discounts = Net sales; net method reports Sales + Sales discounts forfeited (other revenue) with identical total cash-based revenue when outcomes are known
- **Number-variant twin:** same skill path with all invoice amounts, discount rates, and split percentages changed

### Q1 — CORE — Gross method multi-customer sale, collections, and subsequent AR measurement schedule
**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition under gross method; multi-date settlement with and without cash discount; customer AR subsequent measurement schedule; net sales presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Redrock Industrial LLC** transfers control of merchandise on account and accounts for cash discounts using the **gross method**. Credit terms on all sales are **2/10, n/30**. Fiscal month is **April**. Beginning AR related to these two customers is **$0**.

| Date | Customer | Event |
|---|---|---|
| April 2 | Cascade Plastics | Credit sale, gross invoice **$75,000**; cost **$45,000** |
| April 4 | Trailhead Metals | Credit sale, gross invoice **$50,000**; cost **$30,000** |
| April 9 | Cascade Plastics | Pays **60%** of its gross invoice (within the discount period) |
| April 14 | Trailhead Metals | Pays **100%** of its gross invoice (within the discount period) |
| April 25 | Cascade Plastics | Pays the **remaining 40%** (after the discount period) |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare all **initial recognition** journal entries on April 2 and April 4 (sale and COGS for each).  
b. Prepare all **settlement** journal entries on April 9, April 14, and April 25.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** — customer AR subsidiary rollforward by date for April (columns: Cascade, Trailhead, Total). Show beginning balance, each sale, each collection allocated to AR, and ending balance.  
d. Prepare the **income statement excerpt** for net sales revenue related to these two customers for April (gross method).  
e. **Proof:** show that total cash collected + sales discounts taken = original combined gross AR, and that ending AR = $0.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition (gross method)**

*April 2 — Cascade Plastics*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 75,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 75,000 |
| *Credit sale at gross invoice price* | | |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 45,000 | |
| Inventory | | 45,000 |
| *Remove inventory; recognize COGS* | | |

**Check:** Sale Dr = Cr = 75,000; COGS Dr = Cr = 45,000. **Balanced.**

*April 4 — Trailhead Metals*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 50,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 50,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 30,000 | |
| Inventory | | 30,000 |

**Check:** 50,000 = 50,000; 30,000 = 30,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Settlement entries**

*April 9 — Cascade pays 60% within discount period*  
Gross AR relieved = 0.60 × $75,000 = **$45,000**  
Sales discount = $45,000 × 0.02 = **$900**  
Cash = $45,000 − $900 = **$44,100**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 44,100 | |
| Sales Discount | 900 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 45,000 |
| *Partial collection within 2/10; Sales Discount is contra-revenue* | | |

**Check:** 44,100 + 900 = 45,000. **Balanced.**

*April 14 — Trailhead pays 100% within discount period*  
Sales discount = $50,000 × 0.02 = **$1,000**  
Cash = $50,000 − $1,000 = **$49,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 49,000 | |
| Sales Discount | 1,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 50,000 |
| *Full collection within 2/10* | | |

**Check:** 49,000 + 1,000 = 50,000. **Balanced.**

*April 25 — Cascade remaining 40% after discount period*  
Remaining AR = 0.40 × $75,000 = **$30,000** (no discount)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 30,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 30,000 |
| *Final collection after discount window; full remaining gross* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 30,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — AR subsidiary rollforward (April)**

Amounts in the schedule are **AR carrying amounts (gross method)**. Collections reduce AR by the **gross** amount relieved (not by cash received).

| Date / Item | Cascade Plastics | Trailhead Metals | Total AR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| April 1 beginning | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| April 2 sale | 75,000 | — | 75,000 |
| April 4 sale | — | 50,000 | 125,000 |
| April 9 collection (AR relieved) | (45,000) | — | 80,000 |
| April 14 collection (AR relieved) | — | (50,000) | 30,000 |
| April 25 collection (AR relieved) | (30,000) | — | 0 |
| **April 30 ending** | **$0** | **$0** | **$0** |

**Interim balances (measurement checkpoints):**  
- After April 9: Cascade $30,000; Trailhead $50,000; Total **$80,000**  
- After April 14: Cascade $30,000; Trailhead $0; Total **$30,000**  
- After April 25: both **$0**

**d. Income statement presentation — gross method (these customers only)**

| Income Statement — Excerpt (April) | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales revenue ($75,000 + $50,000) | $125,000 |
| Less: Sales discounts ($900 + $1,000) | (1,900) |
| **Net sales revenue** | **$123,100** |

**e. Proof**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash collected ($44,100 + $49,000 + $30,000) | $123,100 |
| + Sales discounts taken | 1,900 |
| **= Original combined gross AR** | **$125,000** |
| Ending AR | $0 |

**Key insight:** Under the gross method, AR is measured at invoice gross until settlement. The **subsequent measurement schedule** tracks AR by customer as portions are settled. Cash discounts reduce **revenue** (Sales Discount) only on amounts paid **inside** the discount window; late portions leave revenue at full gross. Cash + discounts taken always reconcil to original AR when the account is fully collected.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Gross method twin with 1/15 terms and different splits
**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: gross-method initial recognition, split early/late settlement, subsequent AR schedule, net sales  
**Scenario:**  
**Bluehaven Wholesale Co.** uses the **gross method**. Credit terms are **1/15, n/45**. Fiscal month is **May**. Beginning AR for these customers is **$0**.

| Date | Customer | Event |
|---|---|---|
| May 3 | Oakridge Distributors | Credit sale **$120,000**; cost **$72,000** |
| May 7 | Pinecrest Retail Group | Credit sale **$80,000**; cost **$48,000** |
| May 10 | Oakridge Distributors | Pays **70%** of its gross invoice (within the 15-day discount period) |
| May 25 | Pinecrest Retail Group | Pays **100%** of its gross invoice (after the discount period; discount window ended May 22) |
| May 28 | Oakridge Distributors | Pays the **remaining 30%** (after the discount period) |

**Required:**  
a. May 3 and May 7 sale and COGS entries.  
b. All three collection entries (May 10, May 25, May 28).  
c. Subsequent measurement AR rollforward by customer for May (same column structure as Q1).  
d. Net sales revenue for these customers for May.  
e. Prove cash + sales discounts = original combined gross AR.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*May 3 — Oakridge*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 120,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 120,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 72,000 | |
| Inventory | | 72,000 |

**Check:** 120,000 = 120,000; 72,000 = 72,000. **Balanced.**

*May 7 — Pinecrest*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 80,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 80,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 48,000 | |
| Inventory | | 48,000 |

**Check:** 80,000 = 80,000; 48,000 = 48,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Collections**

*May 10 — Oakridge 70% within 1/15*  
Gross AR relieved = 0.70 × $120,000 = **$84,000**  
Sales discount = $84,000 × 0.01 = **$840**  
Cash = $84,000 − $840 = **$83,160**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 83,160 | |
| Sales Discount | 840 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 84,000 |

**Check:** 83,160 + 840 = 84,000. **Balanced.**

*May 25 — Pinecrest full after discount period*  
No discount; cash = AR relieved = **$80,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 80,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 80,000 |

**Check:** 80,000 = 80,000. **Balanced.**

*May 28 — Oakridge remaining 30% after discount period*  
Remaining AR = 0.30 × $120,000 = **$36,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 36,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 36,000 |

**Check:** 36,000 = 36,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — AR rollforward (May)**

| Date / Item | Oakridge | Pinecrest | Total AR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| May 1 beginning | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| May 3 sale | 120,000 | — | 120,000 |
| May 7 sale | — | 80,000 | 200,000 |
| May 10 collection (AR relieved) | (84,000) | — | 116,000 |
| May 25 collection (AR relieved) | — | (80,000) | 36,000 |
| May 28 collection (AR relieved) | (36,000) | — | 0 |
| **May 31 ending** | **$0** | **$0** | **$0** |

**d. Net sales**

Sales revenue $120,000 + $80,000 = **$200,000**  
Sales discounts **$840**  
**Net sales revenue = $199,160**

**e. Proof**

Cash = $83,160 + $80,000 + $36,000 = **$199,160**  
Cash + Sales Discount = $199,160 + $840 = **$200,000** = original combined AR. Ending AR = **$0**.

**Key insight:** Changing the cash-discount rate (here **1%**) and the early-pay fraction scales Sales Discount and cash, but the **measurement path** is unchanged: AR remains at gross until collection; only the early-pay slice creates a Sales Discount; the schedule rolls AR to zero when the last settlement posts.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Net method cycle, subsequent AR schedule, and period-end adjusting entries
**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Net-method initial recognition and settlement schedule; Sales Discount Forfeited on late payment; year-end adjusting JEs under gross vs net for open receivables; revenue presentation  
**Scenario:**  

**Part A — Net method sales, collections, and AR schedule**  
**Silverline Trading Corp.** transfers control of goods to **Westbay LLC** on **June 3**. Gross invoice price is **$35,000**; cost is **$21,000**. Terms **2/10, n/30**. Silverline uses the **net method**.

- **June 10:** Westbay pays **40%** of the **net** receivable (within the discount period).  
- **June 29:** Westbay pays the **remaining** balance after the discount period (remits the full remaining **gross** share).

**Part B — Period-end adjusting entries (open AR at year-end)**  
At **December 31**, two unrelated open trade receivables exist (calendar year-end). Discount terms on both are **2/10, n/30**, and each customer’s discount period still extends into January. Ignore COGS.

| Customer | Gross invoice still open | Management expectation at Dec 31 |
|---|---:|---|
| Solstice Co. | $55,000 | Will take the cash discount on **100%** of this balance |
| Northvale Inc. | $45,000 | Will **forfeit** the cash discount on **100%** of this balance |

1. Assume the company uses the **gross method** for all sales. Record the year-end adjusting entry for expected cash discounts on open receivables (only Solstice is expected to take the discount).  
2. Assume instead the company uses the **net method** for all sales (AR already recorded net of available discount). Record the year-end adjusting entry for expected **forfeitures** (only Northvale is expected to forfeit).

**Part C — Revenue presentation (Part A only)**  
Show how total revenue related to Westbay appears under the **net method** for June.

**Required:**  
a. Part A: June 3 sale and COGS; June 10 and June 29 collections; subsequent AR measurement schedule for Westbay (net carrying amounts).  
b. Part B: both year-end adjusting entries (gross-method version and net-method version).  
c. Part C: net-method income statement excerpt for Part A.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Part A — Net method**

*June 3 — initial recognition at net*  
Net AR / Sales = $35,000 × 0.98 = **$34,300**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 34,300 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 34,300 |
| *Sale recorded at gross less available 2% cash discount* | | |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 21,000 | |
| Inventory | | 21,000 |

**Check:** 34,300 = 34,300; 21,000 = 21,000. **Balanced.**

*June 10 — 40% of net within discount period*  
Cash / AR relieved = 0.40 × $34,300 = **$13,720**  
(Equivalent gross share = 0.40 × $35,000 = $14,000; discount implicit = $280 — **not** recorded separately under net when taken on time.)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 13,720 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 13,720 |
| *Early payment clears net AR; no Sales Discount account* | | |

**Check:** 13,720 = 13,720. **Balanced.**

*June 29 — remaining after discount period*  
Remaining net AR = $34,300 − $13,720 = **$20,580**  
Gross remaining share = 0.60 × $35,000 = **$21,000**  
Sales Discount Forfeited = $21,000 − $20,580 = **$420** (also = $21,000 × 0.02)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 21,000 | |
| Sales Discount Forfeited | | 420 |
| Accounts Receivable | | 20,580 |
| *Late payment: cash at remaining gross; forfeited discount is other revenue* | | |

**Check:** Dr 21,000 = Cr 420 + 20,580. **Balanced.** Ending AR = **$0**.

**Subsequent measurement schedule — Westbay AR (net method carrying amounts)**

| Date / Item | Westbay AR (net) |
|---|---:|
| June 1 beginning | $0 |
| June 3 sale (at net) | 34,300 |
| June 10 collection (within discount) | (13,720) |
| Balance after June 10 | 20,580 |
| June 29 collection (AR relieved at net) | (20,580) |
| **June 30 ending** | **$0** |

**Cash totals:** $13,720 + $21,000 = **$34,720**  
**Total revenue (sales + forfeited):** $34,300 + $420 = **$34,720** (matches cash).

**b. Part B — period-end adjusting entries**

*1. Gross method — reduce AR/revenue for expected discounts still available*  
Expected discount to be taken = $55,000 (Solstice) × 0.02 = **$1,100**  
(Northvale: expected forfeit → $0 reduction under gross YE logic)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Discount | 1,100 | |
| Accounts Receivable (or Allowance for Sales Discounts) | | 1,100 |
| *YE adjust: reduce revenue and AR for cash discounts expected on open gross AR* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,100. **Balanced.**

*2. Net method — increase AR/revenue for expected forfeitures*  
Expected forfeiture = $45,000 (Northvale) × 0.02 = **$900**  
(Solstice: expected to take discount → already correctly measured at net; no forfeit accrual)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 900 | |
| Sales Discount Forfeited | | 900 |
| *YE adjust: increase AR and revenue for discounts expected to be forfeited next period* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 900. **Balanced.**

**c. Part C — Net method income statement excerpt (Part A only)**

| Income Statement — Excerpt (June) | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales revenue | $34,300 |
| Sales discounts forfeited | 420 |
| **Total revenue** | **$34,720** |

*(Sales discounts forfeited is typically reported in **other revenues (expenses)**, not as a reduction of sales.)*

**Key insight:** The net method **measures AR as if the discount will be taken**. The subsequent schedule carries AR at net until settlement. Early payment simply clears cash against net AR. Late payment adds **Sales Discount Forfeited**. At year-end, gross method **pulls down** open AR/revenue for expected takes; net method **pulls up** AR/revenue for expected forfeits. When collection outcomes are known, total cash-based revenue is the same under either method’s final economics for that customer.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of cash-discount accounts and methods (2 items)
**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Classification of Sales Discount vs Sales Discount Forfeited; identification of correct settlement entry by method  

**Question 1:**  
Under the **gross method**, when a customer pays within the cash discount period, the debit to **Sales Discount** is best classified as:  
- A) An operating expense  
- B) A contra-revenue account that reduces sales to net sales  
- C) Other revenue, similar to interest income  
- D) A direct reduction of inventory  

**Answer:** B. Sales Discount is a **contra account to sales**; net sales = sales revenue − sales discounts. It is not an operating expense and is not other revenue (that role belongs to **Sales Discount Forfeited** under the **net** method).

**Question 2:**  
On March 1, a company records a **$20,000** credit sale, terms **2/10, n/30**, under the **net method**. If the customer pays in full on **March 28** (after the discount period), which entry is correct for **March 28 only**?  
- A) Debit Cash $19,600; credit Accounts Receivable $19,600  
- B) Debit Cash $20,000; credit Accounts Receivable $20,000  
- C) Debit Cash $20,000; credit Sales Discount Forfeited $400; credit Accounts Receivable $19,600  
- D) Debit Cash $19,600; debit Sales Discount $400; credit Accounts Receivable $20,000  

**Answer:** C. Net method initially records AR at $20,000 × 0.98 = **$19,600**. After the discount period, the customer remits the full **$20,000**; the $400 difference is **Sales Discount Forfeited** (revenue). Option A would be correct only if paid **within** the discount period. Option D is the **gross**-method early-pay pattern (wrong method and wrong timing). Option B ignores that AR was never recorded at $20,000 under net.
