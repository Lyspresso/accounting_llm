# Agent 351 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-4

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for change in estimate in finite life intangible assets  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Change in estimate (useful life and/or residual value):** revise amortization **prospectively** — no restatement of prior periods; no cumulative catch-up to beginning RE
- **Measurement at change date:** carrying amount at date of change − revised residual = remaining amortizable base; divide by **remaining** useful life under the new estimate
- **Initial recognition JE:** finite-life intangible at cash-equivalent cost
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** roll-forward cost → accum. amort. → carrying amount through pre- and post-change periods
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** Dr Amortization Expense; Cr Patent/intangible (or Accumulated Amortization) using the **new** annual amount after the change; mid-year change years blend old rate (pre-change months) + new rate (post-change months)
- **Change in amortization method:** treated as a change in estimate effected by a change in principle — still **prospective**
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** amortize to disposal date under the revised rate, remove carrying amount, recognize gain or loss vs net proceeds
- **Number-variant twin:** same LO path with all companies, amounts, and lives changed

### Q1 — CORE — Summit Ridge: patent purchase, amort schedule, useful-life change, period-end AJs, mid-year disposal

**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition at cost; subsequent-measurement schedule; **prospective change in useful life**; **period-end amortization adjusting JEs** (emphasis) before and after the change; disposal JE after amortizing to sale date  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Ridge Instruments Inc.** (calendar year-end) purchased a **patent** for **$90,000 cash** on **January 1, 20X1**. Summit estimated a **10-year** useful life and **$0 residual value**, and amortizes the patent **straight-line**. Amortization is credited **directly to the Patent** account (no separate accumulated amortization).

At the **start of 20X4** (after **three** full years of amortization), management revises its estimate of the patent’s **total useful life to 8 years** (remaining useful life under the new estimate = **5 years**). Residual value remains **$0**. The change is a **change in accounting estimate** accounted for **prospectively**.

On **June 30, 20X6**, Summit sells the patent for **$48,000 cash**. Amortize through the sale date under the **revised** amortization rate before derecognizing.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **January 1, 20X1 initial recognition JE**. Prove Dr = Cr.  
(b) Record the **December 31, 20X1 period-end amortization AJ**. State annual amortization for 20X2 and 20X3 (same amount). Prepare a **subsequent-measurement / carrying-value schedule** through **December 31, 20X3** (date immediately before the estimate change).  
(c) Compute the **new annual amortization** effective 20X4. Record the **December 31, 20X4 period-end adjusting JE** (emphasis). Prove balance.  
(d) Extend the carrying-value schedule through **December 31, 20X5**.  
(e) Record: (1) amortization for the **six months** ended June 30, 20X6; (2) the **disposal JE** on June 30, 20X6. Compute gain or loss. Prove each balances.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 90,000 |
| *Record purchase of patent at cash cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**

**(b) Pre-change amortization and schedule**

Original annual amortization = $90,000 / 10 = **$9,000**.

December 31, 20X1 — **period-end adjusting JE**:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 9,000 | |
| Patent | | 9,000 |
| *Period-end amortization: $90,000 / 10* | | |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.**

Same **$9,000** period-end AJ is recorded on December 31, **20X2** and December 31, **20X3**.

| Date / event | Patent carrying amount |
|---|---:|
| Jan 1, 20X1 — acquisition cost | $90,000 |
| Dec 31, 20X1 — amortization | (9,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X2 — amortization | (9,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X3 — amortization | (9,000) |
| **Dec 31, 20X3 carrying amount (at change date)** | **$63,000** |

Proof: accumulated amortization to date of change = $90,000 × 3/10 = **$27,000**; carrying amount = $90,000 − $27,000 = **$63,000**.  
**No retroactive adjustment** is made to prior amortization or beginning retained earnings.

**(c) Prospective change in useful life — period-end AJ (emphasis)**

Remaining useful life under new estimate = 8 − 3 = **5 years**.  
Remaining amortizable base = $63,000 − $0 residual = **$63,000**.  
**New annual amortization** = $63,000 / 5 = **$12,600**.

December 31, 20X4 — **period-end adjusting JE** (emphasis):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 12,600 | |
| Patent | | 12,600 |
| *Period-end amort after change in estimate: $63,000 / 5 remaining years* | | |

**Check:** Dr 12,600 = Cr 12,600. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount Dec 31, 20X4 = $63,000 − $12,600 = **$50,400**.

**(d) Schedule through Dec 31, 20X5**

| Date / event | Patent carrying amount |
|---|---:|
| Dec 31, 20X3 (at change) | $63,000 |
| Dec 31, 20X4 — revised amort | (12,600) |
| Dec 31, 20X5 — revised amort | (12,600) |
| **Dec 31, 20X5 carrying amount** | **$37,800** |

Proof: $63,000 − $12,600 − $12,600 = **$37,800**.

**(e) Mid-year amortization and disposal**

Full-year revised amortization still **$12,600** (3 years of remaining life at Jan 1, 20X6: $37,800 / 3 = $12,600).  
Six months Jan 1–June 30, 20X6 = $12,600 × 6/12 = **$6,300**.

(1) June 30, 20X6 — amortize to date of disposal:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 6,300 | |
| Patent | | 6,300 |
| *Amortize patent 6 months to sale date under revised rate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,300 = Cr 6,300. **Balanced.**

Carrying amount at disposal = $37,800 − $6,300 = **$31,500**.  
Cash proceeds $48,000 − carrying amount $31,500 = **Gain $16,500**.

(2) June 30, 20X6 — disposal:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 48,000 | |
| Patent | | 31,500 |
| Gain on Sale of Patent | | 16,500 |
| *Derecognize patent; record gain on disposal* | | |

**Check:** Dr 48,000 = Cr 31,500 + 16,500 = 48,000. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** A change in estimated useful life of a finite-life intangible is applied **prospectively**. Take the **carrying amount at the change date**, subtract any revised residual, and amortize over the **remaining** life. Period-end AJs after the change use the new amount only; prior periods are **not** restated. On disposal, amortize **to the sale date** first, then remove carrying amount and book G/L.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Copperline Media: copyright life extension, schedule, period-end AJs, year-end disposal

**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition JE; pre-change amortization schedule; **prospective extension of useful life** (change in estimate); **period-end AJs** after change; full-year amort then disposal  
**Scenario:**  
**Copperline Media Corp.** (calendar year-end) purchased a **copyright** for **$120,000 cash** on **January 1, 20X2**. Original estimate: **8-year** useful life, residual **$0**, straight-line, credit amortization **directly to Copyright**.

At the **start of 20X6** (after **four** full years), management revises the estimated **total useful life to 10 years** (remaining life under the new estimate = **6 years**). Residual remains **$0**. Account for the change **prospectively**.

On **December 31, 20X8**, after recording 20X8 amortization, Copperline sells the copyright for **$22,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
(a) Record Jan 1, 20X2 purchase and Dec 31, 20X2 period-end amortization AJ. Prove balances.  
(b) Carrying-value schedule through Dec 31, 20X5 (change date). Compute new annual amortization.  
(c) Record Dec 31, 20X6 **period-end AJ** under the revised estimate (emphasis). Prove balance.  
(d) Carrying-value schedule from change date through Dec 31, 20X8.  
(e) Record Dec 31, 20X8 amortization AJ and the disposal JE. Compute gain or loss. Prove balances.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Initial recognition and first period-end AJ**

Original annual amortization = $120,000 / 8 = **$15,000**.

(1) January 1, 20X2 — purchase:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Copyright | 120,000 | |
| Cash | | 120,000 |
| *Record copyright at cash cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 120,000 = Cr 120,000. **Balanced.**

(2) December 31, 20X2 — period-end AJ:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 15,000 | |
| Copyright | | 15,000 |
| *Period-end amortization: $120,000 / 8* | | |

**Check:** Dr 15,000 = Cr 15,000. **Balanced.**  
(Identical $15,000 AJs apply for 20X3, 20X4, and 20X5.)

**(b) Schedule to change date and new rate**

| Date / event | Copyright carrying amount |
|---|---:|
| Jan 1, 20X2 — cost | $120,000 |
| Dec 31, 20X2 — amort | (15,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X3 — amort | (15,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X4 — amort | (15,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X5 — amort | (15,000) |
| **Dec 31, 20X5 / Jan 1, 20X6 (at change)** | **$60,000** |

Accumulated amortization = $120,000 × 4/8 = **$60,000**.  
Remaining life = 10 − 4 = **6 years**.  
**New annual amortization** = $60,000 / 6 = **$10,000**.  
(Extending life **decreases** annual amortization prospectively.)

**(c) Period-end AJ after change (emphasis)**

December 31, 20X6:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 10,000 | |
| Copyright | | 10,000 |
| *Period-end amort after life extension: $60,000 / 6* | | |

**Check:** Dr 10,000 = Cr 10,000. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount Dec 31, 20X6 = $60,000 − $10,000 = **$50,000**.

**(d) Post-change schedule through disposal date (before sale entry)**

| Date / event | Copyright carrying amount |
|---|---:|
| Jan 1, 20X6 (at change) | $60,000 |
| Dec 31, 20X6 — revised amort | (10,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X7 — revised amort | (10,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X8 — revised amort | (10,000) |
| **Dec 31, 20X8 carrying amount (pre-sale)** | **$30,000** |

Proof: three years × $10,000 = $30,000 of post-change amortization; $60,000 − $30,000 = **$30,000**.

**(e) Year-end amortization and disposal**

(1) December 31, 20X8 — period-end AJ (already reflected in schedule):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 10,000 | |
| Copyright | | 10,000 |
| *Period-end amortization under revised estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 10,000 = Cr 10,000. **Balanced.**

(2) December 31, 20X8 — disposal:

Carrying amount $30,000 − cash proceeds $22,000 = **Loss $8,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 22,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Copyright | 8,000 | |
| Copyright | | 30,000 |
| *Derecognize copyright; record loss on disposal* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,000 + 8,000 = 30,000 = Cr 30,000. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Whether the useful-life change **shortens** (Q1) or **extends** (Q2) the asset’s life, the mechanics are identical: **no prior-period restatement**; allocate current carrying amount over the **remaining** life. Period-end AJs after the change use only the new amount.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Brookfield: mid-year life change (blended period-end AJ) + residual-value change

**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** **Period-end adjusting JE emphasis** when useful life changes **mid-year** (blend old + new rates); separate **change in residual value** (and remaining life) applied prospectively; carrying-value computation  
**Scenario:**  
**Brookfield Analytics LLC** (calendar year-end) has two independent finite-life intangible situations. Amortization is straight-line; residual values are as stated; amortization is credited directly to the asset.

**Situation A — mid-year change in useful life (period-end AJ emphasis)**  
On **January 1, 20X1**, Brookfield purchased a **customer list** for **$51,000 cash**. Original useful life **12 years**, residual **$0**.  
On **July 1, 20X4**, management revises the estimated **total useful life to 16 years**. Residual remains **$0**.

**Situation B — change in residual value and remaining life**  
On **January 1, 20X1**, Brookfield purchased a **technology license** for **$64,000 cash**. Original useful life **8 years**, residual **$0**.  
On **January 1, 20X4** (after three full years), management revises **residual value to $4,000** and **remaining useful life to 5 years** (prospective).

On **June 30, 20X6**, after applying the revised residual/life estimates for 20X4 and 20X5 in full, Brookfield sells the technology license for **$28,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
(a) **Situation A:** Compute amortization expense for the year ended **December 31, 20X4** (show pre-change and post-change halves). Record the **December 31, 20X4 period-end AJ**. State the customer-list carrying amount on Dec 31, 20X4. Prove the JE balances.  
(b) **Situation B:** Compute the new annual amortization effective 20X4. Record the **December 31, 20X4 period-end AJ**. Prepare a carrying-value schedule from acquisition through Dec 31, 20X5.  
(c) **Situation B disposal:** Record (1) six months’ amortization to June 30, 20X6 and (2) the disposal JE. Compute gain or loss. Prove balances.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Situation A — mid-year useful-life change**

Original annual amortization = $51,000 / 12 = **$4,250**.

Elapsed life at July 1, 20X4 = 3 years + 6 months = **3.5 years**.  
Accumulated amortization at change = $4,250 × 3.5 = **$14,875**.  
Carrying amount at July 1, 20X4 = $51,000 − $14,875 = **$36,125**.  
Remaining useful life under new estimate = 16 − 3.5 = **12.5 years**.  
**New annual amortization** = $36,125 / 12.5 = **$2,890**.

Year 20X4 amortization (blended):  
- Jan 1–June 30 (old rate): $4,250 × 6/12 = **$2,125**  
- July 1–Dec 31 (new rate): $2,890 × 6/12 = **$1,445**  
- **Total amortization expense 20X4 = $3,570**

December 31, 20X4 — **period-end adjusting JE** (emphasis; single year-end entry for full-year amount):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 3,570 | |
| Customer List | | 3,570 |
| *Period-end amort: $2,125 (old) + $1,445 (new) after mid-year life change* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,570 = Cr 3,570. **Balanced.**

Carrying amount Dec 31, 20X4 = $36,125 − $1,445 = **$34,680**  
(or $51,000 − $14,875 − $1,445 = **$34,680**).

**(b) Situation B — residual value and remaining life change**

Original annual amortization = $64,000 / 8 = **$8,000**.  
Accumulated amortization after 3 years = $8,000 × 3 = **$24,000**.  
Carrying amount at Jan 1, 20X4 = $64,000 − $24,000 = **$40,000**.  
Remaining amortizable base = $40,000 − **$4,000 residual** = **$36,000**.  
**New annual amortization** = $36,000 / 5 = **$7,200**.

December 31, 20X4 — period-end AJ:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 7,200 | |
| Technology License | | 7,200 |
| *Period-end amort after change in residual and remaining life: $36,000 / 5* | | |

**Check:** Dr 7,200 = Cr 7,200. **Balanced.**

| Date / event | License carrying amount |
|---|---:|
| Jan 1, 20X1 — cost | $64,000 |
| Dec 31, 20X1 — amort | (8,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X2 — amort | (8,000) |
| Dec 31, 20X3 — amort | (8,000) |
| **Jan 1, 20X4 (at change)** | **$40,000** |
| Dec 31, 20X4 — revised amort | (7,200) |
| Dec 31, 20X5 — revised amort | (7,200) |
| **Dec 31, 20X5 carrying amount** | **$25,600** |

Proof: $40,000 − $7,200 − $7,200 = **$25,600**.  
(Terminal residual target: after 5 revised years, carrying amount should equal residual **$4,000**.)

**(c) Situation B — disposal**

Remaining life at Jan 1, 20X6 = 3 years; amortizable base = $25,600 − $4,000 = $21,600; annual still **$7,200**.  
Six months to June 30, 20X6 = **$3,600**.

(1) June 30, 20X6 — amortize to sale date:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 3,600 | |
| Technology License | | 3,600 |
| *Amortize 6 months to disposal under revised estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,600 = Cr 3,600. **Balanced.**

Carrying amount at disposal = $25,600 − $3,600 = **$22,000**.  
Cash $28,000 − carrying amount $22,000 = **Gain $6,000**.

(2) June 30, 20X6 — disposal:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 28,000 | |
| Technology License | | 22,000 |
| Gain on Sale of License | | 6,000 |
| *Derecognize license; record gain* | | |

**Check:** Dr 28,000 = Cr 22,000 + 6,000 = 28,000. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Mid-year useful-life changes require a **blended** period-end AJ (old rate for months before the change, new rate after). Changes in **residual value** reduce (or increase) the remaining amortizable base before dividing by remaining life — still purely **prospective**.

### Q4 — MC — Treatment of change in estimate (method / classification)

**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Prospective treatment of changes in useful life / residual value / amortization method for finite-life intangibles  
**Question:**  
On January 1, 20X1, Pinnacle Brands capitalized a finite-life patent at cost and began amortizing it straight-line over 12 years with zero residual value. On January 1, 20X6, Pinnacle revises the patent’s total useful life to 15 years and continues straight-line amortization. How should Pinnacle account for this change?

- A) Restate prior-period financial statements as if the 15-year life had always been used  
- B) Recognize a cumulative-effect adjustment in current net income for the difference between old and new amortization to date  
- C) Charge the entire difference between book value and the amount that would have been amortized under the new life to retained earnings as a prior-period adjustment  
- D) Make **no** adjustment to prior periods; amortize the **current carrying amount** over the **remaining** useful life under the new estimate (prospective treatment)

**Answer:** **D.**  
Changes in estimated useful life (and residual value) of finite-life intangibles are changes in **accounting estimates** applied **prospectively** to current and future periods only. Prior carrying amounts and prior amortization expense are not restated. (A change in amortization **method** is a change in estimate effected by a change in principle and is likewise prospective.)

### Q5 — MC — Compute amortization after mid-year life revision

**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Compute period amortization expense when useful life is revised mid-year (old rate for first half, new rate for second half)  
**Question:**  
Oak Creek Ltd. purchased a finite-life intangible on January 1, 20X1 for **$51,000** and amortized it over **12 years** with zero residual. On **July 1, 20X4**, Oak Creek revised the total useful life to **16 years**. What amortization expense should Oak Creek recognize for the year ended December 31, 20X4?

- A) $4,250  
- B) $2,890  
- C) $3,570  
- D) $2,125  

**Answer:** **C. $3,570.**  
Old annual rate = $51,000 / 12 = $4,250; half-year pre-change = **$2,125**.  
Carrying amount at July 1, 20X4 = $51,000 − ($4,250 × 3.5) = **$36,125**.  
Remaining life = 16 − 3.5 = **12.5 years**; new annual = $36,125 / 12.5 = **$2,890**; half-year post-change = **$1,445**.  
Total Year 20X4 amortization = $2,125 + $1,445 = **$3,570**.  
(A is a full year at the old rate; B is a full year at the new rate; D is only the first half-year.)
