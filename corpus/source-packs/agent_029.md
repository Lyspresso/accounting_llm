# Agent 29 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-4

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Account for multiple tax differences and prepare a tax reconciliation disclosure  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Combining taxable temporary differences (DTL), deductible temporary differences (DTA), and permanent differences in one period
- Computing taxable income from pretax GAAP income (add/subtract temporary and permanent items)
- Initial recognition of income tax payable, deferred tax liability, deferred tax asset, and income tax expense
- Multi-year deferred tax schedules (basis differences × enacted rate → ending balances; period change = JE amount)
- Period-end adjusting income-tax journal entries (including partial reversals)
- Settlement/reversal of temporary differences (prepaid expires; warranty/litigation paid)
- Rate reconciliation disclosure (statutory dollars/% → permanent-difference reconciling items → effective tax rate)
- Balance-sheet presentation: offset DTA and DTL for same tax-paying component/jurisdiction as single noncurrent amount

### Q1 — CORE — Cascadia Outfitters: multi-year DTL + DTA + municipal interest; JEs, schedules, rate recon
**LO:** LO 18-4  
**Concept:** Multiple tax differences over three years — initial recognition JE, deferred tax schedules, period-end entries, reversals, rate reconciliation  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascadia Outfitters Co.** is a calendar-year company. Pretax GAAP income (after all revenues and expenses, including the items below) is:

| Year | Pretax GAAP income |
|------|-------------------:|
| Year 1 | $200,000 |
| Year 2 | $240,000 |
| Year 3 | $180,000 |

The enacted tax rate is **21%** for all years. Deferred tax accounts have **zero** beginning balances on January 1, Year 1. Accounting and tax periods both end December 31.

Book–tax differences:

1. **Prepaid insurance:** On December 31, Year 1, Cascadia paid and recorded prepaid insurance of **$40,000** for Year 2 coverage. The cost is **deductible for tax when paid** and is expensed for GAAP in Year 2 when coverage is consumed.  
2. **Warranty accrual:** On December 31, Year 1, Cascadia accrued warranty expense of **$60,000** (GAAP liability). Warranty costs are **not deductible for tax until paid**. Settlements are **$20,000 in Year 2, $20,000 in Year 3, and $20,000 in Year 4**.  
3. **Municipal bond interest:** Cascadia earns **$2,000** of municipal bond interest revenue each year (Year 1–Year 3). Interest is **included in pretax GAAP income** but is **nontaxable** (permanent difference).

No other temporary or permanent differences exist. No valuation allowance is required.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute **taxable income** and **income tax payable** for Year 1, Year 2, and Year 3.  
(b) Prepare schedules of the **deferred tax liability** and **deferred tax asset** ending balances at December 31 of Year 1, Year 2, and Year 3.  
(c) Record the **period-end income tax journal entries** for Year 1 (initial recognition), Year 2, and Year 3.  
(d) Prepare the **rate reconciliation** (dollars and percentages) from the statutory rate to the effective tax rate for each of the three years.  
(e) At December 31, Year 3, state the **balance-sheet presentation** of deferred taxes (same component/jurisdiction) and the remaining warranty temporary difference expected to reverse in Year 4.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Taxable income and income tax payable**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pretax GAAP income | $200,000 | $240,000 | $180,000 |
| Insurance (prepaid origin / reverse) | (40,000) | 40,000 | — |
| Warranty (origin / cash settlements) | 60,000 | (20,000) | (20,000) |
| Municipal bond interest (permanent) | (2,000) | (2,000) | (2,000) |
| **Taxable income** | **$218,000** | **$258,000** | **$158,000** |
| Tax rate | × 21% | × 21% | × 21% |
| **Income tax payable** | **$45,780** | **$54,180** | **$33,180** |

Checks: \(218{,}000 \times 0.21 = 45{,}780\); \(258{,}000 \times 0.21 = 54{,}180\); \(158{,}000 \times 0.21 = 33{,}180\).

**(b) Deferred tax schedules**

**Deferred tax liability (prepaid insurance — taxable temporary difference)**

| | Dec 31, Y1 | Dec 31, Y2 | Dec 31, Y3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GAAP basis of prepaid insurance | $40,000 | $0 | $0 |
| Tax basis of prepaid insurance | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Cumulative taxable temporary difference | 40,000 | 0 | 0 |
| Enacted rate | × 21% | × 21% | × 21% |
| **DTL ending balance** | **$8,400** | **$0** | **$0** |

**Deferred tax asset (warranty — deductible temporary difference)**

| | Dec 31, Y1 | Dec 31, Y2 | Dec 31, Y3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GAAP basis of warranty liability | $60,000 | $40,000 | $20,000 |
| Tax basis of warranty liability | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Cumulative deductible temporary difference | 60,000 | 40,000 | 20,000 |
| Enacted rate | × 21% | × 21% | × 21% |
| **DTA ending balance** | **$12,600** | **$8,400** | **$4,200** |

Municipal bond interest creates **neither** a DTA nor a DTL (never reverses).

**(c) Period-end income tax journal entries**

**December 31, Year 1 — initial recognition**  
Income tax expense = payable + ΔDTL − ΔDTA = \(45{,}780 + 8{,}400 - 12{,}600 = \mathbf{\$41{,}580}\).  
Cross-check (permanent only): \(200{,}000 \times 21\% - 2{,}000 \times 21\% = 42{,}000 - 420 = 41{,}580\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 41,580 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 12,600 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deferred Tax Liability | | 8,400 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Income Tax Payable | | 45,780 |

Dr \(41{,}580 + 12{,}600 = 54{,}180\) = Cr \(8{,}400 + 45{,}780\) ✓

**December 31, Year 2 — subsequent measurement / partial settlement**  
ΔDTL = \(0 - 8{,}400 = (8{,}400)\) (full reverse of prepaid).  
ΔDTA = \(8{,}400 - 12{,}600 = (4{,}200)\) (one-third warranty reverse).  
Expense = \(54{,}180 - 8{,}400 + 4{,}200 = \mathbf{\$49{,}980}\).  
Cross-check: \(240{,}000 \times 21\% - 420 = 50{,}400 - 420 = 49{,}980\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 49,980 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 8,400 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deferred Tax Asset | | 4,200 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Income Tax Payable | | 54,180 |

Dr \(49{,}980 + 8{,}400 = 58{,}380\) = Cr \(4{,}200 + 54{,}180\) ✓

**December 31, Year 3 — continued warranty settlement**  
ΔDTA = \(4{,}200 - 8{,}400 = (4{,}200)\).  
Expense = \(33{,}180 + 4{,}200 = \mathbf{\$37{,}380}\).  
Cross-check: \(180{,}000 \times 21\% - 420 = 37{,}800 - 420 = 37{,}380\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 37,380 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deferred Tax Asset | | 4,200 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Income Tax Payable | | 33,180 |

Dr \(37{,}380\) = Cr \(4{,}200 + 33{,}180\) ✓

**(d) Rate reconciliation — statutory to effective**

| | Year 1 $ | Year 1 % | Year 2 $ | Year 2 % | Year 3 $ | Year 3 % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Statutory tax rate | $42,000 | 21.000% | $50,400 | 21.000% | $37,800 | 21.000% |
| Tax-exempt municipal interest | (420) | (0.210)% | (420) | (0.175)% | (420) | (0.233)% |
| **Effective tax rate** | **$41,580** | **20.790%** | **$49,980** | **20.825%** | **$37,380** | **20.767%** |

Supporting computations:  
- Statutory dollars = pretax GAAP × 21%.  
- Tax-exempt effect = \(2{,}000 \times 21\% = 420\) each year; % = \(420 / \text{pretax}\).  
- Effective rate = income tax expense / pretax GAAP income.  
Temporary differences affect **current vs deferred** components of tax expense but **do not** appear as reconciling items between statutory and effective rates when rates are constant.

**(e) Presentation and remaining temporary difference (Dec 31, Year 3)**  
Same tax-paying component and jurisdiction: offset DTA and DTL → **net noncurrent deferred tax asset of $4,200** (DTL = $0; DTA = $4,200).  
Remaining deductible temporary difference = **$20,000** warranty liability expected to reverse in Year 4 → DTA \(20{,}000 \times 21\% = \$4{,}200\).

**Key insight:** With multiple differences, tax expense = current tax payable ± changes in DTL/DTA. Permanent differences change the **effective rate** and appear in the **rate reconciliation**; temporary differences drive deferred balances and reverse in future periods without creating permanent rate reconciling items (when enacted rates are constant).

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Meridian Fabricators: multi-year multi-difference twin
**LO:** LO 18-4  
**Concept:** Multiple tax differences — number-variant twin of Q1 (all amounts and rate changed)  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Fabricators Inc.** reports pretax GAAP income of **$320,000** (Year 1), **$280,000** (Year 2), and **$350,000** (Year 3). The enacted tax rate is **25%** for all years. Beginning deferred tax balances are zero on January 1, Year 1. Calendar year-end for books and tax.

Differences:  
1. **Prepaid insurance** of **$48,000** paid and recorded December 31, Year 1 (tax-deductible when paid; GAAP expense in Year 2).  
2. **Warranty accrual** of **$36,000** on December 31, Year 1; settled **$12,000** in each of Years 2, 3, and 4.  
3. **Municipal bond interest** of **$4,000** each year (Years 1–3), nontaxable permanent difference.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute taxable income and income tax payable for Years 1–3.  
(b) Deferred tax liability and deferred tax asset ending balances for Years 1–3.  
(c) Record the December 31 income tax journal entries for Years 1–3.  
(d) Year 1 rate reconciliation (dollars and percentages).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Taxable income and tax payable**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pretax GAAP income | $320,000 | $280,000 | $350,000 |
| Insurance | (48,000) | 48,000 | — |
| Warranty | 36,000 | (12,000) | (12,000) |
| Municipal interest | (4,000) | (4,000) | (4,000) |
| **Taxable income** | **$304,000** | **$312,000** | **$334,000** |
| **Income tax payable (×25%)** | **$76,000** | **$78,000** | **$83,500** |

**(b) Deferred tax ending balances**

| | Dec 31, Y1 | Dec 31, Y2 | Dec 31, Y3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cumulative taxable TD (prepaid) | $48,000 | $0 | $0 |
| **DTL (×25%)** | **$12,000** | **$0** | **$0** |
| Cumulative deductible TD (warranty) | $36,000 | $24,000 | $12,000 |
| **DTA (×25%)** | **$9,000** | **$6,000** | **$3,000** |

**(c) Journal entries**

**December 31, Year 1**  
Expense = \(76{,}000 + 12{,}000 - 9{,}000 = \mathbf{\$79{,}000}\)  
Cross-check: \(320{,}000 \times 25\% - 4{,}000 \times 25\% = 80{,}000 - 1{,}000 = 79{,}000\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 79,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 9,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deferred Tax Liability | | 12,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Income Tax Payable | | 76,000 |

Dr \(79{,}000 + 9{,}000 = 88{,}000\) = Cr \(12{,}000 + 76{,}000\) ✓

**December 31, Year 2**  
Expense = \(78{,}000 - 12{,}000 + 3{,}000 = \mathbf{\$69{,}000}\)  
Cross-check: \(280{,}000 \times 25\% - 1{,}000 = 70{,}000 - 1{,}000 = 69{,}000\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 69,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 12,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deferred Tax Asset | | 3,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Income Tax Payable | | 78,000 |

Dr \(69{,}000 + 12{,}000 = 81{,}000\) = Cr \(3{,}000 + 78{,}000\) ✓

**December 31, Year 3**  
Expense = \(83{,}500 + 3{,}000 = \mathbf{\$86{,}500}\)  
Cross-check: \(350{,}000 \times 25\% - 1{,}000 = 87{,}500 - 1{,}000 = 86{,}500\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 86,500 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deferred Tax Asset | | 3,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Income Tax Payable | | 83,500 |

Dr \(86{,}500\) = Cr \(3{,}000 + 83{,}500\) ✓

**(d) Year 1 rate reconciliation**

| | Dollars | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Statutory tax rate (\(320{,}000 \times 25\%\)) | $80,000 | 25.000% |
| Tax-exempt municipal interest (\(4{,}000 \times 25\%\)) | (1,000) | (0.313)% |
| **Effective tax rate** (\(79{,}000 / 320{,}000\)) | **$79,000** | **24.688%** |

**Key insight:** Changing the rate and all temporary/permanent amounts scales current tax, deferred balances, and the reconciling item, but the structure is identical: permanent differences alone drive statutory-to-effective rate reconciling items when rates are constant.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Harbor Peak: multi-difference period-end JE, settlement year, disclosure & BS netting
**LO:** LO 18-4  
**Concept:** Single-period multi-difference recognition; Year-2 settlement of temporary differences; rate reconciliation with both nontaxable and nondeductible items; net noncurrent deferred tax presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Harbor Peak Manufacturing LLC** has a December 31 year-end. Enacted tax rate is **25%** for all years. Deferred tax accounts begin at **zero** on January 1, Year 1.

**Year 1** pretax GAAP income: **$360,000**, including:  
- Life insurance **proceeds (gain)** of **$12,000** on a key-person policy (nontaxable permanent difference).  
- Political contribution expense of **$4,000** (nondeductible permanent difference).  

Temporary differences originating in Year 1:  
1. **Prepaid insurance** (GAAP asset) **$40,000**; tax basis **$0** (fully deducted for tax when paid) → taxable temporary difference. Coverage is for Year 2 only.  
2. **Accrued litigation liability** (GAAP) **$28,000**; tax basis **$0** (deductible when paid) → deductible temporary difference.  

**Year 2** pretax GAAP income: **$400,000**. No permanent differences in Year 2. During Year 2: prepaid insurance expires (fully expensed for GAAP); the entire **$28,000** litigation liability is settled in cash (tax deductible when paid). No new temporary differences arise in Year 2.

**Required:**  
(a) For **Year 1**, compute taxable income, income tax payable, DTL, DTA, and income tax expense. Record the December 31, Year 1 income tax entry.  
(b) Prepare the **Year 1 rate reconciliation** in dollars and percentages (include both permanent items).  
(c) State December 31, Year 1 **balance-sheet presentation** of deferred taxes (same component and jurisdiction).  
(d) For **Year 2**, compute taxable income, tax payable, deferred tax changes, and income tax expense. Record the December 31, Year 2 entry that settles/reverses the temporary differences.  
(e) Briefly explain why temporary differences do **not** appear as reconciling items in Harbor Peak’s Year 1 rate reconciliation.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Year 1 — initial multi-difference recognition**

Taxable income:  
\[
360{,}000 - 40{,}000 + 28{,}000 - 12{,}000 + 4{,}000 = \mathbf{\$340{,}000}
\]  
Income tax payable = \(340{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$85{,}000}\).

| Item | Cumulative TD | × 25% | Deferred tax |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Prepaid insurance (taxable TD) | $40,000 | $10,000 | **DTL $10,000** |
| Litigation accrual (deductible TD) | $28,000 | $7,000 | **DTA $7,000** |

Income tax expense = \(85{,}000 + 10{,}000 - 7{,}000 = \mathbf{\$88{,}000}\).  
Cross-check (permanents only):  
\(360{,}000 \times 25\% - 12{,}000 \times 25\% + 4{,}000 \times 25\% = 90{,}000 - 3{,}000 + 1{,}000 = 88{,}000\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 88,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 7,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deferred Tax Liability | | 10,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Income Tax Payable | | 85,000 |

Dr \(88{,}000 + 7{,}000 = 95{,}000\) = Cr \(10{,}000 + 85{,}000\) ✓

**(b) Year 1 rate reconciliation**

| | Dollars | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Statutory tax rate (\(360{,}000 \times 25\%\)) | $90,000 | 25.000% |
| Tax-exempt life insurance proceeds (\(12{,}000 \times 25\%\)) | (3,000) | (0.833)% |
| Nondeductible political contribution (\(4{,}000 \times 25\%\)) | 1,000 | 0.278% |
| **Effective tax rate** (\(88{,}000 / 360{,}000\)) | **$88,000** | **24.444%** |

**(c) Balance-sheet presentation — December 31, Year 1**  
Offset DTA and DTL for the same tax-paying component/jurisdiction:  
Net noncurrent **deferred tax liability** = \(10{,}000 - 7{,}000 = \mathbf{\$3{,}000}\).

**(d) Year 2 — settlement / maturity of temporary differences**

Taxable income:  
\[
400{,}000 + 40{,}000 - 28{,}000 = \mathbf{\$412{,}000}
\]  
(Insurance reverse adds to taxable; litigation cash payment is tax-deductible.)  
Income tax payable = \(412{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$103{,}000}\).

Ending DTL = $0; ending DTA = $0.  
ΔDTL = \((10{,}000)\); ΔDTA = \((7{,}000)\).  
Expense = \(103{,}000 - 10{,}000 + 7{,}000 = \mathbf{\$100{,}000}\).  
Cross-check: \(400{,}000 \times 25\% = 100{,}000\) (no permanent differences in Year 2).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 100,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 10,000 | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Deferred Tax Asset | | 7,000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Income Tax Payable | | 103,000 |

Dr \(100{,}000 + 10{,}000 = 110{,}000\) = Cr \(7{,}000 + 103{,}000\) ✓

**(e) Why temporary differences omit from the rate recon**  
With a constant enacted rate, originating/reversing temporary differences shift tax between **current** (payable) and **deferred** components but leave **total income tax expense** equal to pretax GAAP × statutory rate adjusted **only** for permanent items. Therefore the rate reconciliation explains only permanent (and other non-temporary) effects such as tax-exempt income and nondeductible expenses.

**Key insight:** Nontaxable items **lower** the effective rate; nondeductible items **raise** it. Temporary differences are tracked on deferred tax schedules and reverse at settlement (cash payment, asset consumption) without permanent effective-rate reconciling items when rates do not change.

### Q4 — MC (classification / presentation)
**LO:** LO 18-4  
**Concept:** Rate-reconciliation classification and deferred tax balance-sheet presentation  

**Question 1:**  
Pinecrest Co. has pretax GAAP income of $100,000 and a 21% enacted rate. During the year it recorded (1) warranty expense of $10,000 that is not yet tax-deductible and (2) $3,000 of municipal bond interest included in pretax income. Which item(s) appear as reconciling items in the statutory-to-effective **rate reconciliation**?

- A) Warranty temporary difference only  
- B) Municipal bond interest only  
- C) Both the warranty temporary difference and municipal bond interest  
- D) Neither item  

**Answer:** **B.** Only permanent differences (here, tax-exempt municipal interest) create differences between statutory tax dollars on pretax GAAP income and income tax expense when rates are constant. The warranty creates a deferred tax asset and affects the split between current and deferred tax, but not the effective-rate reconciling line.

**Question 2:**  
For a single tax-paying component in one tax jurisdiction, Aspen Co. has a deferred tax asset of $40,000 and a deferred tax liability of $55,000 (no valuation allowance). How should Aspen present deferred taxes on a classified balance sheet under ASC 740?

- A) Current DTA $40,000 and noncurrent DTL $55,000  
- B) Noncurrent DTA $40,000 and noncurrent DTL $55,000 (gross)  
- C) Single noncurrent deferred tax liability of $15,000  
- D) Single current deferred tax liability of $15,000  

**Answer:** **C.** All deferred tax assets and liabilities for a particular tax-paying component within a particular jurisdiction are offset and presented as a **single noncurrent** amount. Net = \(55{,}000 - 40{,}000 = \$15{,}000\) noncurrent DTL.
