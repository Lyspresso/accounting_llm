# Agent 299 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-5

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Describe required disclosures including that for noncash transactions  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Noncash investing and financing** transactions involve no cash exchange; they are **omitted from the SCF body** but must be **disclosed** (narrative or schedule) under ASC 230
- Partial noncash asset acquisitions: report only the **cash portion** in investing; exclude noncash consideration from financing cash inflows
- **Initial recognition JEs** for equipment/land acquired with stock or notes, and for finance-lease commencement (ROU asset / lease liability)
- **Equipment additions subsequent-measurement schedule** separating cash purchases from noncash components
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** accrue interest on notes issued mid-year for noncash equipment purchases (feeds interest-expense vs interest-paid analysis)
- **Note subsequent-measurement / interest path schedule** through first coupon settlement
- **Disposal** of PPE: investing cash inflow = proceeds only; gain/loss is operating (noncash) adjustment under indirect method
- **Settlement without cash:** debt conversion to equity — noncash disclosure, not a financing cash outflow
- Supplemental disclosures under the **indirect method**: **interest paid** (net of amounts capitalized) and **income taxes paid** (either method)
- **Restricted cash** nature disclosure
- Draft SCF correction: remove noncash lines; matched reclassifications preserve combined inv+fin; unmatched phantom lines distort cash reconciliation

**Why the combined total changed (unlike a pure matched reclass):**  
- Bogus financing inflows on the draft: stock-for-equipment $48,000 + note $36,000 + lease liability $84,000 = **$168,000**.

**Key classification takeaway:** Noncash reclassifications that are **matched** (e.g., stock-for-equipment on both “purchase” and “stock issuance”) preserve the combined inv+fin total; **unmatched** phantom cash lines (lease liability recorded as a financing inflow with no cash) distort the cash reconciliation and must be removed.

**(i) LO 22-5 reporting/disclosure checklist**  
1. Reconciliation of net income to operating cash flows (on face or note under indirect method; major classes of operating accruals/deferrals).  
2. Report investing and financing **inflows and outflows separately** (gross).  
3. Disclose **interest paid** (indirect method; net of capitalized interest) and **income taxes paid** (either method).  
4. Disclose **noncash investing and financing** transactions.  
5. Disclose the **nature of restrictions** on cash, cash equivalents, and amounts generally described as restricted cash/equivalents.

**Key insight:** Mid-year noncash equipment notes create a **period-end interest accrual** (expense and payable) but **no cash interest** until the first coupon date—so interest **paid** excludes that accrual. Only cash portions of PPE acquisitions hit investing; stock, notes, and lease commencements are **disclosed**, not reported as financing cash inflows.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of partial noncash equipment purchase on the SCF
**LO:** LO 22-5  
**Concept:** Classification — cash investing outflow only; note issuance excluded from financing cash inflows  
**Question:**  
On September 1, Year 1, **Pinnacle Rail Works** acquired equipment with a cash price of **$90,000** by paying **$30,000 cash** and signing a **$60,000 long-term note**. No principal or interest was paid in Year 1. In the Year 1 statement of cash flows, how should this transaction be reported?

- A) Investing outflow $90,000; financing inflow $60,000  
- B) Investing outflow $30,000; financing inflow $60,000  
- C) Investing outflow $30,000 only; disclose noncash investing/financing of $60,000  
- D) Financing outflow $30,000; disclose noncash investing of $90,000  

**Answer:** **C.** Only the **cash** portion ($30,000) is an investing outflow. The note issuance is a **noncash investing and financing** activity disclosed outside the body of the SCF (or in a schedule on the same page). Reporting a financing cash inflow of $60,000 would double-count relative to cash and misstate financing cash flows.

### Q5 — MC — Required supplemental SCF disclosures (indirect method)
**LO:** LO 22-5  
**Concept:** Required supplemental disclosures — interest paid, taxes paid, noncash, restricted cash  
**Question:**  
Which package correctly lists **required** statement-of-cash-flows disclosures under U.S. GAAP (ASC 230) as emphasized in LO 22-5?

- A) Free cash flow; interest expense; book value of PPE sold  
- B) Interest paid (indirect method; net of capitalized interest); income taxes paid; noncash investing and financing activities; nature of restricted cash  
- C) Only noncash investing and financing activities; interest expense equals interest paid  
- D) Reconciliation of net income to operating cash flows is never required under the indirect method  

**Answer:** **B.** LO 22-5 requires disclosure of **interest paid** (when the indirect method is used, net of amounts capitalized), **income taxes paid** (either method), **noncash investing and financing** transactions, and the **nature of restrictions** on cash/restricted cash. Interest **expense** is not a substitute for interest **paid**. The NI-to-operating-cash reconciliation is required under the indirect method (face or note).
