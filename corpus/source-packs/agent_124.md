# Agent 124 — CORE DEMO — LO 21-1

**Chapter:** 21  
**LO title:** Demonstrate accounting for change in accounting estimate including a change in accounting estimate effected by change in accounting principle  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize PPE/intangible at cost; no “catch-up” entry when estimates later revise
- **Change in accounting estimate** (useful life, residual/salvage): **prospective** only — recompute depreciation/amortization from **carrying amount** at change date over **remaining life** to revised residual; **no** restatement of prior periods
- **Change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle** (e.g., depreciation method change): treated as a **change in estimate** — **prospective** from carrying amount at method-change date
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** depreciation/amortization rollforward (expense, accumulated, carrying amount) before and after the change
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** record revised depreciation/amortization for the year of change and later years
- **Disposal JE:** remove cost and accumulated depreciation/amortization; gain/loss = cash proceeds − carrying amount after revised depreciation
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** prospective (not retrospective); disclose effect on income from continuing operations and EPS when the change affects several future periods and is material (ASC 250-10-50-4); ordinary recurring estimates (ADA, inventory obsolescence) need disclosure only if material

### Q1 — CORE — Change in useful life and residual: initial recognition through disposal
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Initial equipment capitalization; SL depreciation schedule; prospective change in useful life and residual; period-end JE after change; disposal after revised depreciation  
**Scenario:**  
**Northshore Fabrication Co.** (calendar year-end) purchased specialized production equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$420,000 cash**. Original estimates: **10-year** useful life, residual value **$20,000**, **straight-line** depreciation, zero residual salvage for partial years (full years only).

On **January 1, Year 4**, after three full years of use, management revises estimates based on new production plans: total useful life from acquisition is now **8 years**, and residual value is **$12,000**. No change in depreciation method.

On **December 31, Year 6**, after recording Year 6 depreciation under the revised estimates, Northshore sells the equipment for **$130,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE** for the equipment purchase.  
b. Compute original annual SL depreciation and prepare a **depreciation schedule** for **Years 1–3** (expense, end-of-year accumulated depreciation, carrying amount).  
c. Compute the **carrying amount on January 1, Year 4** and the **revised annual depreciation** under the new estimates.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 4 period-end adjusting JE** for depreciation.  
e. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for **Years 4–6** under the revised estimates (beginning carrying amount, depreciation expense, accumulated depreciation, ending carrying amount).  
f. Prepare the **December 31, Year 6 disposal JE** (after Year 6 depreciation).  
g. **Classification / disclosure:** Is this a change in estimate, change in principle, or error? Retrospective or prospective? What disclosure is required if the effect on Year 4 income is material?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 420,000 | |
| Cash | | 420,000 |
| *Capitalize equipment at cash acquisition cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 420,000 = Cr 420,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Original SL depreciation and Years 1–3 schedule**

Original depreciable base = $420,000 − $20,000 = **$400,000**  
Original annual SL depreciation = $400,000 ÷ 10 = **$40,000**

| Year | Beg. carrying amount | Depreciation expense | End. accum. dep. | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $420,000 | $40,000 | $40,000 | $380,000 |
| 2 | 380,000 | 40,000 | 80,000 | 340,000 |
| 3 | 340,000 | 40,000 | 120,000 | 300,000 |

*Illustrative Year 1–3 period-end JE each year (same amounts):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 40,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 40,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 40,000 each year. **Balanced.**

**c. Carrying amount at change date and revised annual depreciation**

Carrying amount, January 1, Year 4 = $420,000 − $120,000 = **$300,000**  
Revised total useful life = 8 years; elapsed = 3 years → **remaining life = 5 years**  
Revised residual = **$12,000**  
Revised annual depreciation = ($300,000 − $12,000) ÷ 5 = **$57,600**

**Prospective application only** — no entry to restate prior Accumulated Depreciation or Retained Earnings.

**d. December 31, Year 4 — Period-end adjusting JE (year of change)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 57,600 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 57,600 |
| *Prospective depreciation using revised life and residual* | | |

**Check:** Dr 57,600 = Cr 57,600. **Balanced.**

**e. Subsequent measurement schedule — Years 4–6 (revised estimates)**

| Year | Beg. carrying amount | Depreciation expense | End. accum. dep. | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | $300,000 | $57,600 | $177,600 | $242,400 |
| 5 | 242,400 | 57,600 | 235,200 | 184,800 |
| 6 | 184,800 | 57,600 | 292,800 | 127,200 |

Accumulated depreciation check, Dec 31 Year 6:  
$120,000 (pre-change) + 3 × $57,600 = $120,000 + $172,800 = **$292,800**  
Carrying amount: $420,000 − $292,800 = **$127,200**

**f. December 31, Year 6 — Disposal (settlement) JE**

Cash proceeds $130,000 − carrying amount $127,200 = **gain $2,800**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 130,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 292,800 | |
| Equipment | | 420,000 |
| Gain on Disposal of Equipment | | 2,800 |
| *Remove cost and accum. dep.; record cash and gain* | | |

**Check:** Dr 130,000 + 292,800 = **422,800**; Cr 420,000 + 2,800 = **422,800**. **Balanced.**

**g. Classification / presentation / disclosure**  
- **Type:** Change in **accounting estimate** (useful life and residual value).  
- **Treatment:** **Prospective** only (ASC 250-10-45-17) — current and future periods; **no** restatement of prior financial statements; **no** pro forma prior amounts.  
- **Disclosure:** If material and the change affects several future periods, disclose the **effect on income from continuing operations, net income, and related per-share amounts** for the current period (ASC 250-10-50-4). Year 4 depreciation is $17,600 higher than the old $40,000 rate ($57,600 − $40,000), reducing pretax income by **$17,600** relative to the pre-change estimate.

**Key insight:** A change in useful life and/or residual is a pure **change in estimate**. Take the **book value at the start of the change period**, subtract the **new residual**, and spread the remainder over the **remaining** (not original) life. Prior years are left alone; only current/future depreciation changes.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Change in life and residual twin
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: initial capitalization, SL history, prospective life/residual revision, multi-year schedule, period-end JE, and mid-life disposal  
**Scenario:**  
**Westpine Logistics Ltd.** (calendar year-end) acquired delivery-fleet equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$480,000 cash**. Original estimates: **10-year** useful life, residual **$30,000**, **straight-line**.

On **January 1, Year 5**, after four years, management revises total useful life to **8 years** from acquisition and residual value to **$20,000**. Method remains straight-line.

On **January 1, Year 7** (after recording Year 6 depreciation), Westpine sells the equipment for **$145,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **initial recognition JE** on January 1, Year 1.  
b. Compute original annual depreciation; state **accumulated depreciation and carrying amount on January 1, Year 5** (before the change).  
c. Compute **revised annual depreciation** and prepare the **December 31, Year 5 period-end JE**.  
d. Prepare a **depreciation schedule for Years 5–6** under the revised estimates.  
e. Prepare the **January 1, Year 7 disposal JE**.  
f. Compute the effect of the estimate change on **Year 5 pretax income** relative to continuing the old $45,000 annual amount (for disclosure).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 480,000 | |
| Cash | | 480,000 |

**Check:** Dr 480,000 = Cr 480,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Pre-change amounts**

Original annual SL = ($480,000 − $30,000) ÷ 10 = **$45,000**  
Accumulated depreciation, January 1, Year 5 = 4 × $45,000 = **$180,000**  
Carrying amount, January 1, Year 5 = $480,000 − $180,000 = **$300,000**

**c. Revised depreciation and Year 5 period-end JE**

Remaining life = 8 − 4 = **4 years**  
Revised annual depreciation = ($300,000 − $20,000) ÷ 4 = **$70,000**

*December 31, Year 5 — Period-end adjusting JE*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 70,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 70,000 |

**Check:** Dr 70,000 = Cr 70,000. **Balanced.** No prior-period adjustment entry.

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule — Years 5–6**

| Year | Beg. carrying amount | Depreciation expense | End. accum. dep. | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | $300,000 | $70,000 | $250,000 | $230,000 |
| 6 | 230,000 | 70,000 | 320,000 | 160,000 |

End Year 6 check: AD = $180,000 + 2 × $70,000 = **$320,000**; BV = $480,000 − $320,000 = **$160,000**.

**e. January 1, Year 7 — Disposal JE**

Proceeds $145,000 − BV $160,000 = **loss $15,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 145,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 320,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 15,000 | |
| Equipment | | 480,000 |

**Check:** Dr 145,000 + 320,000 + 15,000 = **480,000**; Cr = **480,000**. **Balanced.**

**f. Income effect for disclosure (Year 5)**  
Old annual amount $45,000 vs new $70,000 → Year 5 pretax income lower by **$25,000** solely because of the estimate change. Disclose if material and multi-period (ASC 250-10-50-4).

**Key insight:** Same mechanics as Q1 with new numbers: **BV at change ÷ remaining life after new residual**. Twin reinforces prospective treatment and that disposal gain/loss uses **post-revision** carrying amount.

### Q3 — CORE — Change in estimate effected by change in principle (depreciation method)
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Change from SL to double-declining-balance treated as change in estimate effected by change in principle; prospective DDB from carrying amount; multi-year schedule; income effect disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverbend Packaging Inc.** (calendar year-end) purchased manufacturing equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$320,000 cash**. Estimated useful life **8 years**, residual value **$20,000**, depreciated using **straight-line**.

On **January 1, Year 4**, after three years of SL depreciation, Riverbend changes to the **double-declining-balance (DDB)** method because management concludes benefits will be consumed more heavily in earlier remaining years. Useful life and residual estimates are unchanged. Account for the change as a **change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **initial recognition JE** on January 1, Year 1.  
b. Compute original annual SL depreciation and the **carrying amount on January 1, Year 4**.  
c. Compute the **DDB rate** based on remaining life and **Year 4 depreciation**. Prepare the **December 31, Year 4 period-end JE**.  
d. Prepare a **DDB depreciation schedule for Years 4–6** (beginning carrying amount, rate, expense, accumulated depreciation from method change, total accumulated depreciation, ending carrying amount). Do not depreciate below residual value of $20,000.  
e. Compare Year 4 depreciation under DDB to what Year 4 would have been under continued SL. State classification and whether the difference is reported as a cumulative prior-period adjustment.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 320,000 | |
| Cash | | 320,000 |

**Check:** Dr 320,000 = Cr 320,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Pre-change SL and carrying amount**

Annual SL = ($320,000 − $20,000) ÷ 8 = **$37,500**  
Accumulated depreciation after 3 years = 3 × $37,500 = **$112,500**  
Carrying amount, January 1, Year 4 = $320,000 − $112,500 = **$207,500**

**c. DDB rate, Year 4 expense, and period-end JE**

Remaining useful life = 8 − 3 = **5 years**  
Straight-line rate on remaining life = 1/5 = **20%**  
DDB rate = 2 × 20% = **40%**  
Year 4 DDB depreciation = $207,500 × 0.40 = **$83,000**

*December 31, Year 4 — Period-end adjusting JE*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 83,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 83,000 |
| *Prospective DDB from carrying amount at method change* | | |

**Check:** Dr 83,000 = Cr 83,000. **Balanced.**  
No cumulative catch-up to Retained Earnings (not retrospective).

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule — DDB Years 4–6**

| Year | Beg. CA | Rate | Dep. expense | AD since change | Total AD | End. CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | $207,500 | 40% | $83,000 | $83,000 | $195,500 | $124,500 |
| 5 | 124,500 | 40% | 49,800 | 132,800 | 245,300 | 74,700 |
| 6 | 74,700 | 40% | 29,880 | 162,680 | 275,180 | 44,820 |

Residual floor check: ending CA Year 6 **$44,820** > residual **$20,000** — no residual clamp yet.  
(Years 7–8 would continue DDB subject to not reducing CA below $20,000; not required here.)

Year 4 JE already shown; **Year 5** and **Year 6** period-end JEs:

*December 31, Year 5*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 49,800 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 49,800 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 49,800. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 6*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 29,880 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 29,880 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 29,880. **Balanced.**

**e. Comparison, classification, presentation**  
- Year 4 DDB $83,000 vs continued SL $37,500 → Year 4 expense **$45,500 higher**; pretax income **$45,500 lower**.  
- **Classification:** Change in **accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle** (ASC 250-10-45-18) — depreciation method change inseparable from revised pattern of benefits.  
- **Treatment:** **Prospective** (same as pure estimate changes). The $45,500 difference is **not** a cumulative prior-period adjustment and is **not** reported as an error restatement.  
- **Disclosure:** If material and multi-period, disclose effect on income from continuing operations, net income, and EPS for the current period.

**Key insight:** Changing depreciation methods (SL ↔ DDB/SYD, etc.) is **not** a retrospective change in principle. Treat it like an estimate change: **new method on remaining book value** over remaining life.

### Q4 — CORE — Patent amortization estimate change, disclosure, and disposal
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of finite-life intangible; prospective amortization after useful-life revision; carrying-value schedule; disposal JE; note disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**HarborTech IP LLC** (calendar year-end) purchased a patent on **January 1, Year 1** for **$90,000 cash**. Management amortized the patent over its original estimated economic life of **10 years** with **zero residual** (straight-line).

At the **start of Year 5**, after four years of amortization, HarborTech re-examines the market and revises the **total** useful life from acquisition to **7 years** (still zero residual).

On **January 1, Year 7**, after recording Year 6 amortization, HarborTech sells the patent for **$15,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **initial recognition JE** for the patent.  
b. Compute annual amortization for Years 1–4 and the **carrying amount on January 1, Year 5**.  
c. Compute **revised annual amortization** for Year 5 forward; prepare the **December 31, Year 5 period-end JE**.  
d. Prepare an **amortization schedule for Years 5–6** and state the carrying amount on December 31, Year 6.  
e. Prepare the **January 1, Year 7 disposal JE**.  
f. Draft the **key elements of note disclosure** for the Year 5 change (assume material).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 90,000 |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Pre-change amortization**

Original annual amortization = $90,000 ÷ 10 = **$9,000**  
Accumulated amortization after 4 years = 4 × $9,000 = **$36,000**  
Carrying amount, January 1, Year 5 = $90,000 − $36,000 = **$54,000**

**c. Revised amortization and Year 5 period-end JE**

Remaining life = 7 − 4 = **3 years**  
Revised annual amortization = $54,000 ÷ 3 = **$18,000**

*December 31, Year 5 — Period-end adjusting JE*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 18,000 | |
| Accumulated Amortization—Patent | | 18,000 |
| *Prospective amortization after change in useful-life estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule — Years 5–6**

| Year | Beg. carrying amount | Amortization expense | End. accum. amort. | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | $54,000 | $18,000 | $54,000 | $36,000 |
| 6 | 36,000 | 18,000 | 72,000 | 18,000 |

Carrying amount, December 31, Year 6 = **$18,000**.

**e. January 1, Year 7 — Disposal JE**

Proceeds $15,000 − BV $18,000 = **loss $3,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 15,000 | |
| Accumulated Amortization—Patent | 72,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Patent | 3,000 | |
| Patent | | 90,000 |

**Check:** Dr 15,000 + 72,000 + 3,000 = **90,000**; Cr = **90,000**. **Balanced.**

**f. Classification / disclosure (Year 5)**  
- **Type:** Change in accounting **estimate** (intangible useful life).  
- **Treatment:** Prospective; Years 1–4 amortization of $9,000/year left unchanged on comparative statements.  
- **Note elements (material multi-period change):**  
  - Nature of the change (revised patent economic life from 10 to 7 years total).  
  - Effect on income from continuing operations and net income for Year 5: amortization higher by $18,000 − $9,000 = **$9,000** (pretax income lower by $9,000).  
  - Related **EPS effect** (amount depends on shares outstanding — state computation if share count given).  
  - Statement that the change is applied **prospectively**.

**Key insight:** Finite-life intangibles use the same prospective estimate model as PPE: **remaining book value ÷ remaining life**. Method changes for amortization of intangibles are also treated as estimate changes effected by principle (prospective).

### Q5 — MC — Classification of accounting changes (estimate vs estimate-effected-by-principle vs principle vs error)
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Distinguish change in estimate, change in estimate effected by principle, voluntary change in principle, and error; identify prospective treatment  
**Question:**  
Which of the following is **correctly** matched with its GAAP category and accounting treatment?

- A) Change from LIFO to FIFO inventory costing → change in accounting estimate → prospective  
- B) Change in residual value of equipment → change in accounting principle → retrospective  
- C) Change from straight-line to sum-of-the-years’-digits depreciation → change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle → prospective  
- D) Discovery that last year’s depreciation used an arithmetic error understating expense → change in accounting estimate → prospective  

**Answer:** **C.**  
A change in **depreciation method** is inseparable from a revised pattern of benefits and is classified as a **change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle**, applied **prospectively** from the carrying amount at the change date (ASC 250-10-45-18).  

- **A is wrong:** LIFO→FIFO is a voluntary **change in accounting principle**, typically **retrospective** (LO 21-2), not an estimate change.  
- **B is wrong:** Residual value revision is a pure **change in estimate**, applied **prospectively**, not a principle change and not retrospective.  
- **D is wrong:** An arithmetic mistake is an **error correction** (prior-period adjustment / restatement if material and prior statements issued), not a good-faith estimate change.

### Q6 — MC — Period of income effect for a pure change in estimate
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Prospective application — no restatement; current and future periods only  
**Question:**  
On January 1, Year 1, Summit Co. purchased equipment for $200,000 with a 10-year life and $20,000 residual, straight-line. On January 1, Year 6, Summit revises residual to $10,000 and total life to 12 years. How should Summit account for this change in its Year 6 financial statements?

- A) Restate Years 1–5 depreciation as if the new life and residual had always been used, and adjust beginning retained earnings.  
- B) Record a cumulative catch-up depreciation expense in Year 6 equal to the difference between old and new cumulative depreciation through Year 5.  
- C) Leave prior years unchanged; compute Year 6 (and later) depreciation from the January 1, Year 6 carrying amount using the revised residual and remaining life of 7 years.  
- D) Disclose the change only; continue depreciating $18,000 per year until the asset is fully depreciated under the original plan.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Changes in accounting estimate are applied **prospectively** to the **period of change and future periods** (ASC 250-10-45-17). Carrying amount at January 1, Year 6 = $200,000 − 5 × [($200,000 − $20,000) ÷ 10] = $200,000 − $90,000 = **$110,000**. Remaining life = 12 − 5 = **7 years**. New annual depreciation = ($110,000 − $10,000) ÷ 7 = **$14,285.71** (or $100,000 ÷ 7). No restatement (A), no cumulative catch-up as a separate “correction” (B), and depreciation **must** be revised (D is wrong).
