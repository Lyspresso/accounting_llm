# Agent 50 — CORE DEMO — LO 9-3

**Chapter:** 9  
**LO title:** Demonstrate specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO in a periodic inventory system  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE** (periodic): Purchases and Accounts Payable/Cash at acquisition; no Inventory permanent account until period end
- Sales under periodic: revenue only at sale (no COGS until physical count / cost-flow allocation)
- Cost of goods available for sale (COGAS) schedule: BI + purchases
- **Specific identification** (periodic): EI and COGS from actual unit costs of identified lots
- **Average cost** (periodic): weighted-average unit cost × EI units and sold units
- **FIFO** (periodic): EI from most recent purchases; COGS from earliest layers
- **LIFO** (periodic): EI from earliest layers; COGS from most recent purchases
- **Period-end adjusting JE**: record EI, close Purchases and BI, plug COGS
- Rising-price comparison: FIFO highest EI / lowest COGS; LIFO lowest EI / highest COGS; average in between
- Classification/presentation: Inventory (BS), COGS (IS); method is a cost-flow assumption, not physical flow (except specific ID)

### Q1 — CORE — Periodic cost-flow methods: full cycle (initial recognition emphasis)
**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Initial purchase recognition under periodic; COGAS; EI and COGS under specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO; period-end adjusting JE (FIFO)  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Trail Supply Co.** uses a **periodic inventory system**. Fiscal month is **June**. Beginning inventory on June 1 consists of **200 units at $5.00** each (**$1,000**). The company does not use purchase discounts or returns this month.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| June 6 | Purchased **300 units at $6.00** on account (**$1,800**). |
| June 15 | Purchased **400 units at $7.00** on account (**$2,800**). |
| June 22 | Purchased **100 units at $8.00** on account (**$800**). |
| June (various) | Sold **600 units** on account for total sales revenue of **$9,000**. |
| June 30 | Physical inventory count: **400 units** on hand. |

For **specific identification**, management identifies ending inventory as: **50 units from June 1**, **100 units from June 6**, **150 units from June 15**, and **100 units from June 22**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare **initial recognition journal entries** for the three purchases and the aggregate sale entry (periodic).  
b. Prepare the **cost of goods available for sale** schedule (units and dollars).  
c. Compute **ending inventory** and **cost of goods sold** under each of: (1) specific identification, (2) average cost, (3) FIFO, (4) LIFO. Show layer detail for EI (and for COGS layers where helpful).  
d. Prepare the **June 30 period-end adjusting entry** assuming the company uses **FIFO**.  
e. State the June 30 **Inventory** (balance sheet) and **Cost of goods sold** (income statement) amounts under FIFO.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition and sale (periodic)**

*June 6 — Purchase (initial recognition)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 1,800 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 1,800 |
| *Record purchase at invoice cost; Inventory permanent account not updated in periodic* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,800.

*June 15 — Purchase*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 2,800 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 2,800 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 2,800.

*June 22 — Purchase*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 800 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 800 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 800.

*Purchases total:* $1,800 + $2,800 + $800 = **$5,400**.

*June (aggregate) — Sale (periodic: revenue only)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 9,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 9,000 |
| *No COGS or Inventory relief at sale under periodic* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 9,000.

**b. Cost of goods available for sale**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| June 1 beginning inventory | 200 | $5.00 | $1,000 |
| June 6 purchase | 300 | 6.00 | 1,800 |
| June 15 purchase | 400 | 7.00 | 2,800 |
| June 22 purchase | 100 | 8.00 | 800 |
| **Goods available for sale** | **1,000** | | **$6,400** |

Units sold = 600; units in EI = 1,000 − 600 = **400**.  
COGAS check: BI $1,000 + Purchases $5,400 = **$6,400**.

**c. Ending inventory and COGS — four methods**

**(1) Specific identification — Ending inventory**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| June 1 | 50 | $5.00 | $250 |
| June 6 | 100 | 6.00 | 600 |
| June 15 | 150 | 7.00 | 1,050 |
| June 22 | 100 | 8.00 | 800 |
| **EI** | **400** | | **$2,700** |

COGS = COGAS − EI = $6,400 − $2,700 = **$3,700**.

*(Optional sold-layer check)*  
June 1: 150 × $5 = $750; June 6: 200 × $6 = $1,200; June 15: 250 × $7 = $1,750; June 22: 0.  
Total sold 600; COGS = 750 + 1,200 + 1,750 = **$3,700**.

**(2) Average cost (weighted average, periodic)**

Weighted-average unit cost = $6,400 ÷ 1,000 units = **$6.40** per unit.

| | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Ending inventory | 400 × $6.40 | **$2,560** |
| Cost of goods sold | 600 × $6.40 | **$3,840** |

Check: $2,560 + $3,840 = $6,400.

**(3) FIFO — Ending inventory (most recent layers first)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| June 22 | 100 | $8.00 | $800 |
| June 15 | 300 | 7.00 | 2,100 |
| **EI** | **400** | | **$2,900** |

COGS = $6,400 − $2,900 = **$3,500**.

*(COGS layers — earliest first)*  
June 1: 200 × $5 = $1,000; June 6: 300 × $6 = $1,800; June 15: 100 × $7 = $700.  
Total 600; COGS = **$3,500**.

**(4) LIFO — Ending inventory (earliest layers first)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| June 1 | 200 | $5.00 | $1,000 |
| June 6 | 200 | 6.00 | 1,200 |
| **EI** | **400** | | **$2,200** |

COGS = $6,400 − $2,200 = **$4,200**.

*(COGS layers — most recent first)*  
June 22: 100 × $8 = $800; June 15: 400 × $7 = $2,800; June 6: 100 × $6 = $600.  
Total 600; COGS = **$4,200**.

**Summary (rising unit costs)**

| Method | Ending inventory | COGS |
|---|---:|---:|
| Specific identification | $2,700 | $3,700 |
| Average cost | 2,560 | 3,840 |
| FIFO | **2,900** (highest EI) | **3,500** (lowest COGS) |
| LIFO | **2,200** (lowest EI) | **4,200** (highest COGS) |

**d. June 30 — Period-end adjusting entry (FIFO)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 2,900 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 3,500 | |
| Purchases | | 5,400 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 1,000 |
| *Close Purchases and BI; record EI at FIFO; residual = COGS* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,900 + 3,500 = **6,400**; Cr 5,400 + 1,000 = **6,400**. Balanced.  
Also: EI + COGS = BI + Purchases = COGAS.

**e. Presentation (FIFO)**  
- Balance sheet — Inventory (June 30): **$2,900**  
- Income statement — Cost of goods sold: **$3,500**

**Key insight:** In a **periodic** system, purchases are initially recognized in a temporary **Purchases** account (not perpetual Inventory). Cost-flow methods (specific ID, average, FIFO, LIFO) allocate **COGAS** between EI and COGS **after** the physical count. With rising prices, FIFO assigns newer costs to EI and older costs to COGS; LIFO does the reverse.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Periodic four methods twin
**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin of periodic specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO with initial purchase JEs and period-end COGS entry  
**Scenario:**  
**Pinecrest Wholesale Ltd.** uses a **periodic inventory system**. Fiscal month is **October**. Beginning inventory on October 1: **150 units at $20** each (**$3,000**).

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Oct 4 | Purchased **250 units at $22** cash (**$5,500**). |
| Oct 14 | Purchased **350 units at $24** on account (**$8,400**). |
| Oct 25 | Purchased **50 units at $26** on account (**$1,300**). |
| Oct (various) | Sold **500 units** on account for total sales of **$18,500**. |
| Oct 31 | Physical count: **300 units** on hand. |

**Specific identification** of ending inventory: **40 units from Oct 1**, **80 units from Oct 4**, **130 units from Oct 14**, and **50 units from Oct 25**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare journal entries for the three purchases and the aggregate sale.  
b. Compute COGAS (units and dollars).  
c. Compute EI and COGS under specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO.  
d. Prepare the October 31 period-end adjusting entry under **LIFO**.  
e. Briefly rank EI amounts from highest to lowest and relate the ranking to the price trend.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Journal entries**

*Oct 4 — Purchase for cash*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 5,500 | |
| Cash | | 5,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 5,500.

*Oct 14 — Purchase on account*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 8,400 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 8,400 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 8,400.

*Oct 25 — Purchase on account*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 1,300 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 1,300 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,300.

Purchases total = $5,500 + $8,400 + $1,300 = **$15,200**.

*Sale (periodic)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 18,500 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 18,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 18,500.

**b. COGAS**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct 1 beginning | 150 | $20 | $3,000 |
| Oct 4 | 250 | 22 | 5,500 |
| Oct 14 | 350 | 24 | 8,400 |
| Oct 25 | 50 | 26 | 1,300 |
| **Available** | **800** | | **$18,200** |

EI units = 800 − 500 = **300**. COGAS = $3,000 + $15,200 = **$18,200**.

**c. Four methods**

**(1) Specific identification — EI**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct 1 | 40 | $20 | $800 |
| Oct 4 | 80 | 22 | 1,760 |
| Oct 14 | 130 | 24 | 3,120 |
| Oct 25 | 50 | 26 | 1,300 |
| **EI** | **300** | | **$6,980** |

COGS = $18,200 − $6,980 = **$11,220**.

**(2) Average cost**  
WA unit cost = $18,200 ÷ 800 = **$22.75**.  
EI = 300 × $22.75 = **$6,825**.  
COGS = 500 × $22.75 = **$11,375**.  
Check: $6,825 + $11,375 = $18,200.

**(3) FIFO — EI (most recent)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct 25 | 50 | $26 | $1,300 |
| Oct 14 | 250 | 24 | 6,000 |
| **EI** | **300** | | **$7,300** |

COGS = $18,200 − $7,300 = **$10,900**.  
*(COGS layers)* 150 × $20 + 250 × $22 + 100 × $24 = $3,000 + $5,500 + $2,400 = **$10,900**.

**(4) LIFO — EI (earliest)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct 1 | 150 | $20 | $3,000 |
| Oct 4 | 150 | 22 | 3,300 |
| **EI** | **300** | | **$6,300** |

COGS = $18,200 − $6,300 = **$11,900**.  
*(COGS layers)* 50 × $26 + 350 × $24 + 100 × $22 = $1,300 + $8,400 + $2,200 = **$11,900**.

**Summary**

| Method | EI | COGS |
|---|---:|---:|
| Specific identification | $6,980 | $11,220 |
| Average cost | 6,825 | 11,375 |
| FIFO | 7,300 | 10,900 |
| LIFO | 6,300 | 11,900 |

**d. Oct 31 — Period-end adjusting entry (LIFO)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 6,300 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 11,900 | |
| Purchases | | 15,200 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 3,000 |
| *LIFO allocation of COGAS; close temporary Purchases and BI* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,300 + 11,900 = **18,200**; Cr 15,200 + 3,000 = **18,200**. Balanced.

**e. Ranking and price trend**  
Unit costs are **rising** ($20 → $26). EI ranking highest to lowest: **FIFO ($7,300) > specific ID ($6,980) > average ($6,825) > LIFO ($6,300)**. FIFO matches newest costs to inventory; LIFO leaves oldest costs in inventory. Specific identification depends on which lots remain and need not fall between FIFO and LIFO in every fact pattern, but average cost typically falls between FIFO and LIFO when prices trend monotonically.

**Key insight:** Changing only the unit costs and quantities preserves the same mechanics: build COGAS, apply each cost-flow rule to the physical count, then close Purchases and BI into EI and COGS with one balanced adjusting entry.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end LIFO vs FIFO adjusting entries, comparison schedule, presentation
**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JEs under FIFO and LIFO from the same COGAS; subsequent comparison schedule; BS/IS presentation and rising-price effects  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedar Bend Components Inc.** uses a **periodic inventory system**. For **November**, the company already recorded purchases totaling **$14,400** in the Purchases account. Beginning inventory on November 1 was **$3,600**. No purchase returns, allowances, freight-in, or discounts apply.

Unit data (same for both methods):

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nov 1 beginning inventory | 120 | $30 | $3,600 |
| Nov 8 purchase | 180 | 32 | 5,760 |
| Nov 18 purchase | 200 | 34 | 6,800 |
| Nov 27 purchase | 50 | 36.80 | 1,840 |
| **Available** | **550** | | **$18,000** |

Physical count on November 30: **180 units** remaining (thus **370 units** sold). Sales revenue for November was **$22,200** (already recorded; no COGS entry yet).

**Required:**  
a. Compute ending inventory and COGS under **FIFO** and under **LIFO** (show layers).  
b. Prepare the **November 30 period-end adjusting entry** under FIFO.  
c. Prepare the **November 30 period-end adjusting entry** under LIFO (as if LIFO were the elected method instead).  
d. Prepare a **comparison schedule** of Inventory (BS) and COGS (IS) under FIFO vs LIFO, and compute the difference in pretax income (ignore taxes).  
e. State which method reports higher pretax income when costs are rising, and why (cost-flow assumption, not physical flow).

**Answer key:**  

**a. FIFO and LIFO allocations**

**FIFO — Ending inventory (most recent 180 units)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nov 27 | 50 | $36.80 | $1,840 |
| Nov 18 | 130 | 34.00 | 4,420 |
| **EI** | **180** | | **$6,260** |

COGS = $18,000 − $6,260 = **$11,740**.  
*(COGS check — earliest)* 120 × $30 + 180 × $32 + 70 × $34 = $3,600 + $5,760 + $2,380 = **$11,740**.

**LIFO — Ending inventory (earliest 180 units)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nov 1 | 120 | $30 | $3,600 |
| Nov 8 | 60 | 32 | 1,920 |
| **EI** | **180** | | **$5,520** |

COGS = $18,000 − $5,520 = **$12,480**.  
*(COGS check — most recent)* 50 × $36.80 + 200 × $34 + 120 × $32 = $1,840 + $6,800 + $3,840 = **$12,480**.

**b. Nov 30 adjusting entry — FIFO**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 6,260 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 11,740 | |
| Purchases | | 14,400 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 3,600 |

**Check:** Dr 6,260 + 11,740 = **18,000**; Cr 14,400 + 3,600 = **18,000**. Balanced.

**c. Nov 30 adjusting entry — LIFO**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 5,520 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 12,480 | |
| Purchases | | 14,400 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 3,600 |

**Check:** Dr 5,520 + 12,480 = **18,000**; Cr 14,400 + 3,600 = **18,000**. Balanced.

**d. Comparison schedule**

| | FIFO | LIFO | Difference (FIFO − LIFO) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Inventory (balance sheet) | $6,260 | $5,520 | $740 higher under FIFO |
| Cost of goods sold | 11,740 | 12,480 | $740 lower under FIFO |
| Pretax income effect (Sales $22,200 − COGS) | Gross profit $10,460 | Gross profit $9,720 | **$740 higher pretax income under FIFO** |

Reconciliation: FIFO EI − LIFO EI = $6,260 − $5,520 = **$740** = LIFO COGS − FIFO COGS.

**e. Rising costs and presentation**  
When unit costs are **rising**, **FIFO** reports **higher ending inventory**, **lower COGS**, and **higher pretax income** than LIFO because older, cheaper costs flow to COGS. LIFO reports higher COGS and lower income (tax-deferral motivation under the LIFO conformity rule in practice). These are **cost-flow assumptions**; physical movement of goods often follows FIFO even when the company elects LIFO for accounting.

**Key insight:** The period-end adjusting entry has the same structure under every method—only the **split** between EI and COGS changes. The difference in EI between FIFO and LIFO equals the difference in COGS (and pretax income) for the period.

### Q4 — MC — Method choice under rising prices (periodic)
**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Classification of cost-flow method effects on EI and COGS when acquisition costs are rising  
**Question:**  
A company uses a **periodic inventory system**. Acquisition costs of inventory **rose steadily** during the year. Ending unit count is positive and the same under all methods. Which method produces the **highest ending inventory** and the **lowest cost of goods sold**?

- A) LIFO  
- B) Average cost  
- C) FIFO  
- D) Specific identification (always, regardless of which lots remain)

**Answer:** **C.** Under rising prices, FIFO assigns the **most recent (highest)** costs to ending inventory and the **earliest (lowest)** costs to COGS, so EI is highest and COGS is lowest. LIFO does the opposite. Average cost falls between FIFO and LIFO. Specific identification depends on which actual lots remain and is **not always** highest EI.

### Q5 — MC — Periodic system recognition timing
**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Classification of when COGS is measured under periodic cost-flow assumptions  
**Question:**  
In a **periodic** inventory system that applies FIFO (or LIFO, average cost, or specific identification) to allocate costs, when is cost of goods sold first measured and recorded as a formal allocation of COGAS?

- A) At the moment each sale is made, by relieving Inventory and debiting COGS  
- B) Continuously, using a moving-average after every purchase and sale  
- C) At period end, after the physical count, when COGAS is allocated between EI and COGS  
- D) Only when inventory is written down under lower of cost or NRV

**Answer:** **C.** Periodic systems defer the allocation until **period end**. After the physical count determines EI units, the elected cost-flow method values EI and residual (or layer-built) COGS. Perpetual systems (LO 9-4 territory) update Inventory and COGS at each sale; that is not the periodic model.
