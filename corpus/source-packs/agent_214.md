# Agent 214 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-7

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Account for impairment of property, plant, and equipment  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Indicators only:** PPE held for use is tested when events/circumstances suggest carrying amount may not be recoverable (not a routine annual revaluation)
- **Step 1 — Recoverability test:** compare **undiscounted** expected future net cash inflows (recoverable cost) to carrying amount; proceed to measurement only if recoverable cost **<** carrying amount
- **Step 2 — Impairment measurement:** loss = carrying amount − **fair value** (not recoverable cost)
- **Initial recognition JE (held for use):** Debit **Loss on Impairment**, Credit **Accumulated Depreciation** (Hanlon / Demo 12-7); gross cost remains; fair value becomes **new cost basis**
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** depreciate new carrying amount over **revised remaining useful life** (revised residual as given); schedule must roll forward year by year
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** post-impairment depreciation (and year-end impairment when the indicator is assessed at reporting date)
- **Disposal after impairment:** remove gross cost and accumulated depreciation; recognize gain/loss vs proceeds
- **No restoration** of a previously recognized impairment loss for assets **held for use**
- **Presentation / disclosure:** impairment loss in **income from continuing operations** (other expenses); notes describe the asset, circumstances, amount, and fair-value method

### Q1 — CORE — Northridge Packaging: full cycle with post-impairment depreciation schedule
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Recoverability → impairment initial recognition; **subsequent multi-year depreciation schedule** on new cost basis; period-end dep JE; disposal after impairment  
**Scenario:**  
**Northridge Packaging Co.** (calendar year) owns a specialized bagging line used in operations (**held for use**).

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Original cost (purchased and installed **January 1, Year 1**) | **$1,800,000** |
| Original useful life / residual | **15 years**, residual **$150,000** |
| Depreciation method | Straight-line |
| **January 1, Year 7** | Competitor launches a superior product; demand for Northridge’s related product falls sharply — **impairment indicator** |
| Estimated remaining undiscounted future net cash inflows (use + eventual disposal) | **$720,000** |
| Fair value of equipment (January 1, Year 7) | **$480,000** |
| Revised remaining useful life / residual after write-down | **6 years**, residual **$0** |

On **December 31, Year 9** (after three full years of post-impairment depreciation), Northridge sells the equipment for **$220,000** cash. Assume no further impairment indicators after Year 7 and no change to residual/life after the write-down.

**Required:**  
a. Compute carrying amount immediately before the impairment analysis. Perform the **recoverability test** and, if required, measure the **impairment loss**.  
b. Prepare the **January 1, Year 7** journal entry to initially recognize any impairment loss (textbook accumulated-depreciation credit convention). State the post-impairment carrying amount (new cost basis).  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (depreciation) schedule** for the six years after impairment (Years 7–12 planned), showing beginning CV, depreciation, ending CV, and cumulative accumulated depreciation. Record the **December 31, Year 7** depreciation adjusting entry.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 9** disposal journal entry (after recording Year 9 depreciation).  
e. State how the Year 7 impairment loss is **classified** on the income statement and whether a later recovery of fair value (while still held for use) may reverse the impairment.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Carrying amount, recoverability, measurement**

Annual depreciation (original):  
\[
\frac{\$1{,}800{,}000 - \$150{,}000}{15} = \$110{,}000
\]

Accumulated depreciation after 6 years (end of Year 6 / start of Year 7):  
\(6 \times \$110{,}000 = \mathbf{\$660{,}000}\)  
Carrying amount: \(\$1{,}800{,}000 - \$660{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}140{,}000}\)

**Recoverability test (Step 1):**  
Recoverable cost **$720,000** < carrying amount **$1,140,000** → asset is **not recoverable** → proceed to Step 2.

**Impairment measurement (Step 2):**  
\[
\text{Impairment loss} = \$1{,}140{,}000 - \$480{,}000 = \mathbf{\$660{,}000}
\]

**b. Initial recognition JE — January 1, Year 7**

*To record loss on impairment of equipment held for use*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 660,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 660,000 |

**Check:** Dr 660,000 = Cr 660,000. **Balanced.**

Post-impairment balances:  
- Equipment (gross cost) remains **$1,800,000**  
- Accumulated depreciation: \(660{,}000 + 660{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}320{,}000}\)  
- **Carrying amount / new cost basis** = \(1{,}800{,}000 - 1{,}320{,}000 = \mathbf{\$480{,}000}\) (= fair value)

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule and Year 7 adjusting entry**

New depreciable base = **$480,000**; residual **$0**; remaining life **6 years**.  
Annual depreciation = \(480{,}000 / 6 = \mathbf{\$80{,}000}\)

| Year | Beginning CV | Depreciation | Ending CV | Ending AD (from original cost) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 480,000 | 80,000 | 400,000 | 1,400,000 |
| 8 | 400,000 | 80,000 | 320,000 | 1,480,000 |
| 9 | 320,000 | 80,000 | 240,000 | 1,560,000 |
| 10 | 240,000 | 80,000 | 160,000 | 1,640,000 |
| 11 | 160,000 | 80,000 | 80,000 | 1,720,000 |
| 12 | 80,000 | 80,000 | 0 | 1,800,000 |

*Schedule roll-forward check:* ending AD Year 12 = original cost → CV = 0. Post-impairment depreciation total \(6 \times 80{,}000 = 480{,}000\) equals new cost basis.

*December 31, Year 7 — Depreciation (post-impairment basis)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 80,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 80,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 80,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Disposal — December 31, Year 9**

After Year 9 depreciation: CV = **$240,000**; AD = **$1,560,000**.  
Cash proceeds **$220,000** → loss on disposal = \(240{,}000 - 220{,}000 = \mathbf{\$20{,}000}\)

*December 31, Year 9 — Dispose of equipment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 220,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 1,560,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 20,000 | |
| Equipment | | 1,800,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(220{,}000 + 1{,}560{,}000 + 20{,}000 = 1{,}800{,}000\) = Cr 1,800,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification and restoration**  
- **Income statement:** Loss on Impairment **$660,000** is a component of **income from continuing operations** (typically other expenses).  
- **Restoration:** **Prohibited** for assets held for use. A later increase in fair value does **not** reverse the write-down; the new cost basis continues to be depreciated.

**Key insight:** After the write-down, **fair value becomes the new cost basis**. Exam work often centers on building the **subsequent depreciation schedule** from that new basis over the revised life—not merely computing the loss once.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Copperleaf Dairy: twin with revised residual after impairment
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — recoverability, impairment JE, **subsequent measurement schedule** with nonzero residual, period-end dep JE, disposal  
**Scenario:**  
**Copperleaf Dairy Corp.** (calendar year) uses packaging equipment held for use.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Original cost (**January 1, Year 1**) | **$960,000** |
| Original useful life / residual | **10 years**, residual **$60,000** |
| Depreciation method | Straight-line |
| **January 1, Year 5** | Regulatory packaging-standard change sharply reduces expected use — **impairment indicator** |
| Estimated remaining undiscounted future net cash inflows | **$420,000** |
| Fair value (January 1, Year 5) | **$300,000** |
| Revised remaining useful life / residual after write-down | **5 years**, residual **$0** |

On **December 31, Year 6** (after two full years of post-impairment depreciation), Copperleaf sells the equipment for **$165,000** cash. No further impairment indicators after Year 5.

**Required:**  
a. Compute pre-impairment carrying amount; perform recoverability and (if needed) measure impairment.  
b. Record the January 1, Year 5 impairment JE; state new cost basis.  
c. Prepare the **subsequent depreciation schedule** for Years 5–9 (planned remaining life). Record December 31, Year 5 depreciation.  
d. Record the December 31, Year 6 disposal JE (after Year 6 depreciation).

**Answer key:**  

**a.**  
Annual depreciation (original):  
\[
\frac{\$960{,}000 - \$60{,}000}{10} = \$90{,}000
\]  
Accumulated depreciation after 4 years: \(4 \times \$90{,}000 = \mathbf{\$360{,}000}\)  
Carrying amount: \(\$960{,}000 - \$360{,}000 = \mathbf{\$600{,}000}\)

**Recoverability:** recoverable cost **$420,000** < CA **$600,000** → not recoverable → Step 2.  
**Impairment loss:** \(\$600{,}000 - \$300{,}000 = \mathbf{\$300{,}000}\)

**b. January 1, Year 5 — Impairment**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 300,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 300,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 300,000. **Balanced.**

Post-impairment: gross cost **$960,000**; AD \(360{,}000 + 300{,}000 = \mathbf{\$660{,}000}\); **new cost basis / CV = $300,000**.

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule**

Annual depreciation post-impairment: \(300{,}000 / 5 = \mathbf{\$60{,}000}\)

| Year | Beginning CV | Depreciation | Ending CV | Ending AD |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 300,000 | 60,000 | 240,000 | 720,000 |
| 6 | 240,000 | 60,000 | 180,000 | 780,000 |
| 7 | 180,000 | 60,000 | 120,000 | 840,000 |
| 8 | 120,000 | 60,000 | 60,000 | 900,000 |
| 9 | 60,000 | 60,000 | 0 | 960,000 |

*December 31, Year 5 — Depreciation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 60,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 60,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 60,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Disposal — December 31, Year 6**

After Year 6 depreciation: CV = **$180,000**; AD = **$780,000**.  
Proceeds **$165,000** → loss on disposal = \(180{,}000 - 165{,}000 = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 165,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 780,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 15,000 | |
| Equipment | | 960,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(165{,}000 + 780{,}000 + 15{,}000 = 960{,}000\) = Cr 960,000. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Changing every input (cost, life, residual, recoverable cost, fair value, timing of sale) still follows the same pipeline: **CA → recoverability (undiscounted) → loss (CA − FV) → new-basis depreciation schedule → disposal**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — multi-asset recoverability screen + year-end impairment + post-write-down schedule
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Period-end multi-asset recoverability (pass vs fail); combined impairment JE only for failed assets; **subsequent measurement schedule** with nonzero residual on the written-down asset  
**Scenario:**  
At **December 31, Year 3**, **Redwood Circuits LLC** identifies impairment indicators for three plant assets **currently used in operations**. Management provides:

| Asset | Cost | Accumulated depreciation | Recoverable cost (undiscounted) | Fair value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A — Precision press | $400,000 | $100,000 | $350,000 | $280,000 |
| B — Curing oven | $700,000 | $200,000 | $420,000 | $310,000 |
| C — Assembly conveyor | $900,000 | $150,000 | $750,000 | $600,000 |

After any required write-down, Asset B will be used **5 more years** with a revised residual of **$10,000** (straight-line). Assets A and C continue under their pre-existing policies (no change requested here).

**Required:**  
a. For each asset, compute carrying amount, apply the **recoverability test**, and state any **impairment loss**. Assume indicators are present for all three.  
b. Record a **single December 31, Year 3** journal entry for all impairment losses required (if any).  
c. For **Asset B only**, prepare the **subsequent measurement schedule** of depreciation for the five years after the write-down (Years 4–8). Record the December 31, Year 4 depreciation entry for Asset B.  
d. Briefly explain why an asset with fair value **below** carrying amount might still have **no** impairment loss.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Asset-by-asset analysis**

| Asset | CA (Cost − AD) | Recoverability (RC ? CA) | Impaired? | Loss = CA − FV (if impaired) |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| A | $300,000 | $350,000 **>** $300,000 | **No** (recoverable) | **$0** |
| B | $500,000 | $420,000 **<** $500,000 | **Yes** | \(500{,}000 - 310{,}000 = \mathbf{\$190{,}000}\) |
| C | $750,000 | $750,000 **=** $750,000 | **No** (recoverable; RC not **less than** CA) | **$0** |

Only Asset B fails recoverability. Note Asset C: FV $600,000 < CA $750,000, but **no loss** because the recoverability test does not fail.

**b. December 31, Year 3 — Impairment (Asset B only)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 190,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment (B) | | 190,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 190,000. **Balanced.**

Post-impairment Asset B: cost **$700,000**; AD \(200{,}000 + 190{,}000 = \mathbf{\$390{,}000}\); **CV / new cost basis = $310,000**.

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — Asset B**

Depreciable base after impairment: \(310{,}000 - 10{,}000 = \mathbf{\$300{,}000}\)  
Annual depreciation: \(300{,}000 / 5 = \mathbf{\$60{,}000}\)

| Year | Beginning CV | Depreciation | Ending CV | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 4 | 310,000 | 60,000 | 250,000 | |
| 5 | 250,000 | 60,000 | 190,000 | |
| 6 | 190,000 | 60,000 | 130,000 | |
| 7 | 130,000 | 60,000 | 70,000 | |
| 8 | 70,000 | 60,000 | 10,000 | Ends at residual |

*Schedule check:* total post-impairment depreciation \(5 \times 60{,}000 = 300{,}000\); ending CV = residual **$10,000**.

*December 31, Year 4 — Depreciation, Asset B*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 60,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment (B) | | 60,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 60,000. **Balanced.**

**d.** Recoverability uses **undiscounted** expected future net cash inflows. If recoverable cost ≥ carrying amount, GAAP **stops**—no impairment is measured—even when fair value is below carrying amount. Only clearly unrecovered assets move to the fair-value write-down.

**Key insight:** The recoverability gate is stricter than a pure FV < CA rule. Multi-asset packs train students to **screen first**, write down only failed assets, then **roll a clean subsequent depreciation schedule** on the new basis (including residual).

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Recoverability uses undiscounted cash flows; impairment loss measured with fair value  
**Question:** For PPE **held for use**, an impairment indicator is present. Which statement is correct under U.S. GAAP (ASC 360)?

- A) Compare discounted future cash flows to carrying amount; if lower, measure loss as carrying amount minus recoverable cost.  
- B) Compare **undiscounted** expected future net cash inflows to carrying amount; if lower, measure loss as carrying amount minus **fair value**.  
- C) Always write the asset down to fair value whenever fair value is below carrying amount, regardless of expected undiscounted cash flows.  
- D) Measure loss as carrying amount minus residual value, then reverse the loss in a later year if fair value recovers.

**Answer:** **B.** Step 1 (recoverability) uses **undiscounted** future net cash inflows; Step 2 measures the loss as **CA − fair value**. Discounting is used (when needed) to **estimate fair value**, not as the recoverability hurdle. Restoration is prohibited for held-for-use assets; recoverable cost is not the measurement attribute for the loss.

### Q5 — MC (method / subsequent basis)
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** New cost basis after impairment; no restoration; subsequent depreciation  
**Question:** On January 1, equipment held for use is written down for impairment from a carrying amount of $500,000 to fair value of $320,000. Remaining life is 4 years; residual is $0. Two years later, fair value has risen to $400,000 and the asset is still held for use. Which is correct?

- A) Restore $80,000 of the prior impairment so carrying amount equals $400,000.  
- B) Continue depreciating the **$320,000** new cost basis; annual depreciation is **$80,000**; no restoration of the prior impairment.  
- C) Stop depreciating until fair value falls again.  
- D) Recompute depreciation as if the original pre-impairment carrying amount still applied.

**Answer:** **B.** Fair value at write-down becomes the **new cost basis** ($320,000), depreciated over remaining life (\(320{,}000/4 = \$80{,}000\) per year). **Restoration of a previously recognized impairment loss is prohibited** for assets held for use.
