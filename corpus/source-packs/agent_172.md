# Agent 172 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-3

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Account for the impact of sales returns and allowances  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Sales returns and allowances as **variable consideration** (ASC 606) that reduce the transaction price / Net Sales
- **Sales Returns** (contra revenue) vs **Sales Allowances** (contra revenue; customer keeps merchandise)
- Initial recognition of credit sales and COGS (perpetual inventory)
- Actual return: sales-side (Dr Sales Returns) + cost-side (Dr Inventory / Cr COGS)
- Sales allowance: **sales-side only** (no inventory restore)
- Period-end estimate: Dr Sales Returns / Cr **Refund Liability**; Dr **Inventory—Estimated Returns** / Cr COGS (or reverse when true-up reduces the estimate)
- First-year remaining estimate = total estimated returns − actual returns already recorded
- Subsequent-year practice: charge actual returns to Sales Returns; **true-up** Refund Liability so ending balance = expected remaining returns on **current-year** sales
- **Refund Liability rollforward schedule** and Inventory—Estimated Returns rollforward (subsequent measurement)
- Settlement of prior-period estimate: Dr Refund Liability (not Sales Returns) when a return was already accrued
- Presentation: Net Sales; Refund Liability (current liability); Inventory—Estimated Returns (current asset)

### Q1 — CORE — Aspen Ridge Sporting Goods: two-year sales returns cycle with RL schedule
**LO:** LO 8-3  
**Concept:** subsequent_measurement_schedule — Refund Liability / Inventory—Estimated Returns rollforward across first year and subsequent year; initial_recognition_JE; period_end_adjusting_JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Aspen Ridge Sporting Goods Inc.** sells outdoor equipment on account and uses a **perpetual** inventory system. Merchandise cost is consistently **65%** of selling price. Customers may return goods for **cash** refunds. Aspen Ridge estimates returns from experience and expected conditions.

**Year 1 (first year of operations — no beginning Refund Liability):**

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------:|
| Credit sales | **$640,000** |
| Cost of goods sold on those sales (65%) | **$416,000** |
| Actual cash refunds for merchandise returns | **$11,200** |
| Sales allowances (customers keep goods; credit to AR) | **$1,600** |
| Estimated **total** returns for Year 1 (at YE) | **3%** of credit sales |

**Year 2 (subsequent-period approach — Review 8-3 style):**  
Aspen Ridge records **all actual returns during Year 2 as if they arise from the current year** (debit Sales Returns), then adjusts Refund Liability at December 31 so the **ending** balance equals estimated returns on **Year 2** sales still expected to reverse next period. Assume return rates are reasonably consistent with management’s estimates on average.

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------:|
| Credit sales | **$780,000** |
| Cost of goods sold (65%) | **$507,000** |
| Actual cash refunds (recorded as current-year Sales Returns) | **$24,000** |
| Year-end estimate of returns related to Year 2 sales | **3%** of Year 2 credit sales |

**Required:**  
(a) Record Year 1 entries for credit sales and COGS.  
(b) Record Year 1 actual sales returns (sales-side and cost-side) and the sales allowance.  
(c) Compute total estimated Year 1 returns and the remaining estimate. Record December 31, Year 1 adjusting entries (sales-side and cost-side).  
(d) Prepare a **Refund Liability rollforward for Year 1** and compute Year 1 Net Sales.  
(e) Record Year 2 sales and COGS; record Year 2 actual returns (both sides).  
(f) Compute the **target ending Refund Liability** at December 31, Year 2 and the year-end true-up entries (sales-side and cost-side).  
(g) Prepare full **Year 2 subsequent-measurement schedules** for Refund Liability and Inventory—Estimated Returns. Confirm ending Inv—Est Returns = ending Refund Liability × 65%.  
(h) Compute Year 2 Net Sales under the main method.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Year 1 — sales and COGS**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 640,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 640,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 416,000 | |
| Inventory | | 416,000 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. \(640{,}000 \times 0.65 = 416{,}000\).

**(b) Year 1 — actual returns and allowances**

Sales-side returns (cash refunds):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 11,200 | |
| Cash | | 11,200 |

Cost-side: \(11{,}200 \times 0.65 = \mathbf{\$7{,}280}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 7,280 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 7,280 |

Sales allowances (customer keeps goods — **no** inventory entry):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Allowances | 1,600 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 1,600 |

*Check:* All entries balance.

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — estimated returns**

Total estimated returns = \(0.03 \times 640{,}000 = \mathbf{\$19{,}200}\).  
Remaining estimate (ending Refund Liability) = \(19{,}200 - 11{,}200 = \mathbf{\$8{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 8,000 | |
| Refund Liability | | 8,000 |

Cost of remaining estimated returns = \(8{,}000 \times 0.65 = \mathbf{\$5{,}200}\)  
(equivalently: \(19{,}200 \times 0.65 = 12{,}480\) total estimated cost − \(7{,}280\) inventory already restored).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | 5,200 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 5,200 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. Combined Year 1 Sales Returns = \(11{,}200 + 8{,}000 = 19{,}200\).

**(d) Year 1 schedule and Net Sales**

**Refund Liability rollforward — Year 1**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Refund Liability | $0 |
| + YE estimate plug (remaining) | 8,000 |
| − Actual returns charged to RL during year* | 0 |
| **Ending Refund Liability** | **$8,000** |

\*Under Demo 8-3 first-year practice, actual returns debit **Sales Returns** (not Refund Liability); the YE plug sets RL to remaining estimated returns.

Identity: ending RL = total estimated returns − actual returns = \(19{,}200 - 11{,}200 = 8{,}000\).  
Ending Inventory—Estimated Returns = **$5,200**.

**Net Sales — Year 1**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales Revenue | $640,000 |
| − Sales Returns (\(11{,}200 + 8{,}000\)) | (19,200) |
| − Sales Allowances | (1,600) |
| **Net Sales** | **$619,200** |

**(e) Year 2 — sales, COGS, actual returns**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 780,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 780,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 507,000 | |
| Inventory | | 507,000 |

*Check:* \(780{,}000 \times 0.65 = 507{,}000\).

Actual returns:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 24,000 | |
| Cash | | 24,000 |

Cost-side: \(24{,}000 \times 0.65 = \mathbf{\$15{,}600}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 15,600 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 15,600 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. Under the main method, Refund Liability is **not** reduced when actual returns are recorded; the YE true-up recalibrates it.

**(f) December 31, Year 2 — true-up**

Estimated returns on Year 2 sales = \(0.03 \times 780{,}000 = \mathbf{\$23{,}400}\).

Target ending Refund Liability (Review 8-3 identity):  
\[
\begin{align*}
\text{Ending RL} &= \text{Beg RL} - \text{Actual returns} + \text{Est. on current-year sales} \\
&= 8{,}000 - 24{,}000 + 23{,}400 = \mathbf{\$7{,}400}
\end{align*}
\]

Adjusting plug to Refund Liability = \(7{,}400 - 8{,}000 = \mathbf{-\$600}\)  
(equivalently: \(23{,}400 - 24{,}000 = -600\)).

Because actual returns charged to Sales Returns **exceed** the estimate on current-year sales relative to the needed ending balance, **reduce** the liability:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Refund Liability | 600 | |
| Sales Returns | | 600 |

Cost-side plug: \(600 \times 0.65 = \mathbf{\$390}\) (reduce Inventory—Estimated Returns):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 390 | |
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | | 390 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. Net Year 2 Sales Returns (main method) = \(24{,}000 - 600 = 23{,}400\) (= estimate on current-year sales).

**(g) Subsequent measurement schedules — Year 2**

**Refund Liability rollforward — Year 2**

| Date / component | Debit (decrease) | Credit (increase) | Balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Year 2 beginning | | | 8,000 |
| Actual returns (not charged to RL under main method) | — | — | 8,000 |
| Dec 31 YE true-up (plug) | 600 | | **7,400** |
| **Memo reconciling identity** | Actual 24,000 | Est. on Y2 sales 23,400 | Beg 8,000 − 24,000 + 23,400 = **7,400** |

**Inventory—Estimated Returns rollforward — Year 2**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Inventory—Estimated Returns | $5,200 |
| − YE cost-side true-up (credit to asset) | (390) |
| **Ending Inventory—Estimated Returns** | **$4,810** |

Confirm: ending Refund Liability × cost % = \(7{,}400 \times 0.65 = \mathbf{\$4{,}810}\). ✓

**(h) Net Sales — Year 2**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales Revenue | $780,000 |
| − Sales Returns (\(24{,}000 - 600\)) | (23,400) |
| **Net Sales** | **$756,600** |

**Key insight:** The subsequent-measurement schedule is the control account for variable-consideration refunds. When actual returns are booked entirely to Sales Returns, the year-end plug **forces** ending Refund Liability to equal remaining expected refunds on **current-year** sales: Beg RL − actual + estimate on current sales. A negative plug (Dr Refund Liability / Cr Sales Returns) is normal when actuals exceed the incremental amount needed for the target ending balance.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Brightwater Pet Supply: first-year twin
**LO:** LO 8-3  
**Concept:** initial_recognition_JE and period_end_adjusting_JE (number-variant twin — first-year remaining estimate)  
**Scenario:**  
**Brightwater Pet Supply Co.** is in its **first year** of operations. It sells on account, uses perpetual inventory, and grants **cash** refunds on returns. Merchandise cost is **70%** of selling price. Year 1 data:

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------:|
| Credit sales | **$900,000** |
| Cost of goods sold (70%) | **$630,000** |
| Actual cash refunds for returns | **$27,000** |
| Sales allowances (credit to AR; goods retained) | **$3,600** |
| Estimated **total** returns at year-end | **4%** of credit sales |

No beginning Refund Liability or Inventory—Estimated Returns.

**Required:**  
(a) Record sales and COGS.  
(b) Record actual returns (sales-side and cost-side).  
(c) Record sales allowances.  
(d) Record December 31 estimated-return adjusting entries (both sides).  
(e) Compute Net Sales and ending balances of Refund Liability and Inventory—Estimated Returns. Show the identity: ending Refund Liability = total estimated returns − actual returns. Prepare a one-line YE plug schedule.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Sales and COGS**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 900,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 900,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 630,000 | |
| Inventory | | 630,000 |

*Check:* \(900{,}000 \times 0.70 = 630{,}000\). Dr = Cr.

**(b) Actual returns**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 27,000 | |
| Cash | | 27,000 |

Inventory restored: \(27{,}000 \times 0.70 = \mathbf{\$18{,}900}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 18,900 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 18,900 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr.

**(c) Sales allowances**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Allowances | 3,600 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 3,600 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. **No** inventory or COGS entry for an allowance.

**(d) December 31 estimated returns**

Total estimated returns = \(0.04 \times 900{,}000 = \mathbf{\$36{,}000}\).  
Remaining = \(36{,}000 - 27{,}000 = \mathbf{\$9{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 9,000 | |
| Refund Liability | | 9,000 |

Cost side: \(9{,}000 \times 0.70 = \mathbf{\$6{,}300}\)  
(equivalently: \(36{,}000 \times 0.70 = 25{,}200\) − \(18{,}900\) already restored).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | 6,300 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 6,300 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. Total Sales Returns = \(27{,}000 + 9{,}000 = 36{,}000\).

**(e) Net Sales, ending balances, plug schedule**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales Revenue | $900,000 |
| − Sales Returns | (36,000) |
| − Sales Allowances | (3,600) |
| **Net Sales** | **$860,400** |

| Account | Ending balance |
|---|---:|
| Refund Liability | **$9,000** |
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | **$6,300** |

**YE plug schedule (first year)**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total estimated returns (4% × $900,000) | $36,000 |
| − Actual returns already recorded | (27,000) |
| **= YE plug to Refund Liability / Sales Returns** | **$9,000** |
| × Cost % 70% | → Inv—Est Returns plug **$6,300** |

Identity: ending Refund Liability = total estimated returns − actual returns = \(36{,}000 - 27{,}000 = 9{,}000\).  
Inventory—Estimated Returns = ending RL × 70% = \(9{,}000 \times 0.70 = 6{,}300\).

**Key insight:** Same structure as Demo 8-3 with different numbers: accrue only the **unreturned** portion of the estimate so liability and estimated-returns inventory equal remaining expected refunds and product recoveries.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Holloway Homewares: settlement of prior estimate + YE true-up
**LO:** LO 8-3  
**Concept:** disposal_maturity_or_settlement_JE — settle Refund Liability for returns of prior-period sales; subsequent_measurement_schedule; period_end_adjusting_JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Holloway Homewares LLC** grants a 60-day return privilege. Cost of merchandise is **55%** of selling price. On **January 1, Year 3**:

| Account | Balance |
|---|---:|
| Refund Liability | **$12,500** |
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | **$6,875** |

**During Year 3:**

1. **January 18:** Holloway identifies **$7,500** of cash returns (inventory cost **$4,125**) as returns of **Year 2** sales that had already been accrued in Refund Liability / Inventory—Estimated Returns. Record these as **settlement** of the prior estimate (do **not** debit Sales Returns for this portion).  
2. Credit sales for Year 3: **$500,000** (COGS **$275,000**).  
3. Additional actual cash returns during Year 3 of **$16,000** (cost **$8,800**), recorded under the Review 8-3 practice as **current-year** Sales Returns (not as settlement of RL).  
4. Sales allowances granted on account: **$2,000**.  
5. At December 31, Year 3, Holloway estimates returns related to **Year 3** sales at **4%** of Year 3 credit sales. It adjusts Refund Liability so ending RL reflects remaining expected returns on Year 3 sales (using the identity: Ending RL = Beg RL − settlements of prior estimate − actual returns charged to Sales Returns + estimate on current-year sales).  

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **January 18 settlement** entries (sales-side and cost-side) for the prior-period estimate.  
(b) Record Year 3 sales and COGS.  
(c) Record the additional **$16,000** actual returns (main method) and the sales allowances.  
(d) Compute target ending Refund Liability and record December 31 true-up entries (both sides).  
(e) Prepare a **Refund Liability rollforward schedule** for Year 3 that includes the settlement line. Also roll forward Inventory—Estimated Returns and confirm ending Inv—Est = ending RL × 55%.  
(f) Compute Year 3 Net Sales and classify the two estimated-return balance-sheet accounts.

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 18, Year 3 — settlement of prior-period estimate**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Refund Liability | 7,500 | |
| Cash | | 7,500 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 4,125 | |
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | | 4,125 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. **Sales Returns is not debited** — the reduction of Net Sales was already recognized when the Year 2 estimate was accrued. After settlement: RL = \(12{,}500 - 7{,}500 = \mathbf{\$5{,}000}\); Inv—Est = \(6{,}875 - 4{,}125 = \mathbf{\$2{,}750}\).

**(b) Year 3 sales and COGS**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 500,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 500,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 275,000 | |
| Inventory | | 275,000 |

*Check:* \(500{,}000 \times 0.55 = 275{,}000\).

**(c) Additional actual returns (main method) and allowances**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 16,000 | |
| Cash | | 16,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 8,800 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 8,800 |

*Check:* \(16{,}000 \times 0.55 = 8{,}800\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Allowances | 2,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 2,000 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. No inventory entry for allowance.

**(d) December 31, Year 3 — true-up**

Estimate on Year 3 sales = \(0.04 \times 500{,}000 = \mathbf{\$20{,}000}\).

Beginning RL for the year = **$12,500**. After settlement and main-method actuals, target ending:

\[
\begin{align*}
\text{Ending RL} &= 12{,}500 - 7{,}500\ (\text{settlement}) - 16{,}000\ (\text{actual to Sales Returns}) + 20{,}000\ (\text{est. on Y3}) \\
&= \mathbf{\$9{,}000}
\end{align*}
\]

After settlement, book RL = \(5{,}000\). Main-method actuals do not touch RL, so pre-YE book RL remains **$5,000**.  
YE plug = \(9{,}000 - 5{,}000 = \mathbf{+\$4{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 4,000 | |
| Refund Liability | | 4,000 |

Cost-side: \(4{,}000 \times 0.55 = \mathbf{\$2{,}200}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | 2,200 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 2,200 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. Total Year 3 Sales Returns (main method + plug) = \(16{,}000 + 4{,}000 = 20{,}000\) (= estimate on current-year sales). Settlement returns did **not** hit Sales Returns.

**(e) Subsequent measurement schedules — Year 3**

**Refund Liability rollforward — Year 3**

| Date / component | Debit (decrease) | Credit (increase) | Balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Year 3 beginning | | | 12,500 |
| Jan 18 settlement of prior estimate | 7,500 | | 5,000 |
| Actual returns charged to Sales Returns (not to RL) | — | — | 5,000 |
| Dec 31 YE true-up | | 4,000 | **9,000** |
| **Memo identity** | Settlement 7,500; Actual-to-SR 16,000 | Est. on Y3 20,000 | 12,500 − 7,500 − 16,000 + 20,000 = **9,000** |

**Inventory—Estimated Returns rollforward — Year 3**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Inventory—Estimated Returns | $6,875 |
| − Jan 18 settlement (release of estimated asset) | (4,125) |
| + YE cost-side true-up | 2,200 |
| **Ending Inventory—Estimated Returns** | **$4,950** |

Confirm: ending Refund Liability × cost % = \(9{,}000 \times 0.55 = \mathbf{\$4{,}950}\). ✓

**(f) Net Sales and presentation**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales Revenue | $500,000 |
| − Sales Returns (\(16{,}000 + 4{,}000\); settlement excluded) | (20,000) |
| − Sales Allowances | (2,000) |
| **Net Sales** | **$478,000** |

**Classification / presentation (December 31, Year 3)**  
- **Refund Liability $9,000** — current liability (refund obligation for consideration the entity does not expect to be entitled to under ASC 606).  
- **Inventory—Estimated Returns $4,950** — current asset (right to recover products expected to be returned).  
- Net Sales is reported on the income statement; Sales Returns and Sales Allowances are contra-revenue accounts.

**Key insight:** **Settlement** of a previously accrued refund (Dr Refund Liability) is the disposal/settlement of the accrued obligation—do not debit Sales Returns again. Subsequent measurement still ends with a schedule that reconciles beginning RL, settlements, actuals, and the current-year estimate to the target ending liability.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 8-3  
**Concept:** classification_presentation_or_disclosure — return vs allowance; Refund Liability presentation  

**Question 4.1**  
On June 9, a customer of **Summit Ridge Furniture** keeps a slightly dented dining table after Summit Ridge grants a **$900** price reduction via credit memo to Accounts Receivable. The table originally sold for $4,500 (cost $2,700). Which entry correctly records the June 9 event?

- A) Debit Sales Returns $900; credit Accounts Receivable $900; and debit Inventory $540; credit COGS $540  
- B) Debit Sales Allowances $900; credit Accounts Receivable $900 only  
- C) Debit Refund Liability $900; credit Accounts Receivable $900 only  
- D) Debit Sales Allowances $900; credit Accounts Receivable $900; and debit Inventory $540; credit COGS $540  

**Answer:** **B.** A sales **allowance** is a price concession when the customer **retains** the goods—only the contra-revenue / receivable entry is made. No inventory is restored. A is return-style accounting (wrong for an allowance). C treats a newly granted allowance as settlement of a prior estimate. D incorrectly restores inventory for an allowance.

---

**Question 4.2**  
After year-end adjusting entries for estimated sales returns, which presentation is correct?

- A) Refund Liability is a contra-asset to Accounts Receivable; Inventory—Estimated Returns is a current liability  
- B) Refund Liability is a current liability; Inventory—Estimated Returns is a current asset  
- C) Both Refund Liability and Inventory—Estimated Returns are contra-revenue accounts on the income statement  
- D) Refund Liability reduces Net Sales directly as a credit-balance equity account  

**Answer:** **B.** Refund Liability is a **current liability** (obligation to refund consideration). Inventory—Estimated Returns is a **current asset** (right to recover products). Sales Returns (not the liability itself) is the contra-revenue account that reduces Net Sales. A reverses the classifications; C and D misstate the nature of the balance-sheet accounts. (Note: some practitioners may use an Allowance for Sales Returns contra-AR instead of Refund Liability when returns attach to outstanding receivables—the core Demo 8-3 presentation uses Refund Liability as a liability.)
