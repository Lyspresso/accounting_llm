# Agent 100 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-10

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Describe financing disclosures and analyses using leverage ratios  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** issue long-term bonds (discount or premium) at cash proceeds; record Bonds Payable at face with Discount/Premium contra/adjunct
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** effective-interest amortization table (interest expense, cash coupon, discount/premium amort, carrying value roll-forward)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** accrue bond interest and amortize discount/premium; reclassify **current maturities of LTD**
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JE:** cash interest payment; installment principal payment; early extinguishment (remove face, unamortized discount/premium, cash paid; recognize gain/loss)
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** five-year principal maturities and sinking-fund requirements (ASC 470-10-50); nature of debt, rates, maturities, restrictions, call provisions, conversion privileges; assets pledged as collateral; fair value of debt and FV hierarchy level (ASC 825); unused long-term financing / unused lines of credit; SCF — debt proceeds/retirements = financing; interest paid = operating
- **Leverage ratios:** total liabilities-to-equity; total liabilities-to-total assets; times interest earned (income before taxes and interest ÷ interest expense)
- **Number-variant twin:** same skill path with all amounts changed (premium issue)

### Q1 — CORE — Bond initial recognition, amort schedule, period-end adj, five-year maturities, leverage ratios
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for bonds issued at a discount; effective-interest subsequent schedule; period-end interest adjusting JE; current-maturity reclass; five-year debt maturity disclosure; fair value / collateral / unused credit disclosures; leverage ratios  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Forge Inc.** is a calendar-year manufacturer. It is preparing notes and ratio analysis for its December 31, **Year 1** financial statements.

**Long-term debt outstanding at December 31, Year 1** (after Year 1 activity below):

| Instrument | Terms |
|---|---|
| **1. Unsecured bank note** | Face **$900,000**; 5% annual interest paid each **December 31**; **entire principal due December 31, Year 5**. Outstanding all of Year 1. |
| **2. Equipment installment note (secured)** | Remaining principal **$600,000**; 6% interest on unpaid principal paid each **December 31**; **principal installments of $75,000** due each December 31 for **Year 2 through Year 9** (8 remaining payments). Equipment with carrying amount **$720,000** is pledged as collateral. |
| **3. Bonds payable** | On **July 1, Year 1**, Meridian issued **$4,000,000** face, **6%** bonds dated July 1, Year 1, maturing **June 30, Year 11** (10 years). Interest is payable **semiannually on January 1 and July 1**. Bonds were issued for cash of **$3,760,000** (discount **$240,000**). Effective interest method; **effective rate 7% annual (3.5% per semiannual period)**. Cash coupon each period = **$120,000**. |

**Additional disclosure facts (December 31, Year 1):**
- Estimated **fair value** of the bonds (Level 2 inputs): **$3,920,000**. Carrying amounts of the bank note and installment note approximate fair value.
- Unused committed revolving line of credit: **$2,500,000** (commitment fee 0.20% per year on unused portion); no amounts drawn at year-end.
- No conversion privileges; bonds are callable at 102 after Year 3 (disclose call feature).

**December 31, Year 1 statement amounts (after recording all Year 1 interest and bond amortization):**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total assets | $12,500,000 |
| Total liabilities | 7,500,000 |
| Total stockholders’ equity | 5,000,000 |
| Income before taxes and interest expense (Year 1) | 1,650,000 |

Year 1 interest expense includes: bank note **$45,000**; installment note **$36,000**; and the effective interest on the bonds for the half-year from July 1–December 31 (computed in the required schedule).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **July 1, Year 1 initial recognition journal entry** for the bond issuance.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (amortization) schedule** for the **first three semiannual periods** (Period 1: Jul 1–Dec 31 Year 1; Period 2: Jan 1–Jun 30 Year 2; Period 3: Jul 1–Dec 31 Year 2). Columns: beginning carrying value, interest expense, cash interest, discount amortization, ending carrying value.  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting entry** for bond interest and discount amortization.  
d. Prepare the **period-end reclassification entry** for the **current maturity** of long-term debt at December 31, Year 1.  
e. Prepare a **five-year principal maturity table** (Years 2–6 and thereafter) for all three debt instruments and draft the **note disclosure sentence** for aggregate maturities (use **face/principal** amounts, consistent with ASC 470-10-50).  
f. List the **key financing disclosures** required (beyond the maturity table) given the facts.  
g. Compute **(1)** total liabilities-to-equity, **(2)** total liabilities-to-total assets, and **(3)** times interest earned for Year 1. Briefly interpret leverage and interest coverage.

**Answer key:**  

**a. July 1, Year 1 — initial recognition of bonds at discount**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 3,760,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 240,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 4,000,000 |
| *Issue $4,000,000 face bonds for $3,760,000; record discount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,760,000 + 240,000 = Cr 4,000,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — effective interest (3.5% × beginning CV)**

Cash interest each period = $4,000,000 × 3% = **$120,000**.

| Period | Beg. carrying value | Interest expense (3.5%) | Cash interest | Discount amort. | End. carrying value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (Jul–Dec Y1) | $3,760,000 | $131,600 | $120,000 | $11,600 | $3,771,600 |
| 2 (Jan–Jun Y2) | 3,771,600 | 132,006 | 120,000 | 12,006 | 3,783,606 |
| 3 (Jul–Dec Y2) | 3,783,606 | 132,426 | 120,000 | 12,426 | 3,796,032 |

**Schedule checks:**  
Period 1: 3,760,000 × 0.035 = **131,600**; 131,600 − 120,000 = **11,600**; 3,760,000 + 11,600 = **3,771,600**.  
Period 2: 3,771,600 × 0.035 = **132,006** (rounded); 132,006 − 120,000 = **12,006**; 3,771,600 + 12,006 = **3,783,606**.  
Period 3: 3,783,606 × 0.035 = **132,426**; 132,426 − 120,000 = **12,426**; 3,783,606 + 12,426 = **3,796,032**.

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE (bond interest)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 131,600 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 11,600 |
| Interest Payable | | 120,000 |
| *Accrue semi-annual effective interest; first cash coupon due Jan 1 Year 2* | | |

**Check:** Dr 131,600 = Cr 11,600 + 120,000. **Balanced.**

**d. December 31, Year 1 — reclassify current maturities of LTD**

Only the installment note has principal due within one year: **$75,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable—Long-Term (Installment) | 75,000 | |
| Current Maturities of Long-Term Debt | | 75,000 |
| *Reclassify principal due within one year as current liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 75,000. **Balanced.**

(After reclass: current portion $75,000; noncurrent installment principal $525,000; unsecured note $900,000 all noncurrent; bonds all noncurrent until Year 11.)

**e. Five-year principal maturity table (December 31, Year 1)**

| Debt | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Year 5 | Year 6 | Thereafter |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unsecured bank note | $0 | $0 | $0 | $900,000 | $0 | $0 |
| Secured installment note | 75,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 225,000 |
| Bonds payable (face) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 4,000,000 |
| **Total** | **$75,000** | **$75,000** | **$75,000** | **$975,000** | **$75,000** | **$4,225,000** |

**Table checks:** Installment thereafter = $600,000 − 5 × $75,000 = **$225,000**. Row totals: Years 2–4 and 6 = $75,000; Year 5 = $900,000 + $75,000 = **$975,000**; thereafter = $225,000 + $4,000,000 = **$4,225,000**.

**Sample note disclosure:**  
*At December 31, Year 1, aggregate principal maturities of long-term debt are approximately $75,000, $75,000, $75,000, $975,000, and $75,000 for Year 2 through Year 6, respectively, and $4,225,000 thereafter.*

**f. Other required financing disclosures (given facts)**
- Nature of each debt instrument; **interest rates**; **maturity dates**; **call provision** (callable at 102 after Year 3).
- **Assets pledged as collateral** — equipment CA **$720,000** securing the installment note.
- **Fair value of financial instruments** — bonds FV **$3,920,000** (Level 2); notes approximate carrying amount; hierarchy level disclosed (ASC 825-10-50).
- **Unused long-term / revolving financing** — unused committed LOC **$2,500,000**, terms and commitment fee (ASC 470-10-50-6).
- Creditor restrictions/covenants if any (none given beyond collateral and call feature).

**g. Leverage ratios — Year 1**

Total interest expense Year 1 = $45,000 + $36,000 + $131,600 = **$212,600**.

| Ratio | Formula | Computation | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Total liabilities-to-equity | TL ÷ Equity | $7,500,000 ÷ $5,000,000 | **1.50** |
| Total liabilities-to-total assets | TL ÷ TA | $7,500,000 ÷ $12,500,000 | **0.60** (60%) |
| Times interest earned | EBIT ÷ Interest expense | $1,650,000 ÷ $212,600 | **7.76 times** |

**Interpretation:** Creditors finance **60%** of assets; liabilities are **1.50×** equity (moderate leverage). Times interest earned of **~7.8×** indicates operating earnings cover interest many times — capacity to service debt from current earnings appears adequate despite material long-term borrowings.

**Key insight:** Financing analysis pairs **note disclosures** (maturities, collateral, FV, unused credit) with **leverage ratios**. Bond accounting supplies the **carrying value** on the balance sheet, but the **five-year maturity table uses principal (face) payment dates**, not amortized cost.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Premium bond issue, amort schedule, disclosures, leverage ratios
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition of bonds at a **premium**; effective-interest schedule; period-end adj; five-year maturities; unused credit and FV disclosure; leverage ratios with all new amounts  
**Scenario:**  
**Northridge Alloys Corp.** (calendar-year) has the following long-term debt at December 31, **Year 1**:

| Instrument | Terms |
|---|---|
| **1. Unsecured term note** | Face **$1,500,000**; **4.5%** interest paid each December 31; **entire principal due December 31, Year 4**. |
| **2. Mortgage note (secured by plant)** | Remaining principal **$1,200,000**; **5.5%** interest paid each December 31; **principal installments of $150,000** due each December 31 for **Year 2 through Year 9**. Plant assets with carrying amount **$1,850,000** pledged. |
| **3. Bonds payable** | On **July 1, Year 1**, issued **$5,000,000** face, **6%** bonds, 10-year life (mature June 30, Year 11), interest payable **January 1 and July 1**. Issued for cash **$5,200,000** (premium **$200,000**). Effective interest method; **effective rate 5.5% annual (2.75% per semiannual period)**. Cash coupon each period = **$150,000**. |

**Other facts:** Fair value of bonds at Dec 31 Year 1 (Level 2) = **$5,150,000**. Unused term loan commitment = **$1,000,000**.

**December 31, Year 1 totals (after Year 1 interest and bond amortization):**  
Total assets **$16,000,000**; total liabilities **$9,800,000**; equity **$6,200,000**.  
Income before taxes and interest **$2,400,000**.  
Year 1 interest expense includes term note **$67,500**, mortgage **$66,000**, and bond effective interest for the half-year (from schedule).

**Required:**  
a. **July 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE** for the bond issue.  
b. **Amortization schedule** for the first **three** semiannual periods (premium amortization).  
c. **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** for bond interest.  
d. **Current maturity reclassification JE** at December 31, Year 1.  
e. **Five-year maturity table** (Years 2–6 and thereafter) and short note disclosure.  
f. Compute the three **leverage ratios** and interpret briefly.

**Answer key:**  

**a. July 1, Year 1 — initial recognition at premium**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 5,200,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | | 200,000 |
| Bonds Payable | | 5,000,000 |
| *Issue bonds above face; premium is adjunct to Bonds Payable* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,200,000 = Cr 200,000 + 5,000,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (interest expense = 2.75% × beg. CV)**

| Period | Beg. CV | Interest expense (2.75%) | Cash interest | Premium amort. | End. CV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (Jul–Dec Y1) | $5,200,000 | $143,000 | $150,000 | $7,000 | $5,193,000 |
| 2 (Jan–Jun Y2) | 5,193,000 | 142,808 | 150,000 | 7,192 | 5,185,808 |
| 3 (Jul–Dec Y2) | 5,185,808 | 142,610 | 150,000 | 7,390 | 5,178,418 |

**Checks:** Period 1: 5,200,000 × 0.0275 = **143,000**; premium amort = 150,000 − 143,000 = **7,000**; end CV = 5,200,000 − 7,000 = **5,193,000**.  
Period 2: 5,193,000 × 0.0275 = **142,808**; amort **7,192**; end **5,185,808**.  
Period 3: 5,185,808 × 0.0275 = **142,610**; amort **7,390**; end **5,178,418**.

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 143,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 7,000 | |
| Interest Payable | | 150,000 |
| *Effective interest less than coupon reduces premium* | | |

**Check:** Dr 143,000 + 7,000 = Cr 150,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Current maturity reclass**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mortgage Payable—Long-Term | 150,000 | |
| Current Maturities of Long-Term Debt | | 150,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 150,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Five-year principal maturities (December 31, Year 1)**

| Debt | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Year 5 | Year 6 | Thereafter |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unsecured term note | $0 | $0 | $1,500,000 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Mortgage note | 150,000 | 150,000 | 150,000 | 150,000 | 150,000 | 450,000 |
| Bonds payable (face) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5,000,000 |
| **Total** | **$150,000** | **$150,000** | **$1,650,000** | **$150,000** | **$150,000** | **$5,450,000** |

Mortgage thereafter = $1,200,000 − 5 × $150,000 = **$450,000**.  
*Disclosure:* Aggregate principal payments are approximately $150,000, $150,000, $1,650,000, $150,000, and $150,000 for Years 2–6, respectively, and $5,450,000 thereafter. Also disclose plant collateral CA $1,850,000, bond FV $5,150,000 (Level 2), and unused commitment $1,000,000.

**f. Leverage ratios**

Total interest expense Year 1 = $67,500 + $66,000 + $143,000 = **$276,500**.

| Ratio | Computation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Liabilities-to-equity | $9,800,000 ÷ $6,200,000 | **1.58** |
| Liabilities-to-total assets | $9,800,000 ÷ $16,000,000 | **0.6125** (~61.3%) |
| Times interest earned | $2,400,000 ÷ $276,500 | **8.68 times** |

**Interpretation:** Slightly higher leverage than a 1.0 equity match (liabilities ~1.58× equity; ~61% of assets creditor-financed), but interest coverage of **~8.7×** indicates comfortable capacity to meet interest from earnings.

**Key insight:** Premium bonds **reduce** interest expense relative to the cash coupon; the maturity table still schedules **face** principal, while the balance sheet reports **carrying value** (face + unamortized premium).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Interest settlement, early extinguishment, SCF classification, ratio impact
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Settlement JEs (coupon payment and early extinguishment); update amortization to retirement date; financing vs operating cash-flow classification; recompute leverage ratios after debt retirement; maturity disclosure change  
**Scenario:**  
Continue **Meridian Forge Inc.** from Q1. Bond carrying value at **December 31, Year 1** = **$3,771,600** (discount remaining **$228,400**). Interest payable of **$120,000** is on the books for the January 1, Year 2 coupon.

On **January 1, Year 2**, Meridian pays the scheduled cash interest on the full bond issue.

On **March 1, Year 2**, Meridian retires **half** of the bonds (**$2,000,000 face**) on the open market at **98** (pays **$1,960,000** for principal) **plus accrued interest** for two months on the retired half. Use the **effective-interest** amounts for **Period 2** from Q1 (full-issue Period 2 interest expense **$132,006**; discount amort **$12,006**; cash coupon **$120,000**). Allocate **half** of those Period-2 amounts × **2/6** for the two months from January 1 to March 1 on the retired half.

**Immediately before the March 1 retirement** (after updating books for two months on the retired half only for the extinguishment computation), Meridian’s simplified balances for ratio analysis are:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total assets (before paying retirement cash and accrued interest) | $12,800,000 |
| Total liabilities (before removing retired debt and related accruals) | 7,650,000 |
| Stockholders’ equity | 5,150,000 |

(These balances already include the January 1 coupon payment’s effect on cash and interest payable for the full issue, and include two months’ accrual on the **retired half** only as you record in part b. For ratio part e, apply the **retirement of principal at 98** and the **loss**, and the cash paid for **accrued interest on the half**.)

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 2 settlement JE** for the full-issue cash coupon payment.  
b. Prepare the **March 1, Year 2 updating JE** for two months’ effective interest on the **retired half** only.  
c. Prepare the **March 1, Year 2 extinguishment JE** for retirement of $2,000,000 face at 98 (exclude accrued interest from this entry if already accrued in b; show cash paid for principal).  
d. **Classification / SCF:** Classify each cash flow—(i) January 1 coupon on all bonds, (ii) March 1 accrued interest paid on retired half, (iii) March 1 cash paid to retire principal at 98—as **operating** or **financing**. State the noncash effects related to discount amortization and the extinguishment loss.  
e. Compute **cash paid on March 1** for principal + accrued interest on the half. Compute **liabilities-to-equity** and **liabilities-to-assets** **after** the March 1 retirement entries (b–c and payment of accrued interest), starting from the “before” totals given (adjust assets for cash outflows; liabilities for removal of debt CV and interest payable related to the half; equity for the loss).  
f. How does the **five-year maturity disclosure** change after retiring half the bonds (face remaining **$2,000,000** due June 30, Year 11)?

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 2 — settle full coupon (Interest Payable from Dec 31)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 120,000 | |
| Cash | | 120,000 |
| *Pay semiannual cash interest on $4,000,000 face* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 120,000. **Balanced.**

**b. March 1, Year 2 — update retired half for 2 months of Period 2**

Full-issue Period 2: IE $132,006; amort $12,006; coupon $120,000.  
Retired half × 2/6 of period:  
- Interest expense = $132,006 × ½ × 2/6 = **$22,001**  
- Discount amortization = $12,006 × ½ × 2/6 = **$2,001**  
- Accrued cash interest = $120,000 × ½ × 2/6 = **$20,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 22,001 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 2,001 |
| Interest Payable | | 20,000 |
| *Update effective interest on $2,000,000 face for 2 months* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,001 = Cr 2,001 + 20,000. **Balanced.**

Carrying value of half at Dec 31 Year 1 = $3,771,600 × ½ = **$1,885,800**.  
CV of half at March 1 Year 2 = $1,885,800 + $2,001 = **$1,887,801**.  
Unamortized discount on half = $2,000,000 − $1,887,801 = **$112,199**.

**c. March 1, Year 2 — extinguish $2,000,000 face at 98**

Cash paid for principal = $2,000,000 × 0.98 = **$1,960,000**.  
Loss = cash paid − carrying value retired = $1,960,000 − $1,887,801 = **$72,199**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 2,000,000 | |
| Loss on Early Extinguishment of Debt | 72,199 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 112,199 |
| Cash | | 1,960,000 |
| *Remove half of issue; loss = reacquisition price − CV* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,000,000 + 72,199 = **2,072,199**; Cr 112,199 + 1,960,000 = **2,072,199**. **Balanced.**

Also settle accrued interest on the half:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 20,000 | |
| Cash | | 20,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 20,000. **Balanced.**

**d. SCF classification / disclosure**

| Cash flow | Amount | SCF section |
|---|---:|---|
| (i) Jan 1 coupon (full issue) | $120,000 | **Operating** (cash paid for interest) |
| (ii) Mar 1 accrued interest on half | $20,000 | **Operating** |
| (iii) Mar 1 principal retirement at 98 | $1,960,000 | **Financing** (outflow to retire long-term debt) |

**Noncash / indirect-method adjustments related to debt:**  
- Discount amortization increases interest expense above cash interest (add-back coordination via interest payable changes and amortization).  
- **Loss on early extinguishment** is in net income; under the indirect method it is typically **added back** if the cash paid to retire debt is classified entirely in financing (or presented so cash interest vs financing retirement is not double-counted)—the **cash principal retirement** is financing; the **loss** is a noncash component of the difference between CV removed and financing cash paid.

**e. Ratios after March 1 retirement**

Cash outflows March 1 = $1,960,000 + $20,000 = **$1,980,000**.

Starting “before” totals already reflect Jan 1 coupon and the part-b accrual. After extinguishment + paying $20,000 interest:

- **Assets:** $12,800,000 − $1,960,000 − $20,000 = **$10,820,000**  
- **Liabilities:** $7,650,000 − $1,887,801 (CV removed) − $20,000 (interest payable paid) = **$5,742,199**  
- **Equity:** $5,150,000 − $72,199 (loss) = **$5,077,801**  

**Check:** Assets $10,820,000 = Liabilities $5,742,199 + Equity $5,077,801.

| Ratio | After retirement |
|---|---:|
| Liabilities-to-equity | $5,742,199 ÷ $5,077,801 = **1.13** |
| Liabilities-to-total assets | $5,742,199 ÷ $10,820,000 = **0.53** |

**Interpretation:** Early retirement **deleverages** the balance sheet (L/E falls from about 1.49 before retirement on these balances to **1.13**; L/A to **53%**), at the cost of cash and a recognized **loss**.

**f. Maturity disclosure after half retirement**

Remaining bond face **$2,000,000** still due June 30, Year 11 → still in **“thereafter”** (beyond Year 6).  
Thereafter column for bonds decreases by $2,000,000 (from $4,000,000 to **$2,000,000**). Near-term Years 2–6 totals for notes/installment are **unchanged**. Aggregate thereafter becomes $225,000 + $2,000,000 = **$2,225,000** (plus any other unchanged items).

**Key insight:** Settlement and extinguishment entries **remove** balance-sheet leverage and change **note maturity tables**; cash **interest** stays in **operating** activities while cash to **retire principal** is **financing**—exactly the financing disclosure / cash-flow mapping in LO 16-10.

### Q4 — MC — Five-year debt maturity disclosure
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Classification / disclosure — ASC 470 five-year aggregate maturities requirement  
**Question:**  
Which of the following must be disclosed in the notes for long-term borrowings under U.S. GAAP (ASC 470-10-50)?

- A) Only the total long-term debt carrying value; year-by-year maturities are optional  
- B) The combined aggregate amount of maturities and sinking-fund requirements for each of the **five years** following the balance sheet date  
- C) Fair value of debt only if the company elected the fair value option  
- D) Unused lines of credit only when amounts have been drawn  

**Answer:** **B**. ASC 470-10-50-1 requires disclosure of the combined aggregate amount of maturities and sinking-fund requirements for all long-term borrowings for **each of the five years** following the latest balance sheet date. (A is wrong—tabular/near-term maturities are required. C is wrong—FV of financial instruments is generally disclosed when practicable under ASC 825, not only under the FV option. D is wrong—**unused** commitments and lines must be disclosed, including terms and fees.)

### Q5 — MC — Times interest earned and leverage interpretation
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Times interest earned ratio definition and leverage analysis  
**Question:**  
Northpeak Co. reports income before taxes and interest of **$840,000**, interest expense of **$120,000**, total liabilities of **$4,200,000**, total assets of **$7,000,000**, and equity of **$2,800,000**. Which statement is correct?

- A) Times interest earned = $840,000 ÷ $4,200,000 = 0.20; the company cannot cover interest  
- B) Times interest earned = $840,000 ÷ $120,000 = **7.0**; liabilities-to-assets = **0.60**; interest coverage is relatively strong despite 60% creditor financing  
- C) Times interest earned = ($840,000 − $120,000) ÷ $120,000 = 6.0; liabilities-to-equity = 1.0  
- D) Liabilities-to-equity = $7,000,000 ÷ $2,800,000 = 2.5; times interest earned is not used for leverage analysis  

**Answer:** **B**. Times interest earned = income before taxes and interest ÷ interest expense = $840,000 ÷ $120,000 = **7.0 times**. Liabilities-to-total assets = $4,200,000 ÷ $7,000,000 = **0.60**. A 7× coverage indicates earnings can cover interest seven times—generally comfortable coverage—even though creditors finance 60% of assets. (A uses the wrong denominator. C incorrectly subtracts interest from the numerator and misstates L/E: L/E = 4,200,000 ÷ 2,800,000 = **1.5**, not 1.0. D uses assets in the L/E numerator and wrongly dismisses TIE.)
