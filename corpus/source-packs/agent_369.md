# Agent 369 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-2

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Account for common stock issuance, including par and no-par, cash and noncash, and issue costs  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** par-value common issued for cash — Common Stock at par × shares; excess to Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock
- **True no-par vs stated-value no-par:** true no-par credits Common Stock for **full** proceeds; stated-value no-par treated like par (CS at stated value; remainder to PIC excess of stated value)
- **Noncash issuance / settlement:** measure at FV of stock **or** FV of noncash consideration, whichever is **more clearly evident**
- **Stock issue costs:** reduce net cash (or increase payable) and reduce PIC in excess of par; **not** period expense (ASC 505-10-25-2)
- **Multiple securities (lump-sum):** **proportional** allocation when both FVs known; **incremental** when only one FV known
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** multi-event **paid-in capital and shares roll-forward** (equity not remeasured to market after issuance)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** unpaid stock issue costs accrued at year-end (reduce PIC; credit payable); period-end noncash share settlement for services performed
- **Disposal / maturity or settlement JE:** cash settlement of stock issue costs payable after year-end; completion of noncash share settlement

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-event issuances; unpaid issue-cost AJE (emphasis); services settlement; PIC schedule
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Initial par cash issuance with issue costs; noncash equipment (stock FV more clearly evident); proportional lump-sum common+preferred; year-end cash issue with **accrued unpaid** issue costs (period-end adjusting emphasis); Dec 31 services settlement; subsequent paid-in capital schedule; post-year settlement of payable  
**Scenario:**  
**Flintlock Precision Tools Inc.** is newly incorporated on **January 1, Year 1** (calendar year). The corporate charter authorizes:

| Security | Authorized | Par |
|---|---:|---|
| Common stock | 600,000 shares | **$1** |
| Preferred stock | 80,000 shares | **$10** |

There is **no beginning equity**. During Year 1 (and early Year 2 for settlement) the company completes the following equity transactions. All issued shares remain outstanding (no treasury stock).

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **Jan 20** | Issues **35,000** common shares for cash at **$14** per share. Stock issue costs (legal, underwriting, registration) total **$4,200**, paid in cash from the proceeds on the same day. |
| **Apr 5** | Issues **5,000** common shares in exchange for **specialized equipment**. Flintlock common stock is actively traded at **$15** per share on April 5. The equipment has **no** reliable stand-alone fair value. |
| **Jul 1** | Issues a **combined (lump-sum)** package of **10,000** common shares and **4,000** preferred shares for total cash of **$150,000**. At issuance, common stock is selling at **$12** per share and preferred stock at **$15** per share. |
| **Dec 20** | Issues **20,000** common shares for cash at **$13** per share. Related stock issue costs total **$7,500**, of which **$5,000** is paid in cash at closing and **$2,500** remains unpaid (attorney invoice dated **December 29**, Year 1). Reduce PIC for the **full** $7,500 of issue costs; credit a payable for the unpaid portion. |
| **Dec 31** | Issues **2,000** common shares to settle a year-end obligation for **consulting services** performed entirely in Q4. The fair value of the services is more clearly evident and equals **$28,000**. (Thin market for these privately placed shares; use service FV.) |
| **Jan 15, Year 2** | Pays **$2,500** cash to settle the stock issue costs payable from the December 20 issuance. |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 20** journal entry for the cash common stock issuance **including stock issue costs** (initial recognition).  
b. Record the **April 5** journal entry for the noncash equipment issuance; state the measurement basis.  
c. Compute the **proportional allocation** of the July 1 lump-sum proceeds and record the **July 1** journal entry.  
d. Record the **December 20** journal entry for the cash issuance **including full issue costs and the unpaid portion** (period-end related recognition of the payable).  
e. Record the **December 31** period-end settlement entry for shares issued for consulting services.  
f. Prepare a complete **subsequent measurement schedule** of **shares issued** and **paid-in capital account balances** after **each** Year 1 event (Jan 20, Apr 5, Jul 1, Dec 20, Dec 31). Show beginning zero balances, the effect of each event, and December 31 ending balances. Compute total paid-in capital and legal capital (par of issued common + preferred).  
g. Record the **January 15, Year 2** settlement of the stock issue costs payable.  
h. In one or two sentences, explain why stock issue costs are **not** recorded as an expense.

  (Dec 20 equity increase is $252,500 = net assets +$255,000 cash − liability +$2,500 payable.)  
- **Legal capital** (par of issued shares) = common \(72{,}000 \times \$1 = \$72{,}000\) + preferred \(4{,}000 \times \$10 = \$40{,}000\) = **$112,000**.  
- Common shares issued and outstanding = **72,000** (authorized 600,000; unissued common 528,000).  
- Preferred shares issued and outstanding = **4,000**.  
- **No** fair-value remeasurement of issued stock at period end — balances remain at historical issuance amounts.

(No further effect on paid-in capital; costs already reduced PIC in Year 1.)

**h. Issue costs not expense**  
Stock issue costs relate to a capital transaction in the entity’s **own stock**, not operations. Under ASC 505-10-25-2 they are **excluded from net income** and are treated as a **reduction of paid-in capital in excess of par** (whether paid in cash or accrued as a payable).

**Key insight (period-end emphasis):** After initial recognition, common stock is **not** remeasured to market at year-end. Period-end work for LO 19-2 is about **completing capital transactions timed at year-end**: accrue **unpaid stock issue costs** (debit PIC, credit payable), settle noncash share issuances for services performed, and roll forward the **paid-in capital / shares schedule**. Settlement of the payable later is a pure liability extinguishment — PIC was already reduced in the issuance period.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Cash, noncash land, proportional lump-sum, unpaid costs AJE, year-end settlement

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — No-par / stated-value alternatives; incremental lump-sum; period-end equity presentation
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** True no-par vs stated-value no-par cash issuance; noncash with asset FV; **incremental** lump-sum allocation (only common FV known); period-end adjusting focus — **no** FV remeasurement AJE; stockholders’ equity presentation of paid-in capital  
**Scenario:**  
**Redcedar Marine Systems LLC** is organized January 1, Year 1. Consider the following **independent** issuance scenarios (a–c are alternatives for the same 40,000-share cash raise). Then complete the **sequential** Year 1 path in (d)–(f) using **$2 par** common and **$8 par** preferred as stated.

### Q4 — MC — Stock issue costs and noncash measurement
**LO:** LO 19-2  
**Concept:** Stock issue costs reduce PIC (not expense); noncash issuance measured at more clearly evident FV  
**Question:**  
On December 1, **Oakridge Circuit Co.** issued 10,000 shares of $1 par common stock for cash of $22 per share and incurred $3,000 of stock issue costs (legal and underwriting), paid in cash. On December 15, Oakridge privately issued 2,000 shares of the same $1 par common stock in exchange for land. The common stock is thinly traded; an independent appraisal values the land at $48,000. Which of the following is **correct**?

- A) December 1: debit Cash $220,000; credit Common Stock $10,000 and PIC $210,000. Record the $3,000 issue costs as an expense. December 15: debit Land $44,000 using an assumed $22 market price of the stock.  
- B) December 1: debit Cash $217,000; credit Common Stock $10,000 and PIC $207,000 (issue costs reduce PIC, not expense). December 15: debit Land $48,000; credit Common Stock $2,000 and PIC $46,000 (measure at more clearly evident land FV).  
- C) December 1: debit Cash $217,000; credit Common Stock $217,000 (treat like true no-par). December 15: debit Land $48,000; credit Common Stock $48,000.  
- D) December 1: debit Cash $220,000 and Stock Issue Costs (asset) $3,000; amortize issue costs over five years as interest expense. December 15: no entry until land is sold.

**Answer:** **B.**  
December 1: net cash = \(10{,}000 \times 22 - 3{,}000 = \$217{,}000\); CS = \(10{,}000 \times 1 = \$10{,}000\); PIC = \(10{,}000 \times 21 - 3{,}000 = \$207{,}000\). Issue costs are a **reduction of PIC**, not an expense or deferred asset (ASC 505-10-25-2).  
December 15: stock FV not clearly evident; measure at **land appraisal $48,000**; CS $2,000; PIC $46,000.  
A incorrectly expenses issue costs and ignores more-clearly-evident land FV. C treats par stock as true no-par. D incorrectly capitalizes and amortizes issue costs like debt issue costs.
