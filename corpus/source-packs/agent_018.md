# Agent 18 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-8

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for conversion of debt into equity  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Issuance of convertible debt as straight debt (no equity allocation for conversion feature)
- Straight-line amortization schedule (premium / discount) after issuance
- Period-end interest and amortization adjusting entries
- Conversion under original terms: update books, derecognize debt at carrying amount, credit equity; **no gain or loss**
- Partial conversion (pro-rata unamortized discount/premium)
- Induced conversion: debt conversion expense for excess consideration; still no extinguishment gain/loss on debt carrying amount
- Classification/presentation: convertible debt is a **liability** until conversion; equity accounts replace debt on conversion

### Q1 — CORE — Issuance, premium amort schedule, period-end JE, full conversion
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of convertible bonds as straight debt; subsequent SL premium amortization; conversion at carrying amount with no gain/loss  
**Scenario:** On January 1, Year 1, **Cedarline Industries** issues **$500,000** face of **6%**, **10-year** convertible bonds for **$530,000** cash. Interest is payable **annually each December 31**. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible at any time into **25** shares of Cedarline **$4** par common stock. Cedarline amortizes premium using the **straight-line** method. Market yield information is not used for amortization. On **December 31, Year 3**, after recording interest and amortization for Year 3, **all** bondholders convert under the original conversion terms. Cedarline common stock trades at **$48** per share on the conversion date.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **January 1, Year 1** issuance (initial recognition). State whether any proceeds are allocated to equity for the conversion feature and why (core U.S. GAAP ASC 470-20 treatment for typical embedded conversion features).  
(b) Prepare a **straight-line amortization schedule** for Years 1–3 showing beginning carrying amount, cash interest, premium amortization, interest expense, and ending carrying amount. State the unamortized premium and carrying amount immediately after the Dec 31, Year 3 entry.  
(c) Record the **December 31, Year 1** period-end interest and amortization entry.  
(d) Record the **December 31, Year 3** conversion entry (after the Year 3 interest/amortization entry already recorded). Show share count and equity split. Confirm **Dr = Cr** and that **no gain or loss** is recognized despite the $48 market price.  
(e) **Classification/presentation:** How is the convertible issue classified on the balance sheet **before** conversion, and what replaces the bond accounts **after** conversion?

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — Issuance (straight-debt model)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 530,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | | 30,000 |
| Bonds Payable | | 500,000 |

- Proceeds **$530,000** − face **$500,000** = premium **$30,000**.  
- **No amount is allocated to equity** for the conversion feature. Under ASC 470-20, a typical embedded conversion feature is **not** separated; the instrument is accounted for **entirely as a liability** at issuance (unless a bifurcated embedded derivative or other specialized exception applies—not present here).  
- Debits **$530,000** = credits **$530,000** ✓

**(b) Premium amortization schedule (straight-line)**

- Annual cash interest = $500,000 × 6% = **$30,000**  
- Total premium = **$30,000**; term = **10** years → annual premium amortization = $30,000 ÷ 10 = **$3,000**  
- Annual interest expense = $30,000 − $3,000 = **$27,000**

| Date | Cash interest | Premium amort. | Interest expense | Ending unamort. premium | Ending carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1/1/Y1 (issue) | — | — | — | 30,000 | 530,000 |
| 12/31/Y1 | 30,000 | 3,000 | 27,000 | 27,000 | 527,000 |
| 12/31/Y2 | 30,000 | 3,000 | 27,000 | 24,000 | 524,000 |
| 12/31/Y3 | 30,000 | 3,000 | 27,000 | 21,000 | 521,000 |

- After Dec 31, Year 3 entry: unamortized premium = $30,000 − (3 × $3,000) = **$21,000**; carrying amount = $500,000 + $21,000 = **$521,000**.  
- Rollforward check: $530,000 − $9,000 amortized = **$521,000** ✓

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — Period-end interest and amortization**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 27,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 3,000 | |
| Cash | | 30,000 |

Debits **$30,000** = credits **$30,000** ✓

**(d) December 31, Year 3 — Conversion under original terms**

- Bonds outstanding: $500,000 ÷ $1,000 = **500** bonds  
- Shares issued: 500 × 25 = **12,500** shares  
- Common Stock (par) = 12,500 × $4 = **$50,000**  
- Carrying amount removed = $500,000 + $21,000 = **$521,000**  
- Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common = $521,000 − $50,000 = **$471,000**  
- Market value of shares (12,500 × $48 = $600,000) is **ignored** for measurement of the equity issued under the book-value (carrying-amount) method.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 500,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 21,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 50,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 471,000 |

Debits **$521,000** = credits **$521,000** ✓  
**No gain or loss** in net income on conversion under the original conversion privileges.

**(e) Classification / presentation**  
- **Before conversion:** report as **long-term liability** (Bonds Payable net of unamortized premium/discount), not as equity, even though conversion is possible.  
- **After conversion:** liability accounts are fully derecognized; **Common Stock** and **APIC** replace the net carrying amount of the debt. Dilutive conversion terms may affect EPS disclosures, but the conversion itself is an equity reclassification of the carrying amount—not a fair-value remeasurement through earnings.

**Key insight:** Convertible debt is issued and carried like ordinary debt (entire proceeds to liability); on conversion under original terms, update amortization, then swap the **carrying amount** into equity with **zero P&L impact**.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Discount issue, schedule, partial conversion
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Convertible bonds issued at a discount; SL amort; partial conversion at carrying amount  
**Scenario:** On January 1, Year 1, **HarborVale Fabrication Co.** issues **$360,000** face of **5%**, **6-year** convertible bonds at **97** (**$349,200** cash). Interest is payable **annually each December 31**. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **12** shares of HarborVale **$5** par common stock. HarborVale uses **straight-line** amortization. On **December 31, Year 2**, after recording interest and amortization for Year 2, holders of **50%** of the bonds exercise conversion. HarborVale stock trades at **$62** per share that day.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the January 1, Year 1 issuance.  
(b) Prepare an amortization schedule through December 31, Year 2 (and state remaining unamortized discount for the full issue after Year 2).  
(c) Record the December 31, Year 2 interest/amortization entry for the **full** issue (before conversion).  
(d) Record the **partial conversion** of 50% of the bonds on December 31, Year 2.  
(e) Briefly state the carrying amount of **remaining** convertible debt after the partial conversion.

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — Issuance**

- Issue price = $360,000 × 0.97 = **$349,200**  
- Discount = $360,000 − $349,200 = **$10,800**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 349,200 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 10,800 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 360,000 |

Debits **$360,000** = credits **$360,000** ✓  
No equity allocation for the conversion feature.

**(b) SL amortization schedule (full issue)**

- Annual cash interest = $360,000 × 5% = **$18,000**  
- Annual discount amortization = $10,800 ÷ 6 = **$1,800**  
- Annual interest expense = $18,000 + $1,800 = **$19,800**

| Date | Cash interest | Discount amort. | Interest expense | Ending unamort. discount | Ending carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1/1/Y1 | — | — | — | 10,800 | 349,200 |
| 12/31/Y1 | 18,000 | 1,800 | 19,800 | 9,000 | 351,000 |
| 12/31/Y2 | 18,000 | 1,800 | 19,800 | 7,200 | 352,800 |

Check: $10,800 − (2 × $1,800) = **$7,200** unamortized discount; $360,000 − $7,200 = **$352,800** carrying ✓

**(c) December 31, Year 2 — Interest and amortization (full issue)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 19,800 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,800 |
| Cash | | 18,000 |

Debits **$19,800** = credits **$19,800** ✓

**(d) December 31, Year 2 — 50% conversion**

- Face converted = 50% × $360,000 = **$180,000** (180 bonds)  
- Unamortized discount removed = 50% × $7,200 = **$3,600**  
- Carrying amount converted = $180,000 − $3,600 = **$176,400**  
- Shares issued = 180 × 12 = **2,160**  
- Common Stock = 2,160 × $5 = **$10,800**  
- APIC = $176,400 − $10,800 = **$165,600**  
- Stock market value (2,160 × $62) is **not** used.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 180,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 3,600 |
| Common Stock | | 10,800 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 165,600 |

Debits **$180,000** = credits **$180,000** ✓  
No gain or loss.

**(e) Remaining convertible debt after partial conversion**  
- Remaining face = **$180,000**  
- Remaining unamortized discount = **$3,600**  
- Remaining carrying amount = **$176,400** (liability presentation continues for the unconverted half)

**Key insight:** Partial conversion removes a **proportionate** slice of face and unamortized discount/premium; equity is credited for the **net carrying amount** of the portion converted only.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Mid-period update + induced conversion
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Update interest/amortization to conversion date; induced conversion expense for cash sweetener; equity at carrying amount  
**Scenario:** On January 1, Year 1, **Northpine Logistics Inc.** issues **$300,000** face of **6%**, **5-year** convertible bonds for **$288,000** cash (discount **$12,000**). Interest is payable **annually each December 31**. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **15** shares of Northpine **$1** par common stock. Straight-line amortization is used.

On **October 1, Year 3**, to reduce leverage, Northpine offers bondholders an **inducement of $25 cash per $1,000 bond** if they convert within a limited window under the original share conversion ratio. All holders accept on October 1, Year 3. Northpine stock trades at **$40** per share. Northpine (1) updates interest and amortization from the last interest date (January 1 / prior Dec 31 payment) through October 1, (2) records the induced conversion, and (3) pays the accrued cash interest and the cash inducement.

Assume the inducement qualifies for **induced conversion** accounting (limited-time offer of consideration under the existing instrument’s conversion framework).

**Required:**  
(a) Compute months elapsed from issuance to conversion, total discount amortized to conversion, and unamortized discount on October 1, Year 3 (before conversion entry).  
(b) Record the **October 1, Year 3** adjusting entry for **9 months** of interest and discount amortization.  
(c) Record the **induced conversion** on October 1, Year 3 (including debt conversion expense and cash inducement).  
(d) Record payment of **accrued interest payable**.  
(e) **Classification/disclosure angle:** Contrast (i) ordinary conversion under original terms with (ii) induced conversion regarding **gain/loss on debt carrying amount** vs **expense for the inducement**, and state how the convertible issue was classified immediately before conversion.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Amortization to conversion date**

- Total term = 5 years = **60** months  
- Monthly discount amortization = $12,000 ÷ 60 = **$200**  
- Elapsed to Oct 1, Year 3 = 2 years + 9 months = **33** months  
- Discount amortized = 33 × $200 = **$6,600**  
- Unamortized discount at conversion = $12,000 − $6,600 = **$5,400**  
- Carrying amount at conversion = $300,000 − $5,400 = **$294,600**

**(b) October 1, Year 3 — Accrue 9 months interest and amortize discount**

- 9 months cash interest = $300,000 × 6% × 9/12 = **$13,500**  
- 9 months discount amort. = 9 × $200 = **$1,800**  
- Interest expense = $13,500 + $1,800 = **$15,300**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 15,300 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,800 |
| Interest Payable | | 13,500 |

Debits **$15,300** = credits **$15,300** ✓  
(After this entry, unamortized discount = $12,000 − $6,600 = **$5,400**, consistent with (a).)

**(c) October 1, Year 3 — Induced conversion**

- Bonds: 300; shares = 300 × 15 = **4,500**  
- Common Stock = 4,500 × $1 = **$4,500**  
- APIC from debt carrying amount = $294,600 − $4,500 = **$290,100**  
- Cash inducement = 300 × $25 = **$7,500** → **Debt Conversion Expense $7,500**  
- Market value of stock (4,500 × $40) is **not** used to measure the equity issued for the converted debt; the inducement is measured at FV of **extra** consideration ($7,500 cash).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 300,000 | |
| Debt Conversion Expense | 7,500 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 5,400 |
| Common Stock | | 4,500 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 290,100 |
| Cash | | 7,500 |

Debits **$307,500** = credits **$307,500** ✓  
No separate gain/loss on extinguishment of the **net carrying amount** of the debt; the **$7,500** inducement is recognized as **expense**.

**(d) Pay accrued interest**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 13,500 | |
| Cash | | 13,500 |

Debits **$13,500** = credits **$13,500** ✓

**(e) Classification / presentation / disclosure**  
- Immediately **before** conversion: convertible bonds remain a **liability** (net of unamortized discount), not equity.  
- **Ordinary conversion:** carrying amount → equity; **no** income-statement gain/loss.  
- **Induced conversion:** same book-value treatment of debt-to-equity, **plus** expense equal to FV of consideration **in excess of** securities issuable under original conversion terms (here, cash $7,500). The inducement is **not** treated as a classic early-extinguishment gain/loss on the debt’s carrying amount when induced-conversion criteria are met.  
- Disclosure typically highlights conversion terms, shares reserved, and material conversion activity / inducement costs of the period.

**Key insight:** Always **update** interest and amortization to the conversion date first; induced conversion still equity-credits the debt **carrying amount**, and separately expenses only the **sweetener**.

### Q4 — MC — Issuance classification / method
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Convertible debt proceeds recorded entirely as liability (no equity split for conversion option)  
**Question:** On issuance of conventional convertible bonds with an embedded conversion feature that is **not** required to be bifurcated as a derivative, U.S. GAAP (ASC 470-20) generally requires the issuer to:

- A) Allocate proceeds between Bonds Payable and Paid-In Capital based on relative fair values of the debt without the option and the conversion option  
- B) Record the entire proceeds as a **liability** (with any premium or discount), with **no** portion of proceeds attributed to equity for the conversion feature  
- C) Record the entire proceeds as equity because conversion is probable  
- D) Credit a temporary “mezzanine” equity account equal to the intrinsic value of the conversion option at issuance  

**Answer:** **B.** Typical convertible debt is accounted for **in its entirety as a liability** at issuance; the conversion feature is not allocated to equity under the standard straight-debt model (contrast with bonds issued **with detachable warrants**, which **do** allocate proceeds—outside this LO’s core conversion accounting).

### Q5 — MC — Conversion measurement
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** No gain/loss on conversion; equity measured at debt carrying amount  
**Question:** **LumenRock Corp.** converts $200,000 face convertible bonds (unamortized premium $8,000; carrying amount $208,000) into 10,000 shares of $2 par common stock under original conversion terms when the stock’s market price is $30. What amount of **Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par** is recognized, and is a gain or loss recorded?

- A) APIC $300,000; gain $92,000  
- B) APIC $188,000; no gain or loss  
- C) APIC $180,000; no gain or loss  
- D) APIC $208,000; loss $0 but Common Stock credited $208,000  

**Answer:** **B.** Common Stock = 10,000 × $2 = **$20,000**; APIC = carrying amount $208,000 − $20,000 = **$188,000**; **no** gain or loss. Market value ($300,000) is irrelevant under the carrying-amount method for conversion under original terms.
