# Agent 179 — CORE DEMO — LO 9-1

**Chapter:** 9  
**LO title:** Determine the initial recognition and measurement of inventory  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Control-based recognition:** inventoriable goods controlled on the inventory date are recognized as inventory regardless of physical location
- **Goods in transit:** f.o.b. shipping point → purchaser controls when released to carrier; f.o.b. destination → control passes on receipt
- **Consignment:** consignor includes; consignee excludes until end-customer sale
- **Firm repurchase agreements:** if seller is obligated to buy back, inventory remains on seller’s books (financing)
- **Initial measurement:** cash-equivalent acquisition cost + costs to bring goods to condition and location for sale (invoice, taxes/tariffs, freight-in, transit insurance, prep labor, normal storage/handling)
- **Not inventoriable:** freight-out, selling/G&A, abnormal spoilage/rush freight, interest (generally period charges)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-date rollforward of controlled inventory by location/category (on hand, purchases in transit, own goods on consignment, under firm repurchase)
- **Period-end adjusting JEs:** restore control errors (premature FOB destination sales; firm-repurchase “sales”)
- **Disposal / settlement:** consignee sale to end customer → record revenue, commission, COGS, and remove inventory

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-date subsequent inventory schedule, initial cost JE, YE control adj, consignment settlement
**LO:** LO 9-1  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule of controlled inventory by category; initial inventoriable-cost JE; period-end control adjustments; consignment disposal  
**Scenario:**  
**Solstice Trading Co.** is a wholesale merchandiser using a **perpetual** inventory system. Year-end is **December 31**. Management tracks controlled inventory in four presentation categories for the subsequent-measurement schedule:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| On hand | In Solstice warehouses / receiving |
| In transit (purchases) | Purchased goods, f.o.b. shipping point, not yet received |
| On consignment | Solstice’s goods held by dealers (Solstice is consignor) |
| Under firm repurchase | Inventory subject to obligatory buyback financing |

**Opening composition — July 1, Year 1**

| Category | Amount |
|---|---:|
| On hand | $210,000 |
| In transit (purchases) | 0 |
| On consignment | 0 |
| Under firm repurchase | 0 |
| **Total controlled inventory** | **$210,000** |

**Events (Year 1):**

1. **July 15 — Initial purchase / measurement of a winter-goods lot**

| Cost element | Amount | Settled by |
|---|---:|---|
| Invoice price from vendor | $55,000 | Accounts payable |
| Sales tax on purchase | 3,300 | Accounts payable |
| Freight-in (f.o.b. shipping point) | 1,680 | Cash |
| Transit insurance on the purchase | 420 | Cash |
| Wages to assemble/prep units for resale | 2,250 | Wages payable |
| Normal warehouse storage before sale | 360 | Cash |
| Abnormal rush freight after a warehouse flood damaged unrelated stock | 1,050 | Cash |
| Sales promotion (selling) | 900 | Cash (period cost; **not inventoriable**) |

Goods are received July 15 and placed **on hand**.

2. **August 20:** Ship merchandise **cost $16,500** to **Northpine Dealers** on **consignment**. Solstice remains the consignor (control retained). Reclassify within inventory categories only (no sale).

3. **October 5 — Consignment settlement:** Northpine sells **$5,500** cost of Solstice’s goods for **$8,800** cash to end customers, deducts a **10%** commission, and remits the net to Solstice the same day.

4. **December 22:** Purchase merchandise **f.o.b. shipping point**, invoice **$28,000** plus freight-in **$1,200** (both unpaid; credit Accounts Payable). Goods are **still in transit** at December 31. Record the purchase as inventoriable cost in the **in-transit** category.

5. **December 27:** Sell merchandise **f.o.b. shipping point** to a customer: selling price **$17,500** on account; cost **$11,000**. Goods leave the warehouse Dec 27 (customer controls). Record sale and COGS properly.

6. **December 29:** Sell merchandise **f.o.b. destination**: selling price **$11,400** on account; cost **$7,200**. Sale and COGS were **recorded Dec 29**, but the goods have **not been received by the customer** at December 31 (Solstice still controls). Period-end reverse required.

7. **December 30:** Cash “sale” under a **firm repurchase obligation** (Solstice must buy the goods back at a fixed price). Cash received **$17,000**; inventory cost **$14,000** was removed and sales/COGS were recorded. Financing arrangement — restore inventory and reclassify proceeds as a liability at period-end.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute **inventoriable cost** of the July 15 lot. Record the **July 15 journal entry(ies)** for inventoriable costs and abnormal freight (exclude or separately record the promotion). Show Dr = Cr.  
(b) Record the **August 20** reclassification (on hand → on consignment).

(d) Record the **December 22** purchase (in transit).  
(e) Record the **December 27** sale and COGS.  
(f) Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** (emphasis) after each of July 15, Aug 20, Oct 5, Dec 22, and Dec 27, rolling forward: On hand; In transit; On consignment; Under firm repurchase; Total controlled. Start from the July 1 opening balances. **Ignore** items 6–7 until part (g).  
(g) Record **December 31 period-end adjusting entries** for items 6 and 7.  
(h) Extend the subsequent measurement schedule through the YE adjustments in (g) and report the **December 31** balance-sheet total for Inventory by category.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — period-end physical-count bridge and inventoriable cost classification
**LO:** LO 9-1  
**Concept:** Period-end reconciling schedule from physical count to adjusted ending inventory; inventoriable vs period costs; YE adjusting JEs; consignment settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Home Goods Inc.** took a physical inventory on **December 31**. The unadjusted physical count (goods in the warehouse) is **$462,000**. Books were set equal to this raw count before the controller completed the LO 9-1 control and measurement review.

### Q4 — MC — inventoriable cost classification
**LO:** LO 9-1  
**Concept:** Classification — inventoriable product cost vs period / abnormal charge  
**Question:**  
Which cost is **correctly included** in the initial measurement of a merchandiser’s inventory?

- A) Freight-out on goods shipped f.o.b. destination to a customer  
- B) Abnormal rush shipping costs incurred after a warehouse flood destroyed unrelated merchandise  
- C) Import tariffs and freight-in on goods purchased f.o.b. shipping point that remain in transit at year-end  
- D) Monthly salaries of corporate accountants (general and administrative)

**Answer:** **C.** Import tariffs and freight-in are cash-equivalent costs to acquire inventory and bring it to condition and location for sale; f.o.b. shipping-point goods in transit are controlled by the purchaser and included in inventory. Freight-out is a selling cost; abnormal rush freight is a period charge; G&A salaries are period expenses (ASC 330 product vs period logic).

### Q5 — MC — control recognition at period-end
**LO:** LO 9-1  
**Concept:** Classification — goods in transit / consignment control test  
**Question:**  
On December 31, which item should **Redline Wholesale Co.** **include** in its ending inventory?

- A) Goods Redline holds as **consignee** for a supplier, displayed in Redline’s showroom  
- B) Goods Redline purchased that are still in transit, shipped **f.o.b. destination**, not yet received  
- C) Goods Redline sold that are still in transit to the customer, shipped **f.o.b. shipping point**  
- D) Goods Redline owns that are held by a dealer under a **consignment** arrangement (Redline is consignor)

**Answer:** **D.** The consignor retains control until the consignee sells to the end customer, so consigned-out goods remain in the consignor’s inventory. Consignee goods (A) are excluded; f.o.b. destination purchases (B) remain the seller’s until receipt; f.o.b. shipping sales (C) belong to the customer once released to the carrier.
