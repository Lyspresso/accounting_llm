# Agent 297 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-3

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Prepare the investing activities section of the statement of cash flows  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Investing activities report **gross** cash inflows and outflows from long-lived productive assets, nontrading investments, intangibles, and nontrade loans
- **Period-end adjusting JEs** (depreciation, amortization, fair-value marks) update carrying amounts used in T-account analysis but are **not** investing cash flows
- **Subsequent-measurement schedules / T-accounts** for PPE and Accumulated Depreciation solve for cash purchases and cost/AD removed on disposal
- Reconstruct **cash proceeds** from PPE sale or insurance settlement from book value ± gain/loss; report full proceeds (not the gain/loss) as investing
- **Initial recognition JEs** for cash purchases of PPE, investments, and loans made
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JEs** for sale of equipment, sale of investments, insurance recovery, and principal collection
- Only cash paid at (or soon before/after) purchase of productive assets is an investing outflow; seller-financed portion is **noncash** (disclosed)
- Collection of nontrade loan **principal** = investing inflow; interest = operating
- Equity/debt investments **not held specifically for resale** → purchase and sale cash flows are investing (gains/losses reconciling items under the indirect method)

### Q1 — CORE — Northvale Precision Tools: period-end adjusting entries, PPE schedules, full investing section
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Prepare cash flows from investing activities — emphasize period-end adjusting JEs (depreciation/amortization) that feed PPE/AD schedules; plug cash purchases; record purchase/disposal JEs; report gross investing flows  
**Scenario:**  
**Northvale Precision Tools Inc.** is preparing its statement of cash flows for the year ended **December 31, 2025**. Selected comparative balances and additional information follow.

| Account | Dec 31, 2024 | Dec 31, 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | $640,000 | $895,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation—equipment | 220,000 | 268,000 |
| Equity investments (FV through NI; **not** held for trading/resale) | 72,000 | 58,000 |
| Long-term notes receivable (nontrade) | 110,000 | 95,000 |
| Patent, net | 40,000 | 28,000 |

**Additional information for 2025:**
1. Equipment that cost **$85,000** with accumulated depreciation of **$52,000** was sold for **$29,000** cash.  
2. Depreciation expense on equipment was **$100,000** (recorded as a year-end adjusting entry).  
3. Patent amortization expense was **$12,000** (recorded as a year-end adjusting entry). There were no patent purchases or sales.  
4. All equipment purchases were for **cash** (no notes or stock issued for equipment).  
5. Equity investments were purchased for **$18,000** cash. Investments with a carrying amount of **$32,000** were sold for **$35,000** cash. Ending carrying amount reflects only purchases and sales (no residual year-end fair-value adjustment remains in the ending balance).  
6. Northvale made a new nontrade loan of **$30,000** cash and collected **$45,000** of principal on existing long-term notes receivable. Interest collected is **not** included in the $45,000.  
7. There were no other investing activities.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the **December 31, 2025** period-end adjusting entries for **depreciation** and **patent amortization**.  
(b) Reconstruct the **Equipment** and **Accumulated depreciation—equipment** subsequent-measurement schedules. Solve for **cash purchases of equipment**.  
(c) Reconstruct the **Equity investments**, **Long-term notes receivable**, and **Patent, net** rollforward schedules.  
(d) Prepare the journal entry to record the **sale of equipment**.  
(e) Prepare the journal entries for the **purchase of equipment**, **purchase of equity investments**, **sale of equity investments**, **loan advanced**, and **principal collection** on notes receivable.  
(f) Prepare the **Cash flows from investing activities** section of the 2025 statement of cash flows.  
(g) Briefly state how the **equipment sale loss**, **investment sale gain**, **depreciation**, and **patent amortization** are treated under the **indirect** operating section (do not prepare the full operating section). Explain why the period-end adjusting entries do **not** appear in investing.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Period-end adjusting entries**

Depreciation:  
```
Dr Depreciation Expense .................... 100,000
   Cr Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment ........ 100,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 100,000)

Patent amortization:  
```
Dr Amortization Expense—Patent .............. 12,000
   Cr Patent ........................................ 12,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 12,000)

**(b) PPE subsequent-measurement schedules**

**Equipment**

| | Debit | Credit |

| Beg. bal. | 640,000 | |
| Purchases (plug) | **340,000** | |
| Cost of equipment sold | | 85,000 |
| End. bal. | 895,000 | |

Check: \(640{,}000 + 340{,}000 - 85{,}000 = 895{,}000\).

**Accumulated depreciation—equipment**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | | 220,000 |
| Depreciation expense (period-end adj.) | | 100,000 |
| AD removed on sale | 52,000 | |
| End. bal. | | 268,000 |

Check: \(220{,}000 + 100{,}000 - 52{,}000 = 268{,}000\).

**Cash purchases of equipment = $340,000.**

**(c) Investment, notes, and patent schedules**

**Equity investments**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | 72,000 | |
| Purchases | 18,000 | |
| Carrying amount sold | | 32,000 |
| End. bal. | 58,000 | |

Check: \(72{,}000 + 18{,}000 - 32{,}000 = 58{,}000\).

**Long-term notes receivable (nontrade)**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | 110,000 | |
| Loans advanced | 30,000 | |
| Principal collections | | 45,000 |
| End. bal. | 95,000 | |

Check: \(110{,}000 + 30{,}000 - 45{,}000 = 95{,}000\).

**Patent, net (contrast — period-end amort only):** \(40{,}000 - 12{,}000 = 28{,}000\). No cash investing effect.

**(d) Disposal JE — sale of equipment**  
Book value \(= 85{,}000 - 52{,}000 = 33{,}000\); proceeds \(29{,}000\); **loss = $4,000**.  
```
Dr Cash ..................................... 29,000
Dr Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment ...... 52,000
Dr Loss on Sale of Equipment ................  4,000
   Cr Equipment ...................................... 85,000
```
(Dr 85,000 = Cr 85,000)

**(e) Initial recognition / settlement JEs**

Purchase of equipment:  
```
Dr Equipment ............................... 340,000
   Cr Cash ......................................... 340,000
```

Purchase of equity investments:  
```
Dr Equity Investments ...................... 18,000
   Cr Cash .......................................... 18,000
```

Sale of equity investments (carrying amount $32,000; proceeds $35,000; **gain $3,000**):  
```

   Cr Equity Investments ............................ 32,000
   Cr Gain on Sale of Investments ...................  3,000
```
(Dr 35,000 = Cr 35,000)

Loan advanced (nontrade):  
```
Dr Long-Term Notes Receivable .............. 30,000
   Cr Cash .......................................... 30,000
```

```

  Purchase of equity investments ................   (18,000)
  Loans to others ...............................   (30,000)

```

Inflows \(29{,}000 + 35{,}000 + 45{,}000 = 109{,}000\); outflows \(340{,}000 + 18{,}000 + 30{,}000 = 388{,}000\); net \(109{,}000 - 388{,}000 = \mathbf{\$(279{,}000)}\).

**(g) Indirect operating treatment / non-investing period-end items**  
- **Loss on sale of equipment $4,000:** added back to net income in the operating section (full cash proceeds appear in investing).  
- **Gain on sale of investments $3,000:** deducted from net income in the operating section (full cash proceeds appear in investing).  
- **Depreciation $100,000** and **patent amortization $12,000:** added back to net income in the operating section as noncash expenses. They are **period-end adjusting entries** that update Accumulated Depreciation and Patent for schedule analysis; they do **not** appear in the investing section because **no cash** changed hands.

**Key insight:** Period-end adjusting entries for depreciation and amortization are essential inputs to PPE/AD (and intangible) schedules that solve for cash purchases and verify disposals—but only the **gross cash** purchase, sale, loan, and collection amounts appear in investing activities.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Cedarwick Packaging twin
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Prepare cash flows from investing activities — all numbers changed (period-end depreciation feeds AD schedule; PPE/investment/loan rollforwards)  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarwick Packaging LLC** is preparing its investing activities section for the year ended **December 31, 2026**.

| Account | Dec 31, 2025 | Dec 31, 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | $450,000 | $680,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation—equipment | 140,000 | 175,000 |
| Debt investments (amortized cost; **not** held for trading/resale) | 50,000 | 67,000 |

3. All equipment purchases were cash purchases.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Ashford Renewable: insurance settlement, mixed cash/noncash PPE, land sale, period-end FV adj
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Investing section with insurance recovery on destroyed PPE, cash construction, land sale, investment sale, cash-only portion of equipment purchase, and patent purchase; period-end adjusting JEs (depreciation and FV mark) excluded from investing; noncash disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Ashford Renewable Systems Corp.** had the following events in **2025** (fiscal year-end December 31):

1. A tornado completely destroyed a storage building. Original cost **$525,000**; accumulated depreciation at the date of the tornado **$360,000**. Insurance paid Ashford **$190,000** cash in full settlement.  
2. Ashford constructed a replacement building for **$780,000** cash (amount includes **$18,000** of capitalized cash interest paid during construction).  
3. Purchased production equipment with a cash price of **$185,000**. Payment: **$125,000** cash down and a **$60,000** three-year note payable to the seller.  
4. Sold equity investments (not held for trading) with a carrying amount of **$42,000** for **$48,000** cash.  
5. Purchased a patent for **$28,000** cash.  
6. Sold vacant land (cost **$45,000**) for **$70,000** cash.  
7. At December 31, Ashford recorded the following **period-end adjusting entries** (no cash):  
   - Depreciation expense **$88,000** on remaining depreciable assets.  
   - Unrealized holding gain **$6,000** on remaining equity investments measured at fair value through net income.  
8. No other investing or PPE transactions are needed for analysis beyond the above.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the journal entry to remove the destroyed building and record the **insurance settlement** (settlement/disposal JE).  
(b) Prepare the **initial recognition** journal entries for the replacement building construction and the equipment purchase (cash + note).  
(c) Prepare the equity-investment sale JE, land-sale JE, and patent-purchase JE.  
(d) Prepare the two **period-end adjusting entries** described in item 7.  
(e) Prepare the **Cash flows from investing activities** section for 2025.  
(f) Prepare the **noncash investing and financing activities** disclosure related to the equipment purchase.  
(g) Explain (i) why only **$125,000** of the equipment acquisition is an investing outflow, (ii) why capitalized cash interest is included in the building investing outflow, and (iii) why the **period-end adjusting entries** do not appear in investing.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Insurance settlement / disposal of building**  
Carrying amount \(= 525{,}000 - 360{,}000 = 165{,}000\); cash proceeds \(190{,}000\); **gain = $25,000**.  
```
Dr Cash .................................... 190,000
Dr Accumulated Depreciation—Building ...... 360,000
   Cr Building ...................................... 525,000
   Cr Gain on Insurance Settlement ..................  25,000
```
(Dr 550,000 = Cr 550,000)

**(b) Initial recognition — building construction and equipment**  
```
Dr Building ................................ 780,000
   Cr Cash ......................................... 780,000
```
(Includes $18,000 capitalized cash interest as part of the cost of the productive asset.)

```
Dr Equipment ............................... 185,000
   Cr Cash ......................................... 125,000
   Cr Notes Payable—Seller .........................  60,000
```
(Dr 185,000 = Cr 185,000)

**(c) Investment sale, land sale, patent purchase**  
```
Dr Cash ..................................... 48,000
   Cr Equity Investments ............................ 42,000
   Cr Gain on Sale of Investments ...................  6,000

Dr Cash ..................................... 70,000
   Cr Land ........................................... 45,000
   Cr Gain on Sale of Land ........................... 25,000

Dr Patent ................................... 28,000
   Cr Cash .......................................... 28,000
```
(Investment sale: Dr 48,000 = Cr 48,000; land: Dr 70,000 = Cr 70,000; patent: Dr = Cr = 28,000)

**(d) Period-end adjusting entries (noncash)**  
```
Dr Depreciation Expense ..................... 88,000
   Cr Accumulated Depreciation ...................... 88,000

Dr Equity Investments ........................ 6,000
   Cr Unrealized Holding Gain—Equity Investments .....  6,000
```
(Each balances; neither is a cash flow.)

**(e) Cash flows from investing activities — 2025**

```
Cash flows from investing activities
  Proceeds from insurance settlement ............ $ 190,000
  Proceeds from sale of equity investments ......    48,000
  Proceeds from sale of land ....................    70,000
  Purchase of building (incl. capitalized interest) (780,000)
  Purchase of equipment .........................  (125,000)
  Purchase of patent ............................   (28,000)
Net cash used by investing activities .......... $(625,000)
```

Inflows \(190{,}000 + 48{,}000 + 70{,}000 = 308{,}000\); outflows \(780{,}000 + 125{,}000 + 28{,}000 = 933{,}000\); net \(308{,}000 - 933{,}000 = \mathbf{\$(625{,}000)}\).

**Note:** Full insurance and land-sale proceeds are investing inflows (related gains removed from NI in operating under the indirect method). Depreciation and the FV unrealized gain are **not** listed in investing.

**(f) Noncash disclosure**

```
Noncash investing and financing activities
  Purchased equipment by paying cash and issuing a long-term note:
    Cost of equipment ........................... $185,000
    Cash paid ...................................  125,000
    Note payable issued to seller ............... $ 60,000
```

**(g) Classification rationale**  
(i) ASC 230 reports as investing outflows only amounts paid **at the time of purchase or soon before or after** for PPE. The seller-financed **$60,000** is a noncash exchange (asset acquired by assuming a liability). Subsequent principal payments on that note are **financing** outflows.  
(ii) Capitalized cash interest is part of the cost of acquiring the productive asset and is an investing outflow (ASC 230-10-45-13(c)).  
(iii) Period-end depreciation and fair-value adjusting entries update carrying amounts and affect net income (or AOCI for other classifications), but **no cash** moves—so they never appear as investing inflows or outflows. The unrealized holding gain is a reconciling item in **operating** under the indirect method if it is included in net income.

**Key insight:** The investing section is a **cash** statement: full insurance/land/investment sale proceeds in, cash portion of PPE and intangibles out (including capitalized cash interest), seller financing **disclosed as noncash**, and period-end adjusting JEs used only for analysis—not reported as investing cash flows.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of investing cash flows vs period-end adjustments
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Distinguish investing cash flows from period-end noncash adjusting entries and from operating items  
**Question:**  
Which of the following is **correctly** reported in the **investing activities** section of the statement of cash flows under U.S. GAAP (ASC 230)?

- A) Year-end depreciation adjusting entry of $75,000 is reported as a $75,000 investing cash outflow.  
- B) Purchase of equipment for $200,000 cash and sale of different equipment for $40,000 cash are netted, showing a single $160,000 investing outflow.  
- C) Cash proceeds of $55,000 from sale of equipment (book value $48,000; gain $7,000) are reported as a **$55,000** investing inflow.  
- D) Principal collection of $20,000 and interest collection of $2,400 on a nontrade note receivable are both reported as investing inflows totaling $22,400.

**Answer:** **C.** Investing activities report the **full cash proceeds** from sale of PPE as an inflow; the gain is not the investing amount (the gain is removed from NI in the operating section under the indirect method).  

- A is wrong: depreciation is a **period-end noncash** adjusting entry adjusted in **operating** (indirect method), not an investing flow.  
- B is wrong: ASC 230 requires **gross** presentation of investing inflows and outflows (purchase and sale reported separately).  
- D is wrong: **interest** collected is an **operating** inflow; only principal collection is investing.

### Q5 — MC — Net cash used by investing (insurance + rebuild + land sale)
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Compute net cash used by investing activities when insurance proceeds, cash reconstruction, and a land sale all occur  
**Question:**  
A flood destroyed a building with original cost **$1,200,000** and carrying amount **$450,000**. Insurance paid the company **$410,000** cash. The company then constructed a replacement building for **$1,350,000** cash and sold unrelated vacant land (cost $90,000) for **$120,000** cash. Ignoring all other transactions, what is **net cash used by investing activities** related to these events?

- A) $1,350,000  
- B) $940,000  
- C) $820,000  
- D) $1,640,000  

**Answer:** **C. $820,000.**  
Investing inflows: insurance proceeds **$410,000** + land sale proceeds **$120,000** = **$530,000**.  
Investing outflow: cash paid to construct building **$1,350,000**.  
Net cash used = \(1{,}350{,}000 - 530{,}000 = \mathbf{\$820{,}000}\).  

(The carrying amount and any insurance loss of $40,000 affect the income statement / operating reconciling items, not the investing cash totals. Choice A ignores both inflows; B ignores the land sale; D adds cost instead of netting proceeds.)
