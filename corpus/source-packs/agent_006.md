# Agent 6 — CORE DEMO — LO 14-5

**Chapter:** 14  
**LO title:** Account for equity securities following the equity method  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Initial recognition of equity-method investment at cost
- Cost differential allocation (BV share, depreciable basis difference, goodwill)
- Subsequent equity-method pickup of investee earnings
- Excess depreciation/amortization of depreciable basis difference
- Dividends as reduction of Investment (not dividend revenue)
- Fair value changes ignored under equity method (unless impaired)
- Investment account rollforward schedule
- Partial-year equity pickup and basis amortization
- Disposal of equity-method investment (gain/loss)
- Classification/presentation (noncurrent investment; SCF indirect-method reconciling item)
- Equity method vs FV-NI method choice when significant influence exists

### Q1 — CORE — Harborline Capital: equity-method purchase through year-end (initial recognition emphasis)
**LO:** LO 14-5  
**Concept:** Initial recognition at cost; allocate excess of cost over BV share to depreciable assets and goodwill; record share of earnings, excess depreciation, and dividends; ignore FV changes  
**Scenario:** On **January 1, Year 1**, Harborline Capital Inc. purchases **40%** of the voting common stock of Crestview Fabricators Corp. for **$320,000 cash**. Harborline has **significant influence** over Crestview and accounts for the investment under the **equity method**. Harborline intends to hold the investment indefinitely (noncurrent).

Crestview’s January 1 balance sheet (and fair values) immediately after Harborline’s purchase:

| Item | Book value | Fair value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nondepreciable assets | $500,000 | $500,000 |
| Depreciable assets (net) | 200,000 | 250,000 |
| Total liabilities | 150,000 | 150,000 |
| Stockholders’ equity | 550,000 | — |

Depreciable assets have a **10-year** remaining useful life, straight-line, no residual value. Any excess of Harborline’s cost over the fair value of net assets acquired is attributable to **goodwill**.

During Year 1, Crestview reports **net income of $90,000** and declares and pays cash dividends of **$30,000** on December 31. At December 31, the fair value of Harborline’s 40% interest is **$345,000**. Harborline’s year-end is December 31.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the **January 1** journal entry to record the purchase.  
(b) Prepare a **cost differential allocation schedule** at acquisition (BV share, depreciable excess, goodwill).  
(c) Prepare the **December 31** entry for Harborline’s share of Crestview’s net income.  
(d) Prepare the **December 31** entry for excess depreciation on the depreciable basis difference.  
(e) Prepare the **December 31** entry for dividends declared and received.  
(f) Prepare the entry (if any) on **December 31** to adjust the investment to fair value.  
(g) Prepare an **investment account rollforward** and state the December 31 carrying amount.  
(h) What is the **net Year 1 income-statement effect** of this investment for Harborline?

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Crestview Stock | 320,000 | |
| Cash | | 320,000 |

Check: Dr $320,000 = Cr $320,000.

**(b) Cost differential allocation at acquisition**

- Ownership: **40%**
- Share of book value of net assets = 0.40 × $550,000 = **$220,000**
- Excess of cost over BV share = $320,000 − $220,000 = **$100,000**
- Allocated to depreciable assets = 0.40 × ($250,000 − $200,000) = 0.40 × $50,000 = **$20,000**
- Residual to goodwill = $100,000 − $20,000 = **$80,000** (not amortized)

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Share of book value | $220,000 |
| Depreciable basis difference | 20,000 |
| Goodwill | 80,000 |
| **Acquisition cost** | **$320,000** |

**(c) December 31 — share of investee net income**

Share of NI = 0.40 × $90,000 = **$36,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Crestview Stock | 36,000 | |
| Investment Income | | 36,000 |

Check: Dr $36,000 = Cr $36,000.

**(d) December 31 — excess depreciation (basis difference)**

Annual excess depreciation = $20,000 ÷ 10 = **$2,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment Income | 2,000 | |
| Investment in Crestview Stock | | 2,000 |

Check: Dr $2,000 = Cr $2,000.  
(No adjustment for land/nondepreciable assets or goodwill.)

**(e) December 31 — dividends (return of investment, not revenue)**

Share of dividends = 0.40 × $30,000 = **$12,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 12,000 | |
| Investment in Crestview Stock | | 12,000 |

Check: Dr $12,000 = Cr $12,000.

**(f) Fair value adjustment**

**No entry.** Under the equity method, changes in fair value are **ignored** (unless the investment is impaired — LO 14-6). Quoted FV of $345,000 does not affect measurement.

**(g) Investment account rollforward (subsequent measurement schedule)**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Jan 1 purchase cost | $320,000 |
| + Share of Crestview NI | 36,000 |
| − Excess depreciation | (2,000) |
| − Share of dividends | (12,000) |
| **Dec 31 carrying amount** | **$342,000** |

T-account check: 320,000 + 36,000 − 2,000 − 12,000 = **$342,000**.

**(h) Net Year 1 income-statement effect**

Investment income (net) = $36,000 − $2,000 = **$34,000**.  
(Dividends do **not** increase income under the equity method.)

**Key insight:** Equity-method initial recognition is at **cost**, then the investor “one-line consolidates”: pick up ownership % of investee earnings, amortize only **depreciable/amortizable** basis differences against Investment Income, reduce the Investment for dividends, and **ignore** ordinary FV changes.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Pinebridge Advisors: equity-method cycle (all numbers changed)
**LO:** LO 14-5  
**Concept:** Initial recognition, cost differential (depreciable excess + goodwill), share of earnings, excess depreciation, dividends, FV ignored, ending balance  
**Scenario:** On **January 1, Year 1**, Pinebridge Advisors LLC purchases **25%** of the voting common stock of Oakmont Components Inc. for **$180,000 cash**. Pinebridge has significant influence and uses the **equity method**.

Oakmont’s January 1 net assets:

| Item | Book value | Fair value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nondepreciable assets | $420,000 | $420,000 |
| Depreciable assets (net) | 280,000 | 328,000 |
| Total liabilities | 100,000 | 100,000 |
| Stockholders’ equity | 600,000 | — |

Depreciable assets: **8-year** remaining life, straight-line, no residual. Residual excess of cost over FV of net assets acquired is **goodwill**.

During Year 1, Oakmont reports **net income of $64,000** and declares and pays dividends of **$24,000** on December 31. December 31 fair value of Pinebridge’s interest: **$195,000**. Year-end December 31.

**Required:** Same structure as Q1 (a)–(h): purchase JE; allocation schedule; share of NI; excess depreciation; dividends; FV entry if any; investment rollforward; net IS effect.

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 1 purchase**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Oakmont Stock | 180,000 | |
| Cash | | 180,000 |

Check: Dr $180,000 = Cr $180,000.

**(b) Allocation**

- Share of BV = 0.25 × $600,000 = **$150,000**
- Excess of cost over BV = $180,000 − $150,000 = **$30,000**
- Depreciable allocation = 0.25 × ($328,000 − $280,000) = 0.25 × $48,000 = **$12,000**
- Goodwill = $30,000 − $12,000 = **$18,000**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Share of book value | $150,000 |
| Depreciable basis difference | 12,000 |
| Goodwill | 18,000 |
| **Acquisition cost** | **$180,000** |

**(c) Share of NI** = 0.25 × $64,000 = **$16,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Oakmont Stock | 16,000 | |
| Investment Income | | 16,000 |

**(d) Excess depreciation** = $12,000 ÷ 8 = **$1,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment Income | 1,500 | |
| Investment in Oakmont Stock | | 1,500 |

**(e) Dividends** = 0.25 × $24,000 = **$6,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 6,000 | |
| Investment in Oakmont Stock | | 6,000 |

**(f)** **No FV entry** under equity method.

**(g) Rollforward**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Jan 1 cost | $180,000 |
| + Share of NI | 16,000 |
| − Excess depreciation | (1,500) |
| − Share of dividends | (6,000) |
| **Dec 31 carrying amount** | **$188,500** |

**(h) Net IS effect** = $16,000 − $1,500 = **$14,500**.

**Key insight:** Same equity-method mechanics as Q1 with fully different ownership %, cost, BV, lives, NI, and dividends—ending carrying amount is always cost + cumulative equity income − cumulative dividends − cumulative basis amortization.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Summit Equity Partners: partial-year pickup and disposal
**LO:** LO 14-5  
**Concept:** Partial-year share of earnings and excess depreciation; dividends while held; disposal JE and gain/loss; presentation note  
**Scenario:** Summit Equity Partners holds a **30%** equity-method investment in Redline Systems Inc. At **January 1, Year 3**, the Investment in Redline account has a carrying amount of **$210,000**. Remaining unamortized depreciable basis difference is **$6,000** with **6 years** remaining life (straight-line; annual excess depreciation **$1,000**). No goodwill impairment is indicated.

Redline’s Year 3 net income of **$80,000** is earned evenly throughout the year. On **April 15, Year 3**, Redline declares and pays cash dividends of **$20,000**. On **July 1, Year 3**, Summit sells its **entire** Redline investment for **$228,000 cash**. Summit’s year-end is December 31.

**Required:**  
(a) Journal entry for Summit’s share of Redline earnings for the **holding period** (Jan 1–June 30).  
(b) Journal entry for **half-year excess depreciation**.  
(c) Journal entry for dividends received April 15.  
(d) Compute the Investment carrying amount immediately before sale; record the **July 1 sale**.  
(e) State how the investment would be classified on Summit’s March 31 interim balance sheet (still holding; long-term strategic interest) and how the difference between equity-method income and dividends received would appear in the **statement of cash flows (indirect method)** for the six months ended June 30 (before sale).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Share of earnings — partial year (6/12)**

Share of NI = 0.30 × $80,000 × 6/12 = **$12,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Redline Stock | 12,000 | |
| Investment Income | | 12,000 |

Check: Dr $12,000 = Cr $12,000.

**(b) Excess depreciation — partial year**

Excess dep = $1,000 × 6/12 = **$500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment Income | 500 | |
| Investment in Redline Stock | | 500 |

Check: Dr $500 = Cr $500.

**(c) Dividends (declared/paid while Summit owns the shares)**

Share = 0.30 × $20,000 = **$6,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 6,000 | |
| Investment in Redline Stock | | 6,000 |

Check: Dr $6,000 = Cr $6,000.

**(d) Carrying amount and sale**

Carrying amount at July 1 = $210,000 + $12,000 − $500 − $6,000 = **$215,500**  
Gain on sale = $228,000 − $215,500 = **$12,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 228,000 | |
| Investment in Redline Stock | | 215,500 |
| Gain on Sale of Investment | | 12,500 |

Check: Dr $228,000 = Cr $215,500 + $12,500 = **$228,000**.

**(e) Classification / presentation**

- Balance sheet (while held as a long-term strategic stake): **noncurrent asset** (Investment in Redline / equity-method investee).  
- SCF (indirect method), six months ended June 30: net equity-method income recognized = $12,000 − $500 = **$11,500**; cash dividends received = **$6,000**. Reconcile by **subtracting $5,500** ($11,500 − $6,000) from net income in the operating section so operating cash reflects only dividends received. (Purchase/sale cash flows for equity-method investments that are not short-term trading are **investing** activities.)

**Key insight:** Equity pickup and basis amortization are **time-weighted for the holding period**; dividends after acquisition reduce the Investment. On disposal, remove the **updated carrying amount** and recognize gain/loss versus cash (or other proceeds).

### Q4 — MC — Method choice and dividend treatment under equity method
**LO:** LO 14-5  
**Concept:** Classification of equity securities with significant influence; dividends reduce Investment under equity method  

**Question 4A:** On January 1, Lakewood Manufacturing acquires **35%** of the voting common stock of Shoreline Packaging Inc. Lakewood obtains board representation and participates in Shoreline’s operating and financial policy decisions. Lakewood does **not** elect the fair value option. Which measurement approach is appropriate?

- A) FV-NI with unrealized holding gains/losses in net income each period  
- B) Equity method: Investment adjusted for share of earnings, dividends, and basis amortization  
- C) Consolidation, because ownership exceeds 20%  
- D) Cost less impairment only, with dividend revenue when declared  

**Answer:** **B.** Ownership of 20%–50% creates a **rebuttable presumption of significant influence**; with facts supporting influence and no FV option election, ASC 323 equity method applies. Consolidation requires a **controlling** financial interest (typically >50% voting interest). FV-NI (A) is for equity securities **without** significant influence (ASC 321). Cost-less-impairment (D) is a limited practical expedient for certain equity securities without readily determinable fair value under ASC 321—not the equity method.

**Question 4B:** Under the equity method, when the investee declares and pays a cash dividend to the investor, the investor records:

- A) Debit Cash; credit Dividend Revenue  
- B) Debit Cash; credit Investment in Investee  
- C) Debit Investment in Investee; credit Dividend Revenue  
- D) Debit Cash; credit Unrealized Gain—Income  

**Answer:** **B.** Dividends are a **return of investment** (reduce the Investment account), not dividend revenue. Dividend revenue (A) is the FV-NI treatment when the investor lacks significant influence.
