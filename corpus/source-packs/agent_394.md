## Concept list for this pack
- Initial recognition of HTM debt at amortized cost (discount or premium)
- Purchase-price computation using market (effective) yield
- Effective-interest amortization schedule (discount and premium)
- Period-end interest and amortization journal entries
- Balance-sheet presentation at amortized cost; fair-value note disclosure only
- **Maturity (settlement) JE** and **sale-before-maturity** realized G/L in net income
- Mid-period disposal: partial-period amortization, accrued interest, dirty vs clean proceeds
- Classification: positive intent and ability; circumstances that do not taint HTM
- Number-variant twin (premium path vs discount path)

### Q1 — CORE — HTM discount life cycle: purchase, schedule, period-end, maturity & early sale
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** Initial recognition + effective-interest schedule + maturity/settlement and early-disposal JE (discount)  
**Scenario:** On January 1, Year 1, **Rivergate Industrial Partners** purchased $150,000 face of **Helios Power Authority** 5% bonds. The bonds mature on December 31, Year 3, and pay cash interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The market yield for similar risk on the purchase date is **7%**. Rivergate has the **positive intent and ability** to hold the bonds to maturity and classifies them as **held-to-maturity (HTM)**. Rivergate uses the **effective interest method** and records the investment **net** (no separate discount account). Fiscal year ends December 31.

**Required:**  
**(a)** Compute the January 1, Year 1 purchase price and record the purchase journal entry.  
**(b)** Prepare the full effective-interest amortization schedule (6 semiannual periods).  
**(c)** Record the June 30, Year 1 interest and discount amortization entry.  
**(d)** Record the December 31, Year 1 period-end interest and amortization entry.  
**(e)** Report the investment on the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet. Fair value of the bonds on that date is $143,800. How (if at all) is fair value used? What note disclosure is appropriate?  
**(f)** **Maturity settlement:** Record the entry on December 31, Year 3 to collect face value (assume the final interest entry for that date has already been recorded).  
**(g)** **Alternative disposal (emphasis):** Instead of holding to maturity, Rivergate sells the entire investment on **July 1, Year 2** for **$146,200** cash (immediately after the June 30, Year 2 interest entry), due to a significant deterioration in the issuer’s creditworthiness. Record the sale and compute the realized gain or loss.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Purchase price and initial recognition JE**  
Semiannual market rate = 7% ÷ 2 = **3.5%**  
Semiannual cash interest = 5% ÷ 2 × $150,000 = **$3,750**  
Number of periods = 3 × 2 = **6**  

Purchase price = PV of interest annuity + PV of principal at 3.5%, 6 periods:  
PV = $3,750 × PVA(3.5%,6) + $150,000 × PV(3.5%,6) = **$142,007**  
(Discount = $150,000 − $142,007 = **$7,993**)

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|------|---------|------:|-------:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | Investment in HTM Securities—Helios Bonds | 142,007 | |
| | Cash | | 142,007 |

*(Dr 142,007 = Cr 142,007)*

**(b) Effective interest amortization schedule — Discount**

| Date | Cash (2.5% × Face) | Interest revenue (3.5% × Beg. AC) | Discount amort. | Amortized cost |
|------|-------------------:|----------------------------------:|----------------:|---------------:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | | | | 142,007 |
| June 30, Y1 | 3,750 | 4,970 | 1,220 | 143,227 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | 3,750 | 5,013 | 1,263 | 144,490 |
| June 30, Y2 | 3,750 | 5,057 | 1,307 | 145,797 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 3,750 | 5,103 | 1,353 | 147,150 |
| June 30, Y3 | 3,750 | 5,150 | 1,400 | 148,550 |
| Dec 31, Y3 | 3,750 | 5,200 | 1,450 | 150,000 |
| **Totals** | **22,500** | **30,493** | **7,993** | |

*Check: Interest revenue = cash + discount amort each period; ending AC = face; total discount amort = $7,993. Final period plugs amortization so AC reaches face (rounding).*

**(c) June 30, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 3,750 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Helios Bonds | 1,220 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 4,970 |

*(Dr 4,970 = Cr 4,970)*

**(d) December 31, Year 1 (period-end interest / amortization entry)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 3,750 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Helios Bonds | 1,263 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 5,013 |

*(Dr 5,013 = Cr 5,013)*  
Year 1 total interest revenue = $4,970 + $5,013 = **$9,983**. Amortized cost at Dec 31, Y1 = **$144,490**.

**(e) Presentation and fair value**  
Balance sheet (assets): **Investment in HTM securities — $144,490** (amortized cost after Dec 31, Y1).  
Fair value is **ignored** for subsequent measurement of pure HTM under amortized cost. No fair-value adjustment is recorded.  
**Note disclosure:** Aggregate fair value $143,800; unrecognized holding **loss** = $144,490 − $143,800 = **$690** (disclosed, not recognized in NI or OCI for amortized-cost HTM).

**(f) Maturity settlement — December 31, Year 3** (after final interest entry; AC = face)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 150,000 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Helios Bonds | | 150,000 |

*(Dr 150,000 = Cr 150,000)*  
No gain or loss: cash face equals amortized cost at maturity.

**(g) Sale before maturity — July 1, Year 2 (emphasis)**  
Carrying amount after June 30, Y2 interest entry = **$145,797**.  
Cash proceeds $146,200 → **realized gain** = $146,200 − $145,797 = **$403** (other income in net income).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 146,200 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Helios Bonds | | 145,797 |
| Gain on Sale of Investment | | 403 |

*(Dr 146,200 = Cr 146,200)*  
A sale triggered by significant deterioration in the issuer’s creditworthiness is an ASC 320 change in circumstance that generally **does not taint** the HTM classification of other holdings. Derecognition basis is **amortized cost**, not fair value.

**Key insight:** HTM debt starts at acquisition cost (here a discount). Effective-interest amortization drives amortized cost to face by maturity, so the maturity settlement JE is simply Dr Cash / Cr Investment for face. If sold early, realized G/L = cash − amortized cost and hits **net income**—there is no FVA or OCI path for pure amortized-cost HTM.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — HTM premium (all numbers changed)
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — premium purchase, schedule, period-end JE, maturity & disposal  
**Scenario:** On January 1, Year 1, **Copperline Treasury Partners** purchased $240,000 face of **Westfield Transit** 8% bonds. The bonds mature December 31, Year 2, and pay interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The market yield on the purchase date is **6%**. Copperline has positive intent and ability to hold to maturity (HTM) and uses the **effective interest method** (net method). Fiscal year ends December 31.

**Required:**  
**(a)** Compute the purchase price and record the January 1, Year 1 purchase entry.  
**(b)** Prepare the full effective-interest amortization schedule (4 periods).  
**(c)** Record the June 30, Year 1 interest and premium amortization entry.  
**(d)** Record the December 31, Year 1 period-end interest entry.  
**(e)** Record the maturity entry on December 31, Year 2 (final interest already recorded).  
**(f)** **Alternative disposal:** Sell on January 1, Year 2 (immediately after Dec 31, Y1 entries) for **$245,800**. Record the sale.

**Answer key:**

**(a)**  
Semiannual market rate = 6% ÷ 2 = **3%**  
Semiannual cash interest = 8% ÷ 2 × $240,000 = **$9,600**  
Periods = 2 × 2 = **4**  
Purchase price = PV at 3%, 4 periods of $9,600 annuity + $240,000 principal = **$248,921**  
(Premium = $248,921 − $240,000 = **$8,921**)

| Investment in HTM Securities—Westfield Bonds | 248,921 | |
| Cash | | 248,921 |

**Key insight:** When market rate < stated rate, the investor pays a **premium**. Effective interest uses the **lower** market rate × declining amortized cost, so interest revenue **falls** each period while the cash coupon stays fixed; excess cash reduces the investment until face. Maturity still settles at face with no G/L; early sale compares cash to **amortized cost**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Mid-period disposal (partial amort + accrued interest) vs maturity
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** Disposal/maturity emphasis — partial-period update of amortized cost, then sale; contrast with maturity settlement  
**Scenario:** On January 1, Year 1, **Ashcroft Holdings** purchased $80,000 face of **Pinecrest Utility District** 6% bonds when the market yield was **8%**. Interest is paid **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. Bonds mature December 31, Year 2. Ashcroft classifies the bonds as **HTM**, uses the **effective interest method**, and records the investment net. Fiscal year ends December 31.

On **March 31, Year 2** (three months after the most recent interest payment), Ashcroft sells the bonds because of a major business disposition that requires rebalancing interest-rate risk (an ASC 320 circumstance that does not taint remaining HTM securities). The **clean** selling price is **$78,500**. The buyer also pays **accrued interest** from January 1 to March 31.

**Required:**  
**(a)** Compute the January 1, Year 1 purchase price and record the purchase.  
**(b)** Prepare the amortization schedule through maturity (4 periods).  
**(c)** Record both Year 1 interest entries (June 30 and December 31).  
**(d)** On March 31, Year 2, **first** update amortized cost and accrue interest for the three-month stub period (use effective interest for 3/12 of a year, or equivalently half of a semiannual period). Then record the **sale** for dirty proceeds (clean price + accrued interest).  
**(e)** **Counterfactual maturity path:** Assume instead that Ashcroft holds to maturity. After the final December 31, Year 2 interest entry, record the maturity settlement.  
**(f)** At December 31, Year 1, fair value is $78,000. How is the HTM investment presented, and what is disclosed?

**Answer key:**

**(a)**  
Semiannual market rate = 8% ÷ 2 = **4%**  
Semiannual cash interest = 6% ÷ 2 × $80,000 = **$2,400**  
Periods = **4**  
Purchase price = **$77,096**  
(Discount = $80,000 − $77,096 = **$2,904**)

| Investment in HTM Securities—Pinecrest Bonds | 77,096 | |
| Cash | | 77,096 |

*(Dr 77,096 = Cr 77,096)*

**(b) Effective interest amortization schedule — Discount**

|------|-----:|--------------------------------:|----------------:|---------------:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | | | | 77,096 |
| June 30, Y1 | 2,400 | 3,084 | 684 | 77,780 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | 2,400 | 3,111 | 711 | 78,491 |
| June 30, Y2 | 2,400 | 3,140 | 740 | 79,231 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 2,400 | 3,169 | 769 | 80,000 |
| **Totals** | **9,600** | **12,504** | **2,904** | |

**(c) Year 1 interest entries**

June 30, Y1:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|

|---------|------:|-------:|

Year 1 interest revenue = **$6,195**. AC at Dec 31, Y1 = **$78,491**.

**(d) March 31, Year 2 — partial update then disposal (emphasis)**  
Beginning AC (Jan 1, Y2) = **$78,491**.  
Partial-period interest revenue = $78,491 × 8% × 3/12 = **$1,570** (same as $78,491 × 4% × 3/6).  
Accrued cash interest = $80,000 × 6% × 3/12 = **$1,200**.  
Discount amortization = $1,570 − $1,200 = **$370**.  
Updated amortized cost = $78,491 + $370 = **$78,861**.

*Update entry (Mar 31, Y2):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Interest Receivable | 1,200 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Pinecrest Bonds | 370 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 1,570 |

*(Dr 1,570 = Cr 1,570)*

Dirty cash proceeds = clean $78,500 + accrued interest $1,200 = **$79,700**.  
Realized **loss** on sale = clean proceeds − AC = $78,500 − $78,861 = **$361**  
(equivalently: dirty cash − interest receivable − AC = $79,700 − $1,200 − $78,861 = $361 loss).

*Sale entry (Mar 31, Y2):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 79,700 | |
| Loss on Sale of Investment | 361 | |
| Interest Receivable | | 1,200 |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Pinecrest Bonds | | 78,861 |

*(Dr 80,061 = Cr 80,061)*

**(e) Maturity settlement counterfactual — December 31, Year 2** (after final interest; AC = face)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 80,000 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Pinecrest Bonds | | 80,000 |

*(Dr 80,000 = Cr 80,000)*  
Contrast: maturity has **no** gain/loss; mid-period sale requires a stub amortization/accrual and realizes G/L vs amortized cost.

**(f) Presentation / disclosure at Dec 31, Y1**  
Balance sheet: **Investment in HTM securities $78,491** (amortized cost).  
Fair value $78,000 is **not** booked. Notes disclose fair value and unrecognized holding **loss** of $78,491 − $78,000 = **$491**.

**Key insight:** Before a mid-period disposal, bring amortized cost (and interest receivable) up to date. Realized G/L uses **clean** proceeds vs updated amortized cost; dirty cash also settles the accrued coupon. Maturity settlement needs no G/L once the final interest entry has driven AC to face.

### Q4 — MC (classification / disposal)
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** HTM amortized-cost classification criteria and disposal measurement basis  

**Question 1:** Which condition is **required** for an investor to measure a debt security at **amortized cost** as held-to-maturity?  
- A) The investor intends to sell within the near term if rates move favorably  
- B) The investor has the **positive intent and ability** to hold the security to maturity  
- C) Fair value must equal amortized cost at every reporting date  
- D) The investor must elect the fair value option  

**Answer:** **B.** ASC 320 restricts HTM amortized-cost classification to debt securities for which management has both positive intent **and** ability to hold to maturity. Near-term sale intent (A) is trading. Fair value need not equal AC (C). Fair value option (D) would measure at FV-NI, not pure amortized cost.

---

**Question 2:** An HTM bond (amortized cost $98,500 after the latest interest update) is sold for $97,200 cash because of a significant unforeseen credit deterioration of the issuer. What is the correct **net income** effect of the sale entry under amortized-cost HTM accounting?  
- A) Unrealized loss of $1,300 through OCI only; no NI effect  
- B) Realized **loss of $1,300** in net income  
- C) No gain or loss; investment remains on the books at amortized cost until maturity  
- D) Realized loss of $1,300 in OCI, reclassified only if the portfolio is reclassified to AFS  

**Answer:** **B.** Sale of HTM debt derecognizes the investment at **amortized cost**; difference vs cash is a **realized** gain or loss in **net income**. There is no FVA/OCI path for pure HTM amortized-cost accounting (A and D describe AFS-style treatment). C is incorrect once a sale occurs.
