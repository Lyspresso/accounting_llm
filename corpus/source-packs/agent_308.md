# Agent 308 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-8

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Apply cash controls  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Internal cash controls:** cash drawer / POS reconciliation; petty cash fund; bank reconciliation; separation of duties (someone other than the cashier prepares the bank recon)
- **Cash Over and Short:** expense (debit) or other revenue (credit) when drawer/fund/recon amounts differ; other revenues and expenses on the income statement
- **Initial recognition JE:** establish petty cash (Dr Petty Cash, Cr Cash); cash-drawer short/over adjusting entry
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** petty cash fund rollforward (authorized balance, cash on hand, vouchers, shortage, replenishment); bank reconciliation schedule proving one correct cash balance (bank side + book side)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** book-side bank recon items only — note collections, NSF, service charges, EFT/payments by bank, book errors, cash shortage; **no** AJEs for deposits in transit, outstanding checks, or undeposited cash
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** close or decrease petty cash (Dr Cash / expenses as needed, Cr Petty Cash); replenishment settles vouchers by restoring fund cash via check (expenses recognized on replenishment, not when custodian pays)
- **Presentation:** petty cash is part of **cash**; Petty Cash ledger stays at authorized amount until increased/decreased/closed; replenishment does **not** change the Petty Cash account

- C) Note collected by the bank, NSF checks, bank service charges, and book recording errors  
- D) Undeposited cash and outstanding checks only  

**Answer:** **C.** Book-side reconciling items (bank collections, NSF checks, service charges, EFTs, book errors, and cash over/short plugs) require period-end AJEs that change the Cash ledger. Deposits in transit, outstanding checks, and undeposited cash are **bank-side timing** differences — already on the books correctly — so they appear on the reconciliation schedule but generate **no** journal entry.

### Q5 — MC (classification) — petty cash after replenishment
**LO:** LO 8-8  
**Concept:** Petty cash presentation and replenishment accounting  

**Question:**  
A company establishes a $1,500 petty cash fund. At month-end the fund is replenished for expenses and a small shortage; the authorized fund size is unchanged. Immediately after a normal replenishment, the **Petty Cash** control account balance is:

- A) Zero, because all cash was spent  
- B) Equal to the sum of the month’s expense vouchers  
- C) Still $1,500; replenishment debits expenses (and any shortage) and credits Cash, not Petty Cash  
- D) Equal to cash remaining in the fund before the replenishment check  

**Answer:** **C.** The Petty Cash control account changes only when the fund is established, increased, decreased, or closed. Replenishment restores **cash on hand** by writing a check from the general Cash account and records expenses (and any Cash Over and Short); Petty Cash remains at the authorized fund amount ($1,500) and is reported as part of cash on the balance sheet.
