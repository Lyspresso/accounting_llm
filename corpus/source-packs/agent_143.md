# Agent 143 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-2

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** The issue price of bonds depends on the relation between the market and stated rates (Measure and record bonds at issuance)  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Issue-price relation:** market = stated → par; market > stated → discount; market < stated → premium
- **Selling price formula:** PV of face (at market) + PV of cash interest annuity (at market); cash interest uses stated rate
- **Initial recognition JE:** Dr Cash (± Discount / Premium) / Cr Bonds Payable at face
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** effective-interest amortization table — Interest expense = beginning carrying amount × market rate; amort = expense − cash (discount) or cash − expense (premium)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** prorate cash interest, interest expense, and amort when reporting date ≠ interest date
- **Disposal / maturity JE:** pay face; carrying amount equals face after full amortization
- **Classification / presentation:** Bonds payable, net of unamortized discount (or plus unamortized premium) as long-term liability (current portion if due within one year)
- **Between interest dates:** proceeds include accrued interest at stated rate; first payment nets correctly

### Q1 — CORE — Issue price (par / discount / premium), discount amort schedule, interest JEs, accrual, maturity, presentation
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Bond issue price from market vs stated rates; initial recognition at par/discount/premium; effective-interest subsequent measurement schedule; period-end accrual; maturity settlement; BS presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Ridge Fabrication Co.** (calendar-year) authorizes and issues **$400,000** face of **5%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1**. The bonds mature on **December 31, Year 3** (3-year term) and pay cash interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. Meridian uses the **effective interest method** and rounds all present values and schedule amounts to the nearest dollar (final period plugs residual unamortized discount).

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **issue price** and prepare the **initial recognition JE** under each **separate** market-rate scenario:  
   1. Market rate **5%** (par)  
   2. Market rate **7%** (discount)  
   3. Market rate **4%** (premium)  
b. Using **only the 7% market (discount) case**, prepare the full **effective-interest amortization schedule** for all six semiannual periods.  
c. Prepare the **June 30, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 1** interest payment and discount amortization JEs (discount case).  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet excerpt** (bonds payable, net) and **Year 1 interest expense** for the discount case.  
e. Meridian prepares monthly statements. Record the **January 31, Year 2** period-end **accrual** of bond interest and discount amortization (prorate the next full semiannual row 1/6).  
f. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** entries to (1) record the final interest payment and amortization and (2) **derecognize** the bonds at maturity (assume no Interest Payable balance immediately before the final interest entry).

**Answer key:**  

**Setup (all scenarios)**  
- Semiannual periods: \(3 \times 2 = 6\)  
- Semiannual cash interest: \(400{,}000 \times 5\% \times 1/2 = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\)

**a1. Market 5% = stated 5% → sell at face (par)**  
Semiannual market rate \(2.5\%\).  
Issue price \(= \mathrm{PV}(2.5\%, 6, -10{,}000, -400{,}000) = \mathbf{\$400{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 400,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 400,000 |
| *Issue $400,000 5% bonds at face when market = 5%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 400,000 = Cr 400,000. **Balanced.**

**a2. Market 7% > stated 5% → sell at discount**  
Semiannual market rate \(3.5\%\).  
Issue price \(= \mathrm{PV}(3.5\%, 6, -10{,}000, -400{,}000) = \mathbf{\$378{,}686}\).  
Discount \(= 400{,}000 - 378{,}686 = \mathbf{\$21{,}314}\).

| Cash | 378,686 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 21,314 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 400,000 |
| *Issue $400,000 5% bonds at discount to yield 7%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 378,686 + 21,314 = Cr 400,000. **Balanced.**

**a3. Market 4% < stated 5% → sell at premium**  
Semiannual market rate \(2\%\).  
Issue price \(= \mathrm{PV}(2\%, 6, -10{,}000, -400{,}000) = \mathbf{\$411{,}203}\).  
Premium \(= 411{,}203 - 400{,}000 = \mathbf{\$11{,}203}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|

**Check:** Dr 411,203 = Cr 11,203 + 400,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Effective-interest amortization schedule — discount case (emphasis)**  
Cash each period \(= \$10{,}000\). Interest expense \(=\) beginning carrying amount \(\times 3.5\%\). Discount amort \(=\) interest expense \(-\) cash. Final period plugs residual unamortized discount to zero.

| **Totals** | **60,000** | **81,314** | **21,314** | | |

**Roll-forward checks:**  
- Sum of amort \(= 21{,}314 =\) initial discount.  
- Total interest expense \(= 60{,}000 + 21{,}314 = 81{,}314 >\) total cash interest (discount case).  
- Carrying amount rises each period toward face.

Balance sheet excerpt:

**e. Period-end adjusting JE — January 31, Year 2**  
Next full semiannual row (Jun 30, Y2): Interest expense 13,486; discount amort 3,486; cash interest 10,000. Prorate \(1/6\):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|

**Check:** Dr 13,864 = Cr 3,864 + 10,000. **Balanced.** Unamortized discount → 0; carrying amount → face.

*Derecognize at maturity*

**Key insight:** Issue price is driven only by the **market rate vs stated rate** at issuance. After issuance, the **effective-interest schedule** locks in that original market rate: carrying amount accretes from proceeds to face, and total interest expense exceeds cash coupons by the initial discount.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Discount bond twin (issue, full schedule, interest, maturity, presentation)
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: recompute issue price when market > stated; effective-interest subsequent measurement schedule; interest JEs; maturity settlement; net presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Peak Logistics Inc.** issues **$250,000** face of **6%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1**. Bonds mature **December 31, Year 4** (4 years), pay interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31, and were priced to yield a **market rate of 8%**. Cascade uses the **effective interest method** (round to nearest dollar; plug residual amort in the final period).

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **issue price** and prepare the **initial recognition JE**.  
b. Prepare the **full effective-interest amortization schedule** (8 periods).  
c. Prepare the **June 30, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 1** interest + amortization JEs.  
d. Report **bonds payable, net** and **Year 1 interest expense** at December 31, Year 1.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Premium issue + subsequent schedule; between-interest-date issuance; classification
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Premium when market < stated; effective-interest premium amortization schedule; bonds issued between interest dates (accrued interest in proceeds); classification of net carrying amount  
**Scenario — Part A (premium lifecycle):**  
**Lakeshore Alloy Partners** issues **$500,000** face of **8%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1**, maturing **December 31, Year 3**, interest paid **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. Market rate at issuance is **6%**. Effective interest method; round to nearest dollar; final period plugs residual premium.

**Scenario — Part B (between interest dates):**  
Separately, **Harborline Materials LLC** authorizes **$300,000**, **6%** bonds dated **January 1, Year 1**, interest payable **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The bonds are issued **at face (par)** on **March 1, Year 1** (market = stated = 6%).

**Required:**  
a. **Part A:** Compute issue price and record the **initial recognition JE**.  
b. **Part A:** Prepare the **full effective-interest amortization schedule** (6 periods).  
c. **Part A:** Record **June 30, Year 1** interest + premium amortization.  
d. **Part A:** State **bonds payable, net** at December 31, Year 1 and Year 1 interest expense.  
e. **Part A:** Record **maturity** principal repayment on December 31, Year 3 (ignore final interest entry here).  
f. **Part B:** Record the **March 1, Year 1** issuance (include accrued interest) and the **June 30, Year 1** first cash interest payment.  
g. **Classification:** For Part A at December 31, Year 1, how is the obligation classified/presented on the balance sheet?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Premium issue price and JE**  
- Semiannual market rate: \(6\% / 2 = 3\%\)  
- Periods: 6  
- Semiannual cash interest: \(500{,}000 \times 8\% \times 1/2 = \mathbf{\$20{,}000}\)  
- Issue price \(= \mathrm{PV}(3\%, 6, -20{,}000, -500{,}000) = \mathbf{\$527{,}086}\)  
- Premium \(= 527{,}086 - 500{,}000 = \mathbf{\$27{,}086}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 527,086 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | | 27,086 |
| Bonds Payable | | 500,000 |

**Check:** Dr 527,086 = Cr 27,086 + 500,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Effective-interest amortization schedule — premium (emphasis)**  
Interest expense \(=\) beginning carrying amount \(\times 3\%\). Premium amort \(=\) cash \(-\) interest expense. Carrying amount **declines** toward face.

| Date | Cash | Interest expense | Premium amort. | Unamortized premium | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | — | — | — | 27,086 | 527,086 |
| Jun 30, Y1 | 20,000 | 15,813 | 4,187 | 22,899 | 522,899 |

| Jun 30, Y2 | 20,000 | 15,558 | 4,442 | 14,144 | 514,144 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 20,000 | 15,424 | 4,576 | 9,568 | 509,568 |
| Jun 30, Y3 | 20,000 | 15,287 | 4,713 | 4,855 | 504,855 |
| Dec 31, Y3 | 20,000 | 15,145 | 4,855 | 0 | 500,000 |

**Checks:** Amort sum \(= 27{,}086\); total interest expense \(= 120{,}000 - 27{,}086 = 92{,}914 <\) total cash interest (premium case).

**c. June 30, Year 1 interest JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 15,813 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 4,187 | |
| Cash | | 20,000 |

**Check:** Dr 15,813 + 4,187 = Cr 20,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Presentation — December 31, Year 1**

| Bonds payable | $500,000 |
| Add: Premium on bonds payable | 18,586 |
| **Bonds payable, net** | **$518,586** |

Year 1 interest expense \(= 15{,}813 + 15{,}687 = \mathbf{\$31{,}500}\).

**e. Maturity principal**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 500,000 | |
| Cash | | 500,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 500,000. **Balanced.** (Premium fully amortized before/with final interest so net carrying amount = face.)

**f. Between interest dates (par)**

Accrued interest for 2 months (Jan 1–Mar 1):  
\(300{,}000 \times 6\% \times 2/12 = \mathbf{\$3{,}000}\).  
Cash proceeds \(= 300{,}000 + 3{,}000 = \mathbf{\$303{,}000}\).  
(Bond **price** at par excludes accrued interest; **proceeds** include it.)

*March 1, Year 1 — issue at par between interest dates*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 303,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 300,000 |
| Interest Payable | | 3,000 |

**Check:** Dr 303,000 = Cr 300,000 + 3,000. **Balanced.**

*June 30, Year 1 — first interest payment*  
Full six-month cash: \(300{,}000 \times 6\% \times 6/12 = \mathbf{\$9{,}000}\).  
Interest expense for 4 months outstanding: \(300{,}000 \times 6\% \times 4/12 = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 3,000 | |
| Interest Expense | 6,000 | |
| Cash | | 9,000 |

**Check:** Dr 3,000 + 6,000 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.**

**g. Classification / presentation (Part A, Dec 31, Y1)**  
Report as a **long-term liability**: **Bonds payable, net $518,586** (face plus unamortized premium). No current portion yet (entire principal due Dec 31, Y3). Premium is an **adjunct** liability; do not present premium as a separate asset.

**Key insight:** When market < stated, investors pay a **premium**; subsequent measurement **amortizes the premium against interest expense**, so expense is less than cash coupons. When bonds are sold between interest dates, cash includes **accrued stated interest** that is later offset against the full coupon payment.

### Q4 — MC — Market vs stated: issue classification
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Classification of bond issuance outcome from market vs stated rate relationship  
**Question:** On the authorization date, Riverbend Tools Co. sets a **7% stated (coupon) rate** on a new 10-year bond issue. By the actual settlement (issuance) date, the market yield for similar-risk bonds has risen to **9%**. Ignoring issuance costs and assuming interest payment dates match the issue date, how will the bonds be issued, and what is the relation between total interest expense over the bond term and total cash interest paid?

- A) Issued at a **premium**; total interest expense **exceeds** total cash interest  
- B) Issued at a **discount**; total interest expense **exceeds** total cash interest  
- C) Issued at a **discount**; total interest expense **is less than** total cash interest  
- D) Issued at **face (par)**; total interest expense **equals** total cash interest  

**Answer:** **B.** Market rate (9%) > stated rate (7%) → issue price < face → **discount**. The discount is additional interest cost amortized over the term, so total interest expense = total cash coupons + initial discount > total cash interest.

### Q5 — MC — Balance sheet presentation of discount
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Classification / presentation of Discount on Bonds Payable  
**Question:** Immediately after issuing bonds at a discount, which presentation is correct?

- A) Report Discount on Bonds Payable as a deferred charge (asset) amortized to interest expense  
- B) Report Bonds Payable at face and report Discount on Bonds Payable as a **contra liability** deducted from Bonds Payable  
- C) Report Bonds Payable at the issue proceeds only (no separate discount account required under GAAP)  
- D) Report Discount on Bonds Payable as a reduction of equity (other comprehensive loss)  

**Answer:** **B.** Discount on Bonds Payable is a **contra liability** subtracted from face to report **bonds payable, net** (carrying amount). It is deferred interest expense recognized over the term via amortization, not an asset or equity reduction.
