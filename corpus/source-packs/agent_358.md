# Agent 358 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-3

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Record accruals for payroll, compensated absences, bonuses, and defined contribution plans  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE — employee payroll:** Salaries Expense for gross pay; credit statutory/voluntary withholdings and Salaries Payable (net)
- **Initial recognition JE — employer payroll taxes:** Payroll Tax Expense; FICA (employer match), FUTA, SUTA payables
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** accrue compensated absences (ASC 710 four criteria), unpaid bonuses earned in the service period, and defined contribution employer match
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** Accrued Compensation roll-forward (beginning, earned, used, ending); wage-rate change as change in estimate
- **Settlement / remittance JE:** pay net salaries; remit withholdings and employer taxes; extinguish bonus and pension liabilities when paid
- **Classification:** accrue vacation when payment is *probable*; do **not** accrue sick pay when payment is only *possible*; employee-related accruals are current liabilities
- **Defined contribution plan:** Pension Expense = required employer contribution for the service period (not investment performance)
- **Number-variant twin:** same LO path with all companies and amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Summit Vale Logistics: payroll lifecycle with period-end employee accruals (emphasis)

**LO:** LO 15-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of payroll and employer taxes; **period-end adjusting JEs** for vacation, bonus, and DC match (emphasis); subsequent Accrued Compensation schedule; settlement/remittance JEs  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Vale Logistics Co.** (calendar-year) summarizes late-year payroll and year-end employee obligations as follows.

| Employer FICA | 7.65% of gross |
| FUTA | 0.6% of full gross (all wages within wage base for simplicity) |
| SUTA | 5.4% of full gross |

**Part B — December 31, Year 1 period-end employee accruals (emphasis)**  
1. Vested vacation rights attributable to services already rendered; payment probable; reasonably estimated at **$67,500**. No Accrued Compensation balance existed before this accrual.  
2. Board-approved cash bonus of **$35,000** for Year 1 services; payable January 18, Year 2.  
3. Employer defined contribution match for Year 1 is **$8,400**, payable January 12, Year 2 to the plan trustee.

**Part C — Subsequent measurement of compensated absences**  
- During Year 2, employees take vacation with prior-accrual cost of **$52,000**.  
- Wage rates for that vacation time are **$2,000 higher** than the Year-1 accrual (change in estimate). Credit Cash for cash paid.  
- December 31, Year 2: accrue additional unused vacation earned in Year 2 of **$45,000** (meets ASC 710 criteria).

**Part D — Settlement**  
On **January 2, Year 2**, Summit Vale (1) pays employees their December 27 net salaries and (2) remits **all** December 27 payroll-related withholdings and employer payroll taxes (single combined remittance entry is acceptable).  
On **January 12, Year 2**, remits the DC match. On **January 18, Year 2**, pays the bonus.

**Required:**

c. Prepare a **subsequent-measurement schedule** for Accrued Compensation for Years 1–2 (beginning, earned, used, ending).  
d. Record the **Year 2** entry when vacation is taken (including the wage-rate increase).  
e. Record the **settlement** entries: (1) January 2 payment of net salaries; (2) January 2 remittance of withholdings and employer payroll taxes; (3) January 12 DC payment; (4) January 18 bonus payment.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end AJs only; probable vs possible; settlement of accruals

**LO:** LO 15-3  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JEs only (emphasis); classify vacation (probable) vs sick pay (possible only); Accrued Compensation roll-forward and settlement; extinguish bonus and DC liabilities  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarwick Apparel Group** is preparing **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entries and related Year-2 settlements. Weekly payroll entries were already recorded during the year (not required here).

| Item | Facts |
|---|---|
| Beginning Accrued Compensation (1/1/Y1) | **$12,000** (from prior unused vacation) |
| Vacation pay earned in Year 1 | Rights **vest**; attributable to services already rendered; payment **probable**; reasonably estimated **$84,000** at Dec 31 Y1 |
| Sick pay | Rights **do not vest** but **accumulate**; attributable to services already rendered; reasonably estimated **$31,000**; payment is only **possible** (not probable) |
| Executive bonus | Sales for Year 1 = **$910,000**. Bonus = **2% of annual sales over $750,000**, payable **January 25, Year 2** |
| Defined contribution plan | Employer match for Year 1 = **$6,750**; paid to trustee on **January 8, Year 2** |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare **all December 31, Year 1 adjusting entries** required under GAAP. Explicitly state any item for which **no accrual** is made and why.  
b. Prepare an **Accrued Compensation schedule** from January 1, Year 1 through December 31, Year 1 (after the period-end AJ). What **total liability for compensated absences** is reported on the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet?  
c. Record the **January 8** and **January 25, Year 2** settlement entries for the DC contribution and the bonus.  
d. Record the Year-2 entry when **$55,000** of accrued vacation is paid at higher wage rates.  
e. **Presentation:** Classify each liability accrued in (a) at December 31, Year 1.

*(3) Bonus*

Bonus base = \(\$910{,}000 - \$750{,}000 = \$160{,}000\)

|---|---:|---:|
| Pension Expense | 6,750 | |
| Accrued Pension Liability | | 6,750 |

**(b) Accrued Compensation schedule / liability amount**

| Description | Amount |
|---|---:|

*January 8, Year 2 — pay DC match to trustee*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Salaries Payable | 3,200 | |

|---|---:|---:|
| Accrued Compensation | 55,000 | |
| Salaries Expense | 1,200 | |
| Cash | | 56,200 |
| *Debit liability at prior accrual; rate increase is current expense* | | |

### Q4 — MC — Compensated absences accrual criteria

**LO:** LO 15-3  
**Concept:** When to accrue a liability for compensated absences (ASC 710 four criteria)  
**Question:**  
Under ASC 710, an employer should accrue a liability for employees’ compensation for future absences when **all** of the following are met **except**:

- A) The obligation is attributable to employees’ services already rendered  
- B) The rights vest or accumulate  
- C) Payment of the compensation is probable and the amount can be reasonably estimated  
- D) The absences will be taken within the next fiscal year (timing of use is known with certainty)

**Answer:** D.  
ASC 710-10-25-1 requires (a) services already rendered, (b) rights that vest **or** accumulate, (c) payment **probable**, and (d) amount **reasonably estimable**. There is **no** requirement that management know with certainty **when** within a future period the absences will be taken—only that payment is probable and estimable. Timing of cash settlement may be estimated for classification, but certainty of the use date is not a recognition criterion.

### Q5 — MC — Defined contribution expense measurement

**LO:** LO 15-3  
**Concept:** Measurement of defined contribution pension expense vs investment risk  
**Question:**  
At year-end, Lakeport Co. determines its required employer contribution under a defined contribution 401(k) plan for services already rendered is **$14,000**, to be remitted to the trustee next month. During the year the plan’s investment assets declined in market value by **$9,000**. What amount of pension expense should Lakeport recognize for the defined contribution plan this year?

- A) $0, because the contribution has not yet been paid in cash  
- B) $5,000 (required contribution net of market decline)  
- C) $14,000  
- D) $23,000 (required contribution plus market decline)

**Answer:** C.  
For a **defined contribution** plan, pension expense equals the **required employer contribution** for the period (\( \$14{,}000 \)). Investment performance risk is borne by employees; the employer has no further obligation once the required contribution is made (or accrued as payable). Market declines in plan assets do **not** create additional employer expense under a pure DC arrangement.
