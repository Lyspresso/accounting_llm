# Agent 75 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-1

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Calculate depreciation using straight-line, sum-of-the-years’-digits, declining-balance, and units-of-production methods  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Pack focus:** Straight-line method (Demo 12-1A path) — initial recognition, period-end adjusting JE, multi-year schedule, disposal, presentation

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize PPE at acquisition cost when ready for intended use
- **Straight-line formula:** Annual dep. = (Acquisition cost − Residual value) ÷ Useful life in years
- **Depreciable cost (allocation base)** = cost − residual (salvage); total SL expense over life equals depreciable cost
- **Straight-line rate** = 1 ÷ useful life (also annual dep. ÷ depreciable cost)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** Dr Depreciation Expense / Cr Accumulated Depreciation
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** equal annual expense; AD cumulative; carrying amount = cost − AD (ends at residual)
- **Disposal JE:** update dep through disposal date; remove cost and AD; cash vs book value → gain or loss
- **Classification / presentation:** Equipment cost and Accumulated Depreciation on the balance sheet (gross with contra, or net); dep. expense is allocation of cost (not valuation); nonmanufacturing dep. → SG&A; manufacturing dep. → inventory / COGS

### Q1 — CORE — Straight-line full life cycle (emphasis: initial recognition JE)
**LO:** LO 12-1  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of PPE; period-end SL adjusting JE; multi-year straight-line depreciation schedule; balance sheet presentation; disposal JE after full-year depreciation  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedar Ridge Logistics, Inc.** (calendar-year) purchases a specialized delivery truck on **January 1, Year 1**. The truck is ready for its intended use on that date. The company uses the **straight-line** method and records depreciation at each **December 31**.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Cash acquisition cost | **$58,000** |
| Estimated residual (salvage) value | **$4,000** |
| Estimated useful life | **6 years** |
| Depreciation method | Straight-line |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |
| Disposal (after Year-3 adjusting entry) | Sold for **$29,500** cash on **December 31, Year 3** |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry for the truck purchase.  
b. Compute **annual** straight-line depreciation expense and the **straight-line rate** on depreciable cost. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** period-end adjusting entry.  
c. Prepare a complete **straight-line depreciation schedule** for Years 1–6 showing for each year-end: depreciable cost, rate, depreciation expense, accumulated depreciation, and carrying amount. Confirm Year-6 carrying amount equals residual value.  
d. Show the **balance sheet presentation** of the truck at December 31, Year 1 (gross cost, accumulated depreciation, and net).  
e. Assume instead the company **keeps** the truck through December 31, Year 3, records Year-3 depreciation, then **sells** it for $29,500 cash on that date. Prepare the **disposal** journal entry and compute the gain or loss.

**Answer key:**

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—Delivery Truck | 58,000 | |
| Cash | | 58,000 |
| *Capitalize acquisition cost when asset is ready for intended use* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 58,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Annual SL depreciation and Year-1 adjusting entry**

Depreciable cost = $58,000 − $4,000 = **$54,000**  
Annual straight-line depreciation = $54,000 ÷ 6 = **$9,000**  
Straight-line rate on depreciable cost = $9,000 ÷ $54,000 = **1/6 ≈ 16.67%** (also 1 ÷ 6 years)

*December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Delivery Truck | 9,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Truck | | 9,000 |
| *Allocate $9,000 of depreciable cost for Year 1* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 9,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Straight-line depreciation schedule**

| Reporting period | Depreciable cost | Rate | Depreciation expense | Accumulated depreciation | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan. 1, Year 1 | — | — | — | — | $58,000 |
| Dec. 31, Year 1 | $54,000 | 1/6 | $9,000 | $9,000 | 49,000 |
| Dec. 31, Year 2 | 54,000 | 1/6 | 9,000 | 18,000 | 40,000 |
| Dec. 31, Year 3 | 54,000 | 1/6 | 9,000 | 27,000 | 31,000 |
| Dec. 31, Year 4 | 54,000 | 1/6 | 9,000 | 36,000 | 22,000 |
| Dec. 31, Year 5 | 54,000 | 1/6 | 9,000 | 45,000 | 13,000 |
| Dec. 31, Year 6 | 54,000 | 1/6 | 9,000 | 54,000 | **4,000** (residual) |

**Checks:**  
- Expense constant each year = **$9,000**.  
- Total depreciation over life = $9,000 × 6 = **$54,000** = depreciable cost.  
- Ending carrying amount = $58,000 − $54,000 = **$4,000** = residual.  
- Carrying amount each year-end = cost − AD (e.g., Year 1: $58,000 − $9,000 = $49,000).

**d. Balance sheet presentation — December 31, Year 1**

| Assets | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment—Delivery Truck | $58,000 |
| Less: Accumulated depreciation—Delivery Truck | (9,000) |
| **Equipment, net** | **$49,000** |

(Alternatively, “Equipment, net of accumulated depreciation of $9,000 — $49,000.”) Accumulated depreciation is a **contra asset** in the noncurrent asset section; historical cost is preserved in the equipment account.

**e. December 31, Year 3 — disposal after Year-3 depreciation**

After Year-3 adjusting entry: AD = $9,000 × 3 = **$27,000**  
Book value (carrying amount) = $58,000 − $27,000 = **$31,000**  
Cash proceeds = **$29,500**  
**Loss on disposal** = $31,000 − $29,500 = **$1,500**

*December 31, Year 3 — sale of truck (Year-3 depreciation already recorded)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 29,500 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Truck | 27,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 1,500 | |
| Equipment—Delivery Truck | | 58,000 |
| *Remove cost and AD; recognize loss when cash < book value* | | |

**Check:** Dr 29,500 + 27,000 + 1,500 = **58,000**; Cr = **58,000**. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Straight-line spreads **(cost − residual)** evenly over useful life. The **initial recognition** JE records cost only; subsequent **period-end adjusting entries** build Accumulated Depreciation. The multi-year **schedule** must roll so total expense equals depreciable cost and ending book value equals residual. On **disposal**, remove both gross cost and AD and compare cash to book value for gain/loss.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Straight-line twin
**LO:** LO 12-1  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition JE; SL annual amount and rate; full subsequent measurement schedule; period-end JE; disposal with gain  
**Scenario:**  
**PrairieView Print & Bind LLC** (calendar-year) acquires a commercial press on **January 1, Year 1**, ready for use that day. Straight-line depreciation; December 31 year-end.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Cash acquisition cost | **$125,000** |
| Estimated residual value | **$5,000** |
| Estimated useful life | **8 years** |
| Method | Straight-line |
| Disposal | Sold for **$78,000** cash on **December 31, Year 4** (after Year-4 depreciation) |

**Required:**  
a. Initial recognition JE on January 1, Year 1.  
b. Compute depreciable cost, annual SL depreciation, and SL rate; record December 31, Year 1 adjusting entry.  
c. Prepare the full **8-year** straight-line schedule (expense, AD, carrying amount each year-end).  
d. After recording Year-4 depreciation, record the **December 31, Year 4 disposal** entry for the $78,000 cash sale. Show Dr = Cr.

**Answer key:**

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—Commercial Press | 125,000 | |
| Cash | | 125,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 125,000. **Balanced.**

**b. SL computation and Year-1 adjusting entry**

Depreciable cost = $125,000 − $5,000 = **$120,000**  
Annual SL depreciation = $120,000 ÷ 8 = **$15,000**  
SL rate = $15,000 ÷ $120,000 = **1/8 = 12.5%** of depreciable cost

*December 31, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Commercial Press | 15,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Commercial Press | | 15,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 15,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Straight-line depreciation schedule (Years 1–8)**

| Year-end | Dep. expense | Accumulated depreciation | Carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| (Jan. 1, Y1) | — | — | 125,000 |
| Year 1 | 15,000 | 15,000 | 110,000 |
| Year 2 | 15,000 | 30,000 | 95,000 |
| Year 3 | 15,000 | 45,000 | 80,000 |
| Year 4 | 15,000 | 60,000 | 65,000 |
| Year 5 | 15,000 | 75,000 | 50,000 |
| Year 6 | 15,000 | 90,000 | 35,000 |
| Year 7 | 15,000 | 105,000 | 20,000 |
| Year 8 | 15,000 | 120,000 | **5,000** (residual) |

**Checks:** Total expense $15,000 × 8 = **$120,000** = depreciable cost; ending BV = residual **$5,000**.

**d. December 31, Year 4 — disposal**

AD after 4 years = $15,000 × 4 = **$60,000**  
Book value = $125,000 − $60,000 = **$65,000**  
Cash = **$78,000**  
**Gain on disposal** = $78,000 − $65,000 = **$13,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 78,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Commercial Press | 60,000 | |
| Equipment—Commercial Press | | 125,000 |
| Gain on Disposal of Equipment | | 13,000 |
| *Remove cost and AD; gain when cash > book value* | | |

**Check:** Dr 78,000 + 60,000 = **138,000**; Cr 125,000 + 13,000 = **138,000**. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Changing cost, residual, and life changes annual expense, but the **structure** is identical: capitalize cost → equal annual (cost − residual)/life charges → schedule ends at residual → disposal compares cash to book value.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Partial-year SL, mid-year disposal, classification
**LO:** LO 12-1  
**Concept:** Straight-line annual amount applied to fractional service periods; period-end adjusting JE for partial year; update dep through disposal date; disposal JE with loss; classification of depreciation expense (manufacturing vs selling) and PPE presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Northshore Cabinetworks Co.** (calendar-year) places **two** assets in service as follows. Both use **straight-line** depreciation. The company **prorates** depreciation by months of service (exact fractional periods).

**Asset A — CNC router (manufacturing equipment)**

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Placed in service | **April 1, Year 1** |
| Cost (cash) | **$72,000** |
| Residual value | **$6,000** |
| Useful life | **5 years** |
| Function | Used solely in **factory production** |

**Asset B — Showroom display fixtures (selling)**

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Placed in service | **January 1, Year 1** |
| Cost | **$36,000** |
| Residual | **$0** |
| Useful life | **6 years** |
| Function | **Showroom / selling** |
| Disposal | Sold **September 30, Year 2** for **$24,000** cash (update depreciation through disposal date) |

**Required:**  
a. Compute **annual** SL depreciation for Asset A and Asset B.  
b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entries for depreciation on **both** assets (partial year for A; full year for B). Indicate the **income-statement / inventory classification** of each expense.  
c. For Asset A only, prepare a **brief subsequent measurement schedule** of depreciation expense by calendar year over its life (Years 1–6), showing partial years correctly so total service equals 5 full years.  
d. For Asset B: compute depreciation for **Year 2 through September 30**; prepare the **September 30, Year 2** update entry and the **disposal** entry for the $24,000 sale.  
e. State how **Asset A** would appear on the December 31, Year 1 **balance sheet**.

**Answer key:**

**a. Annual SL amounts**

Asset A: ($72,000 − $6,000) ÷ 5 = **$13,200** per full year  
Asset B: ($36,000 − $0) ÷ 6 = **$6,000** per full year

**b. December 31, Year 1 adjusting entries**

Asset A — 9 months in service (Apr 1–Dec 31): $13,200 × 9/12 = **$9,900**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Factory Overhead—Depreciation (or Inventory/WIP via OH) | 9,900 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—CNC Router | | 9,900 |
| *Manufacturing equipment: depreciation is a product cost (inventoried), not period SG&A* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 9,900.

Asset B — full year: **$6,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Selling (Showroom Fixtures) | 6,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Showroom Fixtures | | 6,000 |
| *Selling asset: depreciation is a period selling expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 6,000.

**c. Asset A — depreciation by calendar year (5 years of service from Apr 1, Y1)**

| Calendar year | Fraction of annual | Depreciation expense | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Year 1 | 9/12 | $9,900 | Apr 1–Dec 31 |
| Year 2 | 12/12 | 13,200 | Full year |
| Year 3 | 12/12 | 13,200 | Full year |
| Year 4 | 12/12 | 13,200 | Full year |
| Year 5 | 12/12 | 13,200 | Full year |
| Year 6 | 3/12 | 3,300 | Jan 1–Mar 31 (completes 5 years) |
| **Total** | **60/12 = 5 years** | **$66,000** | = depreciable cost |

**Check:** $9,900 + 13,200×4 + 3,300 = 9,900 + 52,800 + 3,300 = **$66,000** = $72,000 − $6,000.

**d. Asset B — Year 2 through disposal and disposal JE**

Year 1 AD (already recorded) = **$6,000**  
Year 2 depreciation through Sep 30 = $6,000 × 9/12 = **$4,500**

*September 30, Year 2 — update depreciation through disposal date*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Selling (Showroom Fixtures) | 4,500 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Showroom Fixtures | | 4,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 4,500.

AD at disposal = $6,000 + $4,500 = **$10,500**  
Book value = $36,000 − $10,500 = **$25,500**  
Cash = **$24,000**  
**Loss on disposal** = $25,500 − $24,000 = **$1,500**

*September 30, Year 2 — disposal*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 24,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Showroom Fixtures | 10,500 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 1,500 | |
| Equipment—Showroom Fixtures | | 36,000 |

**Check:** Dr 24,000 + 10,500 + 1,500 = **36,000**; Cr = **36,000**. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification / presentation — Asset A at Dec 31, Year 1**

| Assets | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment—CNC Router | $72,000 |
| Less: Accumulated depreciation—CNC Router | (9,900) |
| **Equipment, net** | **$62,100** |

Manufacturing depreciation of $9,900 is **not** automatically period expense on the income statement; it flows through **inventory** (product cost) and becomes expense as **cost of goods sold** when units are sold. Depreciation is a process of **cost allocation**, not asset valuation—carrying amount need not equal fair value.

**Key insight:** The **same SL formula** applies whether the year is full or fractional: multiply annual SL by the service fraction. Always **update accumulated depreciation** through the disposal date before removing the asset. **Classification** depends on asset function (product cost vs SG&A); **presentation** keeps gross cost and the contra account visible.

### Q4 — MC — Method fit and balance sheet classification
**LO:** LO 12-1  
**Concept:** Classification — when straight-line is most appropriate; balance sheet presentation of cost and accumulated depreciation  

**Question 1 of 2:**  
Straight-line depreciation is **most appropriate** when:  
- A) Units produced vary widely each year and drive the decline in service potential  
- B) The asset’s service potential declines roughly evenly over time, usage is similar each period, and maintenance is relatively constant  
- C) Benefits are much greater in early years and the firm wants maximum early expense for tax conformity only  
- D) Obsolescence is expected to eliminate virtually all remaining value after year 2 of a 10-year physical life  

**Answer:** **B.** Straight-line assumes a **constant rate** of decline tied to the **passage of time**. It fits roughly even service potential, stable use, and stable maintenance. Units-of-production fits output-driven decline (A); accelerated methods better fit early-heavy benefits (C); severe early obsolescence argues against a long even SL life (D).

**Question 2 of 2:**  
At December 31, Year 2, equipment cost is $80,000 and accumulated depreciation (SL) is $30,000. Which presentation is **correct** on the balance sheet?  
- A) Equipment $50,000 (only the net amount, with cost permanently written down)  
- B) Equipment $80,000; Accumulated depreciation $30,000; Equipment, net $50,000  
- C) Accumulated depreciation $30,000 as a liability  
- D) Depreciation expense $30,000 as a noncurrent asset  

**Answer:** **B.** GAAP presentation preserves **historical cost** and reports **accumulated depreciation as a contra asset** deduction (parenthetically or as a line). Carrying amount is $50,000, but the accounts are **not** collapsed into a permanent write-down of the asset account (A). AD is not a liability (C); cumulative AD is not “depreciation expense” on the BS (D).
