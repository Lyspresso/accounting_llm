# Agent 141 — CORE DEMO — LO 14-9

**Chapter:** 14  
**LO title:** Account for equity securities measured at FV-NI with fair value adjustments only at period-end  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** equity investment at purchase price + commissions/brokerage (insignificant influence; ASC 321 FV-NI)
- **Dividend revenue:** recognize only when investee declares (not equity-method share of earnings)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** cost vs year-end fair value vs required Fair Value Adjustment (FVA); portfolio rollforward across periods
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** update FVA only at reporting date; unrealized holding G/L in **net income** (FV-NI)
- **Disposal JE (period-end-only method):** realized G/L = cash proceeds − **original cost**; **no** FVA entry on the sale date
- **Year-end FVA cleanup:** remeasure FVA for securities still held (includes removing FVA amounts associated with securities sold during the period)
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** current vs noncurrent by intent/ability; BS at fair value (cost ± FVA); unrealized G/L and dividends in NI; SCF based on nature/purpose of acquisition
- **Number-variant twin:** same skill path with all amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Two-security portfolio: purchase, dividends, multi-period subsequent measurement schedule, sale, period-end FVA
**LO:** LO 14-9  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for a multi-security equity portfolio under FV-NI with fair value adjustments only at period-end (full life cycle)  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakeshore Advisors LLC** is a calendar-year company. It has **insignificant influence** over the investees below, fair value is **readily determinable**, and Lakeshore measures the investments at **FV-NI** using the **Appendix 14C / LO 14-9** approach: fair value is adjusted **only at period-end** (not on the sale date). Beginning FVA—Equity Securities = **$0**.

**Purchases (Year 1):**
1. On **September 1, Year 1**, Lakeshore purchased **3,000** shares of **Boreal Foods Inc.** common stock at **$42** per share and paid commissions of **$600**. Boreal has **50,000** shares outstanding.
2. On **October 15, Year 1**, Lakeshore purchased **2,000** shares of **Crestview Robotics Corp.** common stock at **$55** per share and paid brokerage of **$400**. Crestview has **40,000** shares outstanding.

**Other Year 1 facts:**
- On **December 10, Year 1**, Boreal declares and pays total dividends of **$30,000**. Crestview pays no dividends in Year 1.
- Fair values on **December 31, Year 1**: Boreal **$45** per share; Crestview **$52** per share.

**Year 2 facts:**
- On **February 20, Year 2**, Lakeshore sells its **entire** Crestview holding for **$108,000** cash (ignore brokerage on sale).
- On **August 5, Year 2**, Boreal declares and pays total dividends of **$20,000**.
- Fair value of Boreal on **December 31, Year 2**: **$48** per share. Lakeshore holds **no** Crestview shares and **no other** equity securities measured at FV-NI at year-end.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **initial recognition JEs** for both purchases.  
b. Prepare the **December 10, Year 1 dividend JE**.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** at December 31, Year 1 (cost, year-end fair value, unrealized holding G/(L) by security and in total; existing FVA; increase/(decrease) needed). Record the **period-end adjusting JE**.  
d. Show **balance sheet** presentation of the investment portfolio and **Year 1 income-statement** effects related to these investments.  
e. Record the **February 20, Year 2 sale** of Crestview under the period-end-only method (no sale-date FVA).  
f. Record the **August 5, Year 2 dividend** on Boreal.  
g. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2 subsequent measurement schedule** for securities still held and the **period-end FVA JE**.  
h. Reconcile cumulative **net income effects from fair-value changes and sale** (exclude dividends) to (1) Crestview economic G/L and (2) Boreal’s remaining unrealized holding amount. State whether Crestview is classified as current or noncurrent if management intends to sell within one year.

**Answer key:**

*Boreal cost* = (3,000 × $42) + $600 = $126,000 + $600 = **$126,600**  
*Crestview cost* = (2,000 × $55) + $400 = $110,000 + $400 = **$110,400**

**Classification:** If management intends and is able to sell Boreal within one year (or operating cycle, if longer), classify as a **current** asset; otherwise **noncurrent**. Reported amount is always **fair value**.

**Key insight:** Under LO 14-9, the **subsequent measurement schedule** is the control: at each period-end, required FVA = aggregate FV of securities **still held** − their **original cost**. Sales use cost-basis realized G/L and never touch FVA until the next period-end entry, which automatically clears FVA related to sold securities.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Partial disposal + two-year subsequent measurement schedule + presentation
**LO:** LO 14-9  
**Concept:** Partial sale at allocated original cost; multi-period subsequent measurement schedule; classification and presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Windrift Capital Partners** (December 31 year-end) purchased **10,000** shares of **Helix Dynamics Inc.** common stock on **March 1, Year 1** for **$25** per share plus fees of **$2,000**. Helix has **200,000** shares outstanding. Windrift has insignificant influence and uses **FV-NI with fair value adjustments only at period-end**. Beginning FVA = **$0**.

Additional facts:
- **December 31, Year 1:** Helix fair value **$27.50** per share.
- **June 15, Year 2:** Windrift sells **4,000** shares for **$29** per share cash.
- **November 30, Year 2:** Helix declares and pays total dividends of **$80,000** (after the June sale, Windrift still holds **6,000** shares).
- **December 31, Year 2:** fair value of Helix is **$26** per share for shares still held.
- Management has the intent and ability to sell the remaining Helix shares within the next year.

**Required:**  
a. Record the March 1, Year 1 purchase.  
b. Prepare the **Year 1 subsequent measurement schedule** and the December 31, Year 1 FVA JE.  
c. Record the June 15, Year 2 **partial sale** (allocate original cost; no FVA at sale).  
d. Record the November 30, Year 2 dividend.  
e. Prepare the **Year 2 subsequent measurement schedule** (shares still held) and the December 31, Year 2 FVA JE. Present a **two-year FVA rollforward** (beginning FVA → YE1 → YE2).  
f. **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** How is the remaining investment reported on the December 31, Year 2 balance sheet? Where do unrealized holding G/L, dividends, and sale G/L appear? Briefly note SCF classification principle for equity security cash flows.

Y1 unrealized $23,000 + Y2 sale gain $15,200 + Y2 unrealized adj $(18,200) = **$20,000**  
= sold-share economic gain $15,200 + remaining UH $4,800 = **$20,000** ✓

**f. Classification / presentation / disclosure**

- **Balance sheet (12/31/Y2):** report **Investment in equity securities $156,000** as a **current asset** (management intends and is able to sell within one year). Amount equals fair value (cost $151,200 + FVA debit $4,800).
- **Income statement:** dividend revenue, unrealized holding gains/losses (period-end FVA plugs), and realized gain/loss on sale all run through **net income** (other income/expense)—**not OCI**.
- **Statement of cash flows:** cash paid to acquire / cash received from sale of equity securities is classified based on the **nature and purpose** for which the securities were acquired (ASC 230); many non-trading equity holdings are **investing** activities, while securities acquired specifically for short-term trading-type profits may be **operating**.

**Key insight:** After a partial sale under LO 14-9, the subsequent measurement schedule uses **remaining cost** and **current FV of shares still held**. The large YE2 FVA reverse is not “double counting”—it removes FVA that related to sold shares and true-ups the remaining position in one period-end plug.

### Q4 — MC — Sale-date measurement under period-end-only FV-NI vs sale-date-and-period-end
**LO:** LO 14-9  
**Concept:** Classification of method — period-end-only FVA (LO 14-9) versus sale-date and period-end FVA (LO 14-4)  
**Question:**  
**Northpine Equity Fund** measures marketable equity securities with insignificant influence at FV-NI. Its policy is the **LO 14-9 / Appendix 14C** approach: fair value is updated **only at period-end**. On April 12 of Year 3, Northpine sells an equity holding that cost **$80,000** for **$87,500** cash. Immediately before the sale, Fair Value Adjustment—Equity Securities still reflects the **prior December 31** fair-value mark (no entry has been made since year-end). Which combination is correct?

- A) On April 12, first debit FVA and credit Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income to bring the investment to current fair value, then remove the investment and FVA against cash with no separate realized gain.  
- B) On April 12, recognize a **$7,500** gain as cash proceeds minus **original cost**; leave FVA unchanged until the December 31 period-end adjusting entry.  
- C) On April 12, recognize gain as cash minus last year-end fair value, and reverse the entire FVA account immediately.  
- D) On April 12, credit AOCI for the $7,500 difference because equity securities use FV-OCI until sold.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Under LO 14-9, sale G/L = proceeds − **original cost** ($87,500 − $80,000 = $7,500). FVA is **not** adjusted on the sale date; the next **period-end** entry remeasures FVA for securities still held (and eliminates FVA amounts associated with securities sold). Choice A describes the **LO 14-4** (sale-date **and** period-end) approach. Choice C invents a last-BS-date cost basis. Choice D confuses AFS debt (FV-OCI) with equity securities under ASC 321 (FV-NI).
