# Agent 275 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-3

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for Bonds Issued at Face Value  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** stated rate = market rate ⇒ proceeds = face; Dr Cash / Cr Bonds Payable (no discount or premium)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** carrying amount stays at face every period; interest expense = cash coupon every period; amortization always $0; total interest expense over term = total cash interest paid
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** when the reporting date is not an interest date, accrue Interest Expense and Interest Payable for the fractional period (face bonds: expense = face × stated rate × time; no amort line)
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JE:** pay face and derecognize Bonds Payable; final coupon recorded separately if not already recorded
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** Bonds Payable at face as long-term liability until within one year of maturity (then current); Interest Payable current; no netting of unamortized discount/premium because none exists
- **Number-variant twin:** same face-value lifecycle with all amounts, rates, dates, and terms changed
- **Between interest dates (par):** proceeds include accrued stated interest; first full coupon nets Interest Payable vs Interest Expense for months outstanding

### Q1 — CORE — Face-value bonds: issuance, constant carrying schedule, interest, period-end accrual (emphasis), settlement of accrual, maturity, presentation
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition at face; subsequent measurement schedule (expense = cash; amort = 0); **period-end adjusting JE** when year-end falls between interest dates; payment settling Interest Payable; maturity derecognition; BS presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Northlake Precision Instruments Inc.** (calendar-year) authorizes and issues **$250,000** face of **6%** bonds on **March 1, Year 1**. The **market rate** on the issue date is also **6%**, so the bonds sell at **face value**. The bonds mature on **March 1, Year 4** (3-year term) and pay cash interest **semiannually** on **March 1** and **September 1**. Because interest dates are March 1 and September 1, **December 31 year-end requires a period-end adjusting accrual** for interest since the last payment date. There is no discount or premium to amortize.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **March 1, Year 1 initial recognition journal entry**.  
b. Compute the **semiannual cash interest** and **total interest expense over the full bond term**; reconcile total expense to total cash interest.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (interest / carrying-value) schedule** for **all six semiannual periods**. Columns: period ending; cash interest; interest expense; amortization of disc./prem.; ending carrying amount.  
d. Prepare the **September 1, Year 1** interest-payment journal entry.  
e. **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** accruing interest for the four months September 1–December 31.  
f. Prepare the **March 1, Year 2** journal entry to record the semiannual cash interest payment (settle the year-end accrual and record expense for January–February).  
g. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet excerpt** (Interest Payable and Bonds Payable) and **Year 1 interest expense**.  
h. Prepare the **March 1, Year 4** entries to (1) record the final interest payment and (2) **derecognize** the bonds at maturity (assume no Interest Payable immediately before the final interest entry).

**Answer key:**  

**Setup**  
- Stated rate = market rate = 6% ⇒ issue price = face = **$250,000**; carrying amount remains **$250,000** until maturity.  
- Semiannual periods: \(3 \times 2 = 6\).  
- Semiannual cash interest: \(250{,}000 \times 6\% \times 1/2 = \mathbf{\$7{,}500}\).

**a. March 1, Year 1 — initial recognition at face value**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 250,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 250,000 |
| *Issue $250,000 6% bonds at face (stated = market = 6%)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 250,000 = Cr 250,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Total interest over term**  
Total cash interest \(= 7{,}500 \times 6 = \mathbf{\$45{,}000}\).  
Total interest expense \(= \mathbf{\$45{,}000}\) (equals total cash interest; amortization of disc./prem. = $0 when bonds are issued at face).

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — face-value bonds**  
Each period: Interest expense \(= 250{,}000 \times 3\% = 7{,}500 =\) cash interest. Amortization **$0**. Ending carrying amount always **$250,000**.

| Period ending | Cash interest | Interest expense | Amort. (disc./prem.) | Ending carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1, Y1 (issue) | — | — | — | 250,000 |
| Sep 1, Y1 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 0 | 250,000 |
| Mar 1, Y2 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 0 | 250,000 |
| Sep 1, Y2 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 0 | 250,000 |
| Mar 1, Y3 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 0 | 250,000 |
| Sep 1, Y3 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 0 | 250,000 |
| Mar 1, Y4 | 7,500 | 7,500 | 0 | 250,000 |
| **Totals** | **45,000** | **45,000** | **0** | |

**Schedule checks:**  
- Every row: expense − cash = amort **0**; ending CV = beginning CV = face.  
- Sum of interest expense = sum of cash interest = **$45,000**.

**d. September 1, Year 1 — interest payment**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 7,500 | |
| Cash | | 7,500 |
| *Semiannual cash interest: $250,000 × 3%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 7,500 = Cr 7,500. **Balanced.**

**e. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 1 (emphasis)**  
Months since last payment (Sep 1–Dec 31) = **4**.  
Interest to accrue: \(250{,}000 \times 6\% \times 4/12 = \mathbf{\$5{,}000}\)  
(equivalently \(7{,}500 \times 4/6 = 5{,}000\)).  
No discount/premium amortization (face-value bonds).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 5,000 | |
| Interest Payable | | 5,000 |
| *Accrue 4 months bond interest (Sep 1–Dec 31)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,000 = Cr 5,000. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount of bonds remains **$250,000** (accrual affects Interest Payable only, not Bonds Payable).

**f. March 1, Year 2 — cash interest payment settling accrual**  
Full six-month coupon = **$7,500**.  
Interest Payable (4 months Sep–Dec) = **$5,000**.  
Interest expense for Jan–Feb (2 months): \(250{,}000 \times 6\% \times 2/12 = \mathbf{\$2{,}500}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 5,000 | |
| Interest Expense | 2,500 | |
| Cash | | 7,500 |
| *Pay full semi coupon; clear YE accrual; expense 2 months* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,000 + 2,500 = Cr 7,500. **Balanced.**

**g. Presentation — December 31, Year 1**

Balance sheet excerpt:
| Liabilities | |
|---|---:|
| Interest payable (current) | $5,000 |
| Bonds payable (long-term) | $250,000 |

(No Discount or Premium line — bonds issued at face.)

Income statement Year 1 interest expense:  
6 months Mar 1–Sep 1 (cash) \(7{,}500\) + 4 months Sep–Dec (accrual) \(5{,}000\) = **$12,500**  
(equivalently \(250{,}000 \times 6\% \times 10/12 = 12{,}500\) for 10 months outstanding in Year 1).

**h. Maturity — March 1, Year 4**

*Final interest payment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 7,500 | |
| Cash | | 7,500 |

**Check:** Dr 7,500 = Cr 7,500. **Balanced.**

*Derecognize at maturity*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 250,000 | |
| Cash | | 250,000 |
| *Pay face; remove Bonds Payable* | | |

**Check:** Dr 250,000 = Cr 250,000. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** At face value, interest expense always equals the **stated** cash interest for the time the bonds are outstanding. When year-end is **not** an interest date, the period-end adjusting entry is a **two-line accrual** (Dr Interest Expense / Cr Interest Payable)—there is **no** amort of discount or premium. Carrying amount of Bonds Payable never moves from face until maturity cash settlement.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Face-value twin with monthly interim period-end accrual
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: recompute face-value interest path; subsequent measurement schedule; **monthly period-end adjusting JE**; interest payment settling accrual; maturity settlement; net presentation at face  
**Scenario:**  
**Silverpine Utilities Corp.** (calendar-year) issues **$480,000** face of **5%** bonds on **April 30, Year 1**. Market rate = stated rate = **5%**, so the bonds sell at **face**. Bonds mature **April 30, Year 4** (3 years) and pay interest **semiannually** on **April 30** and **October 31**. Management also prepares **monthly** interim statements, so a period-end accrual is required at each month-end that is not an interest date (emphasis on the year-end and interim accruals).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **April 30, Year 1 initial recognition JE**.  
b. Compute **semiannual cash interest** and prepare the **full subsequent measurement schedule** (6 periods): cash, interest expense, amort, ending carrying amount.  
c. Prepare the **October 31, Year 1** interest-payment JE.  
d. **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** (prorate the next full semiannual cash interest by **2/6** for Nov–Dec).  
e. Prepare the **January 31, Year 2 monthly period-end adjusting JE** for **one additional month** of interest (assume the Dec 31 accrual remains on the books; record only January’s month).  
f. Report **Bonds Payable** and **Interest Payable** at December 31, Year 1 and **Year 1 interest expense**.  
g. Prepare **maturity** entries on April 30, Year 4 (final interest + principal; no prior Interest Payable immediately before final interest).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**  
Stated = market = 5% ⇒ proceeds = face = **$480,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 480,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 480,000 |

**Check:** Dr 480,000 = Cr 480,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Semiannual cash and schedule**  
Semiannual cash interest: \(480{,}000 \times 5\% \times 1/2 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\).  
Interest expense each period = **$12,000**; amort = **$0**; ending CV always **$480,000**.

| Period ending | Cash interest | Interest expense | Amort. | Ending carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apr 30, Y1 (issue) | — | — | — | 480,000 |
| Oct 31, Y1 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 480,000 |
| Apr 30, Y2 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 480,000 |
| Oct 31, Y2 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 480,000 |
| Apr 30, Y3 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 480,000 |
| Oct 31, Y3 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 480,000 |
| Apr 30, Y4 | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 | 480,000 |
| **Totals** | **72,000** | **72,000** | **0** | |

Total interest expense over term = total cash interest = **$72,000**.

**c. October 31, Year 1 — interest payment**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 12,000 | |
| Cash | | 12,000 |

**Check:** Dr 12,000 = Cr 12,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 1 (emphasis)**  
Next full semiannual cash interest (due Apr 30, Y2) = $12,000. Accrue **2 months / 6 months = 2/6**:

- Interest expense / Interest payable: \(12{,}000 \times 2/6 = \mathbf{\$4{,}000}\)  
  (or \(480{,}000 \times 5\% \times 2/12 = 4{,}000\)).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 4,000 | |
| Interest Payable | | 4,000 |
| *Accrue 2 months face-bond interest (Nov 1–Dec 31)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 = Cr 4,000. **Balanced.**  
Bonds Payable carrying amount still **$480,000**.

**e. Monthly period-end adjusting JE — January 31, Year 2**  
One more month: \(480{,}000 \times 5\% \times 1/12 = \mathbf{\$2{,}000}\)  
(or \(12{,}000 \times 1/6 = 2{,}000\)).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 2,000 | |
| Interest Payable | | 2,000 |
| *Accrue 1 month face-bond interest for January* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,000 = Cr 2,000. **Balanced.**  
Interest Payable balance after this entry: \(4{,}000 + 2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\) (3 months Nov–Jan).

**f. Presentation — December 31, Year 1**

| Interest payable | $4,000 |
| Bonds payable (long-term) | $480,000 |

Year 1 interest expense:  
6 months Apr 30–Oct 31 \(12{,}000\) + 2 months Nov–Dec \(4{,}000\) = **$16,000**  
(\(480{,}000 \times 5\% \times 8/12 = 16{,}000\) for 8 months outstanding in Year 1).

**g. Maturity — April 30, Year 4**

*Final interest*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 12,000 | |
| Cash | | 12,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 12,000. **Balanced.**

*Pay face*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 480,000 | |
| Cash | | 480,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 480,000. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Same LO 16-3 face-value mechanics with fully changed face, coupon, and interest calendar. Monthly or year-end period-end accruals simply **slice stated interest by time**; unlike discount/premium bonds, the adjusting entry never includes an amortization component.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Between-interest-date par issuance; year-end accrual; current maturity reclassification
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Bonds issued at face **between interest dates** (accrued interest in proceeds); first coupon nets Interest Payable; **period-end adjusting JE** when interest dates are mid-year; classification of Bonds Payable as current vs noncurrent near maturity  
**Scenario — Part A (between interest dates + period-end):**  
**Westfork Rail Logistics LLC** (calendar-year) authorizes **$360,000**, **7%** bonds dated **January 1, Year 1**, interest payable **semiannually** on **June 30** and **December 31**. Market rate = stated rate = **7%**. The bonds are issued **at face** on **May 1, Year 1** (between interest dates). The bonds mature on **December 31, Year 3**.

**Scenario — Part B (classification near maturity):**  
Separately, **Oakmere Packaging Inc.** has **$200,000** face of **6%** **par** bonds outstanding at December 31, Year 5 that mature on **June 30, Year 6**. No discount or premium. Interest is paid June 30 and December 31.

**Required:**  
a. **Part A:** Compute cash proceeds on May 1, Year 1 and record the **issuance JE** (include accrued interest).  
b. **Part A:** Record the **June 30, Year 1** first cash interest payment.  
c. **Part A:** Record the **December 31, Year 1** interest payment (year-end is an interest date—no separate accrual).  
d. **Part A:** Compute **Year 1 interest expense** and show Bonds Payable on the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet.  
e. **Part A:** Record **maturity** principal repayment on December 31, Year 3 (assume final interest on that date is recorded in a separate entry already).  
f. **Part B (classification):** At December 31, Year 5, how should Oakmere present the $200,000 Bonds Payable?  
g. **Part B:** If Oakmere’s fiscal year-end were instead **April 30, Year 6** (interest dates still June 30 / Dec 31), prepare the **April 30, Year 6 period-end adjusting JE** for accrued interest on the par bonds (4 months since Dec 31).

**Answer key:**  

**a. May 1, Year 1 — issue at face between interest dates**  
Accrued interest for 4 months (Jan 1–May 1):  
\(360{,}000 \times 7\% \times 4/12 = \mathbf{\$8{,}400}\).  
Cash proceeds \(= 360{,}000 + 8{,}400 = \mathbf{\$368{,}400}\).  
(Bond **price** at par excludes accrued interest; **proceeds** include it.)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 368,400 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 360,000 |
| Interest Payable | | 8,400 |
| *Issue $360,000 face bonds at par between interest dates* | | |

**Check:** Dr 368,400 = Cr 360,000 + 8,400. **Balanced.**

**b. June 30, Year 1 — first interest payment**  
Full six-month cash: \(360{,}000 \times 7\% \times 6/12 = \mathbf{\$12{,}600}\).  
Interest expense for 2 months outstanding (May–June): \(360{,}000 \times 7\% \times 2/12 = \mathbf{\$4{,}200}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 8,400 | |
| Interest Expense | 4,200 | |
| Cash | | 12,600 |

**Check:** Dr 8,400 + 4,200 = Cr 12,600. **Balanced.**

**c. December 31, Year 1 — second interest payment (interest date = year-end)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 12,600 | |
| Cash | | 12,600 |

**Check:** Dr 12,600 = Cr 12,600. **Balanced.**  
No separate year-end adjusting entry is needed when Dec 31 is a contractual interest date and the payment is recorded that day.

**d. Year 1 expense and presentation**  
Year 1 interest expense \(= 4{,}200 + 12{,}600 = \mathbf{\$16{,}800}\)  
(\(360{,}000 \times 7\% \times 8/12 = 16{,}800\) for 8 months outstanding May 1–Dec 31).

Balance sheet Dec 31, Year 1:
| Bonds payable (long-term) | $360,000 |

(No Interest Payable if Dec 31 coupon is paid; carrying amount = face.)

**e. Maturity principal — December 31, Year 3**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 360,000 | |
| Cash | | 360,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 360,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Classification — Oakmere Dec 31, Year 5**  
The entire **$200,000** face matures within one year (June 30, Year 6). Present as a **current liability** (current maturity of long-term debt / Bonds payable—current). Because the bonds were issued at face, the current amount equals face (no unamortized discount or premium).

**g. Period-end adjusting JE — April 30, Year 6 (Part B emphasis)**  
Months since last interest payment (Dec 31–Apr 30) = **4**.  
Accrue: \(200{,}000 \times 6\% \times 4/12 = \mathbf{\$4{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 4,000 | |
| Interest Payable | | 4,000 |
| *Accrue 4 months interest on face-value bonds* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 = Cr 4,000. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Between-interest-date par issues collect **accrued stated interest** so the first coupon does not overstate interest expense. Period-end accruals for face bonds remain a pure Interest Expense / Interest Payable entry. Near maturity, reclassify the **full face** to current—there is no net carrying-amount adjustment for discount/premium.

### Q4 — MC — Classification: face-value outcome and BS presentation
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Classification of bond issuance outcome and balance-sheet presentation when stated rate equals market rate  
**Question:** On January 1, **Harborline Ceramics Co.** issues $100,000 of 8% bonds when the market rate for similar debt is also 8%. Interest is paid semiannually. Which statement is correct?

- A) The bonds are issued at a discount; Bonds Payable is reported net of Discount on Bonds Payable.  
- B) The bonds are issued at a premium; Bonds Payable is reported plus Premium on Bonds Payable.  
- C) The bonds are issued at face value; Bonds Payable is reported at face with no discount or premium adjunct/contra, and interest expense each period equals cash interest for that period.  
- D) The bonds are issued at face value, but interest expense each period equals market rate × face even when that amount differs from cash interest.

**Answer:** C.  
When stated rate = market rate, present value equals face, so proceeds = face and no discount or premium is recorded. Interest expense equals the contractual (stated) cash interest each period. Bonds Payable is presented at face (long-term until within one year of maturity). Options A and B describe discount/premium cases (market ≠ stated). Option D confuses face-value accounting with effective-interest discount/premium amortization (where expense = beginning carrying amount × market rate and can differ from cash).

### Q5 — MC — Structure of the period-end adjusting JE (face-value bonds)
**LO:** LO 16-3  
**Concept:** Structure of the period-end adjusting JE when fiscal year-end falls between interest dates for bonds issued at face  
**Question:** **Pinecroft Foundry Inc.** issued $300,000 of 6% bonds at face on March 1. Interest is paid each March 1 and September 1. At December 31 (year-end), what is the correct period-end adjusting entry for bond interest (ignore closing entries)?

- A) Debit Interest Expense $13,500; credit Cash $13,500.  
- B) Debit Interest Expense $6,000; credit Interest Payable $6,000.  
- C) Debit Interest Expense $7,500; credit Discount on Bonds Payable $1,500 and Interest Payable $6,000.  
- D) Debit Interest Expense $9,000; credit Bonds Payable $9,000.

**Answer:** B.  
From September 1 to December 31 is **4 months**. Accrual = \(300{,}000 \times 6\% \times 4/12 = \$6{,}000\). Face-value bonds: Dr Interest Expense / Cr Interest Payable only—no cash (cash is paid on interest dates), no discount amortization (none exists), and Bonds Payable is not adjusted for interest.  
- A wrongly uses cash and a 9-month amount (\(300{,}000 \times 6\% \times 9/12 = 13{,}500\)).  
- C invents discount amortization (discount bonds only).  
- D incorrectly credits Bonds Payable for interest.
