# Agent 274 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-2

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** The issue price of bonds depends on the relation between the market and stated rates (Measure and record bonds at issuance)  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Issue-price relation:** market = stated → par; market > stated → discount; market < stated → premium
- **Selling price formula:** PV of face (at market) + PV of cash interest annuity (at market); cash coupons use the stated rate
- **Initial recognition JE:** Dr Cash (± Discount / Premium) / Cr Bonds Payable at face
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** effective-interest table — Interest expense = beginning carrying amount × original market rate; amort bridges expense and cash
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** when the reporting date is not an interest date, accrue a pro rata share of the next semi-period’s cash interest, effective interest expense, and discount/premium amortization
- **Disposal / maturity JE:** final interest + amort, then pay face (carrying amount = face after full amortization)
- **Classification / presentation:** Bonds payable net of unamortized discount (or plus unamortized premium) as long-term liability
- **Between interest dates:** proceeds include accrued stated interest; first payment nets correctly

### Q1 — CORE — Issue price (par / discount / premium), discount schedule, interest JEs, period-end accrual (emphasis), maturity, presentation
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Bond issue price from market vs stated rates; initial recognition; effective-interest schedule; **period-end adjusting JE** when year-end falls between interest dates; maturity settlement; BS presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Tamarack Ridge Fabricators Inc.** (calendar-year) authorizes and issues **$320,000** face of **5%** bonds on **April 1, Year 1**. The bonds mature on **April 1, Year 4** (3-year term) and pay cash interest **semiannually** on **April 1** and **October 1**. Tamarack uses the **effective interest method** and rounds present values and schedule amounts to the nearest dollar (final period plugs residual unamortized discount). Because interest dates are April 1 and October 1, **December 31 year-end requires an adjusting accrual** for interest and amortization since the last payment date.

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **issue price** and prepare the **initial recognition JE** under each **separate** market-rate scenario:  
   1. Market rate **5%** (par)  
   2. Market rate **7%** (discount)  
   3. Market rate **4%** (premium)  
b. Using **only the 7% market (discount) case**, prepare the full **effective-interest amortization schedule** for all six semiannual periods.  
c. Prepare the **October 1, Year 1** interest payment and discount amortization JE (discount case).  
d. **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** accruing interest and discount amortization for the three months October 1–December 31 (prorate the next full semiannual row by **3/6**).  
e. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet excerpt** (bonds payable, net, and related interest payable) and **Year 1 interest expense** for the discount case.  
f. Prepare the **April 1, Year 4** entries to (1) record the final interest payment and amortization and (2) **derecognize** the bonds at maturity (assume no Interest Payable balance immediately before the final interest entry).

**Answer key:**  

**Setup (all scenarios)**  
- Semiannual periods: \(3 \times 2 = 6\)  
- Semiannual cash interest: \(320{,}000 \times 5\% \times 1/2 = \mathbf{\$8{,}000}\)

**a1. Market 5% = stated 5% → sell at face (par)**  
Semiannual market rate \(2.5\%\).  
Issue price \(= \mathrm{PV}(2.5\%, 6, -8{,}000, -320{,}000) = \mathbf{\$320{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 320,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 320,000 |
| *Issue $320,000 5% bonds at face when market = 5%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 320,000 = Cr 320,000. **Balanced.**

**a2. Market 7% > stated 5% → sell at discount**  
Semiannual market rate \(3.5\%\).  
Issue price \(= \mathrm{PV}(3.5\%, 6, -8{,}000, -320{,}000) = \mathbf{\$302{,}949}\).  
Discount \(= 320{,}000 - 302{,}949 = \mathbf{\$17{,}051}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 302,949 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 17,051 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 320,000 |
| *Issue $320,000 5% bonds at discount to yield 7%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 302,949 + 17,051 = Cr 320,000. **Balanced.**

**a3. Market 4% < stated 5% → sell at premium**  
Semiannual market rate \(2\%\).  
Issue price \(= \mathrm{PV}(2\%, 6, -8{,}000, -320{,}000) = \mathbf{\$328{,}962}\).  
Premium \(= 328{,}962 - 320{,}000 = \mathbf{\$8{,}962}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 328,962 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | | 8,962 |
| Bonds Payable | | 320,000 |
| *Issue $320,000 5% bonds at premium to yield 4%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 328,962 = Cr 8,962 + 320,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Effective-interest amortization schedule — discount case**  
Cash each period \(= \$8{,}000\). Interest expense \(=\) beginning carrying amount \(\times 3.5\%\). Discount amort \(=\) interest expense \(-\) cash. Final period plugs residual unamortized discount to zero.

| Date | Cash (stated) | Interest expense (mkt) | Discount amort. | Unamortized discount | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apr 1, Y1 (issue) | — | — | — | 17,051 | 302,949 |
| Oct 1, Y1 | 8,000 | 10,603 | 2,603 | 14,448 | 305,552 |
| Apr 1, Y2 | 8,000 | 10,694 | 2,694 | 11,754 | 308,246 |

**Roll-forward checks:**  
- Sum of amort \(= 17{,}051 =\) initial discount.  
- Total interest expense \(= 48{,}000 + 17{,}051 = 65{,}051 >\) total cash interest (discount case).  
- Carrying amount rises each period toward face.

**c. October 1, Year 1 — interest + amortization (discount)**

Next full semiannual row (Apr 1, Y2): Interest expense 10,694; discount amort 2,694; cash interest 8,000.  
Accrue **3 months / 6 months = 1/2**:

- Interest expense: \(10{,}694 \times 1/2 = \mathbf{5{,}347}\)  
- Discount amort: \(2{,}694 \times 1/2 = \mathbf{1{,}347}\)  
- Interest payable: \(8{,}000 \times 1/2 = \mathbf{4{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|

**Check:** Dr 5,347 = Cr 1,347 + 4,000. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount after accrual: \(305{,}552 + 1{,}347 = \mathbf{306{,}899}\). Unamortized discount: \(14{,}448 - 1{,}347 = \mathbf{13{,}101}\).

(6 months outstanding Apr 1–Oct 1 full semi + 3 months accrued Oct–Dec).

**f. Maturity — April 1, Year 4**

*Final interest + amortization*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 11,092 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 3,092 |
| Cash | | 8,000 |

**Check:** Dr 11,092 = Cr 3,092 + 8,000. **Balanced.** Unamortized discount → 0; carrying amount → face.

*Derecognize at maturity*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|

**Key insight:** Issue price is set only by **market rate vs stated rate** at issuance. When the books close **between interest dates**, the period-end adjusting entry must accrue **cash interest, effective interest expense, and discount amortization** in the same proportion so the JE still balances and carrying amount continues to roll toward face.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Discount bond twin (issue, full schedule, interest, monthly period-end accrual, maturity)
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: recompute issue price when market > stated; effective-interest schedule; **monthly period-end adjusting JE**; interest payment and maturity settlement; net presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Blue Heron Conveyor Co.** (calendar-year) issues **$540,000** face of **6%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1**. Bonds mature **December 31, Year 4** (4 years), pay interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31, and were priced to yield a **market rate of 8%**. Blue Heron uses the **effective interest method** (round to nearest dollar; plug residual amort in the final period). Management prepares **monthly** interim statements, so a period-end accrual is required at each month-end that is not an interest date.

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **issue price** and prepare the **initial recognition JE**.  
b. Prepare the **full effective-interest amortization schedule** (8 periods).  
c. Prepare the **June 30, Year 1** interest + amortization JE.  
d. **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **January 31, Year 1** period-end adjusting JE (prorate the first full semiannual row by **1/6**).  
e. Report **bonds payable, net** and **Year 1 interest expense** at December 31, Year 1 (after both full semi periods; no open accrual at year-end because Dec 31 is an interest date).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Premium issue + period-end accrual; between-interest-date issuance; classification
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Premium when market < stated; effective-interest premium amortization; **period-end adjusting JE for a premium bond**; bonds issued between interest dates; classification of net carrying amount  
**Scenario — Part A (premium + period-end):**  
**Quarry Bend Alloys LLC** issues **$180,000** face of **8%** bonds on **January 1, Year 1**, maturing **December 31, Year 2**, interest paid **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. Market rate at issuance is **6%**. Effective interest method; round to nearest dollar; final period plugs residual premium. Quarry Bend prepares interim statements at **August 31**.

**Scenario — Part B (between interest dates):**  
Separately, **Pinnacle Loom Textiles Corp.** authorizes **$240,000**, **7%** bonds dated **January 1, Year 1**, interest payable **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The bonds are issued **at face (par)** on **April 1, Year 1** (market = stated = 7%).

**Required:**  
a. **Part A:** Compute issue price and record the **initial recognition JE**.  
b. **Part A:** Prepare the **full effective-interest amortization schedule** (4 periods).  
c. **Part A:** Record **June 30, Year 1** interest + premium amortization.  
d. **Part A (emphasis):** Record the **August 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** (prorate the next full semiannual row by **2/6**).  
e. **Part A:** State **bonds payable, net** at December 31, Year 1 and Year 1 interest expense (after both full semi entries; year-end is an interest date).  
f. **Part A:** Record **maturity** principal repayment on December 31, Year 2 (ignore final interest entry here).  
g. **Part B:** Record the **April 1, Year 1** issuance (include accrued interest) and the **June 30, Year 1** first cash interest payment.  
h. **Classification:** For Part A at December 31, Year 1, how is the obligation classified/presented on the balance sheet?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Premium issue price and JE**  
- Semiannual market rate: \(6\% / 2 = 3\%\)  
- Periods: 4  
- Semiannual cash interest: \(180{,}000 \times 8\% \times 1/2 = \mathbf{\$7{,}200}\)  
- Issue price \(= \mathrm{PV}(3\%, 4, -7{,}200, -180{,}000) = \mathbf{\$186{,}691}\)  
- Premium \(= 186{,}691 - 180{,}000 = \mathbf{\$6{,}691}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 186,691 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | | 6,691 |
| Bonds Payable | | 180,000 |

**Check:** Dr 186,691 = Cr 6,691 + 180,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Effective-interest amortization schedule — premium**  
Interest expense \(=\) beginning carrying amount \(\times 3\%\). Premium amort \(=\) cash \(-\) interest expense. Carrying amount **declines** toward face.

| Date | Cash | Interest expense | Premium amort. | Unamortized premium | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | — | — | — | 6,691 | 186,691 |
| Jun 30, Y1 | 7,200 | 5,601 | 1,599 | 5,092 | 185,092 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | 7,200 | 5,553 | 1,647 | 3,445 | 183,445 |
| Jun 30, Y2 | 7,200 | 5,503 | 1,697 | 1,748 | 181,748 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 7,200 | 5,452 | 1,748 | 0 | 180,000 |
| **Totals** | **28,800** | **22,109** | **6,691** | | |

**Checks:** Amort sum \(= 6{,}691\); total interest expense \(= 28{,}800 - 6{,}691 = 22{,}109 <\) total cash interest (premium case).

**c. June 30, Year 1 interest JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 5,601 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 1,599 | |
| Cash | | 7,200 |

**Check:** Dr 5,601 + 1,599 = Cr 7,200. **Balanced.**

**d. Period-end adjusting JE — August 31, Year 1 (premium, emphasis)**  
Next full semiannual row (Dec 31, Y1): Interest expense 5,553; premium amort 1,647; cash interest 7,200.  
Prorate **2 months / 6 months = 2/6**:

- Interest expense: \(5{,}553 \times 2/6 = \mathbf{1{,}851}\)  
- Premium amort: \(1{,}647 \times 2/6 = \mathbf{549}\)  
- Interest payable: \(7{,}200 \times 2/6 = \mathbf{2{,}400}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 1,851 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 549 | |
| Interest Payable | | 2,400 |

(Premium amortization reduces the adjunct liability and is debited in the adjusting entry, same structure as the cash-interest entry.)

**e. Presentation — December 31, Year 1**

| Bonds payable | $180,000 |
| Add: Premium on bonds payable | 3,445 |
| **Bonds payable, net** | **$183,445** |

Year 1 interest expense \(= 5{,}601 + 5{,}553 = \mathbf{\$11{,}154}\).

**f. Maturity principal**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 180,000 | |
| Cash | | 180,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 180,000. **Balanced.** (Premium fully amortized before/with final interest so net carrying amount = face.)

**g. Between interest dates (par)**

Accrued interest for 3 months (Jan 1–Apr 1):  
\(240{,}000 \times 7\% \times 3/12 = \mathbf{\$4{,}200}\).  
Cash proceeds \(= 240{,}000 + 4{,}200 = \mathbf{\$244{,}200}\).  
(Bond **price** at par excludes accrued interest; **proceeds** include it.)

*April 1, Year 1 — issue at par between interest dates*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 244,200 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 240,000 |
| Interest Payable | | 4,200 |

**Check:** Dr 244,200 = Cr 240,000 + 4,200. **Balanced.**

*June 30, Year 1 — first interest payment*  
Full six-month cash: \(240{,}000 \times 7\% \times 6/12 = \mathbf{\$8{,}400}\).  
Interest expense for 3 months outstanding: \(240{,}000 \times 7\% \times 3/12 = \mathbf{\$4{,}200}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 4,200 | |
| Interest Expense | 4,200 | |
| Cash | | 8,400 |

**Check:** Dr 4,200 + 4,200 = Cr 8,400. **Balanced.**

**h. Classification / presentation (Part A, Dec 31, Y1)**  
Report as a **long-term liability**: **Bonds payable, net $183,445** (face plus unamortized premium). Entire principal is due December 31, Year 2 (still noncurrent at Dec 31, Y1 if the company classifies only amounts due within one year as current—here the full face becomes current in Year 2; at Dec 31 Y1 the net carrying amount is long-term). Premium is an **adjunct** liability; do not present premium as a separate asset.

**Key insight:** When market < stated, investors pay a **premium**; subsequent measurement **amortizes the premium against interest expense**. Period-end adjusting entries for premium bonds **debit Premium** (with Interest Expense) and credit Interest Payable. Bonds sold between interest dates include **accrued stated interest** in cash proceeds.

### Q4 — MC — Market vs stated: issue classification and interest cost
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Classification of bond issuance outcome from market vs stated rate relationship  
**Question:** On the authorization date, **Silverpine Robotics Ltd.** sets a **6% stated (coupon) rate** on a new multi-year bond issue. By the actual settlement (issuance) date, the market yield for similar-risk bonds has risen to **8%**. Ignoring issuance costs and assuming interest payment dates match the issue date, how will the bonds be issued, and what is the relation between total interest expense over the bond term and total cash interest paid?

- A) Issued at a **premium**; total interest expense **exceeds** total cash interest  
- B) Issued at a **discount**; total interest expense **exceeds** total cash interest  
- C) Issued at a **discount**; total interest expense **is less than** total cash interest  
- D) Issued at **face (par)**; total interest expense **equals** total cash interest  

**Answer:** **B.** Market rate (8%) > stated rate (6%) → issue price < face → **discount**. The discount is additional interest cost amortized over the term, so total interest expense = total cash coupons + initial discount > total cash interest.

### Q5 — MC — Period-end adjusting structure (discount bond)
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Structure of the period-end adjusting JE when fiscal year-end falls between interest dates (discount bond)  
**Question:** A company issued bonds at a **discount**, uses the effective interest method, and pays cash interest only on interest dates. At fiscal year-end, three months have elapsed since the last interest date (and three months remain until the next). Which entry correctly accrues the period-end adjustment?

- A) Debit Interest Expense for three months of **cash (stated) interest only**; credit Interest Payable for the same amount (no discount amortization until cash is paid)  
- B) Debit Interest Expense for three months of **effective interest**; credit Discount on Bonds Payable for three months of amortization; credit Interest Payable for three months of **stated cash interest**  
- C) Debit Interest Expense for six months of effective interest; credit Cash for six months of stated interest; credit Discount for the difference  
- D) Debit Bonds Payable and credit Interest Payable for three months of stated interest  

**Answer:** **B.** Between interest dates, accrue **effective interest expense** on the beginning carrying amount for the elapsed fraction of the semi-period, amortize the related **discount**, and credit **Interest Payable** (not Cash) for the accrued stated coupon. Option A omits discount amortization; C records a full cash interest entry early; D misstates Bonds Payable.
