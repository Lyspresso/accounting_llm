# Agent 357 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-2

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Record customer deposits and advances  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** cash from customer before goods/services → credit **Liability—Returnable Deposit**, **Deferred Revenue**, or **Deferred Gift Card Revenue** (never Sales until earned or forfeited)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** multi-period roll-forward of deferred revenue / gift-card liability (beginning balance, advances/sales, revenue recognized or redemptions/breakage, ending balance)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** year-end (or month-end) entries that reclassify earned deferred revenue to Service/Sales revenue, and proportional gift-card breakage revenue
- **Settlement / maturity JE:** return of deposit (Dr liability / Cr cash); forfeiture (Dr liability / Cr sales + COGS if inventory); final period of advance (Dr deferred revenue / Cr revenue); gift-card redemption
- **Returnable deposits:** liability until returned or forfeited; forfeited deposit → revenue; related inventory cost → COGS when forfeited
- **Customer advances:** contract liability (deferred revenue) until performance obligation satisfied
- **Gift cards (core special case):** sale → deferred gift-card revenue; redemption → sales; breakage under **proportional method** (if criteria met) vs when redemption is **remote**
- Classification: deposits/advances/gift-card liabilities are **current** when settlement expected within one year (or operating cycle)

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-period customer advance with year-end adjusting (emphasis: period_end_adjusting_JE)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE for deferred revenue on a multi-month customer advance; initial recognition JE; subsequent measurement schedule; final-period settlement JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Ridgepath Yoga Collective** (calendar-year company) sells prepaid studio packages.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Date cash collected | **October 1, Year 1** |
| Cash advance received | **$4,800** |
| Service period | **12 months** evenly (October Year 1 – September Year 2) |
| Recognition pattern | Straight-line monthly (performance obligation satisfied evenly over time) |
| Year-end | **December 31** |
| Final service month ends | **September 30, Year 2** |

No refunds; no significant financing component (core Demo 15-2B path only — not the Expanding Your Knowledge financing example).

**Required:**  
a. Record the **October 1, Year 1** journal entry for **initial recognition** of the customer advance.  
b. Compute **monthly service revenue** and the amount of revenue earned in **Year 1** vs **Year 2**.  
c. Prepare a complete **subsequent-measurement schedule** of the deferred revenue liability for each of the **12 months** (beginning balance, revenue recognized, ending balance).  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 1** **period-end adjusting entry** for revenue earned in Year 1 (company books revenue via year-end adjusting entries, not monthly postings). Show the computation that supports the adjusting amount.  
e. State the **December 31, Year 1** balance-sheet amount and classification of any remaining deferred revenue.  
f. Record the **September 30, Year 2** entry that settles the **final month** of the performance obligation (assume August 31 Year 2 books already recognized revenue through August).  
g. Prove that total Service Revenue recognized over the life of the contract equals the original cash advance.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*October 1, Year 1 — Collect customer advance*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 4,800 | |
| Deferred Revenue | | 4,800 |
| *Customer prepayment before performance; contract liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,800 = Cr 4,800. **Balanced.**

**b. Monthly and annual revenue**

Monthly service revenue = \(\$4{,}800 / 12 = \$400\).

Year 1 months satisfied (Oct–Dec) = \(3 \times \$400 = \$1{,}200\).  
Year 2 months satisfied (Jan–Sep) = \(9 \times \$400 = \$3{,}600\).

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule — Deferred Revenue liability**

| Month | Beginning Deferred Revenue | Revenue recognized | Ending Deferred Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct Y1 | 4,800 | 400 | 4,400 |
| Nov Y1 | 4,400 | 400 | 4,000 |
| Dec Y1 | 4,000 | 400 | **3,600** |
| Jan Y2 | 3,600 | 400 | 3,200 |
| Feb Y2 | 3,200 | 400 | 2,800 |
| Mar Y2 | 2,800 | 400 | 2,400 |
| Apr Y2 | 2,400 | 400 | 2,000 |
| May Y2 | 2,000 | 400 | 1,600 |
| Jun Y2 | 1,600 | 400 | 1,200 |
| Jul Y2 | 1,200 | 400 | 800 |
| Aug Y2 | 800 | 400 | 400 |
| Sep Y2 | 400 | 400 | **0** |
| **Totals** | | **4,800** | |

**Roll-forward check:** Beginning cash advance − total revenue recognized = \(\$4{,}800 - \$4{,}800 = \$0\) ending liability.  
Year 1 revenue on schedule = \(3 \times \$400 = \$1{,}200\); YE ending balance = **\$3,600**.

**d. Period-end adjusting entry — December 31, Year 1 (emphasis)**

Computation of adjusting amount:
- Months of performance completed in Year 1 = October, November, December = **3 months**
- Monthly revenue = \$400
- YE adjusting amount = \(3 \times \$400 = \$1{,}200\)
- Equivalently: \(\$4{,}800 \times 3/12 = \$1{,}200\)

*December 31, Year 1 — Recognize 3 months of studio revenue (period-end adjusting JE)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Revenue | 1,200 | |
| Service Revenue | | 1,200 |
| *\$400 × 3 months (Oct–Dec); reclassify earned portion of contract liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,200 = Cr 1,200. **Balanced.**

After the adjusting entry: Deferred Revenue balance = \(\$4{,}800 - \$1{,}200 = \$3{,}600\).

**e. Balance sheet, December 31, Year 1**

Remaining deferred revenue = **\$3,600**.  
All remaining service is due within the next year → report **\$3,600 as a current liability** (Deferred Revenue / contract liability).

**f. Settlement — final month of performance (September 30, Year 2)**

After August recognition, liability balance = **\$400**.  
September satisfies the last month:

*September 30, Year 2 — Recognize final month of prepaid studio package*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Revenue | 400 | |
| Service Revenue | | 400 |
| *Settle remaining contract liability as performance obligation completed* | | |

**Check:** Dr 400 = Cr 400. **Balanced.** Ending deferred revenue = **\$0**.

**g. Life-of-contract reconciliation**

Service Revenue: Year 1 \$1,200 + Year 2 \$3,600 = **\$4,800** = original cash advance.  
All JEs balance; schedule rolls to zero at maturity of the performance period.

**Key insight (period-end adjusting emphasis):** Collecting cash creates a **liability**, not revenue. At **period-end**, the adjusting entry measures how much of the performance obligation has been satisfied (**months completed / total months** × cash advance) and moves that amount from Deferred Revenue to Service Revenue. The remaining liability stays on the balance sheet until later periods settle it.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Multi-period advance, YE adj (emphasis), settlement
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: period-end adjusting JE for deferred revenue; initial recognition; subsequent measurement schedule; final settlement JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Lumen River Tutoring LLC** (calendar year) collects a cash prepayment for a multi-month academic coaching package.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Date cash collected | **September 1, Year 1** |
| Cash advance | **$7,200** |
| Service period | **8 months** evenly (September Year 1 – April Year 2) |
| Recognition | Straight-line monthly |
| Year-end | **December 31** |
| Package fully performed | **April 30, Year 2** |

**Required:**  
a. Record **September 1, Year 1** initial recognition.  
b. Compute monthly revenue; revenue earned in Year 1 and Year 2.  
c. Prepare the **subsequent-measurement schedule** of Deferred Revenue for all **8 months**.  
d. Record **December 31, Year 1** **period-end adjusting entry** (revenue recognized via YE adjusting entry only). Show the fraction-of-contract computation.  
e. Compute December 31, Year 1 deferred revenue balance and classification.  
f. Record **April 30, Year 2** entry for the **final month** (assume revenue through March Year 2 already recognized).  
g. Reconcile: total credits to Service Revenue over the life of the contract must equal the original cash advance.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*September 1, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 7,200 | |
| Deferred Revenue | | 7,200 |

**Check:** Dr 7,200 = Cr 7,200. **Balanced.**

**b. Monthly and annual amounts**

Monthly revenue = \(\$7{,}200 / 8 = \$900\).

Year 1 (Sep–Dec) = \(4 \times \$900 = \$3{,}600\).  
Year 2 (Jan–Apr) = \(4 \times \$900 = \$3{,}600\).

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule**

| Month | Beginning Deferred Revenue | Revenue recognized | Ending Deferred Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sep Y1 | 7,200 | 900 | 6,300 |
| Oct Y1 | 6,300 | 900 | 5,400 |
| Nov Y1 | 5,400 | 900 | 4,500 |
| Dec Y1 | 4,500 | 900 | **3,600** |
| Jan Y2 | 3,600 | 900 | 2,700 |
| Feb Y2 | 2,700 | 900 | 1,800 |
| Mar Y2 | 1,800 | 900 | 900 |
| Apr Y2 | 900 | 900 | **0** |
| **Totals** | | **7,200** | |

**Roll-forward check:** \(\$7{,}200 - \$7{,}200 = \$0\). YE ending balance after 4 months = **\$3,600**.

**d. December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting entry (emphasis)**

Fraction of contract completed in Year 1 = \(4/8 = 1/2\).  
Adjusting amount = \(\$7{,}200 \times 4/8 = \$3{,}600\) (or \(4 \times \$900\)).

*December 31, Year 1 — YE adjusting entry to recognize earned revenue*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Revenue | 3,600 | |
| Service Revenue | | 3,600 |
| *\$900 × 4 months (Sep–Dec)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,600 = Cr 3,600. **Balanced.**

**e. December 31, Year 1 deferred revenue balance**

\(\$7{,}200 - \$3{,}600 = \$3{,}600\) **current liability** (remaining 4 months of coaching all within one year).

**f. April 30, Year 2 — final month settlement**

Remaining liability after March = **\$900**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Revenue | 900 | |
| Service Revenue | | 900 |

**Check:** Dr 900 = Cr 900. **Balanced.** Liability = **\$0**.

**g. Life-of-contract reconciliation**

Service Revenue: Year 1 \$3,600 + Year 2 \$3,600 = **\$7,200** = original cash advance.  
All JEs balance; schedule rolls to zero at maturity of the performance period.

**Key insight:** Changing only the **cash amount**, **start date**, and **contract length** changes monthly revenue and the **period-end adjusting** split of earned vs deferred at year-end, but the mechanics are identical: **Dr Cash / Cr Deferred Revenue**, then **Dr Deferred Revenue / Cr Revenue** at period-end (and each subsequent period) until settlement.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Gift cards: sale, redemption, period-end proportional breakage (emphasis: period_end_adjusting_JE)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE for gift-card breakage under the proportional method; initial sale JE; redemption settlement; subsequent measurement schedule; remote alternative  
**Scenario:**  
**Copperkettle Bakery Co.** (calendar year) sells nonrefundable gift cards. Management can estimate breakage and it is **probable** that a significant reversal of recognized breakage will **not** occur → **proportional method** (core Demo 15-2C).

| Fact | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Gift cards sold for cash during Year 1 | **$15,000** |
| Gift cards redeemed during Year 1 | **$9,000** |
| Estimated portion of sold value that will **never** be redeemed (breakage rate) | **25%** |
| Estimated portion that will eventually be redeemed | **75%** |

Ignore COGS on redemptions. No escheatment (core path only — not the Expanding Your Knowledge escheatment sidebar).

**Required:**  
a. Record the **Year 1 summary entry** for sale of gift cards (**initial recognition**).  
b. Record the **Year 1 summary entry** for redemptions (**settlement** of part of the liability).  
c. Compute expected total redemptions, expected total breakage, the Year 1 **redemption rate**, and **proportional breakage revenue**.  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 1** **period-end adjusting entry** for gift-card breakage under the proportional method (emphasis).  
e. Prepare a **subsequent-measurement schedule** (liability roll-forward) for Deferred Gift Card Revenue for Year 1.  
f. **Alternative:** Assume instead the company does **not** meet proportional-method criteria and the chance of redeeming the **remaining unredeemed** cards is **remote**. Record the December 31 adjusting entry under that assumption (ignore the proportional entry in part d).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Sale of gift cards**

*During Year 1 — Sale of gift cards*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 15,000 | |
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | | 15,000 |
| *Advance for future goods/services; contract liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr 15,000 = Cr 15,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Redemption of gift cards**

*During Year 1 — Redemption of gift cards*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 9,000 | |
| Sales | | 9,000 |
| *Settle liability as performance obligation satisfied on redemption* | | |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Proportional breakage computations**

| Item | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Expected total redemptions | \(\$15{,}000 \times 75\%\) | **11,250** |
| Expected total breakage | \(\$15{,}000 \times 25\%\) | **3,750** |
| Year 1 redemption rate | \(\$9{,}000 / \$11{,}250\) | **80%** |
| Proportional breakage revenue (Year 1) | \(\$3{,}750 \times 80\%\) | **3,000** |

Cross-check: \(\$3{,}750 \times (\$9{,}000 / \$11{,}250) = \$3{,}000\).

**d. Period-end adjusting entry — proportional breakage (emphasis)**

*December 31, Year 1 — YE adjusting entry to recognize gift card breakage revenue*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 3,000 | |
| Gift Card Breakage Revenue | | 3,000 |
| *Proportional method: estimated breakage × (actual redemptions / expected total redemptions)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,000 = Cr 3,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Subsequent measurement schedule — Deferred Gift Card Revenue (Year 1)**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 0 |
| + Gift card sales (cash advances) | 15,000 |
| − Redemptions reclassified to Sales | (9,000) |
| − Proportional breakage (YE adjusting) | (3,000) |
| **Ending Deferred Gift Card Revenue** | **3,000** |

**Check:** \(\$15{,}000 - \$9{,}000 - \$3{,}000 = \$3{,}000\).  
Unredeemed cash still on books before breakage was \(\$15{,}000 - \$9{,}000 = \$6{,}000\); after recognizing \$3,000 breakage, remaining contract liability = **\$3,000** (expected future redemptions still outstanding: \(\$11{,}250 - \$9{,}000 = \$2{,}250\), plus remaining unrecognized breakage \(\$3{,}750 - \$3{,}000 = \$750\); total **\$3,000**).

**f. Alternative — remote redemption method**

Remaining unredeemed after redemptions only = \(\$15{,}000 - \$9{,}000 = \$6{,}000\).  
If chance of redemption is **remote**, recognize the full remaining balance as breakage at period-end:

*December 31, Year 1 — YE adjusting entry when redemption is remote*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 6,000 | |
| Gift Card Breakage Revenue | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr 6,000 = Cr 6,000. **Balanced.** Ending deferred gift card revenue = **\$0**.

**Key insight (period-end adjusting emphasis):** Gift card **sales** create deferred revenue. **Redemptions** settle the liability to Sales when customers use the cards. At **period-end**, if proportional-method criteria are met, an **adjusting entry** recognizes breakage in proportion to the pattern of redemptions (\(\text{estimated breakage} \times \text{redemption rate}\)). If criteria are not met, breakage waits until redemption is **remote**.

### Q4 — CORE alternate angle — Returnable deposits: collection, return, forfeiture (settlement)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of returnable deposits; settlement by cash return; settlement by forfeiture with sales and COGS; deposit liability schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**Trailforge Outdoor Rentals** rents camping and trail equipment. Customers pay a **returnable deposit** that is refunded if gear is returned undamaged. Damaged or unreturned gear causes **forfeiture** of the deposit. Gear is carried in **Inventory** until a related deposit is forfeited.

**May activity (summary):**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Customer deposits collected in May | **$9,600** |
| Deposits returned in cash (gear returned in good condition) | **$8,800** |
| Deposits forfeited (gear damaged or not returned) | **$800** |
| Cost of gear related to forfeited deposits | **70%** of the forfeited deposit amount |
| Usable gear recovered on forfeitures | **None** |

Beginning Liability—Returnable Deposit on May 1 = **\$0**.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **May summary entry** for **collection** of customer deposits (**initial recognition**).  
b. Record the **May summary entry** for **return** of deposits to customers (**settlement by refund**).  
c. Record the **May entries** for **forfeiture** of deposits (revenue and COGS) (**settlement by forfeiture**).  
d. Prepare a **deposit liability roll-forward** for May.  
e. Compute the **net cash effect** of all deposit activity in May and the **net income effect** of forfeitures only.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Collection of customer deposits**

*May — Collect returnable deposits*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 9,600 | |
| Liability—Returnable Deposit | | 9,600 |
| *Cash held as refundable deposit; not revenue* | | |

**Check:** Dr 9,600 = Cr 9,600. **Balanced.**

**b. Return of deposits (settlement by refund)**

Deposits returned = **\$8,800**.

*May — Return deposits to customers*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability—Returnable Deposit | 8,800 | |
| Cash | | 8,800 |
| *Settle liability by cash refund when gear returned undamaged* | | |

**Check:** Dr 8,800 = Cr 8,800. **Balanced.**

**c. Forfeiture of deposits (settlement by revenue recognition)**

Forfeited deposits = **\$800**.  
COGS = \(0.70 \times \$800 = \$560\).

*May — Recognize revenue on deposit forfeiture*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability—Returnable Deposit | 800 | |
| Sales | | 800 |
| *Customer fails to perform; deposit forfeited → revenue* | | |

**Check:** Dr 800 = Cr 800. **Balanced.**

*May — Recognize COGS on deposit forfeiture*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 560 | |
| Inventory | | 560 |
| *Remove inventory cost of gear not recovered* | | |

**Check:** Dr 560 = Cr 560. **Balanced.**

**d. Liability roll-forward (May)**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Liability—Returnable Deposit | 0 |
| + Collections | 9,600 |
| − Cash returns | (8,800) |
| − Forfeitures reclassified to Sales | (800) |
| **Ending Liability—Returnable Deposit** | **0** |

**Check:** \(\$9{,}600 - \$8{,}800 - \$800 = \$0\).

**e. Cash and income effects**

Net cash from deposit activity = collections − returns = \(\$9{,}600 - \$8{,}800 = \$800\) cash retained (equals forfeitures).  
Income effect of forfeitures: Sales \$800 − COGS \$560 = **\$240** gross profit.

**Key insight:** Returnable deposits start as **liabilities**. **Settlement** is either a cash refund (Dr liability / Cr cash) or **forfeiture** (Dr liability / Cr sales), with inventory cost moved to COGS when the company no longer expects recovery of the asset. Forfeiture is a settlement event, not a period-end accrual of unearned revenue from a performance contract.

### Q5 — MC — Initial credit when cash is collected from customer
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Classify initial credit for returnable deposit vs customer advance vs immediate revenue  
**Question:**  
On November 15, **Bluecanopy Event Rentals** collects **\$2,500** cash from a customer. In Case A, the cash is a **returnable security deposit** on rented tents (refundable if tents are returned undamaged). In Case B, the cash is a **nonrefundable advance** for tent-rental services to be performed in January. Which statement is correct?

- A) Case A credits Sales \$2,500; Case B credits Deferred Revenue \$2,500.  
- B) Case A credits Liability—Returnable Deposit \$2,500; Case B credits Deferred Revenue \$2,500.  
- C) Case A credits Deferred Revenue \$2,500; Case B credits Liability—Returnable Deposit \$2,500.  
- D) Both Case A and Case B credit Sales \$2,500 because cash was received.

**Answer:** **B.**  
A returnable deposit is a **refundable liability** until returned or forfeited (Demo 15-2A). A customer **advance** for future services is **Deferred Revenue** (a contract liability) until the performance obligation is satisfied (Demo 15-2B). Neither is Sales at the cash-collection date when the company still has an obligation to the customer.

### Q6 — MC — Period-end gift-card breakage under proportional method
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting amount for proportional gift-card breakage  
**Question:**  
**Sable & Stem Florist** sold **\$4,000** of gift cards and customers redeemed **\$2,400** during the year. Management estimates that **80%** of gift card sales will eventually be redeemed (so **20%** breakage). Using the **proportional method**, what amount of **gift card breakage revenue** should the December 31 adjusting entry credit?

- A) \$800  
- B) \$600  
- C) \$480  
- D) \$320  

**Answer:** **B. \$600.**  
Expected redemptions = \(\$4{,}000 \times 80\% = \$3{,}200\).  
Expected breakage = \(\$4{,}000 \times 20\% = \$800\).  
Redemption rate = \(\$2{,}400 / \$3{,}200 = 75\%\).  
Proportional breakage = \(\$800 \times 75\% = \$600\).  
YE adjusting entry: Dr Deferred Gift Card Revenue \$600 / Cr Gift Card Breakage Revenue \$600.
