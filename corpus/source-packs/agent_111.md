# Agent 111 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-6

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Account for Stock Dividends and Stock Splits  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Small stock dividend (initial recognition):** capitalize **fair value** of additional shares — Dr Retained Earnings (FV), Cr Common Stock Dividends Distributable (par), Cr PIC—Common (excess)
- **Large stock dividend / stock split effected in the form of a dividend:** capitalize **par (or stated) value only** — Dr Retained Earnings (par × new shares), Cr Common Stock Dividends Distributable
- **Distribution (settlement) JE:** Dr Common Stock Dividends Distributable, Cr Common Stock when shares are issued
- **Fractional shares:** cash settlement for fractional entitlements at FV; reduce whole shares issued accordingly
- **True stock split / reverse stock split:** **no journal entry**; par per share and share counts change proportionally; total equity unchanged
- **Subsequent equity / share schedule:** roll forward capital stock, PIC, RE, shares outstanding, and par per share from declaration through distribution (or split memo)
- **Period-end presentation:** Common Stock Dividends Distributable is **equity** (not a liability) if declared but not yet issued at reporting date
- **Classification judgment:** small generally &lt;20–25% of previously outstanding shares (FV); large ≥20–25% treated as stock split effected in form of a dividend (par)

### Q1 — CORE — Small stock dividend declaration & distribution, large dividend at par, true split, equity schedule
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JEs for small (FV) vs large (par) stock dividends; distribution settlement; true 2-for-1 stock split (no JE); subsequent equity and share schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**CedarBay Logistics Inc.** has the following stockholders’ equity balances on **June 1, Year 1**, immediately before any dividend or split action:

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $2 par; 80,000 shares issued and outstanding | $160,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 420,000 |
| Retained earnings | 680,000 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$1,260,000** |

The market price of CedarBay’s common stock is **$18 per share** on June 1. Consider **three independent** scenarios (each starts from the June 1 balances above).

**Scenario A — Small stock dividend**  
On **June 1**, CedarBay declares a **10%** common stock dividend. Distribution date is **June 30** to stockholders of record June 15. All entitlements are whole shares (no fractional shares).

**Scenario B — Large stock dividend (stock split effected in the form of a dividend)**  
On **June 1**, CedarBay declares a **40%** common stock dividend (additional shares = 40% of shares previously outstanding). Distribution date is **June 30**. Market price remains $18 (ignore market for measurement of large dividends).

**Scenario C — True stock split**  
On **June 1**, CedarBay effects a **2-for-1** stock split by reducing par value per share (not structured as a stock dividend). Authorized shares are adjusted as needed for disclosure.

**Required:**  
a. **Scenario A:** Prepare the **June 1 declaration** and **June 30 distribution** journal entries. Show Dr = Cr.  
b. **Scenario A:** Prepare a **subsequent equity / share schedule** showing Common stock, Common stock dividends distributable, PIC—CS, Retained earnings, Total equity, shares outstanding, and par per share at: (1) immediately **before** declaration, (2) **after** declaration but before distribution, and (3) **after** distribution.  
c. **Scenario B:** Prepare the **June 1 declaration** and **June 30 distribution** journal entries.  
d. **Scenario C:** State the journal entry (if any) and the **par per share**, **shares outstanding**, and **total common stock dollar amount** after the split. Prepare a brief equity comparison before vs after.  
e. **Classification / presentation:** Explain where **Common Stock Dividends Distributable** appears on the balance sheet between June 1 and June 30 under Scenario A, and contrast the **Retained earnings** reduction in Scenario A vs Scenario B.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Scenario A — Small stock dividend (record at fair value)**

New shares = 80,000 × 10% = **8,000**  
Fair value capitalized = 8,000 × $18 = **$144,000**  
Par of distributable shares = 8,000 × $2 = **$16,000**  
PIC excess = $144,000 − $16,000 = **$128,000**

**June 1 — Declaration (initial recognition, FV basis)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (8,000 × $18) | 144,000 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable (8,000 × $2) | | 16,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 128,000 |
| *Capitalize fair value of small stock dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr 144,000 = Cr 16,000 + 128,000. **Balanced.**

**June 30 — Distribution (settlement of distributable)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 16,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 16,000 |
| *Issue 8,000 shares for stock dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 16,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Scenario A — Subsequent equity / share schedule**

| Item | Before declaration | After declaration (pre-distribution) | After distribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Common stock ($2 par) | $160,000 | $160,000 | $176,000 |
| Common stock dividends distributable | 0 | 16,000 | 0 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—CS | 420,000 | 548,000 | 548,000 |
| Retained earnings | 680,000 | 536,000 | 536,000 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$1,260,000** | **$1,260,000** | **$1,260,000** |
| Shares outstanding | 80,000 | 80,000* | 88,000 |
| Par per share | $2.00 | $2.00 | $2.00 |

\*Shares outstanding for voting/ownership percentage purposes remain 80,000 until issuance; the **right** to 8,000 additional shares is reflected in equity via Dividends Distributable. Total equity is unchanged at every column.

**Roll-forward checks:**  
RE: 680,000 − 144,000 = 536,000  
PIC: 420,000 + 128,000 = 548,000  
CS after issue: 160,000 + 16,000 = 176,000  
Total: 176,000 + 548,000 + 536,000 = **1,260,000**

**c. Scenario B — Large stock dividend (par value only)**

New shares = 80,000 × 40% = **32,000**  
Amount capitalized = 32,000 × $2 par = **$64,000**  
(Fair value $18 is **not** used.)

**June 1 — Declaration (initial recognition, par basis)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (32,000 × $2) | 64,000 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | | 64,000 |
| *Stock split effected in the form of a dividend at par* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 64,000. **Balanced.**

**June 30 — Distribution**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 64,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 64,000 |
| *Issue 32,000 shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 64,000. **Balanced.**

After full cycle: CS = $224,000 (112,000 shares × $2); PIC unchanged $420,000; RE = $616,000; total equity still **$1,260,000**.

**d. Scenario C — True 2-for-1 stock split**

**Journal entry:** **None** (no change in total dollar amounts of Common stock, PIC, or Retained earnings).

| Item | Before split | After 2-for-1 split |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shares outstanding | 80,000 | **160,000** |
| Par value per share | $2.00 | **$1.00** |
| Common stock (total $) | $160,000 | **$160,000** |
| PIC—CS | 420,000 | 420,000 |
| Retained earnings | 680,000 | 680,000 |
| **Total equity** | **$1,260,000** | **$1,260,000** |

Disclosure: common stock, **$1 par**; 160,000 shares issued and outstanding (authorized shares typically doubled for presentation consistency).

**e. Classification / presentation**  
- Between June 1 and June 30 under Scenario A, **Common Stock Dividends Distributable $16,000** is reported in the **stockholders’ equity** section (with or near common stock / paid-in capital)—**not** as a liability.  
- **Scenario A (small)** reduces RE by **fair value $144,000** and increases PIC by **$128,000** plus capital stock by **$16,000**.  
- **Scenario B (large)** reduces RE by only **par $64,000** with **no** PIC credit from the dividend.  
- Same total equity in both; the **composition** of equity differs substantially.

**Key insight:** Small stock dividends capitalize **FV** (RE ↓, capital stock + PIC ↑); large stock dividends (stock split effected as a dividend) capitalize **par only**; a **true split** needs **no entry**—only share count and par per share change.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Small FV dividend, large par dividend, 3-for-1 split twin
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: small stock dividend at FV; large stock dividend at par; true stock split effects on shares and par  
**Scenario:**  
**Pinecrest Outdoor Co.** reports the following equity on **November 1, Year 2**:

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $1 par; 120,000 shares issued and outstanding | $120,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 360,000 |
| Retained earnings | 540,000 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$1,020,000** |

Market price of common stock on November 1 is **$25 per share**. Treat each case **independently** from the November 1 balances.

**Case 1:** Declares and later issues an **8%** stock dividend (all whole shares). Declaration **November 1**; distribution **November 20**.  
**Case 2:** Declares and later issues a stock split effected in the form of a **100%** stock dividend. Declaration **November 1**; distribution **November 20**.  
**Case 3:** Effects a **3-for-1** true stock split on **November 1** (par reduced proportionally).

**Required:**  
a. **Case 1:** Compute new shares, FV capitalized, par of new shares, and PIC. Prepare **declaration** and **distribution** JEs.  
b. **Case 1:** Complete an equity roll-forward (total equity must be unchanged).  
c. **Case 2:** Prepare declaration and distribution JEs at **par**. State shares outstanding after distribution.  
d. **Case 3:** No JE. Report shares outstanding, par per share, and total common stock $ after the split.  
e. Compute the **difference in ending Retained earnings** between Case 1 and Case 2 after each is fully distributed (both start from RE $540,000).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Case 1 — Small 8% stock dividend at FV**

New shares = 120,000 × 8% = **9,600**  
FV = 9,600 × $25 = **$240,000**  
Par = 9,600 × $1 = **$9,600**  
PIC = 240,000 − 9,600 = **$230,400**

**November 1 — Declaration**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (9,600 × $25) | 240,000 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable (9,600 × $1) | | 9,600 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 230,400 |
| *Small stock dividend at fair value* | | |

**Check:** 240,000 = 9,600 + 230,400. **Balanced.**

**November 20 — Distribution**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 9,600 | |
| Common Stock | | 9,600 |
| *Issue 9,600 shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 9,600. **Balanced.**

**b. Case 1 — Equity after full distribution**

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock ($1 par; 129,600 shares) | $129,600 |
| PIC—CS (360,000 + 230,400) | 590,400 |
| Retained earnings (540,000 − 240,000) | 300,000 |
| **Total equity** | **$1,020,000** |

**Check:** 129,600 + 590,400 + 300,000 = 1,020,000. Unchanged.

**c. Case 2 — 100% stock dividend (large; par only)**

New shares = 120,000 × 100% = **120,000**  
Capitalize = 120,000 × $1 = **$120,000**

**November 1 — Declaration**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings | 120,000 | |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | | 120,000 |
| *Stock split effected in the form of a 100% stock dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 120,000. **Balanced.**

**November 20 — Distribution**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 120,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 120,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 120,000. **Balanced.**

Shares outstanding after distribution = 120,000 + 120,000 = **240,000**  
Equity: CS $240,000; PIC $360,000; RE $420,000; total **$1,020,000**.

**d. Case 3 — 3-for-1 true stock split**

**No journal entry.**

| Item | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shares outstanding | 120,000 | **360,000** |
| Par per share | $1.00 | **$1/3 ≈ $0.3333** |
| Common stock total $ | $120,000 | **$120,000** |
| Total equity | $1,020,000 | **$1,020,000** |

**e. RE difference after full distribution**  
Case 1 ending RE = **$300,000**  
Case 2 ending RE = **$420,000**  
Difference = 420,000 − 300,000 = **$120,000** higher RE under the large (par-only) treatment than under the small (FV) treatment for these independent scenarios.  
(Note: percentages differ by design in this twin; the comparison isolates that **measurement basis**—FV vs par—drives RE capitalization, not merely “more shares.”)

**Key insight:** Same company framework, new numbers: small dividends still hit RE at **market**, large “split-form” dividends at **par**, true splits **memo only**.

### Q3 — CORE — Fractional shares cash settlement; year-end distributable equity presentation; reverse split
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** Small stock dividend with fractional-share cash; period-end equity presentation of dividends distributable; reverse stock split (no JE); settlement on distribution date  
**Scenario:**  
**HarborMill Textiles Corp.** (calendar-year reporter) has **50,000** shares of **$5 par** common stock issued and outstanding on **December 10, Year 1**. Related equity accounts:

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $5 par; 50,000 shares | $250,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common | 175,000 |
| Retained earnings | 410,000 |

On **December 10, Year 1**, HarborMill declares a **15%** common stock dividend when the market price is **$12 per share**. Distribution is scheduled for **January 20, Year 2**.

Of the theoretical **7,500** stock-dividend shares (50,000 × 15%), management determines that **7,100** will be issued as **whole shares** and **400** share-equivalents are **fractional**. HarborMill’s policy is to pay **cash** equal to fair value for fractional entitlements.

On **January 20, Year 2**, HarborMill issues the 7,100 whole shares and pays cash for the fractional portion.

Separately (independent of the dividend), assume instead that on **March 1, Year 2** HarborMill effects a **1-for-5 reverse stock split** when it has **57,100** shares outstanding at **$5 par** (post-dividend shares from a completed whole-share-only alternative—use only for the reverse-split requirement below).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **December 10, Year 1 declaration** journal entry for the small stock dividend with fractional shares (initial recognition at FV).  
b. Identify any **period-end adjusting JE** needed at **December 31, Year 1** related to this dividend, and state how **Common Stock Dividends Distributable** and **Dividends Payable** (fractional) are **classified / presented**.  
c. Prepare the **January 20, Year 2 settlement / distribution** journal entries (issue stock + pay fractional cash).  
d. Prepare a **brief equity schedule** at December 31, Year 1 (after declaration, before distribution). Confirm total equity equals pre-declaration total equity **minus** the cash-related liability still in equity’s net? (Show total equity and liabilities effect carefully.)  
e. For the **independent reverse stock split** on March 1: journal entry? Shares outstanding and par per share after the reverse split?

**Answer key:**  

**a. December 10, Year 1 — Declaration (small dividend + fractional cash)**

Theoretical shares = 50,000 × 15% = **7,500**  
Whole shares to issue = **7,100**  
Fractional share-equivalents = **400**  
Total FV to capitalize / assign = 7,500 × $12 = **$90,000**  
Cash for fractionals = 400 × $12 = **$4,800**  
CS Dividends Distributable = 7,100 × $5 = **$35,500**  
PIC on whole shares = 7,100 × ($12 − $5) = 7,100 × $7 = **$49,700**

**Check components:** 4,800 + 35,500 + 49,700 = **90,000** ✓

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings (7,500 × $12) | 90,000 | |
| Dividends Payable (400 × $12) | | 4,800 |
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable (7,100 × $5) | | 35,500 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 49,700 |
| *Small stock dividend; cash for fractional shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 4,800 + 35,500 + 49,700. **Balanced.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Period-end**  
**No additional adjusting entry** is required solely because the year closed: the December 10 declaration already recorded the full capitalization and the fractional liability.

**Classification / presentation at December 31, Year 1:**  
- **Common Stock Dividends Distributable $35,500** → **stockholders’ equity** (paid-in capital category; not a liability).  
- **Dividends Payable $4,800** → **current liability** (cash obligation for fractionals).  
- Total stockholders’ equity decreases by the **cash-related** portion only when considering that RE fell by $90,000 while equity credits rose by only $35,500 + $49,700 = $85,200; the $4,800 sits in liabilities—so **total equity declines by $4,800** relative to pre-declaration (the amount that will leave as cash). Total assets are unchanged until payment; the balance sheet balances via the liability.

**c. January 20, Year 2 — Settlement / distribution**

**Issue whole shares:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock Dividends Distributable | 35,500 | |
| Common Stock | | 35,500 |
| *Distribute 7,100 shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 35,500. **Balanced.**

**Pay fractional cash:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Dividends Payable | 4,800 | |
| Cash | | 4,800 |
| *Settle fractional-share cash* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 4,800. **Balanced.**

**d. Equity schedule at December 31, Year 1 (post-declaration)**

| Account | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock ($5 par; still 50,000 shares issued) | $250,000 |
| Common stock dividends distributable | 35,500 |
| PIC—CS (175,000 + 49,700) | 224,700 |
| Retained earnings (410,000 − 90,000) | 320,000 |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$830,200** |

Pre-declaration total equity = 250,000 + 175,000 + 410,000 = **$835,000**  
Post-declaration equity = **$830,200**  
Difference = **$4,800** = Dividends Payable (liability for fractionals).  
When cash is paid in Year 2, assets and liabilities both decrease $4,800; equity unchanged at that moment.

**e. Independent 1-for-5 reverse stock split**

**Journal entry: none.**

Starting shares (given for this independent case) = **57,100**  
After 1-for-5 reverse: 57,100 ÷ 5 = **11,420** shares  
Par per share: $5 × 5 = **$25**  
Total common stock $: 11,420 × $25 = **$285,500** (unchanged from 57,100 × $5 = $285,500)  
Total equity: **unchanged**.

**Key insight:** Fractional shares create a **cash liability** at FV inside an otherwise equity-only small stock dividend; year-end simply **presents** Dividends Distributable in equity and any fractional payable as a liability—no second adjusting entry. Reverse splits, like forward splits, need **no JE**.

### Q4 — MC — Small vs large measurement basis
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** Classification of stock dividend size determines FV vs par capitalization  
**Question:**  
On the date of declaration, **Ridgeway Components Inc.** has 200,000 shares of $1 par common stock outstanding. The board declares a stock dividend when the market price is $8 per share. Which statement is correct?

- A) A 10% stock dividend is recorded by debiting Retained Earnings for $20,000 (par only).  
- B) A 10% stock dividend is recorded by debiting Retained Earnings for $160,000 (fair value), with Common Stock Dividends Distributable credited for $20,000 and PIC for $140,000.  
- C) A 50% stock dividend (stock split effected in the form of a dividend) is recorded by debiting Retained Earnings for $800,000 (fair value of the new shares).  
- D) A 2-for-1 true stock split requires a debit to Retained Earnings for the par of the additional shares.

**Answer:** **B.**  
A 10% dividend is a **small** stock dividend → capitalize **FV**: 200,000 × 10% × $8 = **$160,000** to RE; distributable at par 20,000 × $1 = **$20,000**; PIC **$140,000**.  
A is wrong (uses par for a small dividend).  
C is wrong (large / split-form dividends use **par**, not FV: 100,000 × $1 = $100,000).  
D is wrong (true stock splits require **no journal entry**).

### Q5 — MC — Stock split presentation and equity effect
**LO:** LO 19-6  
**Concept:** True stock split — no JE; shares and par change; total equity and capital stock $ unchanged  
**Question:**  
**LumenPath Inc.** has 40,000 shares of $3 par common stock outstanding (Common stock account balance $120,000), PIC—CS of $280,000, and Retained earnings of $500,000. The company effects a **4-for-1** stock split (true split, not a stock dividend). Immediately after the split, which is correct?

- A) Common stock account = $480,000; shares outstanding = 160,000; par = $3.  
- B) Retained earnings decreases by $360,000; no other equity accounts change.  
- C) Shares outstanding = 160,000; par = $0.75; Common stock account remains $120,000; total equity remains $900,000.  
- D) A journal entry debits Retained Earnings $120,000 and credits Common Stock $120,000.

**Answer:** **C.**  
4-for-1 true split: shares 40,000 × 4 = **160,000**; par $3 ÷ 4 = **$0.75**; total Common stock $ still 160,000 × $0.75 = **$120,000**. PIC and RE unchanged; total equity 120 + 280 + 500 = **$900,000** unchanged. **No journal entry.**  
A incorrectly multiplies the Common stock **dollar** balance. B and D treat the split like a large stock dividend.
