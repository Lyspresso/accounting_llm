# Agent 165 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-2

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Prepare the operating activities section of the statement of cash flows using the indirect method  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Indirect-method starting point:** begin the operating section with **net income** (or net loss) and reconcil to **net cash provided (used) by operating activities**
- **Noncash expense/loss add-backs:** depreciation, amortization of intangibles, amortization of bond **discount**, stock-based compensation, losses on PPE disposal or debt extinguishment, unrealized losses in NI
- **Noncash revenue/gain deductions:** amortization of bond **premium**, gains on PPE/insurance/settlement, equity-method income in excess of dividends, unrealized gains in NI
- **Subsequent measurement / reconciliation schedule (emphasis):** rollforward T-accounts and a line-item reconciliation schedule (Δ operating assets/liabilities; AccDep; bond premium/discount)
- **Period-end adjusting JEs:** depreciation, patent amortization, bond premium/discount amortization that feed the SCF adjustments
- **Disposal / settlement JEs:** sale of equipment (gain/loss) and early bond retirement (gain/loss) — full cash effects go to investing/financing; only G/L is reversed in operating
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** operating WC changes vs investing (PPE proceeds) vs financing (debt repayment); interest paid and income taxes paid supplemental disclosures when indirect method is used
- **Number-variant twin:** same indirect OA skill path with fully changed facts and amounts

2. **Bond retirement cash $108,000:** reported as a **financing outflow**. The **$4,000 loss** is **added back** in operating so the loss does not reduce operating cash (full repayment sits in financing).  
3. **Supplemental disclosures (indirect method):** amount of **interest paid** (net of amounts capitalized) and **income taxes paid** during the period (ASC 230-10-50).

**Key insight:** Under the indirect method, start with accrual NI, then (1) reverse noncash expenses/losses and noncash revenues/gains, and (2) reverse the accrual effects of Δ operating assets and liabilities. Disposal and extinguishment **cash** never stays in operating—only the G/L is adjusted so NI becomes cash-from-operations.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Redwood Pathways: equity method, deferred taxes, unrealized holding gain, and partial OA build

### Q4 — MC — Sign of the OA adjustment (classification of reconciling item)
**LO:** LO 22-2  
**Concept:** Classify whether a given item is added to NI, subtracted from NI, or omitted from the indirect OA section  
**Question:**  
Under the **indirect method**, which treatment is correct for **amortization of a premium on bonds payable** that reduced interest expense during the period?

- A) Add the premium amortization to net income in the operating section  
- B) Subtract the premium amortization from net income in the operating section  
- C) Report the premium amortization as an investing cash outflow  
- D) Omit the premium amortization entirely because interest is always financing  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Premium amortization **increases** net income (by reducing interest expense) but does **not** provide cash. The indirect method therefore **subtracts** premium amortization. (Discount amortization is added.) Interest **paid** is an operating cash flow conceptually, but under the indirect method it is embedded in NI and WC (interest payable) adjustments—not reclassified to financing under U.S. GAAP. Premium amortization itself is not an investing item.

### Q5 — MC — Presentation of gain on PPE disposal in the SCF
**LO:** LO 22-2  
**Concept:** Classification/presentation of disposal gains under the indirect method  
**Question:**  
A company sells equipment for **$90,000** cash. Book value is **$70,000**, so a **$20,000** gain is included in net income. Using the **indirect method**, how should this event affect the statement of cash flows?

- A) Show $90,000 operating inflow; ignore the gain  
- B) Show $20,000 operating inflow and $70,000 investing inflow  
- C) Subtract $20,000 in the operating section and report $90,000 as an investing inflow  
- D) Add $20,000 in the operating section and report $70,000 as an investing inflow  

**Answer:** **C.**  
Full cash proceeds (**$90,000**) are an **investing inflow**. Because the **$20,000** gain is already inside NI, the operating section **subtracts** the gain so operating cash is not overstated. Adding the gain (D) or splitting proceeds into “gain vs book value” cash lines (B) is incorrect under standard U.S. GAAP presentation.
