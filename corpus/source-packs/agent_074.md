# Agent 74 — CORE DEMO — LO 11-9

**Chapter:** 11  
**LO title:** Account for exchange of property, plant, and equipment  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Update depreciation** on the asset given up through the exchange date before recording the exchange
- **Commercial substance** vs **lack of commercial substance** (future cash flows risk/timing/amount)
- **Initial recognition** of the new PPE asset: fair value of asset given up ± cash, or fair value of asset received (more clearly determinable)
- Full **gain** recognition when the exchange has commercial substance; full **loss** recognition always
- **No gain** when exchange lacks commercial substance and no cash is received (or cash is paid): new asset at book value ± cash
- **Partial gain** when exchange lacks commercial substance and cash is received: (cash / [cash + FV of other assets received]) × total potential gain
- **Subsequent measurement** of newly acquired PPE (depreciation schedule from exchange date)
- Removing (disposing of) the old asset’s cost and accumulated depreciation in the exchange entry
- Classification/presentation: Gain (Loss) on Asset Exchange on the income statement; ASC 845 25% cash exception (full gain if cash ≥ 25% of exchange FV)

### Q1 — CORE — Commercial substance exchange: update dep, initial recognition JE, subsequent dep
**LO:** LO 11-9  
**Concept:** Period-end (through-date) depreciation update; initial recognition JE for nonmonetary exchange with commercial substance (gain, cash paid); subsequent depreciation of new asset  
**Scenario:**  
**Clearwater Forge Co.** owns a hydraulic press used in production.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Cost of hydraulic press (old) | $120,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation, January 1, 2025 | $72,000 |
| Annual straight-line depreciation (old) | $12,000 |
| Residual value (old) | $0 |
| Exchange date | July 1, 2025 |
| Fair value of hydraulic press given up | $48,000 |
| Cash paid to complete the exchange | $15,000 |
| Fair value of new CNC mill received | not more clearly determinable than FV of asset given up |
| Commercial substance? | **Yes** (risk, timing, and amount of future cash flows expected to change significantly) |
| Estimated useful life of new CNC mill | 7 years |
| Residual value of new CNC mill | $7,000 |
| Depreciation method (new) | straight-line; full months from July 1 |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **July 1 adjusting entry** to update depreciation on the old hydraulic press through the exchange date.  
b. Compute the **carrying amount** of the old press and the **gain or loss** on the exchange.  
c. Prepare the **July 1 exchange journal entry** (initial recognition of the new CNC mill; disposal of the old press). Show that Dr = Cr.  
d. Prepare a brief **subsequent measurement schedule** for the CNC mill’s depreciable base and annual depreciation; record depreciation for the six months ended December 31, 2025.  
e. State how the **gain (or loss)** is presented in the 2025 income statement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. July 1, 2025 — update depreciation through exchange date**  
Half-year depreciation on old press: $12,000 × 6/12 = **$6,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Hydraulic Press | 6,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Hydraulic Press | | 6,000 |
| *Update AD through July 1 before recording the exchange* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 6,000.

**b. Carrying amount and gain**  
Accumulated depreciation at exchange = $72,000 + $6,000 = **$78,000**  
Carrying amount (book value) = $120,000 − $78,000 = **$42,000**  
Fair value of asset given up = **$48,000**  
**Gain on asset exchange** = $48,000 − $42,000 = **$6,000** (fully recognized — commercial substance)

Cost assigned to new CNC mill = FV of asset given up + cash paid = $48,000 + $15,000 = **$63,000**

**c. July 1, 2025 — initial recognition of nonmonetary exchange (commercial substance; gain; cash paid)**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—CNC Mill ($48,000 + $15,000) | 63,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Hydraulic Press | 78,000 | |
| Cash | | 15,000 |
| Gain on Asset Exchange | | 6,000 |
| Equipment—Hydraulic Press | | 120,000 |
| *Remove old press; record new mill at FV given up + cash paid; recognize full gain* | | |

**Check:** Dr 63,000 + 78,000 = **141,000**; Cr 15,000 + 6,000 + 120,000 = **141,000**. Balanced.

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule — CNC mill**

| Schedule item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Capitalized cost (initial recognition) | $63,000 |
| − Residual value | (7,000) |
| = Depreciable base | 56,000 |
| ÷ Useful life (years) | ÷ 7 |
| = **Annual depreciation** | **$8,000** |
| 2025 depreciation (July 1–Dec 31 = 6/12) | **$4,000** |

*December 31, 2025 — depreciation on new asset*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—CNC Mill | 4,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—CNC Mill | | 4,000 |
| *Six months’ SL depreciation: $8,000 × 6/12* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 4,000.  
Carrying amount of CNC mill at Dec 31, 2025 = $63,000 − $4,000 = **$59,000**.

**e. Classification / presentation**  
**Gain on Asset Exchange $6,000** is reported on the **income statement** (typically in other income/expense, not as revenue from customers). Full gain is recognized because the exchange has **commercial substance**.

**Key insight:** Always **update depreciation** on the asset given up before the exchange. With **commercial substance**, the new asset is recorded at **fair value** (of asset given up ± cash, or FV received if clearer), and **gains and losses are fully recognized**. The exchange entry simultaneously **disposes of** the old cost and accumulated depreciation and **initially recognizes** the new PPE.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Commercial substance exchange twin
**LO:** LO 11-9  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — update dep; initial recognition JE for exchange with commercial substance (gain, cash paid); subsequent depreciation schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Rail Systems Inc.** owns a track loader.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Cost of track loader (old) | $160,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation, January 1, 2026 | $100,000 |
| Annual straight-line depreciation (old) | $16,000 |
| Exchange date | April 1, 2026 |
| Fair value of track loader given up | $60,000 |
| Cash paid | $25,000 |
| Fair value of new loader | not more clearly determinable |
| Commercial substance? | **Yes** |
| Useful life of new loader | 5 years |
| Residual value of new loader | $5,000 |
| Depreciation (new) | straight-line from April 1 |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Required:**  
a. Update depreciation through April 1, 2026.  
b. Compute book value and gain; determine capitalized cost of the new loader.  
c. Record the exchange journal entry (must balance).  
d. Subsequent measurement: annual depreciation and 2026 depreciation entry (9 months).

**Answer key:**  

**a. April 1, 2026 — update depreciation**  
3/12 × $16,000 = **$4,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Track Loader | 4,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Track Loader | | 4,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 4,000.

**b. Book value, gain, and new cost**  
AD at exchange = $100,000 + $4,000 = **$104,000**  
Book value = $160,000 − $104,000 = **$56,000**  
Gain = $60,000 − $56,000 = **$4,000** (full recognition — commercial substance)  
New loader cost = $60,000 + $25,000 = **$85,000**

**c. April 1, 2026 — exchange (initial recognition)**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—New Loader | 85,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Track Loader | 104,000 | |
| Cash | | 25,000 |
| Gain on Asset Exchange | | 4,000 |
| Equipment—Track Loader | | 160,000 |

**Check:** Dr 85,000 + 104,000 = **189,000**; Cr 25,000 + 4,000 + 160,000 = **189,000**. Balanced.

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule**

| Schedule item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Capitalized cost | $85,000 |
| − Residual value | (5,000) |
| = Depreciable base | 80,000 |
| ÷ Useful life | ÷ 5 |
| = Annual depreciation | **$16,000** |
| 2026 depreciation (Apr 1–Dec 31 = 9/12) | **$12,000** |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—New Loader | 12,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—New Loader | | 12,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 12,000.  
Dec 31, 2026 carrying amount = $85,000 − $12,000 = **$73,000**.

**Key insight:** Same measurement logic as Q1 with different numbers: update AD → remove old asset at cost and AD → capitalize new asset at FV given up + cash → full gain when commercial substance exists → depreciate the new carrying amount from the exchange date.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Lacks commercial substance (deferral / partial gain) and full loss
**LO:** LO 11-9  
**Concept:** Gain deferral and partial gain when exchange lacks commercial substance; full loss recognition regardless of commercial substance; classification of gain recognition pattern  
**Scenario:**  
**Pinnacle Packaging Corp.** is trading productive equipment. In **all** cases below, depreciation on the asset given up has **already been updated** through the exchange date. For the old asset:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cost of equipment given up | $88,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation (updated) | $52,000 |
| Carrying amount (book value) | $36,000 |

**Case A — Gain, no cash, lacks commercial substance**  
Pinnacle exchanges the old equipment (FV **$44,000**) for similar equipment. No cash. Exchange **lacks commercial substance**.

**Case B — Gain, cash paid, lacks commercial substance**  
Same old equipment (FV **$44,000**). Pinnacle pays **$6,000** cash for similar equipment. Exchange **lacks commercial substance**. FV of new equipment is not more reliable than FV of old + cash.

**Case C — Gain, cash received, lacks commercial substance (partial gain)**  
Old equipment FV **$44,000**. Pinnacle receives similar equipment with FV **$33,000** and **$11,000** cash. Exchange **lacks commercial substance**.

**Case D — Loss (any commercial substance status)**  
Old equipment FV **$30,000**. Pinnacle receives similar equipment and **$11,000** cash (implied FV of equipment received = $19,000). Assume the exchange **lacks commercial substance**.

**Required:**  
a. Case A: compute potential gain and record the exchange JE.  
b. Case B: compute potential gain and record the exchange JE.  
c. Case C: compute total potential gain, **partial gain recognized**, deferred gain, new asset basis, and the exchange JE.  
d. Case D: record the exchange JE and explain the loss rule.  
e. Classification: complete the decision table (full gain / no gain / partial gain / full loss) for commercial substance vs lacks, and cash paid vs received.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Case A — no cash; lacks commercial substance → no gain recognized**  
Potential gain = $44,000 − $36,000 = **$8,000** (fully deferred)  
New equipment recorded at **carrying amount of asset given up** = **$36,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—New | 36,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Old | 52,000 | |
| Equipment—Old | | 88,000 |
| *Gain deferred; new asset at BV of asset given up* | | |

**Check:** Dr 36,000 + 52,000 = **88,000**; Cr = **88,000**. Balanced.

**b. Case B — cash paid; lacks commercial substance → no gain recognized**  
Potential gain = FV of consideration effectively realized: FV old $44,000 − BV $36,000 = **$8,000** (deferred)  
New equipment = BV of old + cash paid = $36,000 + $6,000 = **$42,000**  
(Alternatively: FV old + cash − deferred gain = $44,000 + $6,000 − $8,000 = $42,000)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—New | 42,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Old | 52,000 | |
| Cash | | 6,000 |
| Equipment—Old | | 88,000 |
| *Cash paid; gain fully deferred when lacks commercial substance* | | |

**Check:** Dr 42,000 + 52,000 = **94,000**; Cr 6,000 + 88,000 = **94,000**. Balanced.

**c. Case C — cash received; lacks commercial substance → partial gain**  
Total potential gain = $44,000 − $36,000 = **$8,000**  
Proportion of gain realized in cash = cash received / (cash received + FV of other assets received)  
= $11,000 / ($11,000 + $33,000) = $11,000 / $44,000 = **0.25**  
**Partial gain recognized** = 0.25 × $8,000 = **$2,000**  
Deferred gain = $8,000 − $2,000 = **$6,000**  
New equipment basis = (FV of asset given up − cash received) − deferred gain  
= ($44,000 − $11,000) − $6,000 = **$27,000**  
(Cross-check: BV − cash received + gain recognized = $36,000 − $11,000 + $2,000 = **$27,000**)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—New | 27,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Old | 52,000 | |
| Cash | 11,000 | |
| Gain on Asset Exchange | | 2,000 |
| Equipment—Old | | 88,000 |
| *Partial gain: ($11,000/$44,000) × $8,000 potential gain* | | |

**Check:** Dr 27,000 + 52,000 + 11,000 = **90,000**; Cr 2,000 + 88,000 = **90,000**. Balanced.

**d. Case D — loss fully recognized (even without commercial substance)**  
Loss = BV − FV of asset given up = $36,000 − $30,000 = **$6,000**  
New equipment = FV given up − cash received = $30,000 − $11,000 = **$19,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—New | 19,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Old | 52,000 | |
| Cash | 11,000 | |
| Loss on Asset Exchange | 6,000 | |
| Equipment—Old | | 88,000 |
| *Full loss recognized to avoid capitalizing above fair value* | | |

**Check:** Dr 19,000 + 52,000 + 11,000 + 6,000 = **88,000**; Cr = **88,000**. Balanced.

**Explanation:** Losses are **always fully recognized**, whether or not the exchange has commercial substance, so the new asset is not recorded above fair value.

**e. Classification / presentation decision table (core LO 11-9)**

| Situation | Gain/loss treatment |
|---|---|
| Loss on exchange (CS or no CS) | **Full loss** recognized |
| Gain + commercial substance | **Full gain** recognized |
| Gain + lacks CS + no cash or cash **paid** | **No gain** recognized (deferred in lower asset basis) |
| Gain + lacks CS + cash **received** | **Partial gain** recognized (cash boot formula) |
| Cash exchanged ≥ 25% of FV of exchange (ASC 845) | Entire transaction treated as monetary → **full gain** recognized |

Gains and losses appear as **Gain (Loss) on Asset Exchange** in income (other income/expense), not as sales revenue.

**Key insight:** Lack of commercial substance **suppresses earnings management** via similar-asset trades: gains are deferred (or only boot-realized partially), but **losses are never deferred**. New asset cost is the plug that implements the deferral.

### Q4 — MC — Gain recognition pattern on PPE exchange
**LO:** LO 11-9  
**Concept:** Classification of when full gain, partial gain, or no gain is recognized on a nonmonetary PPE exchange  

**Question:**  
Driftwood Marine LLC exchanges old docking equipment (cost $50,000; updated accumulated depreciation $28,000; fair value $30,000) for similar docking equipment and receives $6,000 cash. The fair value of the equipment received is $24,000. The exchange **lacks commercial substance**. What amount of **gain** does Driftwood recognize, and what is the **initial carrying amount** of the new equipment?

- A) Gain $0; new equipment $22,000  
- B) Gain $1,600; new equipment $17,600  
- C) Gain $8,000; new equipment $24,000  
- D) Gain $1,600; new equipment $24,000  

**Answer:** **B.**  

Book value = $50,000 − $28,000 = **$22,000**  
Total potential gain = $30,000 − $22,000 = **$8,000**  
Partial gain = [$6,000 / ($6,000 + $24,000)] × $8,000 = ($6,000 / $30,000) × $8,000 = **$1,600**  
New equipment = BV − cash received + gain recognized = $22,000 − $6,000 + $1,600 = **$17,600**  
(Alternatively: [$30,000 − $6,000] − deferred gain $6,400 = $24,000 − $6,400 = $17,600.)

A incorrectly uses no-gain treatment (appropriate only if no cash received or cash paid). C recognizes the full potential gain as if commercial substance existed and capitalizes at FV of asset received without reducing for deferred gain. D uses the correct partial gain but fails to reduce the new asset for the deferred portion of the gain.

### Q4b — MC (optional second) — Commercial substance vs loss rule
**LO:** LO 11-9  
**Concept:** Classification — full loss recognition independent of commercial substance  

**Question:**  
Which statement best describes U.S. GAAP for **losses** on exchanges of property, plant, and equipment?

- A) Losses are deferred when the exchange lacks commercial substance, similar to gains.  
- B) Losses are recognized in full only when cash is received.  
- C) Losses are recognized in full whether or not the exchange has commercial substance.  
- D) Losses are recognized only to the extent of cash boot received.

**Answer:** **C.** Full loss recognition prevents capitalizing the new asset above fair value. Commercial substance affects **gain** recognition (full / partial / none), not the loss rule.
