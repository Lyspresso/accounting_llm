# Agent 129 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-6

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Utilize a worksheet to prepare the statement of cash flows  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Cash flow worksheet (optional tool):** organized format that tracks every balance-sheet account change and routes cash effects to operating, investing, or financing in the lower half
- **Reconstructed (worksheet-only) entries:** explain BS changes; **not** posted to the general ledger; each entry must **Dr = Cr**
- **Lower-half convention:** SCF **additions = debits**; SCF **deductions = credits** (indirect method)
- **Initial recognition worksheet JEs:** cash purchase of equipment; cash issuance of common stock (and similar financing/investing recognitions)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule / noncash add-backs:** depreciation; amortization of patent or bond premium/discount
- **Period-end working-capital adjusting worksheet JEs:** AR (gross ± allowance), inventory, prepaid, AP, salaries payable, interest/tax payable
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement worksheet JEs:** sale of equipment (remove cost and AD; cash proceeds investing; gain/loss operating); bond retirement (remove face and premium/discount; cash paid financing; gain/loss operating)
- **Classification / presentation:** transfer lower-half activity sections to a formal SCF (indirect operating); reconcile beginning cash to ending cash
- **Number-variant twin:** same worksheet process with all amounts changed

On a cash flow worksheet (indirect method), which statement about the **lower half** is correct?

- A) An increase in accounts receivable is entered as a **debit** in the operating section of the lower half.  
- B) Depreciation expense is entered as a **credit** in the operating section of the lower half.  
- C) A cash purchase of equipment is entered as a **credit** in the investing section of the lower half (deduction/outflow).  
- D) Net income is entered as a **credit** in the operating section of the lower half.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Under the worksheet convention, **additions** to cash (or add-backs) are **debits** and **deductions**/outflows are **credits** in the lower half. A cash equipment purchase is an investing **outflow**, so it is a **credit** in the investing section (offset by a debit to Equipment in the upper half).  
- A is wrong: an AR increase is a **deduction** → **credit** in operating.  
- B is wrong: depreciation is an **add-back** → **debit** in operating.  
- D is wrong: net income is the starting **addition** → **debit** in operating (credit to RE).

**Question 2:**  
**Summitline Corp.** issued **$50,000** face bonds for cash of **$45,000** (discount **$5,000**). Which reconstructed **worksheet** entry is correct?

- A)  
  Dr Cash—Financing 50,000  
  Cr Bonds Payable 50,000  

- B)  
  Dr Issuance of Bonds—Financing 45,000  
  Dr Discount on Bonds Payable 5,000  
  Cr Bonds Payable 50,000  

- C)  
  Dr Bonds Payable 50,000  
  Cr Discount on Bonds Payable 5,000  
  Cr Issuance of Bonds—Financing 45,000  

- D)  
  Dr Issuance of Bonds—Investing 45,000  
  Dr Discount on Bonds Payable 5,000  
  Cr Bonds Payable 50,000  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Initial recognition on the worksheet: debit the **financing inflow** for cash proceeds **$45,000**, debit **Discount** **$5,000**, credit **Bonds Payable** face **$50,000**. Gross proceeds are financing (not investing). Option A ignores the discount and overstates the cash inflow. Option C reverses debits/credits. Option D misclassifies as investing.

**Key insight:** Worksheet reconstructed entries mirror the economics of the original transaction but label the cash leg by **O/I/F section** so the lower half drops straight into the SCF.
