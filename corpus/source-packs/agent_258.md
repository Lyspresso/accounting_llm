# Agent 258 — CORE DEMO — LO 21-4

**Chapter:** 21  
**LO title:** Demonstrate the accounting for an error correction  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Material error after FS issuance:** record a **prior period adjustment** (RE—PPA + affected asset/liability) and **restate** prior-period financial statements (as soon as practicable); not a change in estimate (prospective) and not a voluntary change in principle only
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** year-by-year **as-recorded vs correct** expense/amortization/depreciation and cumulative pretax/RE effect used to measure the PPA
- **Initial recognition / correcting JE:** capitalize (or remove) omitted or misclassified assets; set up omitted accruals; reduce overstated assets
- **Period-end adjusting JE after correction:** resume correct depreciation, amortization, and insurance expense in the discovery year and forward
- **Disposal / settlement after correction:** dispose of corrected PPE using **corrected cost and AD**; counterbalancing inventory errors **self-settle** with no JE after the two-year cycle (restatement still required if statements are presented)
- **Exceptions:** balance-sheet-only misclassification → reclass (no RE); income-statement-only misclassification → often no entry after accounts closed; counterbalancing error discovered **after** self-correction → no JE
- **Ignore income taxes** in this pack (tax effects of error corrections are LO 21-5)

### Q1 — CORE — Multiple material errors: subsequent measurement schedules, prior period adjustments, period-end JEs, and disposal after correction
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedules for multi-year depreciation, prepaid insurance, patent amortization, and omitted accrual; prior period adjustment JEs; discovery-year period-end adjusting entries; disposal of corrected equipment  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Packaging Corp.** (calendar-year) issued its Year 4 financial statements in early March of Year 5. In **April of Year 5**, after those statements were issued, management discovered the following **material** errors. Ignore income taxes.

| # | Facts |
|---|---|
| **1** | On **January 1, Year 3**, paid **$54,000** cash for a capital improvement to production equipment. The amount was **incorrectly debited to Maintenance Expense**. The improvement should have been capitalized to Equipment and depreciated **straight-line over 6 years with zero residual value**. |
| **2** | On **January 1, Year 3**, paid **$9,000** cash for a **3-year** insurance policy and **debited the entire premium to Insurance Expense**. |
| **3** | At **December 31, Year 4**, **accrued interest payable of $7,400** was omitted. The amount will be paid in Year 5. |
| **4** | A patent purchased for **$180,000** on **January 1, Year 2** has been amortized over its **15-year legal life**. Management failed to use the patent’s **6-year economic life**. Amortization is straight-line with zero residual. |

Assume discovery entries are dated **April 15, Year 5** (books for Year 5 are open; Year 4 books are closed). Straight-line methods apply for all depreciable/amortizable items.

**Required:**  
a. For **Error 1**, prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 3–4 comparing as-recorded vs correct expense and the cumulative effect on pretax income / retained earnings. Then record the **prior period adjustment** journal entry.  
b. For **Error 2**, prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for the insurance cost (Years 3–4) and record the **prior period adjustment**. State the correct prepaid balance immediately after the entry.  
c. For **Error 3**, compute the prior period adjustment and record the correcting entry.  
d. For **Error 4**, prepare a **subsequent measurement (amortization) schedule** for Years 2–4 (as recorded vs correct) and record the **prior period adjustment**.  
e. Record the **December 31, Year 5 period-end adjusting** entries for (i) depreciation of the improvement, (ii) amortization of the patent, and (iii) insurance expense related to the remaining prepaid.  
f. On **December 31, Year 6**, Cedarline **sells** the improved equipment component (the $54,000 improvement only) for **$15,500 cash**. Prepare the **disposal** journal entry (after recording Year 6 depreciation on the improvement). Show the book value computation.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Error 1 — capital improvement expensed (subsequent measurement schedule + PPA)**

Annual correct depreciation = \(54{,}000 / 6 = \mathbf{\$9{,}000}\).

| Year | As-recorded expense | Correct expense | Effect of error on pretax income (that year) |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | Maintenance expense **$54,000** | Depreciation **$9,000** | Income **understated $45,000** |
| 4 | $0 | Depreciation **$9,000** | Income **overstated $9,000** (missing dep) |
| **Cumulative (Y3–Y4)** | **$54,000** | **$18,000** | Income / RE **understated $36,000** |

Proof: incorrect total expensed \(54{,}000\) − correct cumulative dep \(9{,}000 \times 2 = 18{,}000\) = **$36,000** understatement of RE (overstatement of prior expense).

*April 15, Year 5 — Prior period adjustment, Error 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 54,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 18,000 |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 36,000 |
| *Capitalize improvement; record 2 years of omitted depreciation against RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 54,000 = Cr 18,000 + 36,000 = 54,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Error 2 — prepaid insurance fully expensed**

Annual correct insurance expense = \(9{,}000 / 3 = \mathbf{\$3{,}000}\).

| Year | As-recorded insurance expense | Correct expense | Effect on pretax income |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | $9,000 | $3,000 | Income **understated $6,000** |
| 4 | $0 | $3,000 | Income **overstated $3,000** |
| **Cumulative** | **$9,000** | **$6,000** | RE **understated $3,000** |

Remaining coverage at April 15 / Jan 1 Year 5 = **1 year** → Prepaid Insurance = **$3,000**.

*April 15, Year 5 — Prior period adjustment, Error 2*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Prepaid Insurance | 3,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 3,000 |
| *Recognize remaining prepaid; restore RE for excess prior expense* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,000 = Cr 3,000. **Balanced.**  
**Prepaid balance after entry:** **$3,000**.

**c. Error 3 — omitted Year 4 accrual**

Year 4 interest expense and interest payable were each **understated $7,400** → RE **overstated $7,400**.

*April 15, Year 5 — Prior period adjustment, Error 3*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 7,400 | |
| Interest Payable | | 7,400 |
| *Record omitted Year 4 accrual against beginning RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 7,400 = Cr 7,400. **Balanced.**

**d. Error 4 — patent economic life vs legal life (amortization schedule)**

| Measure | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Incorrect annual amort. | \(180{,}000 / 15\) | **$12,000** |
| Correct annual amort. | \(180{,}000 / 6\) | **$30,000** |
| Years elapsed (Y2–Y4) | 3 years | |
| Incorrect cumulative amort. | \(12{,}000 \times 3\) | **$36,000** |
| Correct cumulative amort. | \(30{,}000 \times 3\) | **$90,000** |
| **Prior period adjustment (debit RE)** | \(90{,}000 - 36{,}000\) | **$54,000** |

| Year | As-recorded amort. | Correct amort. | Effect on pretax income |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | 12,000 | 30,000 | Income **overstated $18,000** |
| 3 | 12,000 | 30,000 | Income **overstated $18,000** |
| 4 | 12,000 | 30,000 | Income **overstated $18,000** |
| **Cumulative** | **36,000** | **90,000** | RE **overstated $54,000** |

*April 15, Year 5 — Prior period adjustment, Error 4*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 54,000 | |
| Patent | | 54,000 |
| *Write down patent for additional 3 years of omitted amortization* | | |

**Check:** Dr 54,000 = Cr 54,000. **Balanced.**  
**Patent carrying amount after entry:** \(180{,}000 - 90{,}000 = \mathbf{\$90{,}000}\) (3 years of economic life remaining).

**e. December 31, Year 5 — period-end adjusting entries (after correction)**

*Depreciation on the improvement (remaining life 4 years; \(54{,}000 / 6 = 9{,}000\) continues)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 9,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 9,000 |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.** Ending AD on improvement = \(18{,}000 + 9{,}000 = \mathbf{\$27{,}000}\).

*Patent amortization (remaining book \(90{,}000 / 3\) years = \(30{,}000\))*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense—Patent | 30,000 | |
| Patent | | 30,000 |

**Check:** Dr 30,000 = Cr 30,000. **Balanced.**

*Insurance expense for Year 5 (consume remaining prepaid)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Insurance Expense | 3,000 | |
| Prepaid Insurance | | 3,000 |

**Check:** Dr 3,000 = Cr 3,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Disposal of the improvement component — December 31, Year 6**

Years of depreciation on improvement through Year 6: Y3–Y6 = **4 years** × \(9{,}000\) = **$36,000** AD.  
Book value = \(54{,}000 - 36{,}000 = \mathbf{\$18{,}000}\).  
Cash proceeds **$15,500** → **Loss on disposal $2,500**.

*(Year 6 depreciation \(9{,}000\) is assumed already recorded before the disposal entry.)*

*December 31, Year 6 — Dispose of corrected improvement*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 15,500 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 2,500 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 36,000 | |
| Equipment | | 54,000 |
| *Remove cost and corrected AD; recognize loss on cash sale* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(15{,}500 + 2{,}500 + 36{,}000 = 54{,}000\) = Cr 54,000. **Balanced.**

**Presentation reminder:** Each material error also requires **restatement** of prior-period financial statements (and disclosure of nature and line-item effects). The PPA appears as an adjustment to **beginning retained earnings** in the discovery year (not as current-period “correction expense”).

**Key insight:** The **subsequent measurement schedule** (as-recorded vs correct, multi-year) is what **sizes** the prior period adjustment. After the PPA restores assets/liabilities, the company resumes **correct period-end measurement** and, on disposal, uses the **corrected** cost and AD.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Omitted equipment (charged to land) and improper repair capitalization: schedules, PPA, period-end dep, disposal
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — multi-year subsequent measurement schedules for (1) equipment charged to land and (2) ordinary repairs capitalized; prior period adjustments; discovery-year depreciation; disposal of corrected equipment  
**Scenario:**  
**Northwind Analytics LLC** (calendar-year) discovers the following **material** errors on **January 1, Year 4**, after Year 3 financial statements have been issued. Ignore income taxes.

| # | Facts |
|---|---|
| **1** | On **January 1, Year 1**, equipment costing **$120,000** was purchased for cash but was **incorrectly debited entirely to Land**. Correct accounting: Equipment cost **$120,000**, estimated residual **$12,000**, useful life **9 years**, straight-line. No depreciation has ever been recorded on this equipment. |
| **2** | On **January 1, Year 2**, **ordinary repairs** of **$24,000** cash were **incorrectly capitalized** to Equipment and have been depreciated straight-line over **8 years** with **zero residual** (\(24{,}000 / 8 = \$3{,}000\) per year). Depreciation was taken for Years 2 and 3. |

**Required:**  
a. For **Error 1**, prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–3 (as-recorded vs correct depreciation and cumulative RE effect). Record the **January 1, Year 4 prior period adjustment**.  
b. For **Error 2**, prepare a **subsequent measurement / error-impact schedule** for Years 2–3 (repair expense, depreciation, net RE effect). Record the **January 1, Year 4 prior period adjustment**.  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 4 period-end** depreciation entry for the **correctly measured** equipment from Error 1 only.  
d. On **December 31, Year 5**, after recording Year 5 depreciation, Northwind **sells** the equipment from Error 1 for **$55,000 cash**. Prepare the **disposal** entry and prove book value.  
e. Briefly state how comparative Year 2–Year 3 statements are treated (restatement vs prospective).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Error 1 — equipment charged to land**

Correct annual depreciation = \((120{,}000 - 12{,}000) / 9 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\).

| Year | As-recorded dep. | Correct dep. | Effect on pretax income |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | $0 | $12,000 | Income **overstated $12,000** |
| 2 | $0 | $12,000 | Income **overstated $12,000** |
| 3 | $0 | $12,000 | Income **overstated $12,000** |
| **Cumulative** | **$0** | **$36,000** | RE **overstated $36,000** (expense understated) |

Balance-sheet misstatement immediately before correction (Jan 1, Year 4):  
Land **overstated $120,000**; Equipment **understated $120,000**; AD **understated $36,000**; RE **overstated $36,000**.

*January 1, Year 4 — Prior period adjustment, Error 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 120,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 36,000 | |
| Land | | 120,000 |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 36,000 |
| *Reclassify cost from Land to Equipment; record 3 years of omitted depreciation against RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(120{,}000 + 36{,}000 = 156{,}000\) = Cr \(120{,}000 + 36{,}000\). **Balanced.**  
Net book value after entry = \(120{,}000 - 36{,}000 = \mathbf{\$84{,}000}\) (6 years remaining × \(12{,}000\)).

**b. Error 2 — ordinary repairs capitalized**

| Item | As recorded (Y2–Y3 total) | Correct | Net effect of error |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Repair / maintenance expense | $0 | $24,000 (all in Year 2) | Expense understated **$24,000** |
| Depreciation expense (Y2–Y3) | \(3{,}000 \times 2 = \$6{,}000\) | $0 | Expense overstated **$6,000** |
| **Net pretax / RE** | — | — | RE **overstated $18,000** |

*January 1, Year 4 — Prior period adjustment, Error 2*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 18,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 6,000 | |
| Equipment | | 24,000 |
| *Remove improperly capitalized repairs and related AD; reduce beginning RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(18{,}000 + 6{,}000 = 24{,}000\) = Cr 24,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 4 (Error 1 equipment only)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 12,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 12,000 |
| *Year 4 SL depreciation on corrected equipment* | | |

**Check:** Dr 12,000 = Cr 12,000. **Balanced.**  
AD at Dec 31, Year 4 = \(36{,}000 + 12{,}000 = \mathbf{\$48{,}000}\); BV = \(72{,}000\).

**d. Disposal — December 31, Year 5**

Depreciation Years 1–5 = \(12{,}000 \times 5 = \mathbf{\$60{,}000}\) AD.  
Book value = \(120{,}000 - 60{,}000 = \mathbf{\$60{,}000}\).  
Proceeds **$55,000** → **Loss $5,000**.

*December 31, Year 5 — Sale of corrected equipment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 55,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 5,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 60,000 | |
| Equipment | | 120,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(55{,}000 + 5{,}000 + 60{,}000 = 120{,}000\) = Cr 120,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Comparative reporting**  
Because the errors are **material** and were discovered after the statements were issued, Northwind records the **prior period adjustments** and **restates** all prior periods presented (Years 1–3 comparative amounts as applicable). This is **not** prospective-only treatment.

**Key insight:** Charging equipment to land is a **noncounterbalancing** recognition error: assets and RE stay wrong until a PPA is booked. The multi-year **subsequent measurement schedule** of omitted depreciation is the engine that measures the RE debit.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Counterbalancing inventory error: multi-year subsequent measurement schedule and discovery timing (including settlement after self-correction)
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for a counterbalancing ending-inventory understatement; same-year correcting JE; PPA before self-correction; no JE after self-correction (settlement) with continued restatement duty  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Grocery Distributors** adjusts perpetual inventory to a physical count each **December 31**. It later determines that the **December 31, Year 1** physical count **understated ending inventory by $14,000**. Purchases were correctly recorded in both years, and the **December 31, Year 2 ending inventory is correct**. Ignore income taxes.

Amounts **as recorded with the error** (before any correction):

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | $40,000 | $50,000 (understated) |
| Net purchases | 200,000 | 220,000 |
| Ending inventory | 50,000 (understated) | 70,000 (correct) |
| Net sales | 280,000 | 320,000 |
| Other expenses | 40,000 | 45,000 |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare a **subsequent measurement / impact schedule** for Years 1–2 showing as-recorded, correct, and error effect for ending inventory, COGS, pretax income, and **cumulative pretax income**.  
b. State the Inventory and Retained Earnings misstatement (direction and amount) at **December 31, Year 1**, **December 31, Year 2**, and **January 1, Year 3** (books uncorrected).  
c. Record the correcting entry if the error is discovered **in Year 1** before statements are issued.  
d. Record the entry if discovered **January 1, Year 2** (before self-correction).  
e. State the Year 3 journal entry if discovered **January 1, Year 3** (after self-correction) and explain restatement (**settlement** of the counterbalancing error).  
f. **Classification contrast:** A separate Year 1 error misclassified **work-in-process as finished goods** (same total inventory). The error is discovered in Year 2 and both accounts still exist. Does this require a Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment? Explain.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Subsequent measurement schedule**

Correct Year 1 ending inventory = \(50{,}000 + 14{,}000 = \mathbf{\$64{,}000}\).  
Correct Year 2 beginning inventory = **$64,000**.

| Item | Y1 as recorded | Y1 correct | Y1 effect | Y2 as recorded | Y2 correct | Y2 effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| Ending inventory | 50,000 | 64,000 | **Under $14,000** | 70,000 | 70,000 | **None** |
| COGS | \(40{,}000+200{,}000-50{,}000=\mathbf{190{,}000}\) | \(40{,}000+200{,}000-64{,}000=\mathbf{176{,}000}\) | **Over $14,000** | \(50{,}000+220{,}000-70{,}000=\mathbf{200{,}000}\) | \(64{,}000+220{,}000-70{,}000=\mathbf{214{,}000}\) | **Under $14,000** |
| Pretax income | \(280{,}000-190{,}000-40{,}000=\mathbf{50{,}000}\) | \(280{,}000-176{,}000-40{,}000=\mathbf{64{,}000}\) | **Under $14,000** | \(320{,}000-200{,}000-45{,}000=\mathbf{75{,}000}\) | \(320{,}000-214{,}000-45{,}000=\mathbf{61{,}000}\) | **Over $14,000** |
| Cumulative pretax (from Y1) | 50,000 | 64,000 | **Under $14,000** | \(50{,}000+75{,}000=\mathbf{125{,}000}\) | \(64{,}000+61{,}000=\mathbf{125{,}000}\) | **None ($0)** |

**Math check:** Two-year pretax totals both **$125,000** → classic **counterbalancing** error.

**b. Balance-sheet subsequent measurement (uncorrected books)**

| Date | Inventory vs correct | RE vs correct |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 31, Year 1 | **Under $14,000** | **Under $14,000** |
| Dec 31, Year 2 | **Correct** | **Correct** (Y2 income overstatement offsets Y1 understatement) |
| Jan 1, Year 3 | **Correct** | **Correct** — error has **self-corrected** |

**c. Discovery in Year 1 (same-period correction)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 14,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 14,000 |
| *Correct understated Year 1 physical inventory* | | |

**Check:** Dr 14,000 = Cr 14,000. **Balanced.** No prior-period restatement (error fixed in year of occurrence).

**d. Discovery January 1, Year 2 — prior period adjustment (before self-correction)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 14,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 14,000 |
| *Restore inventory and beginning RE for Year 1 understatement* | | |

**Check:** Dr 14,000 = Cr 14,000. **Balanced.** **Restate** Year 1 comparative statements.

**e. Discovery January 1, Year 3 — settlement after self-correction**

**Journal entry:** **None.** Inventory and RE are already correct in total.  
**Reporting:** Still **restate** any comparative periods presented so year-by-year COGS and income are correct for users. The counterbalancing error has **settled** through operations; books no longer need a PPA.

**f. BS-only misclassification (WIP vs FG)**  
**No** Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment. Total inventory and income are correct; record a **balance-sheet reclassification** only (e.g., Dr Finished Goods / Cr WIP, or reverse depending on which was overstated). Income-statement-only misclassifications that have already closed to RE similarly often need **no** correcting entry.

**Key insight:** For counterbalancing inventory errors, the **subsequent measurement schedule** shows a temporary RE/Inventory misstatement that reverses in the next year. **Timing of discovery** determines whether the books need a COGS fix, a RE—PPA, or **no entry** after settlement—while material restatement of presented periods can still apply.

### Q4 — MC — Accounting treatment for a material error discovered after issuance
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Classification of error correction (prior period adjustment + restatement) vs prospective estimate change or current-period catch-up  
**Question:**  
In Year 5, **Solstice Media Group** discovers that in Year 3 it **materially** understated warranty expense and the related warranty liability because it applied an unrealistic estimate with gross negligence (an accounting **error**, not a good-faith change in estimate). Year 3 and Year 4 financial statements have already been issued. Ignoring taxes, which treatment is correct under ASC 250?

- A) Account for the cumulative effect prospectively as a change in accounting estimate affecting Year 5 only  
- B) Report a cumulative-effect adjustment in Year 5 net income (current-period “catch-up”) and do not restate prior periods  
- C) Record a prior period adjustment to beginning retained earnings (and the warranty liability), and restate prior-period financial statements  
- D) Disclose only; no journal entry is required because the liability will reverse when claims are paid  

**Answer:** **C.**  
A material error in previously issued statements is corrected by a **prior period adjustment** to retained earnings (and affected BS accounts) with **restatement** of the prior-period financial statements. Option A confuses this with a **change in estimate**. Option B is not the ASC 250 treatment for material prior-period errors. Option D ignores the need to correct unreliable prior statements and beginning RE.

### Q5 — MC — Counterbalancing inventory error after self-correction
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** When a counterbalancing error requires no correcting journal entry after self-correction  
**Question:**  
**Pinecroft Retail Co.** overstated December 31, Year 1 ending inventory by a material amount. Purchases were correct, and December 31, Year 2 ending inventory is correct. The error is first discovered on **January 10, Year 3**. Which statement is correct?

- A) Debit Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment and credit Inventory for the full Year 1 overstatement  
- B) No correcting journal entry is required in Year 3 because the error has self-corrected; comparative statements presented should still be restated  
- C) Debit Cost of Goods Sold and credit Inventory in Year 3 for the Year 1 amount  
- D) Treat the item as a change in accounting principle applied retrospectively through a voluntary FIFO/LIFO switch  

**Answer:** **B.**  
A counterbalancing EI error reverses through the next year’s beginning inventory/COGS. After self-correction, **Inventory and RE are already correct**, so **no PPA JE** is recorded. Material comparative statements are still **restated** for period-specific accuracy. Option A would double-correct. Option C misstates current COGS. Option D confuses an **error** with a voluntary change in principle.
