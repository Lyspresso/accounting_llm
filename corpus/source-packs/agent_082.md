# Agent 82 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-6

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Account for errors in reporting property, plant, and equipment  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE (wrong vs correct):** ordinary repairs must be expensed, not capitalized to Equipment
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** depreciation taken on improperly capitalized costs overstates AD and understates net expense effect
- **Prior period adjustment JE:** after FS release, correct BS accounts (Equipment / Accumulated Depreciation) against **Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment**
- **Same-period correction:** reverse the error and record the correct entry if discovered before statements are released
- **Depreciation system error (wrong service life):** excess (or deficient) accumulated depreciation reversed through RE—PPA; prospective depreciation uses correct remaining life
- **Period-end adjusting JE after correction:** resume correct straight-line depreciation on the corrected carrying amount
- **Disposal JE after error correction:** remove cost and corrected AD; gain/loss = cash vs book value
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** material errors → restate prior periods; PPA adjusts beginning RE (not current-period expense)

### Q1 — CORE — Improper capitalization of ordinary repairs (emphasis: initial recognition)
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Incorrect initial recognition of ordinary repairs as Equipment; Year-1 depreciation on the error; prior-period adjustment JE; classification of restatement vs current expense  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverton Fabrication LLC**, a calendar-year company, paid **$18,000** cash on **January 1, Year 1** for **ordinary repairs** on production equipment. The bookkeeper **incorrectly debited Equipment** for the full amount. Riverton depreciates the misposted cost at **10% per year** (straight-line; no residual on this amount). The error is discovered on **January 1, Year 2**, **after** Year 1 financial statements have been released. The error is **material**. **Ignore income taxes.**

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **incorrect initial recognition** journal entry recorded on January 1, Year 1, and the **correct** entry that should have been recorded.  
b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** depreciation entry that was recorded on the improperly capitalized $18,000.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent measurement / error-impact schedule** for Year 1 comparing (i) repair expense, (ii) depreciation expense, and (iii) net effect on pretax income and retained earnings (as recorded vs correct).  
d. Compute the amounts needed for the correction and prepare the **January 1, Year 2 prior period adjustment** journal entry. Prove that the entry balances and restores correct asset balances for the $18,000.  
e. **Classification / presentation:** State how the correction is reported (current-period expense vs beginning RE) and whether prior-period financial statements are restated.  
f. **Alternate discovery timing:** Assume instead the error is discovered on **December 20, Year 1**, **before** Year 1 books are closed and **before** FS release, and that the Year-1 depreciation entry on the $18,000 has already been posted. Prepare the correcting entries in Year 1 (no PPA).

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition — wrong vs correct**

*January 1, Year 1 — Incorrect entry as recorded (initial recognition error)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 18,000 | |
| Cash | | 18,000 |
| *Ordinary repairs improperly capitalized* | | |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000.

*January 1, Year 1 — Correct entry that should have been recorded*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense (or Maintenance Expense) | 18,000 | |
| Cash | | 18,000 |
| *Ordinary repairs expensed as incurred* | | |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000.

**b. Subsequent measurement — depreciation taken on the error**

Depreciation recorded in Year 1 on the improper capitalization:  
\$18,000 × 10% = **\$1,800**

*December 31, Year 1 — Depreciation recorded on improperly capitalized repairs*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 1,800 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 1,800 |

**Check:** Dr 1,800 = Cr 1,800.

**c. Subsequent measurement / error-impact schedule (Year 1)**

| Item | As recorded | Correct | Effect of error on pretax income |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Repair / maintenance expense | $0 | $18,000 | Expense **understated** $18,000 → income **overstated** $18,000 |
| Depreciation expense | 1,800 | 0 | Expense **overstated** $1,800 → income **understated** $1,800 |
| **Net pretax income / RE effect** | — | — | **Overstated by $16,200** |

Balance-sheet carryforward at January 1, Year 2 (before correction):

| Account | Misstatement |
|---|---|
| Equipment | Overstated **$18,000** |
| Accumulated Depreciation | Overstated **$1,800** |
| Net assets | Overstated **$16,200** |
| Retained earnings | Overstated **$16,200** |

**d. Prior period adjustment (January 1, Year 2)**

Correct repair expense (Y1) = \$18,000  
Less: depreciation recorded (Y1) = 1,800  
**Understated expense / overstated RE** = **\$16,200**

*January 1, Year 2 — To correct ordinary repairs improperly capitalized (material; after FS release)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 1,800 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 16,200 | |
| Equipment | | 18,000 |
| *Remove cost and related AD; reduce beginning RE for net prior-period overstatement of income* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,800 + 16,200 = **18,000** = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**

After the entry: Equipment −18,000 and AD −1,800 remove the improper gross and contra; RE reduced by 16,200. Net assets and equity each fall by 16,200.

**e. Classification / presentation / disclosure**  
- The **$16,200** is **not** recorded as Year 2 repair or depreciation expense.  
- It is a **prior period adjustment to beginning Retained Earnings**.  
- **Prior-period financial statements are restated** as if the correct accounting (expense repairs; no related depreciation) had always been applied.  
- Comparative RE statement: beginning RE is adjusted for the cumulative effect of the error (net of tax in practice; taxes ignored here).

**f. Same-period discovery (before FS release)**  
Reverse the improper capitalization and reverse the related depreciation; then record the correct expense.

*To reverse improper capitalization*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense | 18,000 | |
| Equipment | | 18,000 |

*To reverse depreciation recorded on the error*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 1,800 | |
| Depreciation Expense | | 1,800 |

**Check:** each entry Dr = Cr. Net Year-1 expense after correction = \$18,000 repair expense (and \$0 of the spurious \$1,800 dep). No PPA; current-period accounts are used.

**Key insight:** Capitalizing ordinary repairs **overstates assets and income** in the year of the error, partially offset by **extra depreciation**. After statements are out, fix **Equipment, Accumulated Depreciation, and beginning RE**—never dump the cumulative misstatement into current-period expense.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — multi-year improper capitalization
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: repairs capitalized for two full years; multi-year depreciation schedule on the error; PPA removing cost, AD, and net RE overstatement  
**Scenario:**  
**Northwind Tooling Inc.** paid **$45,000** cash on **January 1, Year 1** for ordinary machine repairs and **incorrectly debited Equipment**. Depreciation on the misposted amount is **10% per year**. The error is not found until **January 1, Year 3**, after Year 1 and Year 2 financial statements have been issued. The error is material. **Ignore income taxes.**

**Required:**  
a. Show the **incorrect initial recognition** entry (January 1, Year 1) and the **correct** entry that should have been made.  
b. Prepare a **two-year subsequent measurement schedule** of depreciation recorded on the \$45,000 error (Year 1 and Year 2), with cumulative AD at December 31, Year 2.  
c. Compute the cumulative understatement of expense / overstatement of RE through December 31, Year 2.  
d. Prepare the **January 1, Year 3 prior period adjustment** journal entry.  
e. Briefly state the **presentation** of the correction (restatement; beginning RE).

**Answer key:**

**a. Initial recognition**

*Incorrect (as recorded):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 45,000 | |
| Cash | | 45,000 |

*Correct (should have been):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Repair Expense | 45,000 | |
| Cash | | 45,000 |

**Check:** both balance at 45,000.

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (depreciation on the error)**

Annual depreciation on improper capitalization = \$45,000 × 10% = **\$4,500**

| Year | Beginning “cost” of error | Depreciation recorded | Ending AD on error |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 45,000 | 4,500 | 4,500 |
| 2 | 45,000 | 4,500 | 9,000 |

Cumulative AD related to error at December 31, Year 2 = **\$9,000**

*Illustrative Year-1 and Year-2 period-end entries (each year):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 4,500 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 4,500 |

**c. Cumulative income / RE effect through Year 2**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Repair expense never recorded | $45,000 understated expense |
| Depreciation expense recorded in error (2 × \$4,500) | 9,000 overstated expense |
| **Net understatement of expense (= overstatement of RE)** | **$36,000** |

Net assets overstated by \$45,000 − \$9,000 = **\$36,000**.

**d. Prior period adjustment — January 1, Year 3**

*January 1, Year 3 — Correct multi-year improper capitalization of repairs*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 9,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 36,000 | |
| Equipment | | 45,000 |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 + 36,000 = **45,000** = Cr 45,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Presentation**  
Debit beginning **Retained Earnings** (PPA) for **\$36,000**; restate Years 1–2 statements so repairs are expensed in Year 1 and the related \$4,500 of depreciation is removed from each year. Year 3 income is **not** charged for the cumulative prior error.

**Key insight:** The longer the improper cost sits in Equipment, the larger the **AD offset**—but the **RE correction equals cost minus cumulative AD on the error**, not the full gross cost alone.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — wrong useful life, post-correction depreciation, disposal
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Depreciation system error (service life entered wrong); PPA to reduce overstated AD / restore RE; period-end adjusting JE after correction; disposal JE using corrected book value; classification of PPA vs change in estimate  
**Scenario:**  
**Helix Packaging Co.** purchased equipment on **January 1, Year 1**:

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cost | **$265,000** |
| Estimated residual value | **$15,000** |
| Correct useful life | **10 years** |
| Method | Straight-line |
| System entry error | Useful life entered as **5 years** instead of 10 |

The error is detected on **January 1, Year 4** (after three full years of depreciation under the wrong life). Financial statements for Years 1–3 have been issued. **Ignore income taxes.**

On **January 1, Year 5**, after recording Year-4 depreciation correctly, Helix sells the equipment for **$168,000** cash.

**Required:**  
a. Compute **incorrect** annual depreciation, **correct** annual depreciation, and the **cumulative depreciation error** at January 1, Year 4. Prepare a brief **comparison schedule** for Years 1–3.  
b. Prepare the **January 1, Year 4 prior period adjustment** journal entry.  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 4 period-end adjusting** entry for depreciation after the correction (state remaining life and depreciable base).  
d. Prepare the **January 1, Year 5 disposal** journal entry (sale for \$168,000). Prove the gain or loss.  
e. **Classification:** Explain why this is an **error (retrospective restatement / PPA)** and not a **change in estimate (prospective only)**.

**Answer key:**

**a. Depreciation comparison schedule**

Incorrect annual depreciation:  
\[
\frac{\$265{,}000 - \$15{,}000}{5} = \$50{,}000
\]

Correct annual depreciation:  
\[
\frac{\$265{,}000 - \$15{,}000}{10} = \$25{,}000
\]

Excess depreciation per year = \$50,000 − \$25,000 = **\$25,000**

| Year | Dep recorded (wrong life) | Correct dep | Excess dep |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 50,000 | 25,000 | 25,000 |
| 2 | 50,000 | 25,000 | 25,000 |
| 3 | 50,000 | 25,000 | 25,000 |
| **Cumulative at 1/1/Y4** | **150,000** | **75,000** | **75,000** |

At January 1, Year 4 **before** correction:  
- Accumulated Depreciation **overstated \$75,000**  
- Retained earnings **understated \$75,000** (three years of excess expense)  
- Carrying amount understated by \$75,000

**b. Prior period adjustment — January 1, Year 4**

*January 1, Year 4 — To correct depreciation error (wrong service life entered)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 75,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 75,000 |
| *Reduce overstated AD; restore beginning RE for prior over-depreciation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 75,000 = Cr 75,000. **Balanced.**

Correct AD immediately after entry = \$150,000 − \$75,000 = **\$75,000** (= 3 × \$25,000).  
Correct carrying amount = \$265,000 − \$75,000 = **\$190,000**.

**c. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 4**

Remaining useful life from January 1, Year 4 = 10 − 3 = **7 years**  
Depreciable base remaining = \$190,000 − \$15,000 = **\$175,000**  
Annual depreciation (Y4 onward) = \$175,000 / 7 = **\$25,000** (matches original correct annual amount)

*December 31, Year 4 — Depreciation after correction*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 25,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 25,000 |

**Check:** Dr 25,000 = Cr 25,000.

AD at December 31, Year 4 = \$75,000 + \$25,000 = **\$100,000**  
Book value = \$265,000 − \$100,000 = **\$165,000**

**d. Disposal — January 1, Year 5**

Cash proceeds \$168,000 − book value \$165,000 = **Gain \$3,000**

*January 1, Year 5 — Sale of equipment*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 168,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 100,000 | |
| Equipment | | 265,000 |
| Gain on Disposal of Equipment | | 3,000 |

**Check:** Dr 168,000 + 100,000 = **268,000**; Cr 265,000 + 3,000 = **268,000**. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification — error vs change in estimate**  
- Entering **5 years instead of the intended 10** is a **mistake in applying facts already known**—an **accounting error**, not a revision of estimate based on new information.  
- **Errors:** retrospective **restatement** of prior periods and a **PPA to beginning RE** (plus correction of AD).  
- **Changes in estimate** (e.g., revising remaining life because of new usage data): **prospective only**—no PPA, no restatement of prior depreciation.  
- After the error is fixed, **future** depreciation uses the correct remaining life (same arithmetic as a prospective estimate change, but only for periods after restatement).

**Key insight:** Wrong service life **over-depreciates** the asset → debit **AD** and **credit RE—PPA**. Disposal uses the **corrected** book value; skipping the PPA would misstate gain/loss on sale.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of error correction path
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Classification of same-period correction vs prior period adjustment / restatement for PPE reporting errors  
**Question:**  
In Year 1, a company incorrectly capitalized \$12,000 of ordinary repairs as Equipment and took \$1,200 of depreciation on that amount. Which statement is **correct**?

- A) If the error is found in Year 2 after Year 1 statements were issued, charge Repair Expense \$12,000 in Year 2 and ignore the prior depreciation.  
- B) If the error is found in Year 2 after Year 1 statements were issued, debit Retained Earnings—PPA for \$12,000 and credit Equipment for \$12,000 only (leave Accumulated Depreciation unchanged).  
- C) If the error is found in Year 2 after Year 1 statements were issued, debit Accumulated Depreciation \$1,200, debit Retained Earnings—PPA \$10,800, and credit Equipment \$12,000; restate prior-period statements.  
- D) Material PPE errors discovered after statement issuance are always treated prospectively like a change in depreciation estimate.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Net prior overstatement of income = \$12,000 − \$1,200 = **\$10,800**. Remove the gross asset and related AD against **beginning RE** (PPA) and **restate** prior periods. A dumps a prior error into current expense (incorrect after FS release). B omits the AD reversal and uses the wrong RE amount. D confuses **errors** (retrospective) with **changes in estimate** (prospective).

### Q5 — MC — Net retained earnings effect of two PPE errors
**LO:** LO 12-6  
**Concept:** Classification / measurement of net prior period adjustment when depreciation was both double-recorded and omitted  
**Question:**  
In Year 1, **Bayside Assembly Inc.** (1) recorded annual equipment depreciation of **\$52,000 twice**, and (2) failed to depreciate equipment purchased **November 1, Year 1** (cost **\$288,000**, 8-year life, zero residual, straight-line). Errors are discovered in Year 2 after Year 1 statements were issued. Ignoring taxes, the January 1, Year 2 prior period adjustment **increases or decreases retained earnings** by how much?

- A) \$58,000 increase  
- B) \$46,000 increase  
- C) \$46,000 decrease  
- D) \$58,000 decrease  

**Answer:** **B. \$46,000 increase.**  

Double-recorded depreciation: expense overstated \$52,000 → RE **understated** \$52,000 → **credit RE \$52,000** (and debit AD \$52,000).  

Omitted depreciation: Nov 1–Dec 31 = **2/12** year  
\[
\frac{\$288{,}000}{8} \times \frac{2}{12} = \$6{,}000
\]  
Expense understated \$6,000 → RE **overstated** \$6,000 → **debit RE \$6,000** (and credit AD \$6,000).  

**Net credit to Retained Earnings—PPA** = \$52,000 − \$6,000 = **\$46,000 increase**.
