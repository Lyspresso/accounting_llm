# Agent 298 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-4

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Prepare the financing activities section of the statement of cash flows  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- Financing section: **gross** cash inflows (debt proceeds, equity issuances) and outflows (debt principal, cash dividends, treasury stock, debt retirements, debt issue costs)
- **Initial recognition JE:** cash stock issuance and cash borrowing → financing inflows
- **Subsequent measurement schedules:** T-account / rollforward for bonds (premium/discount), mortgage, finance lease liability, dividends payable
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** year-end amortization of bond premium/discount and year-end interest accrual on bonds — **not** financing cash lines; also year-end dividend declaration that creates dividends payable
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JE:** early bond retirement cash paid = financing outflow; loss/gain → operating (indirect method)
- **Classification / presentation:** principal vs interest split (installment notes, finance leases); cash dividends **paid** (not merely declared); noncash investing/financing disclosures
- **Number-variant twin** with all facts and amounts changed

**Concept:** Prepare cash flows from financing activities — stock issuance JE; bond retirement settlement JE; mortgage/lease/dividends schedules; **period-end adjusting JE** to amortize remaining bond premium; financing section presentation

**Required:**

Reconstruct dividends **paid** via the dividends payable rollforward; report **gross** inflows and outflows.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Ashford Fabric Works: bond issue, period-end interest/premium accrual, financing section
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of bonds issued at premium; subsequent measurement (straight-line semi amort); **period-end adjusting JE** when year-end falls between interest dates; equity issue and dividends payable → prepare **financing activities** section and correctly exclude interest accruals from financing  
**Scenario:**  
**Ashford Fabric Works Inc.** (calendar-year) completes the following **20X4** financing-related events:

1. On **March 1, 20X4**, Ashford issues **$300,000** face of **6%** bonds for cash proceeds of **$309,000** (premium **$9,000**). Interest is paid **semiannually** on **March 1** and **September 1**. The bonds mature on **March 1, 20X9** (5-year term; **10** semiannual periods). For this problem, use **straight-line** amortization of the premium.  
2. On **October 1, 20X4**, Ashford issues **5,000** shares of **$10** par common stock for **$85,000** cash.  
3. On **December 15, 20X4**, the board declares a cash dividend of **$24,000**. Dividends payable was **$0** at January 1, 20X4. Ashford pays **$18,000** of the declared dividends on **December 28**; the remainder remains unpaid at year-end.  
4. No other nontrade debt principal was borrowed or repaid in 20X4. No treasury stock transactions.  
5. **(Emphasis)** December 31 falls between interest dates, so Ashford must record a **period-end adjusting JE** for interest and premium amortization for **October 1–December 31** (4 months of the next full semiannual period).

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **March 1 initial recognition JE** for the bond issue. Identify the 20X4 financing cash inflow.  
(b) Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** of premium amortization for the first full semiannual period ending **September 1, 20X4**, and show the **September 1** cash interest + amortization JE.  
(c) **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **December 31, 20X4 period-end adjusting JE** accruing interest and amortizing premium for 4 months (prorate the next full semiannual amounts by **4/6**).  
(d) Record the **October 1 stock issuance JE** and the **December 15 dividend declaration** and **December 28 dividend payment** JEs. Compute cash dividends paid for the financing section.  
(e) Prepare the **Cash flows from financing activities** section for 20X4. Explicitly list items that are **not** financing cash flows from the bond activity (interest paid, period-end accrual, premium amortization).  
(f) Briefly classify cash bond interest paid on September 1 under U.S. GAAP SCF presentation.

**Answer key:**

**Setup**  
- Semiannual cash interest: \(300{,}000 \times 6\% \times 1/2 = \mathbf{\$9{,}000}\)  
- Premium: **$9,000** over **10** semiannual periods → SL amort per full semi = \(9{,}000 / 10 = \mathbf{\$900}\)  
- Interest expense per full semi (SL) = cash interest − amort = \(9{,}000 − 900 = \mathbf{\$8{,}100}\)

**(a) March 1, 20X4 — Initial recognition (bond issue)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 309,000 | |
| Premium on bonds payable | | 9,000 |
| Bonds payable | | 300,000 |
| *Issue $300,000 6% bonds at premium* | | |

**Check:** Dr 309,000 = Cr 9,000 + 300,000. **Balanced.**  
→ **Financing inflow: Proceeds from issuance of bonds $309,000**.

**(b) Subsequent measurement — first semi period and September 1 JE**

| Date | Cash interest | Premium amort. | Interest expense | Unamortized premium | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1, 20X4 (issue) | — | — | — | 9,000 | 309,000 |
| Sep 1, 20X4 | 9,000 | 900 | 8,100 | 8,100 | 308,100 |

**September 1, 20X4 — cash interest + amortization**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest expense | 8,100 | |
| Premium on bonds payable | 900 | |
| Cash | | 9,000 |
| *Semiannual bond interest and SL premium amort* | | |

**Check:** Dr 8,100 + 900 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount after entry: \(309{,}000 − 900 = \mathbf{308{,}100}\).

**(c) Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, 20X4 (emphasis)**  
Next full semiannual period (Mar 1, 20X5): cash interest 9,000; premium amort 900; interest expense 8,100.  
Accrue **4 months / 6 months = 4/6**:

- Interest payable (cash interest portion): \(9{,}000 \times 4/6 = \mathbf{6{,}000}\)  
- Premium amort: \(900 \times 4/6 = \mathbf{600}\)  
- Interest expense: \(8{,}100 \times 4/6 = \mathbf{5{,}400}\)  
  (Check: \(6{,}000 − 600 = 5{,}400\))

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest expense | 5,400 | |
| Premium on bonds payable | 600 | |
| Interest payable | | 6,000 |
| *Accrue 4 months bond interest/amort (Sep 1–Dec 31)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,400 + 600 = Cr 6,000. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount after accrual: \(308{,}100 − 600 = \mathbf{307{,}500}\). Unamortized premium: \(8{,}100 − 600 = \mathbf{7{,}500}\).  
**None of these amounts is a financing cash flow** (no cash moved on Dec 31 for the bond).

**(d) Equity and dividends**

*October 1 stock issuance*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 85,000 | |
| Common stock, $10 par | | 50,000 |
| Additional paid-in capital | | 35,000 |
| *Issue 5,000 shares for cash $85,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr 85,000 = Cr 50,000 + 35,000. **Balanced.**  
→ Financing inflow **$85,000**.

*December 15 — dividend declaration*

|---|---:|---:|
| Retained earnings (or Dividends) | 24,000 | |
| Dividends payable | | 24,000 |
| *Declare cash dividend* | | |

**Check:** Dr 24,000 = Cr 24,000. **Balanced.**

*December 28 — cash dividend payment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Dividends payable | 18,000 | |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**  
Ending dividends payable = $24,000 − $18,000 = **$6,000**.  
→ **Cash dividends paid (financing) = $18,000** (or beg $0 + declared $24,000 − end $6,000).

**(e) Cash flows from financing activities — 20X4**

```
Cash flows from financing activities
  Proceeds from issuance of bonds ................. $ 309,000
  Proceeds from issuance of common stock ..........    85,000
  Cash dividends paid .............................   (18,000)
  Net cash provided by financing activities ....... $ 376,000
```

Cross-check: $309,000 + $85,000 − $18,000 = **$376,000**.

**Items from the bond story that are NOT financing cash flows in 20X4:**

| Item | Amount | Why excluded from financing |
|---|---:|---|
| Cash interest paid Sep 1 | $9,000 | **Operating** (U.S. GAAP) |
| Dec 31 interest accrual / Interest payable | $6,000 | Noncash period-end adj. (operating); cash will leave next period as interest (operating) |
| Premium amortization (Sep 1 $900 + Dec 31 $600) | $1,500 | Noncash reduction of interest expense (operating) |

**(f) Classification of cash bond interest**  
Under U.S. GAAP, **cash interest paid** on bonds is classified as an **operating** cash outflow (direct method) or is embedded in the operating section via net income and related accruals (indirect method). Only the **principal proceeds at issuance** (and later principal repayments/retirements) belong in **financing**.

**Key insight:** When year-end is **between interest dates**, the **period-end adjusting JE** correctly states interest expense and carrying amount, but it does **not** create a financing cash line. Financing reports **cash** from debt/equity issuances, **cash** principal repayments, and **cash** dividends paid — reconstructed from balance-sheet changes and cash facts.

### Q4 — MC — Classification for the financing section
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Classify cash flows — financing vs operating vs investing vs noncash  
**Question:**  
During the year, **Redcliff Instruments Corp.** entered into the following transactions. Which item is reported as a **cash outflow in the financing activities** section of the statement of cash flows?

- A) Interest paid of $18,000 on a long-term bank note  
- B) Principal payment of $52,000 on a finance lease liability  
- C) Purchase of equipment for $95,000 cash  
- D) Acquisition of a building by signing a $95,000 long-term note (no cash exchanged at acquisition)

**Answer:** **B**.  
Principal payments on finance leases (and other nontrade debt) are **financing** outflows. Interest paid (A) is **operating** under U.S. GAAP. Cash equipment purchase (C) is **investing**. Signing a note for a building (D) is a **noncash investing and financing** disclosure, not a financing cash flow on the face of the SCF.

### Q5 — MC — Dividends declared vs cash dividends paid; period-end payable
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Compute financing outflow for dividends using dividends payable (period-end unpaid declaration)  
**Question:**  
**Westpine Media Inc.** declared cash dividends of **$72,000** during the year. Dividends payable was **$9,000** at the beginning of the year and **$15,000** at year-end (after a year-end declaration that was not yet paid). What amount of cash dividends is reported as a financing outflow?

- A) $72,000  
- B) $78,000  
- C) $66,000  
- D) $81,000  

**Answer:** **C**.  
Cash dividends paid = dividends declared − increase in dividends payable  
\(= 72{,}000 - (15{,}000 - 9{,}000) = 72{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$66{,}000}\).  
(Alternatively: beg $9,000 + declared $72,000 − end $15,000 = $66,000.) Financing reports **cash paid**, not dividends declared. The period-end unpaid declaration increases dividends payable and **reduces** cash dividends paid relative to declared.
