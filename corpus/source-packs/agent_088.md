# Agent 88 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-3

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for impairment and derecognition of finite life intangible assets  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize finite-life intangible (patent/customer list) at cash-equivalent cost
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** straight-line amortization over useful life (limited to legal life); residual typically zero
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** year-end amortization; two-step impairment (recoverability → measurement) when an indicator is present
- **Impairment measurement:** if undiscounted future net cash flows < carrying amount **and** carrying amount > fair value, loss = CV − FV; new CV is new cost basis (no subsequent recovery)
- **Disposal / derecognition JE:** amortize to date of disposal; remove carrying amount; gain/loss = proceeds (FV of consideration) − adjusted CV
- **Classification / presentation:** impairment loss and disposal gain/loss in income from continuing operations; gross carrying amount and accumulated amortization (or direct write-down) disclosed by major class

### Q1 — CORE — Patent life cycle: initial recognition → amortization → impairment (emphasis: initial recognition JE)
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of finite-life patent at cost; multi-year amortization schedule; recoverability and impairment tests; period-end impairment JE; post-impairment amortization  
**Scenario:**  
**HarborTech Labs**, a calendar-year company, acquires exclusive rights to a manufacturing process patent.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Purchase date | **January 1, Year 1** |
| Cash purchase price (includes transfer/legal fees to ready for use) | **$84,000** |
| Estimated useful life (also remaining legal life) | **8 years**, straight-line, **zero** residual |
| Year-end | December 31 |
| Amortization policy | Credit **Patent** directly (no separate Accumulated Amortization account) |
| **Impairment indicator (Year 3):** competitor releases a superior substitute product | Present before year-end Year 3 |
| Estimated **undiscounted** future net cash inflows from the patent (Dec 31, Year 3) | **$42,000** |
| **Fair value** of the patent (PV of expected cash flows) at Dec 31, Year 3 | **$28,000** |
| Remaining useful life after impairment | **5 years** (through Dec 31, Year 8) |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry for the patent purchase.  
b. Compute annual amortization. Prepare an **amortization / carrying-value schedule** for Years 1–3 (before any impairment). Record the **December 31, Year 1** amortization adjusting entry.  
c. On **December 31, Year 3**, after recording Year-3 amortization, perform the **recoverability test** and, if required, the **impairment test**. Record any impairment journal entry.  
d. Compute **Year-4** amortization on the new basis and record the December 31, Year 4 adjusting entry.  
e. State the **carrying amount** of the patent reported on the December 31, Year 3 and December 31, Year 4 balance sheets. Briefly note how impairment is classified on the income statement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition (emphasis)**

*January 1, Year 1 — Purchase patent for cash*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 84,000 | |
| Cash | | 84,000 |
| *Finite-life intangible recorded at cash-equivalent acquisition cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 84,000 = Cr 84,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Amortization and schedule (Years 1–3, pre-impairment)**

Annual amortization = \$84,000 / 8 = **\$10,500**

*December 31, Year 1 — Period-end amortization*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 10,500 | |
| Patent | | 10,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,500. **Balanced.**

**Carrying-value schedule (straight-line, pre-impairment)**

| Year | Beginning CV | Amortization | Ending CV (pre-impairment) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 84,000 | 10,500 | 73,500 |
| 2 | 73,500 | 10,500 | 63,000 |
| 3 | 63,000 | 10,500 | **52,500** |

**Roll-forward check:** \$84,000 − (\$10,500 × 3) = **\$52,500**.

(Year-2 and Year-3 amortization entries are identical in form and amount to Year 1.)

**c. Impairment analysis and JE — December 31, Year 3**

Carrying amount after Year-3 amortization = **\$52,500**

**Step 1 — Recoverability test** (indicator present: competitive product)  
Compare **undiscounted** future net cash inflows to carrying amount:  
\$42,000 **<** \$52,500 → asset is **not recoverable** → proceed to impairment measurement.

**Step 2 — Impairment test (measurement)**  
Fair value = **\$28,000**  
Impairment loss = Carrying amount − Fair value = \$52,500 − \$28,000 = **\$24,500**

*December 31, Year 3 — Record impairment loss on patent*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Impairment Loss | 24,500 | |
| Patent | | 24,500 |

**Check:** Dr 24,500 = Cr 24,500. **Balanced.**

New cost basis (new accounting basis) of patent = **\$28,000**.  
Subsequent **reversal of impairment is prohibited**.

**d. Post-impairment amortization — Year 4**

New annual amortization = \$28,000 / 5 remaining years = **\$5,600**

*December 31, Year 4 — Amortization on new basis*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 5,600 | |
| Patent | | 5,600 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 5,600. **Balanced.**

**e. Balance sheet carrying amounts and classification**  
- **December 31, Year 3** (after impairment): Patent **\$28,000**  
- **December 31, Year 4**: Patent \$28,000 − \$5,600 = **\$22,400**  
- **Income statement:** Impairment Loss **\$24,500** is reported as a component of **income from continuing operations** (not OCI; not a prior-period restatement).

**Key insight:** Finite-life intangibles are **capitalized at cost** at initial recognition, **amortized systematically**, then tested for impairment only when an **indicator** is present—first with the **recoverability test** (undiscounted cash flows), then measurement at **fair value**. The write-down becomes the new basis for future amortization.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Patent twin (purchase → amort → impair → remeasure)
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: initial recognition; amortization schedule; recoverability + impairment; post-impairment amortization; no-impairment contrast when recoverability passes  
**Scenario:**  
**Northfield Pharma Inc.** (calendar year) purchases a drug-delivery patent.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Purchase date | **January 1, Year 1** |
| Cash cost | **$90,000** |
| Useful life | **6 years**, SL, **zero** residual |
| Credit amortization and impairment | Directly to **Patent** |
| Indicator at Dec 31, Year 3 | Adverse change in regulatory environment for related products |
| Undiscounted future net cash inflows (Dec 31, Year 3) | **$40,000** |
| Fair value (Dec 31, Year 3) | **$27,000** |
| Remaining life after impairment | **3 years** |

**Additional (part e):** Northfield also holds **Patent B** with carrying amount **\$15,000**, estimated undiscounted cash flows **\$18,000**, and fair value **\$12,000**. An indicator is present for Patent B as well.

**Required:**  
a. Record initial purchase of the \$90,000 patent on January 1, Year 1.  
b. Prepare amortization / CV schedule for Years 1–3; record Year-1 amortization.  
c. At Dec 31, Year 3 (after Year-3 amortization), complete recoverability and impairment tests; record impairment if any.  
d. Compute and record Year-4 amortization on the new basis.  
e. For **Patent B only**, determine whether an impairment loss is recognized and explain (no entry if none).  
f. Compute total amortization expense on the \$90,000 patent over its **entire remaining life after impairment** (Years 4–6).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 90,000 |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Amortization schedule (pre-impairment)**

Annual amortization = \$90,000 / 6 = **\$15,000**

*December 31, Year 1*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 15,000 | |
| Patent | | 15,000 |

**Check:** 15,000 = 15,000. **Balanced.**

| Year | Beginning CV | Amortization | Ending CV |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 90,000 | 15,000 | 75,000 |
| 2 | 75,000 | 15,000 | 60,000 |
| 3 | 60,000 | 15,000 | **45,000** |

**Check:** \$90,000 − \$45,000 = **\$45,000** CV at Dec 31, Year 3 before impairment.

**c. Impairment — December 31, Year 3**

Recoverability: \$40,000 **<** \$45,000 → **fail** → measure impairment.  
Impairment loss = \$45,000 − \$27,000 = **\$18,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Impairment Loss | 18,000 | |
| Patent | | 18,000 |

**Check:** 18,000 = 18,000. **Balanced.**  
New basis = **\$27,000**.

**d. Year-4 amortization**

\$27,000 / 3 = **\$9,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 9,000 | |
| Patent | | 9,000 |

**Check:** 9,000 = 9,000. **Balanced.**  
Dec 31, Year 4 CV = \$27,000 − \$9,000 = **\$18,000**.

**e. Patent B — recoverability passes**

Undiscounted cash flows \$18,000 **>** carrying amount \$15,000 → **recoverable**.  
**No impairment test** is performed and **no impairment loss** is recognized, even though fair value (\$12,000) is below carrying amount.  
**No journal entry.**

**f. Total post-impairment amortization (Years 4–6)**  
\$9,000 × 3 = **\$27,000** (fully amortizes the new basis to zero).

**Key insight:** Changing only the numbers, the **same two-step model** applies. If recoverability **passes**, fair value is **ignored** for measurement—no write-down.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Derecognition (disposal) + period-end dual-asset impairment
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Amortize finite-life intangible to disposal date; derecognition JE with gain/loss; installment-note sale (PV of consideration); dual-asset year-end impairment classification  
**Scenario:**  
**Silverline Design Co.** (calendar year) holds a purchased **customer list** (finite life) and two patents evaluated at year-end.

**Part A — Customer list disposal**

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Customer list purchased | **January 1, Year 1** for **\$48,000** cash |
| Useful life | **6 years**, SL, zero residual |
| Annual amortization | \$48,000 / 6 = **\$8,000** (credit Customer List) |
| Disposal date | **April 1, Year 4** |
| Cash proceeds | **\$25,000** |

**Part B — Installment sale of a second intangible (franchise right)**  
On **January 1 of the current year**, Silverline sells a franchise right with **carrying amount \$18,000**. Terms: four annual payments of **\$5,500** due each December 31; implicit rate **8%** (no stated interest). No cash down payment.

**Part C — Year-end impairment of two patents** (indicator present for both)

| Asset | Carrying amount | Undiscounted future net CF | Fair value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Patent X | \$48,000 | \$35,000 | \$22,000 |
| Patent Y | \$18,000 | \$20,000 | \$15,000 |

**Required:**  
a. Compute the customer list carrying amount on **December 31, Year 3**. Record amortization for **January 1–March 31, Year 4**, then record the **April 1, Year 4 cash sale**.  
b. Compute the present value of the franchise note and record the **January 1 installment sale** (including discount on note receivable and gain or loss).  
c. Record **any** impairment entries for Patents X and Y at year-end. Explain any asset with **no** entry.  
d. State where the **disposal gain/loss** and **impairment loss** appear in the financial statements.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Amortize to date and cash derecognition**

Through Dec 31, Year 3: 3 full years × \$8,000 = \$24,000 amortized.  
CV Dec 31, Year 3 = \$48,000 − \$24,000 = **\$24,000**.

Amortization for 3/12 of Year 4: (3/12) × \$8,000 = **\$2,000**.

*March 31 / April 1, Year 4 — Update amortization to disposal date*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 2,000 | |
| Customer List | | 2,000 |

**Check:** 2,000 = 2,000. **Balanced.**

Adjusted CV at sale = \$24,000 − \$2,000 = **\$22,000**.  
Gain on sale = \$25,000 − \$22,000 = **\$3,000**.

*April 1, Year 4 — Derecognize customer list on cash sale*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 25,000 | |
| Customer List | | 22,000 |
| Gain on Sale of Intangible Asset | | 3,000 |

**Check:** Dr 25,000 = Cr 22,000 + 3,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Installment-note derecognition**

Face of note receivable = 4 × \$5,500 = **\$22,000**.  
Present value of ordinary annuity of \$5,500 for 4 periods at 8%:

\[
PV = \$5{,}500 \times \frac{1 - (1.08)^{-4}}{0.08} = \$5{,}500 \times 3.312127 = \$18{,}216.70 \approx \$18{,}217
\]

(Excel-style: `PV(0.08,4,-5500)` → **\$18,217** rounded.)

Discount on note = \$22,000 − \$18,217 = **\$3,783**.  
Gain on sale = PV of consideration − CV = \$18,217 − \$18,000 = **\$217**.

*January 1 — Sale of franchise right for installment note*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Receivable | 22,000 | |
| Franchise Right | | 18,000 |
| Discount on Notes Receivable | | 3,783 |
| Gain on Sale of Intangible Asset | | 217 |

**Check:** Dr 22,000 = Cr 18,000 + 3,783 + 217. **Balanced.**

**c. Dual-asset impairment at year-end**

**Patent X**  
Recoverability: \$35,000 < \$48,000 → fail.  
Impairment loss = \$48,000 − \$22,000 = **\$26,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Impairment Loss | 26,000 | |
| Patent X | | 26,000 |

**Check:** 26,000 = 26,000. **Balanced.**

**Patent Y**  
Recoverability: \$20,000 > \$18,000 → **passes**.  
Even though FV \$15,000 < CV \$18,000, **no impairment** is recognized.  
**No journal entry.**

**d. Presentation / classification**  
- **Impairment Loss** (Patent X \$26,000) and **Gain on Sale of Intangible Asset** (cash sale \$3,000; note sale \$217) are both components of **income from continuing operations**.  
- On the **balance sheet**, remaining finite-life intangibles are reported at **post-impairment carrying amount** (new basis); notes receivable are reported **net of discount** (effective interest method subsequently).  
- Disclosures typically include gross carrying amount and accumulated amortization (or amortization to date) **by major intangible class**, aggregate amortization expense, and impairment losses recognized.

**Key insight:** Before disposal, **always amortize to the sale date**. Derecognition compares **FV of proceeds** (cash or PV of a noninterest note) to **updated CV**. Impairment uses a **two-step** model—recoverability can spare an asset even when FV is below CV.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Sequence of finite-life intangible impairment testing; measurement basis of the loss  

**Question:**  
On December 31, **Ridgeway Co.** has a finite-life patent with carrying amount **\$60,000**. An adverse change in the legal environment is an impairment indicator. Estimated **undiscounted** future net cash inflows are **\$52,000**; estimated **fair value** is **\$40,000**. Which statement is **correct**?

- A) No impairment is recognized because fair value is used only for indefinite-life intangibles.  
- B) An impairment loss of **\$8,000** is recognized (\$60,000 − \$52,000) because recoverability is measured using undiscounted cash flows.  
- C) An impairment loss of **\$20,000** is recognized (\$60,000 − \$40,000) after the recoverability test fails (\$52,000 < \$60,000).  
- D) An impairment loss of **\$12,000** is recognized (\$52,000 − \$40,000) because measurement uses only the difference between undiscounted cash flows and fair value.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Indicator → **recoverability test** first: \$52,000 < \$60,000 means the carrying amount is not recoverable. **Measurement** of the loss is then carrying amount minus **fair value**: \$60,000 − \$40,000 = **\$20,000**. The loss is **not** limited to the recoverability “gap” of \$8,000, and undiscounted cash flows are **not** the measurement attribute. Subsequent reversal is prohibited; \$40,000 becomes the new basis.

### Q5 — MC (presentation)
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Income-statement classification of finite-life intangible impairment and disposal results  

**Question:**  
Which presentation is appropriate under U.S. GAAP for a **finite-life** intangible asset?

- A) Impairment losses are recorded in OCI; gains on sale of patents are recorded in continuing operations.  
- B) Both impairment losses and gains/losses on disposal are reported as components of **income from continuing operations** (unless the asset is part of a discontinued operation).  
- C) Impairment losses may be reversed through earnings in a later period if fair value recovers.  
- D) Disposal of a fully amortized patent with zero carrying amount never requires a journal entry even if cash is received.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Finite-life intangible **impairment losses** and **gains/losses on disposal** are recognized in **income from continuing operations** (same guidance family as long-lived asset impairment under ASC 360). **Reversal of a previously recognized impairment is prohibited**. If cash is received for a fully amortized intangible, Dr Cash / Cr Gain (or revenue-like gain on sale)—an entry **is** required.
