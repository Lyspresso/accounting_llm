# Agent 310 — CORE DEMO — LO 9-1

**Chapter:** 9  
**LO title:** Determine the initial recognition and measurement of inventory  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Control-based recognition:** inventoriable goods controlled on the inventory date are recognized as inventory regardless of physical location
- **Goods in transit:** f.o.b. shipping point → purchaser controls when goods are released to the carrier; f.o.b. destination → control passes when the buyer receives the goods
- **Consignment:** consignor includes goods held by the consignee; consignee excludes goods of others
- **Firm repurchase agreements:** if the seller is obligated to buy the goods back, the seller retains control and inventory remains on the seller’s balance sheet (financing)
- **Initial measurement:** cash-equivalent acquisition cost + costs to bring goods to condition and location for sale (invoice, sales tax/tariffs, freight-in, transit insurance, prep labor, normal storage/handling)
- **Not inventoriable:** freight-out, selling/G&A, abnormal spoilage or abnormal rush freight, interest (generally period charges)
- **Period-end reconciling schedule:** physical count → adjusted ending inventory (control tests + inventoriable add-ons)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** record unrecorded FOB shipping purchases and inventoriable freight; remove consignee goods; restore own consigned goods; reverse premature FOB destination sales; restore inventory and reclassify proceeds under firm repurchase
- **Disposal / settlement:** when the consignee sells to an end customer, the consignor records sales, commission expense, COGS, and removes inventory
- **Classification / presentation:** Inventory as a current asset; firm-repurchase proceeds as a liability (not revenue)

**Method note (core exam path):** Recognize inventory when the entity **controls** the goods (FOB terms, consignment ownership, firm repurchase). Measure at **sum of expenditures to acquire and prepare for sale**. At period-end, build a **reconciling schedule** from the physical count and post **balanced adjusting JEs** so books reflect control and inventoriable cost. Do **not** capitalize abnormal costs or selling costs.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — period-end cut-off adjusting JEs only (emphasis deep-dive)
**LO:** LO 9-1  
**Concept:** Period-end inventory cut-off: reconciling schedule from booked physical count; full set of **period-end adjusting JEs**; inventoriable add-on costs; classification of repurchase liability  
**Scenario:**  
**Redfern Supply Co.** took a physical inventory on **December 31** and recorded the count of **$318,500** to the perpetual Inventory account (books now equal the raw count). The controller later compiles control and measurement corrections **before** statements are issued:

| Item | Detail | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Goods purchased **f.o.b. shipping point** left vendor’s dock Dec 29; arrived Jan 3; purchase invoice **$19,200 already recorded** Dec 29 (Dr Inventory / Cr AP) but goods were **not in the physical count** (count booking removed them) | 19,200 |
| B | Goods purchased **f.o.b. destination**; left vendor Dec 28; arrived Jan 2; **neither recorded nor counted** | 10,800 |
| C | Redfern is **consignee** for $6,750 of **Silveroak Goods** inventory **in the warehouse and in the count** | 6,750 |
| D | Redfern is **consignor**; $15,400 of its goods sit at a dealer; **not counted** | 15,400 |
| E | Customer shipment **f.o.b. destination** left Redfern Dec 31; cost $5,600; **removed from inventory and sold** on books Dec 31 at SP **$8,900** on account | 5,600 |
| F | Inventory under **obligatory repurchase**; cost $10,500 remains off books after a “sale”; cash proceeds of **$12,750** were recorded as sales | 10,500 |
| G | **Measurement add-on:** sales tax and freight-in on a correctly controlled Dec 28 FOB shipping receipt already in inventory **at invoice only** — missing inventoriable costs still unpaid | 2,350 |

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare a **reconciling schedule** from booked physical count to correct inventory for the balance sheet.  
(b) Record **all period-end adjusting journal entries** needed so Inventory and related accounts are correct. Prove Dr = Cr on each entry.  
(c) **Classification / presentation:** State the **line-item presentation** of the corrected inventory on the classified balance sheet. Identify which of A–G (if any) is a **purchase commitment disclosure** rather than on-balance-sheet inventory. State how the firm-repurchase liability is presented.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Reconciling schedule**

|---|---:|---:|

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 15,400 | |

**(c) Classification / presentation**

- Corrected inventory of **$364,800** is reported as a **current asset** (Inventory) on the classified balance sheet. Composition may be disclosed (on hand, in transit, on consignment, under firm repurchase) but the single line is typically **Inventory**.  
- **Item B** is neither inventory nor a recorded liability (purchase not recorded; control with supplier). If Redfern has a noncancelable purchase commitment at a loss, that is a **disclosure / contingency** matter—not inventoriable until control and cost are recognized. None of A–G is automatically a “purchase commitment disclosure” solely from the facts; **B** is the only item still controlled by the vendor and unrecorded.  
- **Liability under Repurchase Agreement $12,750** is a **liability** (often current if settlement is within one year)—**not** sales revenue and **not** a reduction of inventory. Cash already received is financing.

**Key insight:** After the physical count is booked, every cut-off error is fixed with a **period-end adjusting JE**. Control tests (FOB, consignment, firm repurchase) determine **whether** inventory is recognized; inventoriable cost rules determine **how much**. Journals must still balance.

### Q4 — MC — inventoriable cost classification
**LO:** LO 9-1  
**Concept:** Classification — inventoriable product cost vs period / abnormal charge  
**Question:**  
Which of the following costs is **included** in the initial measurement of merchandise inventory under U.S. GAAP (ASC 330)?

- A) Freight-out to deliver goods to a customer  
- B) Abnormal spoilage from a warehouse flood  
- C) Sales tax paid on merchandise purchased for resale  
- D) General administrative salaries for the corporate office  

**Answer:** C.  
Sales tax on purchases is part of the cash-equivalent outlay to acquire inventory. Freight-out is a selling cost; abnormal spoilage and G&A are period charges, not inventoriable product costs.

### Q5 — MC — goods in transit / consignment control
**LO:** LO 9-1  
**Concept:** Classification — goods in transit and consignment control test  
**Question:**  
At December 31, which of the following items should **Cedarline Co.** **include** in its ending inventory?

- A) Goods purchased f.o.b. destination that left the vendor’s dock on December 28 and arrived January 4  
- B) Goods held in Cedarline’s warehouse that belong to a consignor (Cedarline is the consignee)  
- C) Goods sold f.o.b. shipping point that left Cedarline’s dock December 30 and are still in transit to the customer  
- D) Goods owned by Cedarline held by a dealer on consignment (Cedarline is the consignor)  

**Answer:** D.  
The consignor retains control and includes consigned goods in inventory. A is the supplier’s inventory until receipt (FOB destination). B is the consignor’s inventory, not the consignee’s. C has transferred to the customer at shipping point.
