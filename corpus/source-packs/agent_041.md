# Agent 41 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-3

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Account for the impact of sales returns and allowances  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Sales returns and allowances as **variable consideration** (reduce transaction price / net sales)
- **Sales Returns** (contra revenue) vs **Sales Allowances** (contra revenue; customer keeps goods)
- Initial recognition of credit sales and COGS (perpetual inventory)
- Actual sales return: sales-side (Dr Sales Returns) + cost-side (restore Inventory, reduce COGS)
- Sales allowance: **sales-side only** (no inventory restore)
- Period-end estimate: Dr Sales Returns / Cr **Refund Liability**; Dr **Inventory—Estimated Returns** / Cr COGS
- First-year remaining estimate = total estimated returns − actual returns already recorded
- Subsequent-year true-up: record all actual returns as current-period; adjust Refund Liability so ending balance = estimated remaining returns on current-year sales
- Refund Liability rollforward schedule
- Settlement of Refund Liability when a return is applied against the prior estimate (alt. recording)
- Classification / presentation: Net Sales; Refund Liability (current liability); Inventory—Estimated Returns (current asset)

### Q1 — CORE — Cedarline Outdoor Gear: first-year sales, returns, allowances, YE estimate
**LO:** LO 8-3  
**Concept:** initial_recognition_JE — actual returns & allowances; period-end Refund Liability and Inventory—Estimated Returns  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Outdoor Gear Co.** begins operations in Year 1 and sells outdoor apparel on account. Cedarline uses a **perpetual** inventory system. Customers may return merchandise for a **cash refund**. Cost of merchandise is consistently **62%** of selling price. During Year 1:

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------:|
| Credit sales | **$850,000** |
| Cost of goods sold on those sales (62%) | **$527,000** |
| Actual cash refunds for merchandise returns | **$22,100** |
| Sales allowances granted (customers keep goods; credit to AR) | **$3,400** |

At December 31, Year 1, Cedarline estimates **total** sales returns for the year at **3.5%** of credit sales (based on experience of similar retailers and expected economic conditions). No Refund Liability or Inventory—Estimated Returns balances existed at the start of Year 1.

**Required:**  
(a) Record the Year 1 entries for **credit sales** and related **cost of goods sold**.  
(b) Record the entries for **actual sales returns** during the year (sales-side and cost-side).  
(c) Record the entry for **sales allowances**.  
(d) Compute total estimated returns and the **remaining** estimate at year-end. Record the December 31 adjusting entries for estimated returns (sales-side and cost-side).  
(e) Prepare a **Refund Liability rollforward** (beginning → actual → estimate plug → ending). Compute **Net Sales** for Year 1 and state year-end balance-sheet classification of Refund Liability and Inventory—Estimated Returns.  
(f) Briefly contrast the accounting for a **sales return** with a **sales allowance**.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Credit sales and COGS — Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 850,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 850,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 527,000 | |
| Inventory | | 527,000 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr on each entry. Cost check: \(850{,}000 \times 0.62 = 527{,}000\).

**(b) Actual sales returns during Year 1**

Sales-side (cash refunds):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 22,100 | |
| Cash | | 22,100 |

Cost-side (restore inventory; reverse COGS):  
\(22{,}100 \times 0.62 = \mathbf{\$13{,}702}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 13,702 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 13,702 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr on each entry.

**(c) Sales allowances (customer keeps goods — no inventory entry)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Allowances | 3,400 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 3,400 |

*Check:* Dr 3,400 = Cr 3,400. **No** Inventory or COGS entry for an allowance.

**(d) Period-end estimated returns — December 31, Year 1**

Total estimated returns = \(0.035 \times 850{,}000 = \mathbf{\$29{,}750}\).  
Remaining estimate (Refund Liability) = \(29{,}750 - 22{,}100 = \mathbf{\$7{,}650}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 7,650 | |
| Refund Liability | | 7,650 |

Cost of remaining estimated returns = \(7{,}650 \times 0.62 = \mathbf{\$4{,}743}\)  
(equivalently: total cost of estimated returns \(29{,}750 \times 0.62 = 18{,}445\) minus inventory already restored \(13{,}702\)).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | 4,743 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 4,743 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr on each entry. Combined Year 1 Sales Returns = \(22{,}100 + 7{,}650 = 29{,}750\) (= total estimate).

**(e) Schedule, Net Sales, presentation**

**Refund Liability rollforward — Year 1**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Refund Liability | $0 |
| + Additional estimate (YE plug) | 7,650 |
| − Actual returns applied to liability during year* | 0 |
| **Ending Refund Liability** | **$7,650** |

\*Under the common practice used in Demo 8-3 / first-year start-up, actual returns are charged to **Sales Returns** (not Refund Liability); the YE plug sets the liability equal to remaining estimated returns.

Cross-check identity: ending liability = total estimated returns − actual returns recorded = \(29{,}750 - 22{,}100 = 7{,}650\).

**Net Sales — Year 1**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales Revenue | $850,000 |
| − Sales Returns (\(22{,}100 + 7{,}650\)) | (29,750) |
| − Sales Allowances | (3,400) |
| **Net Sales** | **$816,850** |

**Balance sheet (December 31, Year 1) — classification**  
- **Refund Liability $7,650** — current liability (expected refunds of consideration).  
- **Inventory—Estimated Returns $4,743** — current asset (right to recover products expected to be returned).

**(f) Return vs allowance**  
A **sales return** brings merchandise back: reduce net sales **and** restore Inventory / reduce COGS. A **sales allowance** is a price concession so the customer **keeps** the goods: only a contra-revenue entry (Sales Allowances); inventory and COGS are unchanged.

**Key insight:** In practice, companies record **actual** returns (and allowances) as they occur, then at period-end accrue the **remaining** expected returns with Refund Liability and Inventory—Estimated Returns so Net Sales reflects the transaction price the entity expects to be entitled to (ASC 606 variable consideration).

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Pinehaven Home Textiles: first-year twin
**LO:** LO 8-3  
**Concept:** initial_recognition_JE and period_end_adjusting_JE (number-variant twin of Q1)  
**Scenario:**  
**Pinehaven Home Textiles Inc.** is in its first year of operations. It sells on account, uses perpetual inventory, and grants **cash** refunds on returns. Merchandise cost is **58%** of selling price. Year 1 data:

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------:|
| Credit sales | **$1,250,000** |
| Cost of goods sold (58%) | **$725,000** |
| Actual cash refunds for returns | **$31,250** |
| Sales allowances (credit to AR; goods retained) | **$5,000** |
| Estimated **total** returns at year-end | **4%** of credit sales |

No beginning Refund Liability or Inventory—Estimated Returns.

**Required:**  
(a) Record sales and COGS.  
(b) Record actual returns (sales-side and cost-side).  
(c) Record sales allowances.  
(d) Record December 31 estimated-return adjusting entries (both sides).  
(e) Compute Net Sales and ending balances of Refund Liability and Inventory—Estimated Returns. Show the identity: ending Refund Liability = total estimated returns − actual returns.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Sales and COGS**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 1,250,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 1,250,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 725,000 | |
| Inventory | | 725,000 |

*Check:* \(1{,}250{,}000 \times 0.58 = 725{,}000\). Dr = Cr.

**(b) Actual returns**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 31,250 | |
| Cash | | 31,250 |

Inventory restored: \(31{,}250 \times 0.58 = \mathbf{\$18{,}125}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 18,125 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 18,125 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr.

**(c) Sales allowances**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Allowances | 5,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 5,000 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. No inventory entry.

**(d) December 31 estimated returns**

Total estimated returns = \(0.04 \times 1{,}250{,}000 = \mathbf{\$50{,}000}\).  
Remaining = \(50{,}000 - 31{,}250 = \mathbf{\$18{,}750}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 18,750 | |
| Refund Liability | | 18,750 |

Cost side: \(18{,}750 \times 0.58 = \mathbf{\$10{,}875}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | 10,875 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 10,875 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. Total Sales Returns = \(31{,}250 + 18{,}750 = 50{,}000\).

**(e) Net Sales and ending balances**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales Revenue | $1,250,000 |
| − Sales Returns | (50,000) |
| − Sales Allowances | (5,000) |
| **Net Sales** | **$1,195,000** |

| Account | Ending balance |
|---|---:|
| Refund Liability | **$18,750** |
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | **$10,875** |

Identity: ending Refund Liability = total estimated returns − actual returns = \(50{,}000 - 31{,}250 = 18{,}750\).  
Inventory—Estimated Returns = ending Refund Liability × cost % = \(18{,}750 \times 0.58 = 10{,}875\).

**Key insight:** Changing only the sales base, cost %, return rate, and actual returns still yields the same core structure: accrue the **unreturned** portion of the estimate so the liability and estimated-returns inventory equal remaining expected refunds and recoveries.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Riverton Appliance: subsequent-year true-up, schedule, settlement
**LO:** LO 8-3  
**Concept:** subsequent_measurement_schedule; period_end_adjusting_JE; disposal_maturity_or_settlement_JE; classification_presentation_or_disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverton Appliance Distributors** grants a 45-day return privilege. Cost of merchandise is **60%** of selling price. Riverton records **all actual returns during the year as if they arise from the current year** (debit Sales Returns), then adjusts Refund Liability at year-end so the **ending** balance equals estimated returns on **current-year** sales still expected next period. (This is the subsequent-period approach in Review 8-3.)

**January 1, Year 2 balances:**

| Account | Balance |
|---|---:|
| Refund Liability | **$14,000** |
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | **$8,400** |

**Year 2 activity:**

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------:|
| Credit sales | **$420,000** |
| COGS (60%) | **$252,000** |
| Actual returns (cash refunds), recorded as current-year Sales Returns | **$18,600** |
| Sales allowances on account | **$2,100** |
| Year-end estimate of returns related to Year 2 sales | **5%** of Year 2 credit sales |

Assume actual return rates are reasonably consistent with management’s estimates on average.

**Required:**  
(a) Record Year 2 sales and COGS.  
(b) Record actual returns (both sides) and sales allowances.  
(c) Compute the **target ending Refund Liability** and the year-end **adjusting entries** for estimated returns (sales-side and cost-side).  
(d) Prepare a full **Refund Liability rollforward schedule** from beginning to ending balance. Also roll forward Inventory—Estimated Returns and confirm ending Inv—Est Returns = ending Refund Liability × 60%.  
(e) **Settlement alternate:** Suppose instead that on **January 20, Year 2**, Riverton identifies **$9,000** of cash returns (cost **$5,400**) as returns of Year 1 sales that had already been accrued in Refund Liability / Inventory—Estimated Returns. Record the **settlement** entries that reduce the liability and the estimated-returns asset (do **not** debit Sales Returns for this portion).  
(f) Report Net Sales for Year 2 under the main (Review 8-3) method in parts (a)–(d), and state balance-sheet presentation of the two estimated-return accounts.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Year 2 sales and COGS**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 420,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 420,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 252,000 | |
| Inventory | | 252,000 |

*Check:* \(420{,}000 \times 0.60 = 252{,}000\).

**(b) Actual returns and allowances (main method — charge Sales Returns)**

Sales-side returns:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 18,600 | |
| Cash | | 18,600 |

Cost-side: \(18{,}600 \times 0.60 = \mathbf{\$11{,}160}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 11,160 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 11,160 |

Allowances:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Allowances | 2,100 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 2,100 |

*Check:* All entries balance. Refund Liability is **not** reduced when actual returns are recorded under this method; the YE true-up handles it.

**(c) Period-end adjusting entries — December 31, Year 2**

Estimated returns on Year 2 sales = \(0.05 \times 420{,}000 = \mathbf{\$21{,}000}\).

Target ending Refund Liability (Review 8-3 identity):  
\[
\begin{align*}
\text{Ending RL} &= \text{Beg RL} - \text{Actual returns} + \text{Est. on current-year sales} \\
&= 14{,}000 - 18{,}600 + 21{,}000 = \mathbf{\$16{,}400}
\end{align*}
\]

Adjusting plug to Refund Liability = \(16{,}400 - 14{,}000 = \mathbf{+\$2{,}400}\)  
(equivalently: \(21{,}000 - 18{,}600 = 2{,}400\)).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Returns | 2,400 | |
| Refund Liability | | 2,400 |

Cost-side plug: \(2{,}400 \times 0.60 = \mathbf{\$1{,}440}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | 1,440 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 1,440 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr. Total Year 2 Sales Returns (main method) = \(18{,}600 + 2{,}400 = 21{,}000\) (= estimate on current-year sales).  
If the plug had been negative (actual returns > estimate on current sales), debit Refund Liability and credit Sales Returns (as in Review 8-3 solution).

**(d) Subsequent measurement schedules**

**Refund Liability rollforward — Year 2**

| Date / component | Debit (decrease) | Credit (increase) | Balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, Year 2 beginning | | | 14,000 |
| Actual returns (not charged to RL under main method) | — | — | 14,000 |
| Dec 31 YE true-up (est. on Y2 sales − actual) | | 2,400 | **16,400** |
| **Memo reconciling identity** | Actual 18,600 | Est. on Y2 sales 21,000 | Beg 14,000 − 18,600 + 21,000 = **16,400** |

**Inventory—Estimated Returns rollforward — Year 2**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Inventory—Estimated Returns | $8,400 |
| + YE cost-side true-up | 1,440 |
| **Ending Inventory—Estimated Returns** | **$9,840** |

Confirm: ending Refund Liability × cost % = \(16{,}400 \times 0.60 = \mathbf{\$9{,}840}\). ✓

**(e) Settlement of prior-period estimate (alternate recording of returns of Year 1 sales)**

When a return is explicitly identified with amounts already accrued:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Refund Liability | 9,000 | |
| Cash | | 9,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 5,400 | |
| Inventory—Estimated Returns | | 5,400 |

*Check:* Dr = Cr on each entry. **Sales Returns is not debited** for this settlement—doing so would double-count the reduction already recognized when the estimate was accrued. After settlement, Refund Liability would be \(14{,}000 - 9{,}000 = 5{,}000\) before any further Year 2 true-up; Inventory—Estimated Returns would be \(8{,}400 - 5{,}400 = 3{,}000\).

**(f) Net Sales (main method) and presentation**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales Revenue | $420,000 |
| − Sales Returns (\(18{,}600 + 2{,}400\)) | (21,000) |
| − Sales Allowances | (2,100) |
| **Net Sales** | **$396,900** |

**Classification / presentation**  
- **Refund Liability $16,400** — current liability (refund obligation for consideration the entity does not expect to be entitled to).  
- **Inventory—Estimated Returns $9,840** — current asset (inventory expected to be recovered).  
- Net Sales is reported on the income statement; Sales Returns and Sales Allowances are contra-revenue accounts that reduce gross sales to Net Sales.

**Key insight:** In subsequent periods, if actual returns are booked entirely to Sales Returns, the year-end true-up **recalibrates** Refund Liability so ending RL = beginning RL − actual returns + estimate on current-year sales. Alternatively, returns of previously estimated amounts may **settle** the liability directly (Dr Refund Liability), which is a disposal/settlement of the accrued refund obligation.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 8-3  
**Concept:** classification_presentation_or_disclosure — return vs allowance; Refund Liability vs Inventory—Estimated Returns  

**Question 4.1**  
On March 12, a customer of Harborwick Lighting keeps slightly scuffed fixtures after Harborwick grants a **$1,200** price reduction (credit memo to Accounts Receivable). The fixtures originally sold for $8,000 (cost $4,800). Which entry correctly records the March 12 event?

- A) Debit Sales Returns $1,200; credit Accounts Receivable $1,200; and debit Inventory $720; credit COGS $720  
- B) Debit Sales Allowances $1,200; credit Accounts Receivable $1,200 only  
- C) Debit Refund Liability $1,200; credit Accounts Receivable $1,200 only  
- D) Debit Sales Allowances $1,200; credit Accounts Receivable $1,200; and debit Inventory $720; credit COGS $720  

**Answer:** **B.** A sales **allowance** is a price reduction when the customer **retains** the goods—only the contra-revenue / receivable entry is made. No inventory is restored. A is a return-style pair (wrong account and unwarranted inventory). C treats an already-granted allowance as settlement of an estimate. D incorrectly restores inventory for an allowance.

---

**Question 4.2**  
After year-end adjusting entries for estimated sales returns, which presentation is correct?

- A) Refund Liability is a contra-asset to Accounts Receivable; Inventory—Estimated Returns is a current liability  
- B) Refund Liability is a current liability; Inventory—Estimated Returns is a current asset  
- C) Both Refund Liability and Inventory—Estimated Returns are contra-revenue accounts on the income statement  
- D) Refund Liability reduces Net Sales directly as a credit-balance equity account  

**Answer:** **B.** Refund Liability is a **current liability** (obligation to refund consideration). Inventory—Estimated Returns is a **current asset** (right to recover products). Sales Returns (not the liability itself) is the contra-revenue account used to reduce Net Sales. A reverses the classifications; C and D misstate the nature of the balance-sheet accounts.
