# Agent 83 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-7

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Account for impairment of property, plant, and equipment  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Indicators of impairment:** review for impairment only when events/circumstances suggest carrying amount may not be recoverable (not a routine annual test)
- **Step 1 — Recoverability test (held for use):** compare **undiscounted** expected future net cash inflows (recoverable cost) to carrying amount; impaired only if recoverable cost **<** carrying amount
- **Step 2 — Impairment measurement:** loss = carrying amount − **fair value** (not recoverable cost)
- **Initial recognition JE:** Debit **Loss on Impairment**, Credit **Accumulated Depreciation** (Hanlon / Demo 12-7 convention); new cost basis = fair value
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** depreciate new carrying amount over **revised remaining useful life** (revised residual as applicable); **no restoration** of prior impairment for assets held for use
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** recognize impairment when indicators exist at reporting date and both steps fail/require a loss
- **Disposal after impairment:** remove cost and accumulated depreciation; gain/loss vs cash (or other proceeds)
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** impairment loss in **income from continuing operations** (other expenses); notes describe the asset, facts/circumstances, amount, and fair-value method; restoration prohibited for held-for-use PPE

### Q1 — CORE — Full impairment cycle (emphasis: initial recognition JE)
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Indicators → recoverability → impairment loss initial recognition JE; post-impairment depreciation schedule; later disposal JE; presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Metalworks Inc.** (calendar year) owns specialized production equipment used in operations (held for use).

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Original cost (installed early January, 8 years ago) | **$1,800,000** |
| Original useful life / residual | **15 years**, residual **$180,000** |
| Depreciation method | Straight-line |
| Accumulated depreciation at Dec 31, just before current-year review | (compute) |
| **January 2 of current year (Year 9)** | Competitor launches a superior product; demand for Cascade’s related product falls sharply — **impairment indicator** |
| Estimated remaining undiscounted future net cash inflows (use + eventual disposal) | **$420,000** |
| Fair value of equipment (Jan 2, Year 9) | **$300,000** |
| Revised remaining useful life / residual after review | **4 years**, residual **$0** |

On **December 31, Year 10** (after two full years of post-impairment depreciation), Cascade sells the equipment for **$140,000** cash. Assume no further impairment indicators after Year 9 and no change to residual/life after the Year 9 write-down.

**Required:**  
a. Compute carrying amount immediately before the impairment analysis. Perform the **recoverability test** and, if required, measure the **impairment loss**.  
b. Prepare the **January 2, Year 9** journal entry to **initially recognize** any impairment loss (textbook AD-credit convention). State the post-impairment carrying amount and new cost basis for depreciation.  
c. Prepare a **subsequent depreciation schedule** for the remaining 4 years after impairment (Years 9–12 planned). Record the **December 31, Year 9** depreciation adjusting entry.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 10** disposal journal entry (after recording Year 10 depreciation).  
e. State how the Year 9 impairment loss is **classified** on the income statement and list **two required note disclosures**.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Carrying amount, recoverability, measurement**

Annual depreciation (original):  
\[
\frac{\$1{,}800{,}000 - \$180{,}000}{15} = \$108{,}000
\]

Accumulated depreciation after 8 years: \(8 \times \$108{,}000 = \mathbf{\$864{,}000}\)  
Carrying amount: \(\$1{,}800{,}000 - \$864{,}000 = \mathbf{\$936{,}000}\)

**Recoverability test (Step 1):**  
Recoverable cost **$420,000** < carrying amount **$936,000** → asset is **not recoverable** → proceed to Step 2.

**Impairment measurement (Step 2):**  
\[
\text{Impairment loss} = \$936{,}000 - \$300{,}000 = \mathbf{\$636{,}000}
\]

**b. Initial recognition JE — January 2, Year 9**

*To record loss on impairment of equipment held for use*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 636,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 636,000 |
| *Write down carrying amount to fair value; increase AD* | | |

**Check:** Dr 636,000 = Cr 636,000. **Balanced.**

Post-impairment balances:  
- Equipment (gross cost) remains **$1,800,000**  
- Accumulated depreciation: \(864{,}000 + 636{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}500{,}000}\)  
- **Carrying amount / new cost basis** = \(1{,}800{,}000 - 1{,}500{,}000 = \mathbf{\$300{,}000}\) (= fair value)

**c. Subsequent depreciation schedule and Year 9 adjusting entry**

New depreciable base = **$300,000**; residual **$0**; remaining life **4 years**.  
Annual depreciation = \(300{,}000 / 4 = \mathbf{\$75{,}000}\)

| Year | Beginning CV | Depreciation | Ending CV | Ending AD (cumulative from original) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 9 | 300,000 | 75,000 | 225,000 | 1,575,000 |
| 10 | 225,000 | 75,000 | 150,000 | 1,650,000 |
| 11 | 150,000 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 1,725,000 |
| 12 | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 | 1,800,000 |

*December 31, Year 9 — Depreciation (post-impairment basis)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 75,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 75,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 75,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Disposal — December 31, Year 10**

After Year 10 depreciation: CV = **$150,000**; AD = **$1,650,000**.  
Cash proceeds **$140,000** → loss on disposal = \(150{,}000 - 140{,}000 = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\)

*December 31, Year 10 — Dispose of equipment*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 140,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 1,650,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 10,000 | |
| Equipment | | 1,800,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(140{,}000 + 1{,}650{,}000 + 10{,}000 = 1{,}800{,}000\) = Cr 1,800,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification and disclosure**  
- **Income statement:** Loss on Impairment **$636,000** is a component of **income from continuing operations** (typically other expenses), **not** an extraordinary item and not OCI.  
- **Note disclosures (ASC 360-10-50-2 examples):** (1) description of the impaired asset and facts/circumstances (competitor product / demand decline); (2) amount of the loss and income-statement caption; (3) method(s) used to determine fair value; (4) segment, if applicable.

**Key insight:** For PPE **held for use**, impairment is a **two-step** process. Step 1 uses **undiscounted** cash flows (recoverability). Only if that test fails is a loss recorded equal to carrying amount minus **fair value**. The **initial recognition JE** writes the asset down (via AD under Demo 12-7), establishing a new depreciable basis; **subsequent recovery of value is prohibited** while held for use.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Impairment twin (different numbers)
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: recoverability, impairment initial recognition JE, post-impairment depreciation schedule, period-end presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Packaging Systems** (calendar year) uses a high-speed cartoning line in operations.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Original cost (in service Jan 1, 6 years ago) | **$2,400,000** |
| Original useful life / residual | **12 years**, residual **$240,000** |
| Depreciation | Straight-line |
| **Dec 31, Year 6** (after Year-6 depreciation already recorded) | Regulatory change sharply reduces expected product volume — **impairment indicator** |
| Estimated remaining undiscounted future net cash inflows | **$780,000** |
| Fair value at Dec 31, Year 6 | **$550,000** |
| Revised remaining useful life / residual | **5 years**, residual **$0** |

**Required:**  
a. Compute carrying amount at Dec 31, Year 6 (before impairment). Perform recoverability and measure any loss.  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 6 period-end adjusting JE** for impairment (initial recognition of the write-down).  
c. Prepare the **post-impairment depreciation schedule** for Years 7–11. Record Year 7 depreciation.  
d. Compute total depreciation that will be recognized over the **remaining 5 years** after impairment, and state whether a subsequent increase in fair value to **$600,000** in Year 8 may reverse any of the Year 6 impairment (held for use).  
e. Briefly state **balance-sheet presentation** of the equipment immediately after the Year 6 impairment entry (gross, AD, net).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Carrying amount, recoverability, measurement**

Annual depreciation:  
\[
\frac{\$2{,}400{,}000 - \$240{,}000}{12} = \$180{,}000
\]

AD after 6 years: \(6 \times \$180{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}080{,}000}\)  
Carrying amount: \(\$2{,}400{,}000 - \$1{,}080{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}320{,}000}\)

Recoverability: **$780,000** < **$1,320,000** → **fails** → measure loss.  
Impairment loss: \(1{,}320{,}000 - 550{,}000 = \mathbf{\$770{,}000}\)

**b. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 6**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 770,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Cartoning Line | | 770,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 770,000. **Balanced.**

New carrying amount = **$550,000**  
New AD = \(1{,}080{,}000 + 770{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}850{,}000}\)

**c. Subsequent depreciation schedule (Years 7–11)**

Annual depreciation: \(550{,}000 / 5 = \mathbf{\$110{,}000}\)

| Year | Beginning CV | Depreciation | Ending CV |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 550,000 | 110,000 | 440,000 |
| 8 | 440,000 | 110,000 | 330,000 |
| 9 | 330,000 | 110,000 | 220,000 |
| 10 | 220,000 | 110,000 | 110,000 |
| 11 | 110,000 | 110,000 | 0 |

*December 31, Year 7 — Depreciation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 110,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Cartoning Line | | 110,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 110,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Total remaining depreciation and restoration rule**  
Total depreciation over remaining life = \(5 \times \$110{,}000 = \mathbf{\$550{,}000}\) (equals the new cost basis).  
A fair-value increase to **$600,000** in Year 8 **may not reverse** any of the Year 6 impairment: **restoration of a previously recognized impairment loss is prohibited** for long-lived assets **held for use**.

**e. Presentation immediately after Year 6 impairment**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment—Cartoning Line (gross) | $2,400,000 |
| Less: Accumulated depreciation | (1,850,000) |
| **Equipment, net** | **$550,000** |

**Key insight:** Changing only the numbers, the logic is identical: **undiscounted** recoverability first; measure with **fair value**; period-end **Loss / AD** entry sets the new basis; future depreciation follows that basis with **no write-up** if markets recover.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Multi-asset screen, classification, disclosure
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Multi-asset recoverability screen (pass vs fail); combined impairment JE; classification that recoverability pass means no loss even if FV < CV; disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Redwood Appliance Manufacturing** has three plant assets held for use. Management has identified impairment indicators for **all three**. Data at year-end (after current-year depreciation):

| Plant asset | Carrying amount | Recoverable cost (undiscounted future net CF) | Fair value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| #R-11 Stamping press | $250,000 | $280,000 | $230,000 |
| #R-22 Coating line | 500,000 | 450,000 | 380,000 |
| #R-33 Assembly cell | 720,000 | 600,000 | 410,000 |

**Required:**  
a. For each asset, state whether an impairment loss must be **recognized** and compute the loss amount (show recoverability outcome first).  
b. Prepare a **single combined journal entry** for all required impairment losses (if any).  
c. Explain why **#R-11** does or does not record a loss even though fair value is below carrying amount.  
d. After recording impairments, **#R-22** will be used 4 more years with residual $0. Compute annual straight-line depreciation on the new basis.  
e. List the **minimum note disclosures** when impairment losses are recognized in the period.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Asset-by-asset analysis**

| Asset | Recoverability (RC ? CV) | Impaired? | Loss = CV − FV |
|---|---|---|---:|
| #R-11 | $280,000 **>** $250,000 → **passes** | **No** | **$0** |
| #R-22 | $450,000 **<** $500,000 → **fails** | **Yes** | \(500{,}000 - 380{,}000 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}\) |
| #R-33 | $600,000 **<** $720,000 → **fails** | **Yes** | \(720{,}000 - 410{,}000 = \mathbf{\$310{,}000}\) |

**Total impairment loss recognized = $430,000**

**b. Combined impairment JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Impairment | 430,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Plant Assets | | 430,000 |
| *#R-22 $120,000 + #R-33 $310,000; #R-11 no loss* | | |

**Check:** Dr 430,000 = Cr 430,000. **Balanced.**

(Alternatively, separate AD credits by asset account — same totals.)

**c. Why #R-11 has no loss**  
The **recoverability test** uses **undiscounted** future net cash flows. Because recoverable cost **exceeds** carrying amount, the asset is considered recoverable through use and disposal. **No impairment loss is recognized**, even though fair value (**$230,000**) is below carrying amount (**$250,000**). Fair value is used only **after** recoverability fails.

**d. Subsequent depreciation — #R-22**  
New cost basis = fair value = **$380,000**  
Annual depreciation = \(380{,}000 / 4 = \mathbf{\$95{,}000}\)

**e. Disclosure (ASC 360-10-50-2)**  
Disclose: (1) description of impaired asset(s)/group and facts/circumstances; (2) amount of impairment loss and income-statement caption (if not separately presented on the face); (3) method(s) for determining fair value; (4) reportable segment, if applicable under Topic 280.

**Key insight:** The recoverability gate **filters** write-downs. Assets that pass Step 1 keep their historical cost basis (net of normal depreciation) **despite** temporary FV shortfalls; only assets that fail Step 1 are written down to FV in Step 2.

### Q4 — MC — Recoverability vs measurement
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Recoverability uses undiscounted cash flows; impairment loss measured with fair value  
**Question:**  
On December 31, Year 5, **Solstice Ceramics** reviews a kiln (held for use) after a permanent decline in product demand. Carrying amount is **$480,000**. Estimated future **undiscounted** net cash inflows from use and disposal are **$390,000**. The **present value** of those cash flows (and estimated fair value) is **$310,000**. Which amount of impairment loss, if any, should Solstice recognize under US GAAP for PPE held for use?

- A) $0, because depreciation will continue to allocate cost  
- B) $90,000  
- C) $170,000  
- D) $480,000  

**Answer:** **C.**  
Recoverability fails because undiscounted CF **$390,000** < CV **$480,000**. Loss = CV − FV = \(480{,}000 - 310{,}000 = \mathbf{\$170{,}000}\).  
Option B incorrectly measures loss as CV − recoverable cost (\(480{,}000 - 390{,}000\)). Option A ignores a failed recoverability test. Option D writes the asset to zero without using fair value.

### Q5 — MC — No restoration for held-for-use assets
**LO:** LO 12-7  
**Concept:** Prohibition on restoring impairment losses for PPE held for use; new cost basis  
**Question:**  
In Year 3, **Pinecrest Mills** recognized a **$200,000** impairment loss on a finishing machine that continues in use. In Year 4, the machine’s fair value increases by **$75,000** due to a market rebound. Pinecrest still holds and uses the machine. Under US GAAP, how should Pinecrest account for the Year 4 fair-value increase?

- A) Recognize a **$75,000** recovery (gain) and increase the machine’s carrying amount  
- B) Recognize recovery only up to previously recognized cumulative impairment (**$200,000** cap)  
- C) **No** recovery; continue depreciating the post-impairment carrying amount over remaining useful life  
- D) Reclassify the machine to held for sale and reverse the entire Year 3 loss  

**Answer:** **C.**  
For long-lived assets **held for use**, restoration of a previously recognized impairment loss is **prohibited**. Fair value becomes the new cost basis at impairment; subsequent depreciation continues from that basis. Options A and B describe recovery models that do **not** apply to held-for-use PPE under ASC 360 (recovery of prior write-downs can apply in limited ways to **held-for-sale** assets under LO 12-8, not to assets remaining in use). Option D confuses held-for-use with held-for-sale classification criteria.
