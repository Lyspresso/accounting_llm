# Agent 324 — CORE DEMO — LO 10-6

**Chapter:** 10  
**LO title:** Describe the accounting treatment for changes in inventory methods  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- Voluntary change in inventory costing method is a **change in accounting principle** (presumption of consistency unless justified)
- **Retrospective application** is the general rule: adjust **Inventory** and **Retained Earnings** for the cumulative pretax effect at the **beginning of the year of change**; restate comparative periods presented under the new method
- **Prospective application** when retrospective application is **impracticable** — especially a change **to LIFO** (layers often cannot be reconstructed); **no journal entry** at the change date; current-year beginning inventory becomes the LIFO **base layer**
- **Initial recognition / opening adjusting JE:** cumulative Inventory ↔ Retained Earnings entry (ignore taxes unless given)
- **Subsequent-measurement schedule:** multi-year inventory and net-income under old vs new method; prove inventory difference = cumulative pretax income difference (when operations began under the old method with equal opening inventory)
- **Period-end / period-opening adjusting JE (emphasis):** the retrospective cumulative-effect entry recorded as of **January 1** of the year of change (after-tax effects deferred to later chapters)
- **“Settlement” of the cumulative effect:** the difference is closed into **Retained Earnings** (equity), not current-period income; prior periods presented are restated rather than reporting a current “cumulative effect” on the income statement
- Comparative presentation after a **prospective** change mixes methods across years (comparability reduced)

### Q1 — CORE — Retrospective change LIFO → FIFO: opening adjusting JE, multi-year schedules, restated comparatives
**LO:** LO 10-6  
**Concept:** Retrospective inventory-method change (LIFO to FIFO) — opening Inventory/RE adjusting JE; subsequent inventory–income reconciliation schedule; restated comparative NI and inventory  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Outdoor Gear Inc.** began operations on **January 1, Year 1**, and elected the **LIFO** inventory method. The company has a December 31 year-end. On **January 1, Year 4**, management voluntarily changes from **LIFO to FIFO** because FIFO better matches the physical flow of goods and improves comparability with industry peers. The change is practicable (sufficient historical cost data exist). **Ignore income taxes.**

Available data:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Net income **as reported under LIFO** | $42,000 | $55,000 | $63,000 | — |
| Net income **if under FIFO** | 47,000 | 61,000 | 72,000 | $80,000 |
| Inventory, Dec. 31 **under LIFO** | 90,000 | 105,000 | 118,000 | — |
| Inventory, Dec. 31 **under FIFO** | 95,000 | 116,000 | 138,000 | 155,000 |

At the end of Year 4, Cedarline will issue **comparative** financial statements presenting **Year 2, Year 3, and Year 4**.

**Required:**  
(a) **(Initial / opening recognition)** Prove that the **December 31, Year 3 inventory difference** equals the **cumulative pretax net-income difference** for Years 1–3. Prepare the **January 1, Year 4** journal entry to record the retrospective change (ignore taxes). Prove the entry balances.  
(b) **(Subsequent-measurement schedule)** Prepare a three-year **inventory and income difference schedule** (Years 1–3) reconciling annual ΔNI to ending inventory differences under FIFO vs LIFO.  
(c) **(Emphasis — period-opening adjusting JE context)** Explain in one or two sentences why the retrospective entry is recorded as of **January 1 of the year of change** (not as a December 31, Year 3 “closing” entry to current income). State the effect on **equity** at January 1, Year 4.  
(d) **(Restatement / settlement into equity)** For the Year 4 comparative package presenting Years 2–4:  
   - Report **net income** for Year 2, Year 3, and Year 4 after the change.  
   - Report **inventory** at December 31 of Year 2, Year 3, and Year 4 after the change.  
   - Compute the **adjustment to beginning retained earnings of the earliest year presented (Year 2)** for periods prior to Year 2.  
(e) Briefly state what account would be credited (or debited) instead of Retained Earnings if the cumulative inventory difference had been **negative** (FIFO inventory lower than LIFO).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Cumulative proof and January 1, Year 4 retrospective JE**

Annual pretax NI differences (FIFO − LIFO):

| Year | FIFO NI | LIFO NI | ΔNI (FIFO − LIFO) | Cumulative ΔNI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 47,000 | 42,000 | 5,000 | 5,000 |
| 2 | 61,000 | 55,000 | 6,000 | 11,000 |
| 3 | 72,000 | 63,000 | 9,000 | **20,000** |

Inventory differences (FIFO − LIFO) at each year-end:

| Date | FIFO Inv | LIFO Inv | ΔInv | Equals cum. ΔNI? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| Dec. 31, Y1 | 95,000 | 90,000 | 5,000 | Yes |
| Dec. 31, Y2 | 116,000 | 105,000 | 11,000 | Yes |
| Dec. 31, Y3 | 138,000 | 118,000 | **20,000** | Yes |

\[
\text{Cumulative effect at Jan 1, Y4} = 138{,}000 - 118{,}000 = \mathbf{\$20{,}000}
\]

**January 1, Year 4 — retrospective adjustment for change to FIFO (ignore taxes):**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 20,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | | 20,000 |
| *Cumulative effect of change from LIFO to FIFO* | | |

**Check:** Dr 20,000 = Cr 20,000. **Balanced.**

**(b) Subsequent-measurement schedule (inventory–income reconciliation)**

| Year | Beg. ΔInv (FIFO−LIFO) | + ΔNI for year | = End. ΔInv (FIFO−LIFO) | End. FIFO Inv | End. LIFO Inv |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 (operations began) | 5,000 | 5,000 | 95,000 | 90,000 |
| 2 | 5,000 | 6,000 | 11,000 | 116,000 | 105,000 |
| 3 | 11,000 | 9,000 | 20,000 | 138,000 | 118,000 |

Roll-forward identity each year:  
\[
\text{End ΔInv} = \text{Beg ΔInv} + \text{Δ pretax NI}
\]
(Higher FIFO ending inventory ⇒ lower COGS ⇒ higher NI, holding other items constant.)

**(c) Why January 1 of the year of change (emphasis)**

The cumulative effect of a voluntary change in principle is applied **retrospectively** as of the **beginning of the earliest period affected / year of change**, by adjusting **Inventory** and the **opening balance of Retained Earnings** — **not** by running the difference through **current-period income**. Recording as of **January 1, Year 4** puts the books on FIFO for Year 4 operations and restates equity for prior-period effects.  

**Equity effect at January 1, Year 4:** Retained earnings (and total equity) **increase by $20,000**.

**(d) Comparative presentation after change (Years 2–4 package)**

All amounts under **FIFO** for years presented:

| | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Net income reported** | $61,000 | $72,000 | $80,000 |
| **Inventory, December 31** | $116,000 | $138,000 | $155,000 |

Adjustment to **beginning retained earnings of the earliest year presented (Year 2)** for periods **prior to Year 2** (i.e., Year 1 only):

\[
\text{Year 1 ΔNI (FIFO − LIFO)} = 47{,}000 - 42{,}000 = \mathbf{\$5{,}000}
\]

So beginning RE of Year 2 is increased by **$5,000** for the pre-presentation cumulative effect (ignore taxes). (The Year 2 and Year 3 income statement lines already use restated FIFO NI of $61,000 and $72,000.)

**(e) If cumulative difference negative**

If FIFO inventory were **lower** than LIFO inventory at the change date, the entry would **debit Retained Earnings** and **credit Inventory** for the absolute difference (still ignore taxes).

**Key insight:** Retrospective inventory-method changes do **not** put a “cumulative effect” on the current income statement; they **restate** Inventory and equity (RE) and recast comparative periods under the **new** method.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Average cost → FIFO retrospective change
**LO:** LO 10-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — retrospective change from average cost to FIFO; opening Inventory/RE JE; multi-year ΔInv = cum ΔNI schedule; restated comparatives  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborlight Components Corp.** began operations on **January 1, Year 1**, using the **weighted-average** inventory method (periodic system). On **January 1, Year 3**, Harborlight voluntarily changes to **FIFO** to align with peers. Historical data allow full retrospective application. **Ignore income taxes.** Year-end is December 31. Comparative statements at the end of Year 3 present **Year 1, Year 2, and Year 3**.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net income **under average cost** | $36,000 | $48,000 | — |
| Net income **under FIFO** | 48,000 | 55,000 | $62,000 |
| Inventory, Dec. 31 **average cost** | 84,000 | 102,000 | — |
| Inventory, Dec. 31 **FIFO** | 96,000 | 121,000 | 140,000 |

**Required:**  
(a) Prove Dec. 31, Year 2 inventory difference = cumulative pretax NI difference for Years 1–2. Record the **January 1, Year 3** retrospective journal entry. Prove balance.  
(b) Prepare the annual difference schedule (beg ΔInv + ΔNI = end ΔInv) for Years 1–2.  
(c) **(Emphasis)** State the equity effect of the January 1, Year 3 adjusting entry and whether Year 3 **current** pretax income is increased by the $19,000 cumulative difference.  
(d) Report restated **net income** and **December 31 inventory** for Years 1–3 after the change.  
(e) Compute Year 2 **COGS effect** of using FIFO instead of average cost for Year 2 only (from the NI difference), assuming the only pretax difference between methods is COGS/inventory.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Cumulative proof and January 1, Year 3 JE**

| Year | FIFO NI | Avg NI | ΔNI | Cum. ΔNI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 48,000 | 36,000 | 12,000 | 12,000 |
| 2 | 55,000 | 48,000 | 7,000 | **19,000** |

| Date | FIFO Inv | Avg Inv | ΔInv |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dec. 31, Y1 | 96,000 | 84,000 | 12,000 |
| Dec. 31, Y2 | 121,000 | 102,000 | **19,000** |

\[
121{,}000 - 102{,}000 = \mathbf{\$19{,}000}
\]

**January 1, Year 3 — change from average cost to FIFO:**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 19,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | | 19,000 |
| *Retrospective cumulative effect of change to FIFO* | | |

**Check:** Dr 19,000 = Cr 19,000. **Balanced.**

**(b) Subsequent-measurement / difference schedule**

| Year | Beg. ΔInv | + ΔNI | = End. ΔInv |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 12,000 | 12,000 |
| 2 | 12,000 | 7,000 | 19,000 |

**(c) Emphasis — equity vs current income**

- Equity (RE) **increases $19,000** at January 1, Year 3.  
- Year 3 **current** pretax income is **not** increased by the $19,000 cumulative prior-period difference; that amount is settled in **retained earnings**. Year 3 NI of **$62,000** is simply FIFO NI for Year 3 operations only.

**(d) Restated comparative amounts (all under FIFO)**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net income | $48,000 | $55,000 | $62,000 |
| Inventory, Dec. 31 | $96,000 | $121,000 | $140,000 |

**(e) Year 2 COGS effect (FIFO vs average)**

Holding sales and other items constant, Δ pretax NI = −Δ COGS.  
\[
\text{Year 2 ΔNI} = +\$7{,}000 \Rightarrow \text{COGS under FIFO is \$7{,}000 **lower** than under average cost}
\]

Cross-check via inventory:  
\[
\Delta\text{COGS}_{Y2} = \text{Beg ΔInv} - \text{End ΔInv} = 12{,}000 - 19{,}000 = -7{,}000
\]
(COGS lower by $7,000 under FIFO.) ✓

**Key insight:** Same mechanics as LIFO→FIFO: open with Inventory/RE, restate all years presented under the new method, and keep the cumulative prior effect **out of current income**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Prospective change FIFO → LIFO; base layer; mixed-method comparatives
**LO:** LO 10-6  
**Concept:** Prospective application when changing **to LIFO** (impracticable to reconstruct layers); no opening JE; base-layer subsequent schedule; mixed-method comparative presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Ridgeway Appliance Distributors** used **FIFO** in Year 1 and Year 2. On **January 1, Year 3**, Ridgeway changes to the **LIFO** method for external reporting. Reconstructing LIFO layers for prior years is **impracticable** (detailed historical purchase-price data for each layer are unavailable). **Ignore taxes.** December 31 year-end.

Data under FIFO (as originally reported):

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Net income (FIFO) | $50,000 | $66,000 |
| Inventory, Dec. 31 (FIFO) | 75,000 | 90,000 |

Under LIFO for **Year 3 only**:

| | Year 3 |
|---|---:|
| Net income (LIFO) | $44,000 |
| Inventory, Dec. 31 (LIFO) | 82,000 |

Additional Year 3 LIFO layer data (for the subsequent-measurement schedule):  
- Beginning inventory (FIFO carrying amount becomes LIFO base) = **$90,000**  
- Year 3 ending inventory at current-year LIFO cost structure = **$82,000**  
- Management identifies that ending inventory consists of: base layer **$90,000** less a **decrement** (partial liquidation of the base) of **$8,000** measured at base-layer cost (no new incremental layer was added).

**Required:**  
(a) **(Initial recognition)** Prepare the January 1, Year 3 journal entry, if any, for the change from FIFO to LIFO.  
(b) **(Subsequent-measurement schedule)** Prepare a short LIFO layer / base-layer schedule from January 1, Year 3 through December 31, Year 3; state beginning LIFO base and ending LIFO inventory.  
(c) Report **net income** and **December 31 inventory** for Years 1–3 in comparative statements prepared **after** the change. Identify which years use FIFO and which use LIFO.  
(d) **(Emphasis)** Contrast in one sentence the period-opening JE for a **retrospective** LIFO→FIFO change vs this **prospective** FIFO→LIFO change.  
(e) **(“Settlement” of the accounting change)** Explain why there is **no** Retained Earnings adjustment when changing **to** LIFO under the prospective approach, and what amount serves as the LIFO base layer.

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 1, Year 3 entry**

**No journal entry.** Prospective application leaves beginning inventory at its existing carrying amount (**$90,000**); that amount becomes the LIFO base layer. Books are not adjusted for a cumulative prior-period difference.

**(b) LIFO base / layer subsequent-measurement schedule (Year 3)**

| Layer | Description | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Base layer, Jan 1, Y3 | Beginning inventory (former FIFO EI) | $90,000 |
| Year 3 layer addition | None (inventory decreased) | 0 |
| Year 3 decrement (liquidation of base at base cost) | Partial base liquidation | (8,000) |
| **Ending inventory, Dec. 31, Y3 (LIFO)** | | **$82,000** |

Proof: \(90{,}000 - 8{,}000 = 82{,}000\). ✓

**(c) Comparative presentation after prospective change**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Method reflected | **FIFO** | **FIFO** | **LIFO** |
| Net income reported | $50,000 | $66,000 | $44,000 |
| Inventory, December 31 | $75,000 | $90,000 | $82,000 |

Comparability across years is **impaired** because two methods appear in the same comparative set; disclosure of the nature of and justification for the change is required.

**(d) Emphasis contrast**

A **retrospective** change (e.g., LIFO→FIFO) requires a **January 1 Inventory/Retained Earnings adjusting entry** equal to the cumulative inventory difference; a **prospective** change **to LIFO** requires **no** opening Inventory/RE entry because prior layers cannot be reliably reconstructed.

**(e) Settlement / base layer**

Under the prospective approach there is **no cumulative-effect “settlement” into Retained Earnings** — prior financial statements remain as originally reported under FIFO. The **beginning inventory of the year of change ($90,000)** is treated as the **LIFO base layer** going forward.

**Key insight:** Change **to LIFO** is the classic inventory exception to full retrospective application: **no books entry at change date**; begin LIFO with existing beginning inventory as the base.

### Q4 — MC — Equity effect of retrospective change JE
**LO:** LO 10-6  
**Concept:** Classification of equity effect from the January 1 retrospective Inventory/RE entry (LIFO → FIFO)  
**Question:**  
**Pinetop Supply Co.** changed from **LIFO to FIFO** on January 1 of Year 3. Inventory data (ignore taxes):

| Inventory, Dec. 31 | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LIFO | $40,000 | $38,000 | $42,000 |
| FIFO | 65,000 | 61,000 | 63,000 |

What is the change in **equity** resulting from the journal entry recorded on **January 1 of Year 3** for the change in inventory method?

- A) $44,000 increase  
- B) $44,000 decrease  
- C) $23,000 increase  
- D) $23,000 decrease  

**Answer:** C.  
Cumulative effect at the change date uses **beginning-of-Year-3 inventory** = **December 31, Year 2** balances:  
\[
61{,}000 - 38{,}000 = \mathbf{\$23{,}000}
\]  
Entry: Dr Inventory 23,000 / Cr Retained Earnings 23,000 → **equity increases $23,000**.  
(Year 3 ending inventory is irrelevant to the January 1, Year 3 entry. $65,000 − $40,000 = $25,000 is only the Year 1 layer of the cumulative difference, not the full cumulative amount at the change date; $65k − $42k and similar pairings misuse dates.)

### Q5 — MC — Retrospective vs prospective method choice
**LO:** LO 10-6  
**Concept:** Method classification — when inventory-method changes are retrospective vs prospective  
**Question:**  
Which statement best describes U.S. GAAP treatment for a voluntary change in inventory costing method?

- A) All voluntary inventory-method changes are applied prospectively with no restatement of prior periods.  
- B) All voluntary inventory-method changes are applied retrospectively, including a change to LIFO with reconstructed layers in every case.  
- C) Voluntary changes are generally applied **retrospectively** (Inventory and Retained Earnings adjusted; prior periods presented restated), but a change **to LIFO** is often applied **prospectively** because reconstructing prior LIFO layers is frequently impracticable.  
- D) A change from LIFO to FIFO is always prospective, and a change from FIFO to LIFO is always retrospective.  

**Answer:** C.  
General rule = **retrospective** application for voluntary principle changes. **Impracticability** exception commonly applies when changing **to LIFO** (missing historical layer costs) → **prospective** treatment with existing beginning inventory as the LIFO base. A is wrong (ignores retrospective general rule). B is wrong (ignores impracticability). D reverses the typical pattern.
