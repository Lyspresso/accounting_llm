# Agent 356 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-1

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Record accounts payable and sales taxes payable  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** trade purchases create Accounts Payable at invoice face (gross method, perpetual); freight-in capitalized to Inventory
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** when policy does **not** isolate tax at sale, credit entire customer total to Sales; at period end reclassify tax via **Sales → Sales Taxes Payable** using Total ÷ (1 + rate)
- **Point-of-sale sales taxes (contrast):** company acts as agent; credit Sales Taxes Payable for tax portion at each sale; Sales = pre-tax selling price
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** multi-period liability rollforwards for AP and Sales Taxes Payable (begin + recognize − settle = end); no interest accretion on ordinary short-term trade payables (ASC 835-30 scope exception)
- **Settlement / remittance JE:** pay supplier (with or without cash discount under gross method); remit sales taxes to taxing authority (customer collection does **not** settle STP)
- **Classification:** both AP and Sales Taxes Payable are **current liabilities** measured at face/settlement amount
- **Number-variant twin:** same multi-period path with all amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Period-end sales-tax adjusting JE (emphasis) + AP purchase, discount settlement, remittance, rollforward
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis) — reclassify embedded sales taxes from Sales; initial recognition of AP (gross method + freight); settlement within discount; remittance of sales taxes; subsequent measurement schedules  
**Scenario:**  
**Fox Valley Outfitters LLC** uses a **perpetual** inventory system and the **gross method** for purchases. Company policy is to **not** record sales taxes payable at the point of sale. Cash and receivables are recorded for the **total** amount received/billed and credited entirely to **Sales**; at **month-end** an **adjusting entry** isolates Sales Taxes Payable by reducing Sales. Fiscal year-end is December 31.

Beginning balances on **March 1, 2026** (before March transactions):

| Account | Beginning balance |
|---|---:|
| Accounts Payable | $11,600 |
| Sales Taxes Payable | $0 |

The March 1 AP balance relates to a **February** purchase on **n/30** terms (discount window already expired; due **March 14**). February sales taxes were remitted in full before March 1.

| Date | Transaction |
|---|---|
| Mar 2 | Purchased merchandise for resale on account. Invoice price **$32,000**; terms **2/10, n/30**. Paid freight-in in cash **$480** the same day (FOB shipping point; control passes at shipment). |
| Mar 5 | Cash sales for the day: customers paid **$19,080** total (this amount **includes** 6% sales tax). Cost of goods sold **$10,500**. Per policy, the entire **$19,080** was credited to **Sales** (no tax liability recorded yet). |
| Mar 11 | Paid the Mar 2 supplier invoice **in full** within the discount period. |
| Mar 14 | Paid the February AP balance of **$11,600** in full (no discount available). |
| Mar 19 | Credit sales: customers billed **$12,720** total (**includes** 6% sales tax). Cost **$7,200**. Entire **$12,720** debited to Accounts Receivable and credited to **Sales**. |
| Mar 31 | Month-end: prepare the **adjusting entry** to reclassify the sales-tax portion of the Mar 5 and Mar 19 sales from Sales to Sales Taxes Payable. (No other taxable sales in March.) |
| Apr 22 | Remitted March sales taxes to the state taxing authority. |

**Required:**

b. Record the **Mar 5** cash sale and COGS **as recorded under company policy** (tax not broken out).

**Key insight:** The **period-end adjusting JE** is the core LO skill when policy embeds tax in Sales: divide total collections/billings by (1 + tax rate) to recover pre-tax Sales, credit **Sales Taxes Payable** for the difference, and only then remittance settles the agency liability. AP still follows face recognition → settlement (with Inventory reduced for discounts taken under the gross method). The **subsequent measurement schedule** ties every recognition and settlement line to ending current-liability balances.

After the adjusting entry, ending Sales and STP equal the point-of-sale method results. Remittance of April’s **$2,400** is unaffected.

**Key insight:** Point-of-sale recognition credits **Sales Taxes Payable** immediately; the **period-end adjusting** path (Q1/Q2 emphasis) reaches the same ending liability and pre-tax Sales after the adjusting JE. In both cases, the **subsequent measurement schedule** is the control document, and customer collection never settles the agency tax liability.

### Q4 — MC — Classification and measurement of AP and sales taxes payable
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Classification / presentation — current liability nature; agency role for sales taxes; face measurement of short-term trade AP  
**Question:**  
Which statement best describes the balance-sheet classification and measurement of **accounts payable** from ordinary inventory purchases due in 30 days and **sales taxes payable** collected from customers but not yet remitted?

- A) Accounts payable is a current liability measured at present value; sales taxes payable is deferred revenue until remitted.  
- B) Both are current liabilities measured at settlement/face amount; sales taxes payable is an agency obligation to the taxing authority, not revenue of the seller.  
- C) Both are noncurrent liabilities because remittance timing to suppliers and tax authorities is uncertain.  
- D) Accounts payable is recorded at fair value each period; sales taxes payable is an equity appropriation until paid.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Ordinary trade accounts payable due within the operating cycle/year are **current liabilities** measured at **settlement/face amount** (ASC 835-30 generally does not require separate interest for normal short-term supplier payables). Sales taxes collected are held as an **agent** for the taxing authority and reported as **Sales Taxes Payable (current liability)** until remitted—they are **not** the seller’s revenue and are not deferred revenue for goods/services.

### Q4b — MC — Period-end adjusting method for sales taxes
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Classification of method — period-end adjusting entry when sales taxes were not isolated at the point of sale  
**Question:**  
A retailer collects **$15,900** cash from customers on a day when the sales tax rate is **6%**. Company policy is to credit the **entire $15,900** to Sales at the point of sale. At month-end, the correct adjusting entry to isolate sales taxes payable is:

- A) Debit Sales Taxes Payable $900; credit Sales $900.  
- B) Debit Sales $900; credit Sales Taxes Payable $900.  
- C) Debit Sales $15,000; credit Sales Taxes Payable $15,000.  
- D) Debit Cash $900; credit Sales Taxes Payable $900.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Pre-tax sales = \(15{,}900 / 1.06 = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\); tax = \(15{,}900 - 15{,}000 = \mathbf{\$900}\) (or \(15{,}000 \times 0.06\)). The adjusting entry **reduces Sales** and **increases Sales Taxes Payable** so that revenue is stated net of tax and the agency liability is reported. Option A reverses the accounts; C uses the wrong amount; D double-counts cash already recorded.
