# Agent 217 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-1

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Identify and classify intangible items  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Classification:** finite-life intangible asset vs indefinite-life intangible asset (other than goodwill) vs goodwill vs expense (acquired vs internally generated)
- **Useful life factors:** legal/contractual limits, expected use, obsolescence, renewals; finite → amortize; indefinite → do not amortize
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize acquired intangibles and **external** registration/successful defense costs; expense internal R&D, internal brand-building, and unsuccessful defense
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period straight-line amortization of finite-life intangibles; carrying-value roll-forward; mid-life cost add-on reamortized over remaining useful life
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** amortization expense with credit to the intangible (or Accumulated Amortization)
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** update amortization to disposal date; remove carrying amount; gain or loss vs proceeds
- **Presentation:** goodwill separate from other intangibles; amortizing vs non-amortizing; crypto assets (when ASC 350-60 criteria met) separate from other intangibles
- **Number-variant twin:** same skill path with all amounts, lives, and dates changed

### Q1 — CORE — Classify portfolio; initial JEs; multi-year amortization schedule; patent defense remeasurement; period-end AJEs; disposal

**Concept:** Classify intangible items (finite / indefinite / goodwill / expense); initial recognition JEs; multi-year subsequent measurement amortization schedule (including mid-life successful defense reamortization); period-end adjusting JEs; disposal (derecognition) JE

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Basket asset acquisition allocation; classify; amort schedule; failed defense expense; maturity of license
**LO:** LO 13-1  
**Concept:** Classify intangible items including basket-purchase allocation (no goodwill in asset acquisition); initial recognition by relative FV; subsequent measurement schedule; unsuccessful defense expensed; license maturity (fully amortized / settled)  
**Scenario:**  
**Oakridge Media Partners** (calendar year) completes an **asset acquisition** (not a business combination) on **January 1, Year 1**, paying **$200,000** cash for a group of identifiable intangibles. Fair values of the group members (no liabilities assumed):

| Identifiable intangible | Fair value | Estimated useful life |
|---|---:|---|
| Patent | **$100,000** | **8 years** (finite) |
| Trademark | **$75,000** | **Indefinite** (renewals expected indefinitely) |
| Customer list | **$50,000** | **5 years** (finite) |
| Capitalized software (acquired) | **$25,000** | **4 years** (finite) |
| **Total fair value** | **$250,000** | |

No goodwill is recognized in a pure asset acquisition; allocate the **$200,000** cost based on **relative fair values**. Residual values are zero; SL amortization; credit assets directly.

**Additional Year 1–Year 4 facts**  
1. **Internal design salaries** developing a new logo: **$42,000** (Year 1) — classify and record.  
2. **September 1, Year 2:** External counsel **fails** to defend the patent; legal fees **$8,200** cash. (Do **not** capitalize unsuccessful defense; evaluate patent for impairment under LO 13-3 — for this problem, assume **no impairment** is required and continue original amortization of the allocated patent cost.)  
3. **Acquired in-process R&D** is **not** in this basket; separately, Oakridge pays **$55,000** cash on Jan 1, Year 1 for an incomplete research project acquired from a lab (identifiable IPR&D). Classify (indefinite until project complete) and record; no amortization while indefinite.  
4. The **software** is used fully through its 4-year life and has **zero** residual at Dec. 31, Year 4 (maturity / full amortization).

**Required:**  
a. Classify each of the following as finite-life IA, indefinite-life IA (not goodwill), goodwill, or expense: (1) allocated patent, (2) allocated trademark, (3) allocated customer list, (4) allocated software, (5) internal design salaries, (6) failed patent defense costs, (7) acquired IPR&D, (8) the \$200,000 basket residual above aggregate FV (if any).

c. Prepare a **Years 1–4 subsequent measurement amortization schedule** for finite-life basket assets (annual amort and year-end carrying values). State Year 1 total amortization from the basket.  
d. Record the **failed defense** JE (Sept 1, Year 2) and the **December 31, Year 1** amortization AJE for basket finite-life assets only.  
e. Show that at **December 31, Year 4** the software carrying amount is **zero** (maturity of finite life) and state remaining carrying amounts of patent, trademark, customer list, and IPR&D (assume IPR&D still incomplete / indefinite).

### Q4 — MC — Classification of intangible items
**LO:** LO 13-1  
**Concept:** Finite vs indefinite vs goodwill vs expense; crypto asset classification when ASC 350-60 criteria are met  

**Question 4-1:**  
Which of the following is recognized as **goodwill**?

- A) Excess of cash paid over the fair value of identifiable net assets in an **asset acquisition** of a group of patents and trademarks only  
- B) Excess of cash paid over the fair value of identifiable net assets in a **business combination**  
- C) Internal expenditures that improved brand recognition over five years  
- D) External legal fees to register a trademark renewable indefinitely  

**Answer:** **B.** Goodwill arises only in a **business combination** as the residual excess of consideration over the FV of identifiable net assets. Pure **asset acquisitions** allocate cost by relative FV and do **not** recognize goodwill (A). Internally generated brand value is **expensed** (C). External trademark registration is an **indefinite-life intangible asset**, not goodwill (D).

**Question 4-2:**  
On January 1, **Harborline Motors** (a large auto manufacturer) purchases three fungible **bitcoins** for investment, including exchange fees, for **$96,000**. The holdings meet all ASC 350-60 criteria (intangible definition, no claim on underlying goods/services, blockchain-based, cryptographic security, fungible, not issued by the reporting entity). How should Harborline **initially classify** the holding?

- A) Finite-life intangible asset, amortized over an estimated useful life  
- B) Inventory (held for sale in the ordinary course of business)  
- C) Intangible asset reported **separately** from other intangible assets; **not** amortized solely because of the crypto classification  
- D) Goodwill  

**Answer:** **C.** Qualifying crypto assets are classified as **intangible assets** and presented **separately** from other intangibles under ASC 350-60. They are not goodwill (D). They are not inventory merely because the company occasionally accepts crypto for products when the holding is an **investment** (B). Crypto classification itself does not assign a finite life for amortization the way a patent does (A); measurement after initial recognition follows the crypto / intangible guidance, not automatic finite-life SL amortization from LO 13-1’s patent model.
