### Q1 — CORE — Bond types, features, initial recognition, and Year 1 measurement
**LO:** LO 16-1  
**Concept:** Bond types/features + issuer initial recognition at discount + effective-interest schedule  
**Scenario:**  
On January 1, Year 1, **Cedar Ridge Works** authorizes and issues **$200,000** face of **4-year, 6%** bonds. Interest is payable **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The bonds are **unsecured** (no liens on specific assets) and are **convertible** into Cedar Ridge common stock at the bondholders’ option after two years (conversion rate: 20 shares per $1,000 bond). The market rate for similar nonconvertible debt of comparable risk on the issue date is **8%**. Cedar Ridge uses the **effective interest method** and a calendar year-end.

**Required:**  
a. Identify the **bond type(s)** that apply.  
b. Specify the following **bond features**: (1) face value, (2) maturity date, (3) stated rate per interest period, (4) interest payment dates, (5) bond authorization date, (6) market rate per interest period, (7) bond selling price, (8) bond issue date.  
c. Compute the issue price (round to nearest dollar) and record the **January 1, Year 1** issuance journal entry.  
d. Prepare the **full effective-interest amortization schedule** for the 4-year term (round interest expense to the nearest dollar each period; plug the final amortization so carrying amount equals face at maturity).  
e. Record the **June 30, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 1** interest payment / discount amortization entries.  
f. Show the **December 31, Year 1** balance-sheet presentation of the bonds and Year 1 interest expense.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Bond types:**  
- **Corporate** (issued by a corporation)  
- **Debenture** (unsecured; backed only by issuer credit)  
- **Term** (single maturity date)  
- **Convertible** (bondholder option to convert to equity)

**b. Bond features:**  

| Feature | Cedar Ridge bonds |
|---|---|
| Face value | $200,000 |
| Maturity date | December 31, Year 4 |
| Stated rate per interest period | 3% (6% ÷ 2) |
| Interest payment dates | June 30 and December 31 |
| Bond authorization date | January 1, Year 1 |
| Market rate per interest period | 4% (8% ÷ 2) |
| Bond selling price | $186,535 (see computation) |
| Bond issue date | January 1, Year 1 |

**c. Issue price and initial recognition JE**  
Semiannual cash interest = $200,000 × 3% = **$6,000**  
n = 4 × 2 = **8** periods; i = **4%**  
\[
PV = 6{,}000 \times \frac{1-(1.04)^{-8}}{0.04} + \frac{200{,}000}{(1.04)^{8}} = 40{,}396 + 146{,}139 = \mathbf{\$186{,}535}
\]  
(Discount = $200,000 − $186,535 = **$13,465**)

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Jan. 1, Y1 | Cash | 186,535 | |
| | Discount on Bonds Payable | 13,465 | |
| | Bonds Payable | | 200,000 |

*Check:* 186,535 + 13,465 = 200,000 (Dr = Cr).

**d. Effective-interest amortization schedule (discount)**  

| Date | Cash (3% × face) | Interest expense (4% × beg. carry) | Discount amort. | Unamortized discount | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan. 1, Y1 | — | — | — | 13,465 | 186,535 |
| June 30, Y1 | 6,000 | 7,461 | 1,461 | 12,004 | 187,996 |
| Dec. 31, Y1 | 6,000 | 7,520 | 1,520 | 10,484 | 189,516 |
| June 30, Y2 | 6,000 | 7,581 | 1,581 | 8,903 | 191,097 |
| Dec. 31, Y2 | 6,000 | 7,644 | 1,644 | 7,259 | 192,741 |
| June 30, Y3 | 6,000 | 7,710 | 1,710 | 5,549 | 194,451 |
| Dec. 31, Y3 | 6,000 | 7,778 | 1,778 | 3,771 | 196,229 |
| June 30, Y4 | 6,000 | 7,849 | 1,849 | 1,922 | 198,078 |
| Dec. 31, Y4 | 6,000 | 7,922 | 1,922 | 0 | 200,000 |
| **Totals** | **48,000** | **61,465** | **13,465** | | |

*Notes:* Interest expense = beginning carrying amount × 4% (rounded). Discount amort. = interest expense − cash. Final period plugs amortization so unamortized discount = 0. Total interest expense ($61,465) exceeds total cash interest ($48,000) by the original discount ($13,465).

**e. Year 1 interest / amortization JEs**

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| June 30, Y1 | Interest Expense | 7,461 | |
| | Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,461 |
| | Cash | | 6,000 |

*Check:* 7,461 = 1,461 + 6,000.

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Dec. 31, Y1 | Interest Expense | 7,520 | |
| | Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,520 |
| | Cash | | 6,000 |

*Check:* 7,520 = 1,520 + 6,000.

**f. Presentation (Dec. 31, Year 1)**  

**Balance sheet (excerpt)**  
Long-term liabilities:  
Bonds payable — $200,000  
Less: Discount on bonds payable — 10,484  
**Bonds payable, net — $189,516**

**Income statement (Year 1)**  
Interest expense — $(14,981)$  [= 7,461 + 7,520]

**Key insight:** Bond *types* and *indenture features* (face, stated rate, payment dates, convertibility) are fixed in the bond agreement; the *market rate* sets the issue price. When market > stated, the issuer records a **discount** and recognizes extra interest expense over the term via amortization.

### Q3 — CORE — Period-end adjusting JE and maturity settlement
**LO:** LO 16-1  
**Concept:** Period-end interest accrual (payment dates ≠ reporting date) + maturity derecognition  
**Scenario:**  
**Riverbend Packaging** issued **$160,000** of **5% term corporate bonds** at **face** on **January 1, Year 1** (stated rate = market rate). Interest is payable **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The bonds mature on **December 31, Year 3**. Riverbend prepares **quarterly** financial statements.

**Required:**  
a. Identify bond type(s) and list face value, maturity date, stated rate per interest period, interest payment dates, and selling price.  
b. Record the **January 1, Year 1** issuance entry.  
c. Record the **March 31, Year 1** period-end **adjusting** entry for accrued bond interest (no prior accruals this quarter).  
d. Record the **June 30, Year 1** cash interest payment (reverse/clear the Q1 accrual and recognize Q2 interest).  
e. Record the **December 31, Year 3** maturity settlement, assuming the final semiannual interest payment has **already** been recorded separately.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Types / features**  
- Types: **Corporate**, **term** (and at face, so no premium/discount feature in measurement).  
- Face value: **$160,000**  
- Maturity date: **December 31, Year 3**  
- Stated rate per interest period: **2.5%** (5% ÷ 2)  
- Interest payment dates: **June 30 and December 31**  
- Selling price: **$160,000** (market = stated)

Semiannual cash interest = $160,000 × 2.5% = **$4,000**.

**b. Issuance at face**

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Jan. 1, Y1 | Cash | 160,000 | |
| | Bonds Payable | | 160,000 |

*Check:* 160,000 = 160,000.

**c. Period-end adjusting JE (3 months of interest)**  
Accrued interest = $160,000 × 5% × 3/12 = **$2,000**  
(equivalently $4,000 × 3/6).

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Mar. 31, Y1 | Interest Expense | 2,000 | |
| | Interest Payable | | 2,000 |

*Check:* 2,000 = 2,000.

**d. June 30 cash interest (clear accrual + remaining 3 months)**

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| June 30, Y1 | Interest Payable | 2,000 | |
| | Interest Expense | 2,000 | |
| | Cash | | 4,000 |

*Check:* 2,000 + 2,000 = 4,000.  
Net interest expense for the full half-year remains $4,000 ($2,000 in Q1 + $2,000 in Q2).

**e. Maturity settlement (principal only; final interest already booked)**

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Dec. 31, Y3 | Bonds Payable | 160,000 | |
| | Cash | | 160,000 |

*Check:* 160,000 = 160,000.

*(If final interest were combined with maturity on Dec. 31, Y3: Dr Interest Expense 4,000; Dr Bonds Payable 160,000; Cr Cash 164,000.)*

**Key insight:** Indenture **interest payment dates** may not match **reporting dates**. Accrue contractual interest to period-end with Interest Payable; pay the full contractual cash amount on the payment date. At maturity, the issuer pays face and **derecognizes** Bonds Payable.

### Q3B — CORE alternate (continuing discount) — Mid-period accrual on discount bonds
**LO:** LO 16-1  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE with effective-interest discount amortization  
**Scenario:**  
Use **Cedar Ridge Works** facts from Q1. After recording both Year 1 semiannual entries, Cedar Ridge prepares **monthly** statements. Record the **January 31, Year 2** adjusting entry for one month of bond interest and discount amortization (prorate the next full semiannual amounts from the Q1 schedule: June 30, Year 2 row).

**Answer key:**  
From Q1 schedule, June 30, Y2 full period: Interest expense **$7,581**; discount amort. **$1,581**; cash interest **$6,000**.

One month (1/6 of the semiannual period):  
- Interest expense = $7,581 × 1/6 = **$1,264** (rounded)  
- Discount amortization = $1,581 × 1/6 = **$264** (rounded)  
- Interest payable = $6,000 × 1/6 = **$1,000**  

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Jan. 31, Y2 | Interest Expense | 1,264 | |
| | Discount on Bonds Payable | | 264 |
| | Interest Payable | | 1,000 |

*Check:* 1,264 = 264 + 1,000.

**Key insight:** When bonds are at a discount, period-end accruals allocate **both** cash-interest accrual and **discount amortization** (effective interest), not only the stated cash portion.

### Q4 — MC (classification / features)
**LO:** LO 16-1  
**Concept:** Classify bond types from indenture facts  

**Question 1:**  
Oak & Pine Transit issued bonds with these terms: issued by a private corporation; bondholders have a first claim on specified rolling-stock assets; principal is repaid in equal annual installments over 10 years; the issuer may retire the remaining principal early at 101 after year 5. Which set of bond types is **most complete**?

- A) Corporate, debenture, term, convertible  
- B) Corporate, secured, serial, callable  
- C) Municipal, secured, term, convertible  
- D) Corporate, secured, term, callable  

**Answer:** **B.** Corporate (private corporation issuer); **secured** (lien on rolling stock); **serial** (principal in installments, not a single maturity); **callable** (issuer early-retirement option). Not debenture (those are unsecured), not municipal, not purely term, and convertibility is not described.

---

**LO:** LO 16-1  
**Concept:** Indenture features vs market-determined features  

**Question 2:**  
Which feature is **not** fixed in the bond indenture and instead depends on market conditions at issuance?

- A) Face (maturity) value  
- B) Stated (coupon) rate and interest payment dates  
- C) Maturity date  
- D) Market (effective) rate and resulting selling price  

**Answer:** **D.** Face value, stated rate, payment dates, and maturity are contractual indenture features. The **market rate** (and therefore selling price, premium, or discount) is determined by market conditions at the issue date and can differ from the stated rate.

### QL — CORE long path — Full life-cycle recap (discount bonds to maturity)
**LO:** LO 16-1  
**Concept:** Types/features → issuance → schedule → final interest → maturity settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Lumen Trail Co.** issues **$100,000**, **4%**, **2-year** corporate **debenture term** bonds on January 1, Year 1. Interest payable annually on December 31. Market rate **6%**. Effective interest method.

**Required:**  
a. Bond types and features (face, maturity, stated rate per period, market rate per period, issue price).  
b. Issuance JE.  
c. Full amortization schedule (annual).  
d. Dec. 31, Y1 and Dec. 31, Y2 interest entries.  
e. Maturity principal JE on Dec. 31, Y2 (after interest entry).

**Answer key:**  

**a.** Types: corporate, debenture, term.  
Face $100,000; maturity Dec. 31, Y2; stated rate per period **4%**; market rate per period **6%**; annual cash interest **$4,000**.  
Exact PV:  
4,000 × (1 − 1.06^−2)/0.06 = **7,333.57**  
100,000 / 1.06^2 = **88,999.64**  
Price = **$96,333** (rounded to nearest dollar); discount = **$3,667**.

**b. Issuance**

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Jan. 1, Y1 | Cash | 96,333 | |
| | Discount on Bonds Payable | 3,667 | |
| | Bonds Payable | | 100,000 |

*Check:* 96,333 + 3,667 = 100,000.

**c. Schedule (annual)**  

| Date | Cash | Interest exp. (6% × beg. carry) | Discount amort. | Unamort. disc. | Carry |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan. 1, Y1 | — | — | — | 3,667 | 96,333 |
| Dec. 31, Y1 | 4,000 | 5,780 | 1,780 | 1,887 | 98,113 |
| Dec. 31, Y2 | 4,000 | 5,887 | 1,887 | 0 | 100,000 |
| Totals | 8,000 | 11,667 | 3,667 | | |

(Y1: 96,333 × 6% = 5,779.98 → **5,780**; Y2 plugs amort. **1,887** so carry → 100,000; int. exp. = 4,000 + 1,887 = **5,887**.)

**d. Interest entries**

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Dec. 31, Y1 | Interest Expense | 5,780 | |
| | Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,780 |
| | Cash | | 4,000 |

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Dec. 31, Y2 | Interest Expense | 5,887 | |
| | Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,887 |
| | Cash | | 4,000 |

**e. Maturity principal**

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Dec. 31, Y2 | Bonds Payable | 100,000 | |
| | Cash | | 100,000 |

**Key insight:** From LO 16-1 identification of types/features through issuance pricing, the discount is fully amortized by maturity so carrying amount equals face when principal is settled.
