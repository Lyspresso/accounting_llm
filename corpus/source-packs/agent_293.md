# Agent 293 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-6

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Explain how a change in tax rates impacts deferred taxes  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Enacted rates only:** remeasure deferred taxes using rates signed into law; do not adjust for anticipated or proposed rates
- **Match rates to years of reversal:** schedule each future taxable/deductible amount × the enacted rate in the year it reverses
- **Initial recognition JE:** Income Tax Expense / Deferred Tax Asset or Liability / Income Tax Payable (current rate × taxable income)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** roll temporary-difference balances and recompute ending DTA/DTL at enacted future rates
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** remeasure existing DTA/DTL when a rate change is enacted; effect is recognized in income tax expense in the period of enactment
- **Rate-cut economics:** DTA write-down → higher tax expense; DTL write-down → lower tax expense (benefit)
- **Settlement / reverse JE:** when the temporary difference reverses, deferred tax is drawn down as current taxes absorb the taxable/deductible amount
- **Classification / disclosure:** deferred taxes noncurrent; rate-change effects appear in the statutory-to-effective rate reconciliation

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-rate DTL schedule, initial tax JE, rate-change period-end adjust, reversal settlement, presentation
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Match enacted future rates to taxable temporary differences by year of reversal; initial recognition; period-end remeasurement when rates change; settlement as differences reverse; BS and rate-reconciliation presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedar Peak Outfitters Inc.** (calendar-year) reports pretax GAAP income of **$240,000** for Year 1. Its only temporary difference is installment gross profit recognized in full for financial reporting and taxed when cash is collected. At December 31, Year 1, uncollected installment gross profit is **$60,000**, expected to reverse as follows: **Year 2 $20,000; Year 3 $25,000; Year 4 $15,000**. Beginning deferred tax balances are zero.

Enacted rates known at December 31, Year 1:  
- Year 1 (current) and Year 2: **21%**  
- Year 3: **25%**  
- Year 4: **30%**

**Year 2 facts:** Pretax GAAP income **$200,000**. Cash collections reverse **$20,000** of the Year 1 installment difference (no new installment originations). Current Year 2 enacted rate remains **21%**. Late in Year 2, a new law is enacted that sets the rates for **Year 3 and Year 4 at 28% each** (replacing the prior 25% and 30% schedules).

**Year 3 facts:** Pretax GAAP income **$150,000**. Collections reverse **$25,000**. Current Year 3 rate is **28%**. Remaining difference **$15,000** will reverse in Year 4 at **28%**.

**Year 4 facts:** Pretax GAAP income **$100,000**. Final **$15,000** reverses. Current rate **28%**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **schedule to compute the deferred tax liability** at December 31, Year 1.  
b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 income tax journal entry** (initial recognition / period-end tax JE).  
c. Prepare a **roll-forward schedule** of the temporary difference and DTL from Dec. 31, Year 1 through Dec. 31, Year 2 (show rate-change impact separately).  
d. **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **December 31, Year 2 period-end income tax journal entry**, including the effect of the enacted rate change.  
e. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** and **December 31, Year 4** income tax journal entries (reversal / settlement of deferred tax).  
f. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet excerpt** for deferred taxes and a **statutory-to-effective rate reconciliation** for Year 1.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Schedule — Deferred tax liability, Dec. 31, Year 1**

| Reversal year | Future taxable amount | Enacted rate in year of reversal | Deferred tax liability |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 2 | $20,000 | 21% | $4,200 |
| Year 3 | 25,000 | 25% | 6,250 |
| Year 4 | 15,000 | 30% | 4,500 |
| **Total** | **$60,000** | | **$14,950** |

**b. December 31, Year 1 — initial recognition / period-end tax JE**

Taxable income = pretax GAAP − originating installment gross profit not yet taxed  
\(= 240{,}000 - 60{,}000 = \mathbf{\$180{,}000}\)  
Income tax payable \(= 180{,}000 \times 21\% = \mathbf{\$37{,}800}\)  
Deferred tax expense \(= \) ending DTL \(= \mathbf{\$14{,}950}\)  
Income tax expense \(= 37{,}800 + 14{,}950 = \mathbf{\$52{,}750}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 52,750 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | | 14,950 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 37,800 |
| *Record current and deferred income taxes — multi-rate DTL* | | |

**Check:** Dr 52,750 = Cr 14,950 + 37,800. **Balanced.**

**c. Temporary difference & DTL roll-forward through Year 2**

| Item | Taxable TD | Rate / notes | DTL |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Dec. 31, Y1 balance | $60,000 | multi-rate (see a) | $14,950 |
| Y2 reversal (collection) | (20,000) | was measured @ 21% → release | (4,200) |
| Subtotal before rate change | 40,000 | Y3 @25% = 6,250; Y4 @30% = 4,500 | 10,750 |
| Rate change (enacted Y3 & Y4 → 28%) | — | Y3: 25k×(28%−25%)= **+750**; Y4: 15k×(28%−30%)= **−300** | +450 |
| **Dec. 31, Y2 balance** | **$40,000** | 25k×28% + 15k×28% | **$11,200** |

Net DTL decrease in Year 2: \(14{,}950 - 11{,}200 = \mathbf{\$3{,}750}\)  
\(= \) reversal release \(4{,}200\) \(-\) net rate remeasurement increase \(450\).

**d. December 31, Year 2 — period-end tax JE (emphasis: rate change)**

Taxable income \(= 200{,}000 + 20{,}000\) (prior installment now taxable) \(= \mathbf{\$220{,}000}\)  
Income tax payable \(= 220{,}000 \times 21\% = \mathbf{\$46{,}200}\)  
Required ending DTL \(= \mathbf{\$11{,}200}\); beginning DTL \(14{,}950\); **debit DTL \(3{,}750\)**  
Income tax expense \(= 46{,}200 - 3{,}750 = \mathbf{\$42{,}450}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 42,450 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 3,750 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 46,200 |
| *Current tax @ 21%; reverse DTL for collection; remeasure remaining TD at newly enacted 28%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 42,450 + 3,750 = Cr 46,200. **Balanced.**

Optional two-step view of the **rate-change component only** (after recording deferred taxes as if prior future rates still applied, ending DTL would be \(10{,}750\)):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 450 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | | 450 |
| *Period-end remeasurement for enacted rate change on remaining TD* | | |

(Net with the \(4{,}200\) reversal release, overall DTL still decreases \(3{,}750\).)

**e. Settlement / reverse years**

**December 31, Year 3**  
Taxable income \(= 150{,}000 + 25{,}000 = 175{,}000\)  
Payable \(= 175{,}000 \times 28\% = \mathbf{\$49{,}000}\)  
Ending DTL \(= 15{,}000 \times 28\% = \mathbf{\$4{,}200}\); decrease \(11{,}200 - 4{,}200 = \mathbf{\$7{,}000}\)  
Expense \(= 49{,}000 - 7{,}000 = \mathbf{\$42{,}000}\) \((= 150{,}000 \times 28\%)\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 42,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 7,000 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 49,000 |

**Check:** Dr 42,000 + 7,000 = Cr 49,000. **Balanced.**

**December 31, Year 4 — full settlement of remaining DTL**  
Taxable income \(= 100{,}000 + 15{,}000 = 115{,}000\)  
Payable \(= 115{,}000 \times 28\% = \mathbf{\$32{,}200}\)  
Ending DTL \(= \$0\); decrease \(\mathbf{\$4{,}200}\)  
Expense \(= 32{,}200 - 4{,}200 = \mathbf{\$28{,}000}\) \((= 100{,}000 \times 28\%)\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 28,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 4,200 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 32,200 |

**Check:** Dr 28,000 + 4,200 = Cr 32,200. **Balanced.** DTL life cycle closed.

**f. Presentation & disclosure (Year 1)**

**Balance sheet excerpt — Dec. 31, Year 1**  
Noncurrent liabilities: Deferred tax liability — **$14,950**  
(ASC 740: deferred tax assets/liabilities classified as noncurrent.)

**Rate reconciliation — Year 1**

| | Dollars | Percent of pretax |
|---|---:|---:|
| Tax at statutory rate (\(240{,}000 \times 21\%\)) | $50,400 | 21.00% |
| Effect of future enacted rates different from current rate on deferred taxes* | 2,350 | 0.98% |
| **Income tax expense / effective rate** | **$52,750** | **21.98%** |

\* \(14{,}950 - (60{,}000 \times 21\%) = 14{,}950 - 12{,}600 = 2{,}350\)

**Key insight:** Deferred taxes are always measured at the **enacted rate(s) in the year(s) of reversal**, not necessarily the current statutory rate. When those future rates are revised by new legislation, remeasure immediately at the **enactment date** through income tax expense.

### Q2 — CORE number variant twin — Multi-rate DTL, rate change period-end JE, settlement
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin of multi-rate deferred tax liability measurement, period-end rate-change remeasurement, and reverse-year settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Mesa Verde Logistics Co.** (calendar-year) reports pretax GAAP income of **$360,000** in Year 1. Excess tax depreciation creates a **taxable temporary difference of $90,000** (book basis of equipment exceeds tax basis by $90,000). Expected reversals: **Year 2 $30,000; Year 3 $40,000; Year 4 $20,000**. Beginning deferred tax balances are zero.

Enacted rates at December 31, Year 1:  
- Year 1 (current) and Year 2: **25%**  
- Year 3: **30%**  
- Year 4: **35%**

**Year 2:** Pretax GAAP income **$280,000**. Temporary difference reverses **$30,000** (tax depreciation catch-up / book depreciation pattern); no new originating difference. Current rate remains **25%**. Late Year 2, law is enacted setting **Year 3 and Year 4 rates at 28% each**.

**Year 3:** Pretax GAAP **$220,000**; reverse **$40,000**; current rate **28%**.  
**Year 4:** Pretax GAAP **$190,000**; reverse remaining **$20,000**; current rate **28%**.

**Required:**  
a. Schedule the DTL at Dec. 31, Year 1 and record the Year 1 income tax JE.  
b. Compute ending DTL at Dec. 31, Year 2 after reversal and rate change; record the Year 2 period-end tax JE.  
c. Record Year 3 and Year 4 income tax JEs (settlement of DTL).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1 schedule and JE**

| Reversal year | Future taxable amount | Rate | DTL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 2 | $30,000 | 25% | $7,500 |
| Year 3 | 40,000 | 30% | 12,000 |
| Year 4 | 20,000 | 35% | 7,000 |
| **Total** | **$90,000** | | **$26,500** |

Taxable income \(= 360{,}000 - 90{,}000 = \mathbf{\$270{,}000}\)  
Payable \(= 270{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$67{,}500}\)  
Expense \(= 67{,}500 + 26{,}500 = \mathbf{\$94{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 94,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | | 26,500 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 67,500 |

**Check:** Dr 94,000 = Cr 26,500 + 67,500. **Balanced.**

**b. Year 2 period-end (emphasis)**

Remaining TD after $30,000 reversal: **$60,000** (Y3 $40k; Y4 $20k).  
Ending DTL at new rates: \(40{,}000 \times 28\% + 20{,}000 \times 28\% = 11{,}200 + 5{,}600 = \mathbf{\$16{,}800}\).

| Component of Δ DTL | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Release for Y2 reversal (\(30{,}000 \times 25\%\)) | $(7,500) |
| Rate change Y3 (\(40{,}000 \times (28\%-30\%)\)) | (800) |
| Rate change Y4 (\(20{,}000 \times (28\%-35\%)\)) | (1,400) |
| **Total DTL decrease** | **$(9,700)** |

Beg DTL 26,500 − 9,700 = end **16,800** ✓  

Taxable income \(= 280{,}000 + 30{,}000 = 310{,}000\)  
Payable \(= 310{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$77{,}500}\)  
Expense \(= 77{,}500 - 9{,}700 = \mathbf{\$67{,}800}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 67,800 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 9,700 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 77,500 |

**Check:** Dr 67,800 + 9,700 = Cr 77,500. **Balanced.**

**c. Settlement years**

**Year 3:** Taxable income \(= 220{,}000 + 40{,}000 = 260{,}000\); payable \(= 260{,}000 \times 28\% = \mathbf{\$72{,}800}\).  
Ending DTL \(= 20{,}000 \times 28\% = \mathbf{\$5{,}600}\); DTL decrease \(16{,}800 - 5{,}600 = \mathbf{\$11{,}200}\).  
Expense \(= 72{,}800 - 11{,}200 = \mathbf{\$61{,}600}\) \((= 220{,}000 \times 28\%)\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 61,600 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 11,200 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 72,800 |

**Check:** Dr 61,600 + 11,200 = Cr 72,800. **Balanced.**

**Year 4:** Taxable income \(= 190{,}000 + 20{,}000 = 210{,}000\); payable \(= 210{,}000 \times 28\% = \mathbf{\$58{,}800}\).  
Ending DTL \(= \$0\); DTL decrease \(\mathbf{\$5{,}600}\).  
Expense \(= 58{,}800 - 5{,}600 = \mathbf{\$53{,}200}\) \((= 190{,}000 \times 28\%)\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 53,200 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 5,600 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 58,800 |

**Check:** Dr 53,200 + 5,600 = Cr 58,800. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Same mechanics as Q1 with all new numbers — schedule by year of reversal, remeasure at enactment, and settle deferred tax as the underlying temporary difference reverses.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — DTA rate-cut remeasurement (period-end emphasis) and warranty settlement
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE for a decrease in enacted rates on an existing deferred tax asset; income-statement impact of DTA revaluation; settlement when the deductible temporary difference reverses  
**Scenario:**  
**Ridgeway Appliance Co.** (calendar-year) records a **warranty expense accrual of $80,000** in Year 1 that is not deductible for tax until claims are paid. The entire deductible temporary difference is expected to reverse in **Year 2**. Pretax GAAP income for Year 1 is **$150,000**. The current Year 1 enacted tax rate is **35%**. On **December 31, Year 1**, a new tax law is enacted reducing the rate to **21% effective January 1, Year 2**. Beginning deferred tax balances are zero.

**Year 2:** Pretax GAAP income **$200,000**. Warranty claims of **$80,000** are paid and become tax-deductible. Enacted rate for Year 2 is **21%**.

**Required:**  
a. Compute taxable income and income tax payable for Year 1.  
b. Schedule the deferred tax asset **before** and **after** the rate change; quantify the remeasurement.  
c. **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end income tax journal entry** (combined). Also show a **standalone rate-change adjusting entry** as if DTA had first been recorded at the old rate.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2** entry when the warranty difference fully settles.  
e. Briefly explain the **classification** of the rate-change effect in the Year 1 effective tax rate reconciliation.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1 taxable income and payable**  
Taxable income \(= \) pretax GAAP \(+ \) nondeductible warranty accrual \(= 150{,}000 + 80{,}000 = \mathbf{\$230{,}000}\)  
Income tax payable \(= 230{,}000 \times 35\% = \mathbf{\$80{,}500}\)

**b. DTA schedule and remeasurement**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Deductible temporary difference (warranty liability) | $80,000 |
| DTA if measured at current 35% rate | 28,000 |
| Enacted rate in year of reversal (Y2) | 21% |
| **Required ending DTA** (\(80{,}000 \times 21\%\)) | **$16,800** |
| **Rate-change write-down** (\(28{,}000 - 16{,}800\)) | **$11,200** |

**c. December 31, Year 1 period-end JEs (emphasis)**

Combined entry (measure DTA at enacted future rate from the start):  
Income tax expense \(= 80{,}500 - 16{,}800 = \mathbf{\$63{,}700}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 63,700 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 16,800 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 80,500 |
| *Current tax @ 35%; DTA @ 21% enacted reversal rate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 63,700 + 16,800 = Cr 80,500. **Balanced.**

Standalone **rate-change adjusting JE** (if DTA was first booked at 35%):

Step 1 — record DTA at old rate (illustrative):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Tax Asset | 28,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 28,000 |

Step 2 — **period-end remeasurement for enacted rate cut**:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 11,200 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 11,200 |
| *Remeasure DTA for rate decrease enacted Dec. 31, Year 1* | | |

Net DTA = 16,800; net deferred tax benefit in expense = 16,800 (same as combined). The **$11,200** debit increases Year 1 tax expense solely because of the rate change.

**d. December 31, Year 2 — settlement of DTA**

Taxable income \(= 200{,}000 - 80{,}000 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}\)  
Payable \(= 120{,}000 \times 21\% = \mathbf{\$25{,}200}\)  
Ending DTA \(= \$0\); reverse remaining DTA \(\mathbf{\$16{,}800}\)  
Expense \(= 25{,}200 + 16{,}800 = \mathbf{\$42{,}000}\) \((= 200{,}000 \times 21\%)\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 42,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 16,800 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 25,200 |

**Check:** Dr 42,000 = Cr 16,800 + 25,200. **Balanced.** Temporary difference and DTA fully settled.

**e. Classification / rate reconciliation (Year 1)**  
Pretax GAAP \(150{,}000 \times 35\% = 52{,}500\) statutory tax.  
Because the warranty deduction will be taken when the statutory rate is **lower**, the DTA is smaller than \(80{,}000 \times 35\%\). The **$11,200** rate-change effect **increases** income tax expense and is disclosed as a reconciling item (effect of enacted rate change / temporary difference reversing at a different rate). Effective tax expense \(63{,}700 / 150{,}000 = 42.47\%\).

| | Dollars | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| Statutory tax (\(150{,}000 \times 35\%\)) | $52,500 | 35.00% |
| Warranty difference reversing at lower future rate (\(80{,}000 \times (35\%-21\%)\)) | 11,200 | 7.47% |
| **Income tax expense / ETR** | **$63,700** | **42.47%** |

**Key insight:** A tax **rate decrease** reduces the value of deferred tax **assets** and is recognized as **higher** income tax expense in the period of enactment — even though the underlying warranty liability did not change.

### Q4 — MC — Rate change effect on expense: DTA vs DTL
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Classification of income-statement impact when enacted rates decrease for a company with a net deferred tax asset versus a net deferred tax liability  
**Question:** On December 31, Year 1, **Harborline Marine Inc.** has a **net deferred tax asset** measured using a 30% enacted rate. On that same date, a new law is enacted that **decreases** the tax rate to 22% for all future years. Separately, **Stonegate Foundry Corp.** has a **net deferred tax liability** measured at 30%, and the same rate decrease to 22% is enacted. Ignoring current-year originating differences, how does each company’s **income tax expense** change solely because of the rate decrease?

- A) Harborline expense decreases; Stonegate expense decreases  
- B) Harborline expense increases; Stonegate expense decreases  
- C) Harborline expense decreases; Stonegate expense increases  
- D) Harborline expense increases; Stonegate expense increases  

**Answer:** **B.**  
A lower enacted rate **reduces DTA** (credit DTA → debit tax expense → **higher** expense for Harborline). A lower rate **reduces DTL** (debit DTL → credit tax expense → **lower** expense / tax benefit for Stonegate). Remeasurement hits net income in the **period of enactment**.

### Q5 — MC — Timing of recognition of a tax rate change
**LO:** LO 18-6  
**Concept:** Classification of when to adjust deferred taxes for a change in tax rates (enactment date)  
**Question:** **Pinecrest BioLabs LLC** expects Congress to reduce the corporate tax rate next year and has already prepared draft calculations of the effect on its deferred tax liability. The bill is **not yet enacted**. Which statement is correct under ASC 740?

- A) Remeasure deferred taxes now using the expected new rate because it is more likely than not the bill will pass.  
- B) Remeasure deferred taxes only when the temporary differences reverse.  
- C) Remeasure deferred taxes at the **date of enactment**; do not adjust for anticipated changes.  
- D) Remeasure deferred taxes prospectively starting in the first year the new rate is effective, with no catch-up in the enactment year if enactment is mid-year.  

**Answer:** **C.**  
Use a new rate **only if enacted into law**. The entire effect of the rate change on existing deferred tax balances is recognized in income tax expense in the **period of enactment**, not when differences later reverse and not based on anticipated legislation.
