# Agent 79 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-3

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Calculate depreciation using group and composite depreciation methods  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Group vs composite:** group = homogeneous (similar) assets; composite = heterogeneous (dissimilar) assets; calculations and journal entries are the same
- **Composite annual depreciation rate** = Total annual straight-line depreciation ÷ Total acquisition cost (round rate to three decimals when required)
- **Composite useful life** = Total depreciable cost ÷ Total annual straight-line depreciation
- **Composite annual depreciation** = Rate × Total acquisition cost (control-account approach)
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize composite (or group) assets at total acquisition cost
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** Dr Depreciation Expense / Cr Accumulated Depreciation (one AD control account)
- **Disposal JE:** no gain or loss recognized; cash + AD plug = original cost removed from asset control account
- **Subsequent measurement:** after non-material addition/disposal, apply **old rate** × **new** total acquisition cost
- Classification/presentation/disclosure: PPE at cost less AD control account; method disclosed (e.g., straight-line composite); gains/losses absorbed in AD

### Q1 — CORE — Composite schedule, rate/life, initial recognition, Year-1 dep, disposal, Year-2 dep
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Composite depreciation schedule; composite rate and useful life; initial recognition JE; period-end composite depreciation JE; disposal with no G/L (AD plug); subsequent dep at old rate × new cost  
**Scenario:**  
**Ridgeway Fabrication LLC** (calendar year) places the following **dissimilar** plant assets into a single composite early in **Year 1** (full-year depreciation in Year 1). All were purchased for cash.

| Asset class | Quantity | Unit acquisition cost | Unit residual value | Unit useful life (years) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CNC presses | 6 | $40,000 | $4,000 | 8 |
| Conveyor lines | 6 | $30,000 | $3,000 | 6 |
| Inspection stations | 15 | $6,000 | $0 | 3 |
| Work tables | 9 | $10,000 | $1,000 | 9 |

On **March 10, Year 2**, Ridgeway sells **one conveyor line** for **$22,000** cash. Management judges the disposal **not material** to the makeup of the composite (continue the original rate).

**Required:**  
a. Complete a **Year 1 composite depreciation schedule** (total acquisition cost, total residual, total annual SL depreciation by class and in total).  
b. Compute the **composite annual depreciation rate** (three decimal places), **composite useful life** (two decimal places), and **Year 1 composite annual depreciation**.  
c. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry for the composite purchase (cash).  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entry for composite depreciation.  
e. Prepare the **March 10, Year 2 disposal** journal entry for the one conveyor line.  
f. Compute and record **Year 2** composite depreciation expense (old rate × revised total acquisition cost).  
g. Briefly state how composite PPE and related AD are **presented/disclosed**, and why **no gain or loss** appears on the Year 2 disposal.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1 composite depreciation schedule**

| Asset class | Qty | Unit cost | Unit residual | Life | Total acquisition cost | Total residual | Total annual SL depreciation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CNC presses | 6 | $40,000 | $4,000 | 8 | $240,000 | $24,000 | ($240,000 − $24,000) / 8 = **$27,000** |
| Conveyor lines | 6 | 30,000 | 3,000 | 6 | 180,000 | 18,000 | ($180,000 − $18,000) / 6 = **27,000** |
| Inspection stations | 15 | 6,000 | 0 | 3 | 90,000 | 0 | $90,000 / 3 = **30,000** |
| Work tables | 9 | 10,000 | 1,000 | 9 | 90,000 | 9,000 | ($90,000 − $9,000) / 9 = **9,000** |
| **Totals** | | | | | **$600,000** | **$51,000** | **$93,000** |

Total depreciable cost = $600,000 − $51,000 = **$549,000**

**b. Rate, life, and Year 1 depreciation**

\[
\text{Composite annual depreciation rate} = \frac{\$93{,}000}{\$600{,}000} = \mathbf{0.155}
\]

\[
\text{Composite useful life} = \frac{\$549{,}000}{\$93{,}000} = \mathbf{5.90\ \text{years}}
\]

\[
\text{Composite annual depreciation (Year 1)} = 0.155 \times \$600{,}000 = \mathbf{\$93{,}000}
\]

(Composite life is **not** a simple average of the unit lives; it weights classes by their contribution to total depreciable cost.)

**c. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (composite purchase)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Machinery and Equipment (composite control) | 600,000 | |
| Cash | | 600,000 |
| *Capitalize heterogeneous plant assets in one composite control account at total acquisition cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 600,000. **Balanced.**

**d. December 31, Year 1 — period-end composite depreciation**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Machinery and Equipment | 93,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Machinery and Equipment | | 93,000 |
| *Composite annual depreciation = rate × total acquisition cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 93,000. **Balanced.**

**e. March 10, Year 2 — disposal of one conveyor line (no G/L)**

Original cost of one conveyor = **$30,000**  
Cash proceeds = **$22,000**  
Accumulated depreciation (plug) = $30,000 − $22,000 = **$8,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 22,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Machinery and Equipment | 8,000 | |
| Machinery and Equipment (composite control) | | 30,000 |
| *Remove asset cost; difference vs cash plugged to AD — no gain or loss recognized* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,000 + 8,000 = Cr 30,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Year 2 composite depreciation**

Revised total acquisition cost = $600,000 − $30,000 = **$570,000**  
(Original rate retained — disposal not material.)

\[
\text{Year 2 composite depreciation} = 0.155 \times \$570{,}000 = \mathbf{\$88{,}350}
\]

*December 31, Year 2 — composite depreciation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Machinery and Equipment | 88,350 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Machinery and Equipment | | 88,350 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 88,350. **Balanced.**

**g. Presentation / disclosure**  
- **Balance sheet:** Machinery and equipment reported at **gross cost** less a **single accumulated depreciation control account** (not unit-by-unit AD).  
- **Notes:** disclose use of the **straight-line composite method** and estimated composite lives/categories as appropriate.  
- **Disposal:** under group/composite methods, **gains and losses are not recognized**; any difference is absorbed in AD so that over time unrecorded gains tend to offset unrecorded losses (ASC 360-10-S99-1 guidance for group/composite fleets).

**Key insight:** Composite (and group) methods replace unit-by-unit tracking with a **rate × cost** control-account system. **Initial recognition** is at total cost; each period’s depreciation is systematic; **disposals** never produce a separate G/L — the AD account is the balancing plug.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Composite twin (different company and numbers)
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: composite schedule, rate/life, initial recognition JE, Year-1 dep, disposal with AD plug, Year-2 dep at old rate × new cost  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Bakery Systems Inc.** (calendar year) combines the following **heterogeneous** production assets into a composite at the start of **Year 1** (full-year depreciation). Purchased for cash.

| Asset class | Quantity | Unit acquisition cost | Unit residual value | Unit useful life (years) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Industrial mixers | 4 | $60,000 | $6,000 | 9 |
| Baking ovens | 4 | $30,000 | $0 | 5 |
| Packaging machines | 6 | $15,000 | $0 | 5 |
| Transport carts | 10 | $3,000 | $0 | 5 |

On **August 20, Year 2**, Summit sells **one baking oven** for **$11,000** cash. The disposal is **not material**; continue the original composite rate.

**Required:**  
a. Complete the composite schedule (totals and annual SL depreciation by class).  
b. Compute composite rate (three decimals), composite useful life (two decimals), and Year 1 depreciation.  
c. Journal entries: (1) **initial recognition** of the composite, (2) **Year 1** depreciation, (3) **disposal** of the oven, (4) **Year 2** depreciation.  
d. Show Dr = Cr for every entry.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Composite schedule**

| Asset class | Qty | Total acquisition cost | Total residual | Total annual SL depreciation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Industrial mixers | 4 | $240,000 | $24,000 | ($240,000 − $24,000) / 9 = **$24,000** |
| Baking ovens | 4 | 120,000 | 0 | $120,000 / 5 = **24,000** |
| Packaging machines | 6 | 90,000 | 0 | $90,000 / 5 = **18,000** |
| Transport carts | 10 | 30,000 | 0 | $30,000 / 5 = **6,000** |
| **Totals** | | **$480,000** | **$24,000** | **$72,000** |

Total depreciable cost = $480,000 − $24,000 = **$456,000**

**b. Rate, life, Year 1 depreciation**

\[
\text{Rate} = \frac{\$72{,}000}{\$480{,}000} = \mathbf{0.150}
\]

\[
\text{Composite useful life} = \frac{\$456{,}000}{\$72{,}000} = \mathbf{6.33\ \text{years}}
\]

\[
\text{Year 1 composite depreciation} = 0.150 \times \$480{,}000 = \mathbf{\$72{,}000}
\]

**c–d. Journal entries (all balanced)**

*(1) January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment (composite control) | 480,000 | |
| Cash | | 480,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 480,000.

*(2) December 31, Year 1 — period-end depreciation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Equipment | 72,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 72,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 72,000.

*(3) August 20, Year 2 — dispose of one oven (cost $30,000; cash $11,000; AD plug $19,000)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 11,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 19,000 | |
| Equipment (composite control) | | 30,000 |

**Check:** Dr 11,000 + 19,000 = Cr 30,000. **No gain/loss.**

*(4) December 31, Year 2 — composite depreciation on revised cost*

Revised cost = $480,000 − $30,000 = **$450,000**  
Depreciation = 0.150 × $450,000 = **$67,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Equipment | 67,500 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 67,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 67,500.

**Key insight:** Changing every input (costs, lives, residual, disposal proceeds) still follows the same pipeline: **build schedule → rate/life → capitalize total cost → dep = rate × cost → dispose with AD plug → reapply old rate to new cost**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Group depreciation (homogeneous fleet): schedule, dep JE, multi-unit disposal
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Group depreciation for similar assets; group rate and life; period-end adjusting JE; disposal of multiple units with AD plug; subsequent group depreciation  
**Scenario:**  
**Northline Logistics Co.** acquires a **homogeneous fleet** of **20 identical delivery vans** on **January 1, Year 1** for cash (full-year depreciation).

| Fact | Amount |
|---|---|
| Unit cost | $40,000 |
| Unit residual value | $4,000 |
| Estimated useful life | 5 years |
| Quantity | 20 vans |
| Depreciation system | **Group** (similar assets) |

On **April 5, Year 2**, Northline sells **3 vans** for **$18,000 cash each** ($54,000 total). The retirement is **not material** to the group’s overall composition; retain the original group rate.

**Required:**  
a. Compute total acquisition cost, total residual, total depreciable cost, **annual group depreciation**, **group depreciation rate** (three decimals), and **group useful life**.  
b. Prepare the **initial recognition** JE and the **December 31, Year 1** group depreciation JE.  
c. Prepare the **April 5, Year 2** multi-unit disposal JE.  
d. Compute and record **Year 2** group depreciation.  
e. Explain in one sentence how **group** differs from **composite** (classification concept).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Group measures**

| Item | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Total acquisition cost | 20 × $40,000 | **$800,000** |
| Total residual | 20 × $4,000 | **$80,000** |
| Total depreciable cost | $800,000 − $80,000 | **$720,000** |
| Annual group depreciation | $720,000 / 5 | **$144,000** |
| Group depreciation rate | $144,000 / $800,000 | **0.180** |
| Group useful life | $720,000 / $144,000 | **5.00 years** |

(For identical assets, group life equals each unit’s useful life.)

**b. Initial recognition and Year 1 depreciation**

*January 1, Year 1 — purchase fleet*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Delivery Equipment (group control) | 800,000 | |
| Cash | | 800,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 800,000.

*December 31, Year 1 — group depreciation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Delivery Equipment | 144,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Equipment | | 144,000 |
| *0.180 × $800,000 = $144,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 144,000.

**c. April 5, Year 2 — dispose of 3 vans**

Cost removed = 3 × $40,000 = **$120,000**  
Cash = 3 × $18,000 = **$54,000**  
AD plug = $120,000 − $54,000 = **$66,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 54,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Equipment | 66,000 | |
| Delivery Equipment (group control) | | 120,000 |
| *No gain or loss; difference absorbed in AD* | | |

**Check:** Dr 54,000 + 66,000 = Cr 120,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Year 2 group depreciation**

Revised group cost = $800,000 − $120,000 = **$680,000**  
Depreciation = 0.180 × $680,000 = **$122,400**

*December 31, Year 2*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Delivery Equipment | 122,400 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Delivery Equipment | | 122,400 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 122,400.

**e. Group vs composite**  
**Group** depreciation combines **homogeneous/similar** assets (e.g., a fleet of like vans); **composite** depreciation combines **heterogeneous/dissimilar** assets (e.g., presses, conveyors, and tables) — **computations and JEs are otherwise identical**.

**Key insight:** Group method is the practical fleet application of the same control-account logic: average rate on total cost, and **retirements never trigger G/L** — only cost removal and an AD plug.

### Q4 — MC — Classification / method treatment (composite disposal)
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Classification of disposal accounting under group/composite methods (no separate G/L)  
**Question:**  
Under the **composite (or group) depreciation method**, when an individual asset is sold before the end of the composite life for cash less than its original cost, the company typically:

- A) Recognizes a loss equal to original cost minus cash proceeds.  
- B) Recognizes a gain or loss equal to cash proceeds minus the asset’s carrying amount computed with unit-level accumulated depreciation.  
- C) Debits Cash for proceeds, credits the asset control account for original cost, and plugs **Accumulated Depreciation** for the difference (no gain or loss).  
- D) Leaves the asset cost on the books and continues depreciating it until the composite life ends.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Group/composite methods maintain cost by class/control account and a single AD control account. On disposal, remove original cost, record cash, and balance the entry through AD so that **unrecorded gains and losses offset within AD over time**. Unit-level book value and separate G/L are not used.

### Q5 — MC — Group vs composite classification
**LO:** LO 12-3  
**Concept:** Distinguishing group depreciation from composite depreciation by asset homogeneity  
**Question:**  
Which statement correctly classifies the two combining methods?

- A) Group depreciation is for **dissimilar** assets; composite is for **similar** assets; the rate formulas differ.  
- B) Group depreciation is for **similar (homogeneous)** assets; composite is for **dissimilar (heterogeneous)** assets; calculations and journal entries are the **same**.  
- C) Only composite depreciation is allowed under U.S. GAAP; group depreciation is a tax-only method.  
- D) Both methods require recognition of gains and losses on every partial disposal.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Textbook LO 12-3: group = homogeneous; composite = heterogeneous; **identical calculations and journal entries**. Gains/losses on partial disposals are generally **not** recognized under either method.
