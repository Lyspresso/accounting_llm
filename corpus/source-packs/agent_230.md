# Agent 230 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-6

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Explain liability, commitment, and contingency disclosures and analyses using liquidity ratios  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** accrue a loss contingency when the loss is **probable** and **reasonably estimable** (ASC 450-20); if a range and a better estimate exists, accrue that amount; if no better estimate, accrue the **minimum** of the range
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-year **warranty liability rollforward** (beginning + provisions − claims = ending) and multi-period **contingency liability rollforward** (beginning + additional provisions − settlements/payments = ending), as illustrated by product-warranty and program liability reconciliations in the chapter
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** year-end warranty true-up; year-end contingency accrual
- **Settlement / maturity JE:** remove accrued contingency liability against cash; difference is gain or loss (or remeasure before issuance if a recognized subsequent event)
- **Commitments vs contingencies:** future commitments (plant acquisition, unused letters of credit, working-capital covenants) are generally **disclosed, not accrued** until a present obligation exists; contingencies are accrued, disclosed only, or neither based on likelihood and estimability
- **Disclosure content:** nature of contingency + estimate/range of possible loss (or that estimate cannot be made); ASC 440 commitment items; SEC 5% current-liability composition; balance-sheet line **“Commitments and Contingencies”** pointing to notes; warranty tabular reconciliation (ASC 460)
- **Liquidity ratios:** current ratio = CA ÷ CL; quick ratio = (Cash + marketable securities + receivables) ÷ CL; multi-year ratio schedule and impact of accruals / reclassifications on short-term solvency measures

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-year warranty schedule, litigation cycle, disclosures & liquidity (emphasis: subsequent measurement schedule)
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for multi-year warranty liability; initial recognition of probable litigation; period-end warranty and contingency accruals; settlement JE; commitment/contingency disclosures; current and quick ratios before/after Year-2 accruals  
**Scenario:**  
**Silverpine Fitness Equipment Co.** (calendar-year) sells fitness machines with assurance-type warranties. Management prepares a multi-year warranty liability rollforward and evaluates litigation contingencies, commitments, and liquidity at December 31, **Year 2**. Statements for Year 2 will be issued March 12, Year 3.

**Warranty facts (assurance-type; multi-year subsequent measurement):**

| Year | Product sales | Estimated warranty cost rate | Actual warranty claims paid in cash during year |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $4,800,000 | 2.5% of sales | $105,000 |
| 2 | $5,400,000 | 2.5% of sales | $118,000 |
| 3 | $5,700,000 | 2.0% of sales | $122,000 |

Warranty liability, **January 1, Year 1** (beginning balance): **$52,000**.

**Company policy:** During each year, cash claims are debited to **Warranty Expense** as incurred. At each year-end, Silverpine records an adjusting entry so that **ending warranty liability = beginning warranty liability + estimated warranty cost for that year’s sales − claims paid that year**. Total warranty expense for a year equals estimated warranty cost for that year’s sales.

**Litigation (Year 2):** In Year 2 a customer filed suit alleging injury from a defective machine. Outside counsel concludes a loss is **probable**. Estimated range of loss: **$120,000–$250,000**; the **best estimate** within the range is **$160,000**. Nothing has been recorded yet at December 31, Year 2.

**Settlement (Year 3):** On March 5, Year 3 (before Year-2 statements are issued), the suit settles for cash of **$175,000**. Counsel confirms the settlement relates to conditions existing at December 31, Year 2. For the instructional Year-2 initial-recognition and ratio parts below, first record the **$160,000** best-estimate accrual; then address settlement / remeasurement separately.

**Commitments at December 31, Year 2 (not on books):**
- Unused bank letter of credit: **$600,000**
- Noncancelable contract to acquire a production plant expansion for **$1,500,000** (closing expected May Year 3)

**Unadjusted balances at December 31, Year 2** (after recording Year-2 warranty *claims* as Warranty Expense, but **before** the Year-2 warranty true-up and **before** the litigation accrual). Warranty liability on the books is still the **January 1, Year 2** balance.

| Current assets | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash and cash equivalents | $380,000 |
| Short-term marketable securities | 140,000 |
| Accounts receivable (net) | 920,000 |
| Inventory | 1,650,000 |
| Prepaid expenses | 110,000 |
| **Total current assets** | **$3,200,000** |

| Current liabilities | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Accounts payable | $710,000 |
| Accrued expenses (payroll, interest, taxes) | 390,000 |
| Short-term notes payable | 450,000 |
| Unearned revenue | 180,000 |
| Warranty liability (Jan 1, Year 2 balance still on books) | 67,000 |
| **Total current liabilities (unadjusted)** | **$1,797,000** |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare a complete **three-year subsequent-measurement schedule** for the **warranty liability** (Years 1–3): beginning balance, provision (estimated cost for that year’s sales), claims paid, and ending balance. Verify Year-1 and Year-2 ending balances.  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 2 period-end adjusting JE** for the warranty true-up (using the Year-2 row of the schedule).  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 2 initial recognition JE** for the litigation loss contingency.  
d. Prepare a **contingency liability subsequent-measurement schedule** from Year 2 through settlement in Year 3 (beginning, provisions / remeasurements, payments, ending). Then record the **March 5, Year 3 settlement JE** against the $160,000 Year-2 accrual when cash of $175,000 is paid. Briefly state how Year-2 issued statements would treat the $175,000 settlement if it is a **recognized subsequent event**.  
e. **Classification / disclosure:** For each of (i) litigation accrual, (ii) warranty liability, (iii) plant expansion commitment, and (iv) unused letter of credit—state whether the item is **accrued**, **disclosed only**, both, or neither, and note the core disclosure content.  
f. Compute the **current ratio** and **quick ratio** (1) on the **unadjusted** December 31, Year 2 balances and (2) **after** the Year-2 warranty true-up and litigation accrual in (b)–(c). Briefly analyze the effect on short-term liquidity measures.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Subsequent measurement schedule — warranty liability (emphasis)**

| Year | Beginning warranty liability | + Provision (rate × sales) | − Claims paid | Ending warranty liability |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $52,000 | $120,000 (2.5% × $4,800,000) | $(105,000) | **$67,000** |
| 2 | 67,000 | 135,000 (2.5% × $5,400,000) | (118,000) | **84,000** |
| 3 | 84,000 | 114,000 (2.0% × $5,700,000) | (122,000) | **76,000** |

**Rollforward checks:**  
Year 1: \(52{,}000 + 120{,}000 - 105{,}000 = 67{,}000\).  
Year 2: \(67{,}000 + 135{,}000 - 118{,}000 = 84{,}000\).  
Year 3: \(84{,}000 + 114{,}000 - 122{,}000 = 76{,}000\).

**b. Period-end adjusting JE — Year 2 warranty true-up**

Estimated Year-2 warranty cost (provision) = **$135,000**.  
Claims already expensed in Year 2 = **$118,000**.  
Additional year-end provision = \(135{,}000 - 118{,}000 = \$17{,}000\).  
Target ending liability = \(67{,}000 + 17{,}000 = \$84{,}000\) (matches schedule).

*December 31, Year 2 — Warranty period-end adjusting entry*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 17,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 17,000 |
| *True up so ending liability = beg + estimated cost − claims* | | |

**Check:** Dr 17,000 = Cr 17,000. **Balanced.**

(Total Warranty Expense Year 2 = $118,000 already recorded + $17,000 adjust = **$135,000** = provision.)

**c. Initial recognition JE — litigation (probable; best estimate in range)**

Best estimate within range = **$160,000** (ASC 450-20-30-1).

*December 31, Year 2 — Accrue loss contingency (initial recognition)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation (Litigation Expense) | 160,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 160,000 |
| *Probable and reasonably estimable; best estimate accrued* | | |

**Check:** Dr 160,000 = Cr 160,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Contingency subsequent-measurement schedule and settlement**

| Period | Beginning accrued litigation | + Provision / remeasurement | − Payments | Ending |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 2 (initial recognition) | $0 | $160,000 | $0 | **$160,000** |
| Year 3 through settlement (illustrative vs $160k carrying amount) | 160,000 | 15,000 (additional loss) | (175,000) | **$0** |

*March 5, Year 3 — Settle litigation (instructional entry against $160,000 accrual)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated Litigation Liability | 160,000 | |
| Loss on Litigation Settlement | 15,000 | |
| Cash | | 175,000 |
| *Remove accrued liability; excess cash paid is additional loss* | | |

**Check:** Dr 160,000 + 15,000 = Cr 175,000. **Balanced.**

**Recognized subsequent event:** Settlement after the balance-sheet date but **before issuance**, providing evidence about conditions that **existed at December 31, Year 2**, is a **recognized subsequent event**. Issued Year-2 statements should report the liability (and related loss) at **$175,000**, not $160,000. Illustrative remeasurement before issuance:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation | 15,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 15,000 |

**Check:** Dr 15,000 = Cr 15,000. **Balanced.** Settlement would then be Dr Estimated Litigation Liability 175,000 / Cr Cash 175,000 (no further gain or loss).

**e. Classification / disclosure**

| Item | Accrue? | Disclose? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) Litigation ($160k best estimate; probable) | **Yes** | **Yes** | Accrue $160,000; disclose nature; additional reasonably possible exposure above accrual up to high end of range: $250,000 − $160,000 = **$90,000** |
| (ii) Warranty liability ending Y2 $84,000 | **Yes** | **Yes** | Current liability; accounting policy and **tabular reconciliation** of warranty liability (ASC 460 / chapter Demo style) |
| (iii) Plant expansion commitment $1,500,000 | **No** | **Yes** | Commitment for plant acquisition (ASC 440-10-50) — nature, amount, timing; not a present liability solely from the future acquisition commitment |
| (iv) Unused letter of credit $600,000 | **No** | **Yes** | Unused letters of credit must be disclosed (ASC 440-10-50-1) |

Face of balance sheet may include a **“Commitments and Contingencies”** line (often with no dollar amount) directing users to the notes. SEC registrants separately state any current-liability component **in excess of 5%** of total current liabilities.

**f. Liquidity ratios — December 31, Year 2**

Quick assets = Cash + ST marketable securities + AR  
= \(380{,}000 + 140{,}000 + 920{,}000 = \$1{,}440{,}000\).

After Year-2 accruals:  
Adjusted CL = \(1{,}797{,}000 + 17{,}000 + 160{,}000 = \$1{,}974{,}000\).  
CA unchanged at **$3,200,000** for this analysis (no cash paid yet).

| Ratio | Formula | Unadjusted | After Y2 warranty true-up + litigation accrual |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Current ratio** | CA ÷ CL | \(3{,}200{,}000 \div 1{,}797{,}000 = \mathbf{1.78}\) | \(3{,}200{,}000 \div 1{,}974{,}000 = \mathbf{1.62}\) |
| **Quick ratio** | Quick assets ÷ CL | \(1{,}440{,}000 \div 1{,}797{,}000 = \mathbf{0.80}\) | \(1{,}440{,}000 \div 1{,}974{,}000 = \mathbf{0.73}\) |

**Analysis:** Accruing the incremental warranty liability and the litigation contingency **increases current liabilities** with no matching increase in current assets, so **both** the current ratio and the quick ratio **decline**. The quick ratio is lower than the current ratio in both columns because inventory and prepaids are excluded from the numerator. Users rely on these ratios for **short-term solvency**, not profitability or long-term debt-paying ability.

**Key insight:** The **subsequent measurement schedule** is the LO 15-6 warranty/contingency core: roll liability balances forward (beg + provisions − claims/settlements). Accruals that feed those schedules also **worsen liquidity ratios**; commitments that never enter the rollforward still require **note disclosure**.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Multi-year warranty schedule twin; min-of-range contingency; settlement loss; liquidity

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Two-year liquidity schedule, classification table, period-end contingency accrual, callable reclass
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Multi-year liquidity ratio schedule (subsequent measurement of short-term solvency); classification of commitments vs contingencies; period-end adjusting JE for probable contingency and warranty true-up; callable debt reclassification impact on current liabilities and ratios  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakeshore Packaging Inc.** is reviewing December 31, **Year 5** reporting for commitments, contingencies, and liquidity. Comparative Year 4 ratios are also presented for analysis.

**Selected items under review at December 31, Year 5:**

| Item | Facts |
|---|---|
| A | Unused revolving credit capacity **$1,000,000** (no borrowings on the unused portion) |
| B | Noncancelable commitment to purchase specialized packaging equipment in Year 6 for **$900,000** |
| C | Pending lawsuit (events occurred in Year 5): loss **reasonably possible**, estimated range **$50,000–$95,000**, no best estimate |
| D | Product liability claims: loss **probable**, best estimate **$120,000** (not yet recorded) |
| E | Assurance-type warranty true-up still needed: additional **$25,000** to bring warranty liability to the correct subsequent-measurement ending balance (claims already expensed) |
| F | Guarantee of a distributor’s bank loan; likelihood Lakeshore will have to perform is **remote**; guarantee maximum **$350,000** |

**Year 4 year-end balances (after all Year-4 accruals) — for comparative liquidity schedule:**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash | $290,000 |
| Short-term marketable securities | 80,000 |
| Accounts receivable (net) | 510,000 |
| Inventory | 890,000 |
| Prepaid expenses | 70,000 |
| **Total current assets** | **$1,840,000** |
| **Total current liabilities** | **$1,200,000** |

**Year 5 unadjusted current assets and liabilities** (before Items D and E accruals; before any debt reclassification):

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash | $310,000 |
| Short-term marketable securities | 100,000 |
| Accounts receivable (net) | 560,000 |
| Inventory | 940,000 |
| Prepaid expenses | 90,000 |
| **Total current assets** | **$2,000,000** |
| Accounts payable and accrued expenses | $680,000 |
| Short-term notes payable | 220,000 |
| Current portion of long-term debt (installments due in Year 6) | 150,000 |
| Warranty liability (before Year-5 true-up of Item E) | 100,000 |
| **Total current liabilities (unadjusted)** | **$1,150,000** |
| Long-term note payable (contractual maturity Year 11) | 300,000 *(classified noncurrent)* |

**Debt covenant / callable debt:** The $300,000 long-term note requires a **minimum current ratio of 1.50**. On December 31, Year 5, the company is **in violation** of a separate working-capital covenant on the **same** note; the lender has **not** waived the right to call the note, and the violation is not cured within the contractual grace period before issuance. Under ASC 470-10-45, the **entire $300,000** becomes **callable** and must be classified as **current**.

**Required:**  
a. For items **A–F**, complete a classification table: Accrue (yes/no), Disclose (yes/no), and a one-line rationale.  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 5 period-end adjusting JEs** for Item D and Item E only.  
c. Prepare a **two-year liquidity ratio subsequent-measurement schedule** showing Year 4 (given) and Year 5 **after** accruing Items D and E **and** reclassifying the $300,000 callable note to current: total CA, quick assets, CL, current ratio, and quick ratio.  
d. Draft minimum **note disclosure** elements for Item C and for the equipment commitment (Item B).  
e. Suppose instead the lender **waives** the call right for more than one year from the balance-sheet date before issuance. Recompute Year-5 current and quick ratios after accruing only Items D and E (note stays noncurrent). Comment on the 1.50 covenant.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Classification table**

| Item | Accrue? | Disclose? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Unused revolving capacity $1,000,000 | No | **Yes** | Unused credit / letter-of-credit type commitment — ASC 440 disclosure; not a present liability |
| B Equipment purchase commitment $900,000 | No | **Yes** | Future commitment for plant/equipment acquisition — disclose nature and amount |
| C Reasonably possible lawsuit $50k–$95k | No | **Yes** | At least reasonably possible → disclose nature + range; do **not** accrue (not probable) |
| D Probable product liability $120,000 | **Yes** | **Yes** | Probable and reasonably estimable → accrue; disclose nature |
| E Warranty true-up $25,000 | **Yes** | **Yes** | Completes warranty subsequent measurement; policy + tabular reconciliation |
| F Remote distributor-loan guarantee $350,000 | No* | **Yes** | Guarantees require disclosure even when remote (*stand-ready guarantee measurement under ASC 460 may apply at fair value of the guarantee—exam core for ASC 450: remote contingency → no loss accrual; still disclose guarantee*) |

**b. Period-end adjusting JEs — Items D and E**

*December 31, Year 5 — Accrue product liability contingency (Item D)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Product Liability Loss (Expense) | 120,000 | |
| Accrued Product Liability | | 120,000 |

**Check:** Dr 120,000 = Cr 120,000. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 5 — Warranty subsequent-measurement true-up (Item E)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 25,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 25,000 |

**Check:** Dr 25,000 = Cr 25,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Two-year liquidity ratio subsequent-measurement schedule**

Year 4 quick assets = \(290{,}000 + 80{,}000 + 510{,}000 = \$880{,}000\).  
Year 5 quick assets = \(310{,}000 + 100{,}000 + 560{,}000 = \$970{,}000\).

Year 5 adjusted CL after D + E + callable reclass:  
\(1{,}150{,}000 + 120{,}000 + 25{,}000 + 300{,}000 = \$1{,}595{,}000\).

| Measure | Year 4 (given) | Year 5 after D, E, and callable reclass |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total current assets | $1,840,000 | $2,000,000 |
| Quick assets | 880,000 | 970,000 |
| Total current liabilities | 1,200,000 | **1,595,000** |
| **Current ratio** | \(1{,}840{,}000 \div 1{,}200{,}000 = \mathbf{1.53}\) | \(2{,}000{,}000 \div 1{,}595{,}000 = \mathbf{1.25}\) |
| **Quick ratio** | \(880{,}000 \div 1{,}200{,}000 = \mathbf{0.73}\) | \(970{,}000 \div 1{,}595{,}000 = \mathbf{0.61}\) |

**Analysis:** Year-over-year, CA and quick assets rose, but **reclassifying callable long-term debt to current** plus contingency/warranty accruals increased CL enough that **both liquidity ratios declined**. Contingency accruals alone are smaller drivers; **classification** of debt as current is often the first-order hit to the current ratio—central to LO 15-6 liquidity analysis.

**d. Minimum disclosure content**

- **Item C (reasonably possible litigation):** (1) nature of the contingency; (2) estimate of possible loss or range (**$50,000–$95,000**) or a statement that an estimate cannot be made.  
- **Item B (equipment commitment):** existence of the commitment, approximate amount (**$900,000**), and timing (Year 6) as a **commitment** note—not recorded as a liability solely from signing the future purchase commitment under this LO framework.

**e. Waiver alternative (note remains noncurrent)**

CL = \(1{,}150{,}000 + 120{,}000 + 25{,}000 = \$1{,}295{,}000\).  
Current ratio = \(2{,}000{,}000 \div 1{,}295{,}000 = \mathbf{1.54}\).  
Quick ratio = \(970{,}000 \div 1{,}295{,}000 = \mathbf{0.75}\).

With a valid long-term waiver, the current ratio **1.54** clears the **1.50** covenant. Accruals of Items D and E alone still **reduce** headroom versus a pre-accrual ratio of \(2{,}000{,}000 \div 1{,}150{,}000 = 1.74\).

**Key insight:** LO 15-6 links **note disclosures** (commitments; reasonably possible contingencies) to **on-book subsequent measurement** (warranty and probable contingency accruals) and to a **liquidity ratio schedule**. Callable-debt **reclassification** and contingency **accruals** both inflate current liabilities and are primary drivers of the current and quick ratios.

### Q4 — MC — Warranty subsequent measurement (rollforward)
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement of product warranty liability (beginning + provision − claims = ending)  
**Question:** A company reports a warranty liability of **$40,000** at the beginning of the year. Estimated warranty cost related to current-year sales is **$95,000**. Cash warranty claims paid during the year are **$88,000**. What is the **ending warranty liability** under the standard rollforward (subsequent measurement)?

- A) $7,000  
- B) $40,000  
- C) $47,000  
- D) $95,000  

**Answer:** **C.** Ending warranty liability = \(40{,}000 + 95{,}000 - 88{,}000 = \$47{,}000\). Option A is provision − claims only (ignores beginning balance). Option B is the beginning balance only. Option D is the provision only.

### Q5 — MC — Accrue vs disclose; liquidity impact
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Classification of commitments vs probable contingencies and effect on the current ratio  
**Question:** At year-end, a company has (1) an **unused letter of credit** of $500,000 and (2) a **probable, reasonably estimable** litigation loss of $80,000 expected to be paid within six months. Neither item has been recorded. Which statement is correct?

- A) Accrue both $500,000 and $80,000; both reduce the current ratio.  
- B) Disclose the unused letter of credit only; accrue the $80,000 litigation loss, which decreases the current ratio.  
- C) Accrue the unused letter of credit only; disclose the litigation.  
- D) Disclose both items only; neither affects the current ratio until paid.  

**Answer:** **B.** Unused letters of credit are **disclosed commitments** (not present liabilities). A **probable and estimable** loss contingency is **accrued**, increasing current liabilities and **decreasing** the current ratio (CA unchanged). Accrual also typically requires disclosure of the nature of the contingency.
