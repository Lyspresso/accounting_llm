# Agent 194 — CORE DEMO — LO 10-7

**Chapter:** 10  
**LO title:** Explain accounting treatment of inventory errors  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Three discovery factors (Exhibit 10-2):** (1) same period vs subsequent period; (2) balance-sheet-only vs income + BS; (3) counterbalancing vs noncounterbalancing
- **Subsequent measurement / multi-year impact schedule (emphasis):** rollforward of as-recorded vs correct BI, purchases, EI, COGS, pretax income, and cumulative retained earnings across periods
- **Counterbalancing EI errors:** Year 1 EI error reverses through Year 2 beginning inventory → COGS; two-year net pretax effect = $0
- **Initial recognition correcting JE (same year):** Dr/Cr Inventory and COGS before statements issue
- **Period-end / discovery PPA JE:** Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment and Inventory when found before self-correction
- **Settlement after self-correction:** no JE once counterbalancing error has reversed; still restate all periods presented
- **Cutoff / combined purchase–EI errors:** effect matrix (O / U / NE) under a periodic system
- **Noncounterbalancing misclassification:** purchase recorded as depreciable equipment requires full reclass + RE correction until fixed

### Q1 — CORE — Subsequent measurement schedule for counterbalancing EI overstatement (discovery Y1 / Y2 / Y3)
**LO:** LO 10-7  
**Concept:** Multi-year subsequent measurement schedule of inventory error effects; same-period correcting JE; PPA before self-correction; no entry after self-correction (settlement)  
**Scenario:**  
**Frostpeak Outfitters Inc.** adjusts its inventory records each **December 31** to a physical count. Management later determines that the **December 31, Year 1** count **overstated ending inventory by $22,000**. Purchases and sales were correctly recorded in both years. The **December 31, Year 2** ending inventory is **correct**. Ignore income taxes.

Amounts **as recorded with the error** (before any correction):

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | $68,000 | $95,000 (overstated) |
| Net purchases | 340,000 | 365,000 |
| Ending inventory | 95,000 (overstated) | 80,000 (correct) |
| Net sales | 520,000 | 560,000 |
| Other expenses | 75,000 | 82,000 |

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare a **subsequent measurement / impact schedule** (emphasis) for Year 1 and Year 2 showing **as recorded**, **correct**, and **error effect** (amount and over/under direction) for: ending inventory, cost of goods sold, pretax income, and **cumulative pretax income from Year 1 forward**.  
(b) Extend the schedule with a **balance-sheet subsequent measurement** of Inventory and Retained Earnings (relative to correct amounts) at **December 31, Year 1**, **December 31, Year 2**, and **January 1, Year 3** (before any discovery entry).  
(c) Assume discovery **in Year 1** before statements issue. Record the **period-end correcting journal entry** and state prior-period FS impact.  
(d) Assume discovery **January 1, Year 2** (before self-correction). Record the **prior-period adjustment** entry and describe restatement.  
(e) Assume discovery **January 1, Year 3** (after self-correction). Record any Year 3 journal entry and describe restatement (**settlement / maturity of the counterbalancing error**).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Subsequent measurement / income-statement impact schedule**

Correct Year 1 ending inventory = \(95{,}000 - 22{,}000 = \mathbf{\$73{,}000}\).  
Correct Year 2 beginning inventory = **$73,000**.

| Item | Y1 as recorded | Y1 correct | Y1 effect | Y2 as recorded | Y2 correct | Y2 effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| Ending inventory | 95,000 | 73,000 | **Over $22,000** | 80,000 | 80,000 | **None** |
| COGS | \(68{,}000+340{,}000-95{,}000=\mathbf{313{,}000}\) | \(68{,}000+340{,}000-73{,}000=\mathbf{335{,}000}\) | **Under $22,000** | \(95{,}000+365{,}000-80{,}000=\mathbf{380{,}000}\) | \(73{,}000+365{,}000-80{,}000=\mathbf{358{,}000}\) | **Over $22,000** |
| Pretax income | \(520{,}000-313{,}000-75{,}000=\mathbf{132{,}000}\) | \(520{,}000-335{,}000-75{,}000=\mathbf{110{,}000}\) | **Over $22,000** | \(560{,}000-380{,}000-82{,}000=\mathbf{98{,}000}\) | \(560{,}000-358{,}000-82{,}000=\mathbf{120{,}000}\) | **Under $22,000** |
| Cumulative pretax (Y1→) | 132,000 | 110,000 | **Over $22,000** | \(132{,}000+98{,}000=\mathbf{230{,}000}\) | \(110{,}000+120{,}000=\mathbf{230{,}000}\) | **None ($0)** |

**Math check:** Two-year pretax totals match (**$230,000**); Year 1 overstatement and Year 2 understatement of pretax income **counterbalance**.

**(b) Balance-sheet subsequent measurement of Inventory and RE misstatement (as books stand, no discovery entry)**

| Measurement date | Inventory vs correct | Retained earnings vs correct | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dec 31, Year 1 | **Over $22,000** | **Over $22,000** | EI over → COGS under → NI over |
| Dec 31, Year 2 | **Correct** | **Correct** | Y2 BI over → COGS over → NI under by $22,000; offsets Y1 NI overstatement |
| Jan 1, Year 3 (pre-discovery) | **Correct** | **Correct** | Error fully self-corrected through operations |

**(c) Discovery in Year 1 — initial recognition / period-end correcting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 22,000 | |
| Inventory | | 22,000 |
| *To correct overstated Year 1 physical inventory count* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,000 = Cr 22,000. **Balanced.**  
**FS impact:** No prior-period restatement. Year 1 statements report correct inventory, COGS, and pretax income.

**(d) Discovery January 1, Year 2 — period-end / discovery PPA (before self-correction)**

Inventory and beginning RE are still overstated by $22,000.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 22,000 | |
| Inventory | | 22,000 |
| *To correct Year 1 inventory overstatement discovered before self-correction* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,000 = Cr 22,000. **Balanced.**  
**FS impact:** **Restate** all comparative financial statements presented (Year 1 EI, COGS, NI, RE). Year 2 then starts from correct BI = $73,000.

**(e) Discovery January 1, Year 3 — settlement after self-correction**

By this date, Inventory and Retained Earnings are already correct in total (see schedule in (b)).

**Journal entry in Year 3:** **None.**  
**FS impact:** Still **restate** all comparative periods presented so year-by-year income is correct for users; do not book a current-period PPA after self-correction.

**Key insight:** The subsequent measurement schedule shows the error “lives” on Inventory and RE only until the next period’s COGS absorbs it. Same-year discovery fixes COGS; discovery before self-correction uses RE—PPA; after self-correction the counterbalancing error has **settled** with no JE, but restatement remains.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Subsequent measurement schedule for EI understatement
**LO:** LO 10-7  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — multi-year subsequent measurement schedule for understated ending inventory; flipped correcting JEs; two-year net $0  
**Scenario:**  
**Redline Cycle Parts Co.** discovers that its **December 31, Year 1** ending inventory was **understated by $15,600** because two storage bays were omitted from the count. Purchases were correct both years. **Year 2 ending inventory is correct**. Ignore taxes.

Amounts **as recorded with the error**:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | $52,000 | $61,000 (understated) |
| Net purchases | 198,000 | 215,000 |
| Ending inventory | 61,000 (understated) | 70,000 (correct) |
| Net sales | 310,000 | 355,000 |
| Other expenses | 48,000 | 51,000 |

**Required:**  
(a) Complete the two-year **subsequent measurement schedule** (as recorded / correct / effect) for EI, COGS, pretax income, and cumulative pretax income.  
(b) State Inventory and RE misstatement direction/amount at Dec 31, Year 1 and Dec 31, Year 2 (books uncorrected).  
(c) Correcting entry if discovered **December 31, Year 1**.  
(d) Correcting entry if discovered **January 1, Year 2**.  
(e) Entry (if any) if discovered **January 1, Year 3**.  
(f) Is the error counterbalancing? Two-year net pretax effect?

**Answer key:**

**(a) Subsequent measurement schedule**

Correct Year 1 EI = \(61{,}000 + 15{,}600 = \mathbf{\$76{,}600}\).

| Item | Y1 recorded | Y1 correct | Effect | Y2 recorded | Y2 correct | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| EI | 61,000 | 76,600 | **Under $15,600** | 70,000 | 70,000 | **None** |
| COGS | \(52{,}000+198{,}000-61{,}000=\mathbf{189{,}000}\) | \(52{,}000+198{,}000-76{,}600=\mathbf{173{,}400}\) | **Over $15,600** | \(61{,}000+215{,}000-70{,}000=\mathbf{206{,}000}\) | \(76{,}600+215{,}000-70{,}000=\mathbf{221{,}600}\) | **Under $15,600** |
| Pretax | \(310{,}000-189{,}000-48{,}000=\mathbf{73{,}000}\) | \(310{,}000-173{,}400-48{,}000=\mathbf{88{,}600}\) | **Under $15,600** | \(355{,}000-206{,}000-51{,}000=\mathbf{98{,}000}\) | \(355{,}000-221{,}600-51{,}000=\mathbf{82{,}400}\) | **Over $15,600** |
| Cumulative pretax | 73,000 | 88,600 | **Under $15,600** | \(73{,}000+98{,}000=\mathbf{171{,}000}\) | \(88{,}600+82{,}400=\mathbf{171{,}000}\) | **None ($0)** |

**Math checks:** Y1 COGS over 15,600 → pretax under 15,600. Y2 reverses. Two-year pretax totals both **$171,000**.

**(b) BS subsequent measurement (uncorrected books)**

| Date | Inventory | Retained earnings |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 31, Year 1 | **Under $15,600** | **Under $15,600** |
| Dec 31, Year 2 | **Correct** | **Correct** (Y2 NI over by $15,600 offsets Y1) |

**(c) Discovered Year 1 (same period)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 15,600 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 15,600 |
| *To correct understated Year 1 ending inventory* | | |

**Check:** Dr 15,600 = Cr 15,600. **Balanced.**

**(d) Discovered January 1, Year 2 (before self-correction)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 15,600 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 15,600 |
| *To restore inventory and correct understated Year 1 NI via PPA* | | |

**Check:** Dr 15,600 = Cr 15,600. **Balanced.** Restate Year 1 comparative amounts.

**(e) Discovered January 1, Year 3**  
**No journal entry** — counterbalancing error has self-corrected. Restate comparative FS if prior years are presented.

**(f)** **Yes, counterbalancing.** Two-year net pretax effect = **$0**.

**Key insight:** Understated EI is the mirror of overstatement: Dr Inventory / Cr COGS (or Cr RE—PPA). The subsequent measurement schedule still shows full reversal of cumulative pretax misstatement by the end of Year 2.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Cutoff effect matrix, dual-year EI schedule, noncounterbalancing settlement
**LO:** LO 10-7  
**Concept:** Period-end cutoff effect analysis; subsequent measurement when consecutive EI errors exist; noncounterbalancing purchase-as-equipment correcting JEs  
**Scenario:**  
Parts A–B use **Northfork Distributors LLC** (periodic inventory system). Part C uses **Alpine Supply Co.** Ignore income taxes.

**Part A — Period-end effect classification (separate errors)**  
For each **separate** error, indicate effect on **current-year COGS**, **current-year pretax income**, and **current-year ending inventory**: overstated (O), understated (U), or no effect (NE).

| # | Error |
|---|---|
| A1 | Ending inventory overstated (count error only; purchases correct) |
| A2 | Ending inventory understated (count error only; purchases correct) |
| A3 | Ending inventory and purchases both overstated by the same amount |
| A4 | Ending inventory and purchases both understated by the same amount |
| A5 | Beginning inventory overstated |
| A6 | Beginning inventory understated |
| A7 | Goods held on consignment (not owned) included in the physical count; purchases correct |
| A8 | FOB shipping-point purchase recorded; goods omitted from physical count |

**Part B — Subsequent measurement with two consecutive EI overstatements**  
Early in Year 3, Northfork learns that ending inventory was overstated by **$19,000** at December 31, Year 1 and by **$8,500** at December 31, Year 2. Purchases were correct each year. Year 2 books have closed; Year 3 has just begun.

**Required (Part B):**  
1. Prepare a brief **subsequent measurement schedule** showing how much of the inventory/RE misstatement remains open at January 1, Year 2 and January 1, Year 3 (before any PPA).  
2. Record the **January 1, Year 3** correcting journal entry (if any). Explain which prior-year error amount appears and which does not.

**Part C — Noncounterbalancing error (settlement / full correction)**  
On **April 1, Year 1**, Alpine Supply purchased merchandise costing **$48,000** on account but incorrectly debited **Equipment**. Equipment is depreciated straight-line over **8 years**, residual value zero; Alpine took **full-year depreciation of $6,000** on this item in Year 1. **None** of the merchandise had been sold by December 31, Year 1 (still on hand). The error is discovered on **January 8, Year 2**, before Year 2 statements. Accounts Payable was correctly credited when the purchase was recorded (only the debit was wrong). Ignore taxes.

**Required (Part C):**  
1. Classify the error as counterbalancing or noncounterbalancing and explain.  
2. Prepare the **January 8, Year 2** correcting entry(ies) to place cost in Inventory, remove Equipment and related Accumulated Depreciation, and adjust Retained Earnings for improper Year 1 depreciation.  
3. State presentation treatment for prior periods.

**Answer key:**

**Part A**

| # | Current COGS | Current pretax income | Current EI |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | U | O | O |
| A2 | O | U | U |
| A3 | NE (Purch↑ and EI↑ offset) | NE | O |
| A4 | NE | NE | U |
| A5 | O | U | NE |
| A6 | U | O | NE |
| A7 | U | O | O |
| A8 | O | U | U |

**Logic notes:**  
- A1/A2/A7: EI alone moves COGS opposite to EI; income moves with EI.  
- A3/A4: Equal purchase and EI errors cancel in the COGS equation; only BS inventory (and often AP) is wrong.  
- A5/A6: BI errors flow fully into COGS; year-end physical EI is independent of the BI error.  
- A8: Purchase recorded but goods not counted → EI under → COGS over.

**Part B**

**1. Subsequent measurement of remaining misstatement**

| Date (books uncorrected) | Inventory / RE vs correct | Composition |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 1, Year 2 | **Over $19,000** | Entire Year 1 EI overstatement still open |
| During Year 2 | Y1 $19,000 **self-corrects** through overstated BI → overstated COGS → understated Y2 NI | Cumulative RE misstatement from Y1 returns to zero |
| Jan 1, Year 3 | **Over $8,500** | Only the **Year 2** EI overstatement remains open |

**2. January 1, Year 3 correcting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 8,500 | |
| Inventory | | 8,500 |
| *To remove remaining Year 2 EI overstatement still on the books* | | |

**Check:** Dr 8,500 = Cr 8,500. **Balanced.**

**Why not $19,000 or $27,500?** The Year 1 overstatement of $19,000 **self-corrected in Year 2**. Only the latest uncorrected EI error (**$8,500**) still overstates Inventory and RE at January 1, Year 3. Restate comparative statements for both prior years as presented.

**Part C — Noncounterbalancing misclassification**

**1.** **Noncounterbalancing.** Debiting Equipment instead of Inventory (or Purchases) does **not** reverse within two consecutive periods solely through the inventory equation. Equipment remains and is depreciated over multiple years until a full correcting entry is recorded.

**2. Correcting entry January 8, Year 2**

Improper Year 1 depreciation closed to RE = **$6,000** (expense overstated → RE understated). Goods still on hand → full **$48,000** to Inventory.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 48,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 6,000 | |
| Equipment | | 48,000 |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 6,000 |
| *To reclassify Year 1 merchandise purchase from equipment to inventory and reverse improper Year 1 depreciation closed to RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(48{,}000 + 6{,}000 = 54{,}000\); Cr \(48{,}000 + 6{,}000 = 54{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**3. Presentation:** Restate **all** financial statements presented. Cumulative effect of periods before those presented adjusts **opening retained earnings**. Disclose nature of the error and quantitative effects on inventory, equipment (net), and income as applicable.

**Key insight:** Consecutive counterbalancing EI errors leave only the **most recent** uncorrected EI error on Inventory/RE at discovery. Purchase-as-equipment is noncounterbalancing and requires a multi-account settlement JE (Inventory, Equipment, Accumulated Depreciation, RE—PPA).

### Q4 — MC — Subsequent measurement timing and error classification
**LO:** LO 10-7  
**Concept:** When the subsequent measurement schedule shows self-correction; classify counterbalancing vs noncounterbalancing  

**Question 1:**  
**Lakeshore Merchandising** overstated December 31, Year 1 ending inventory by $11,200 (purchases correct). Year 2 ending inventory was correct. The error is discovered on **February 15, Year 3**, after Year 2 books closed. Ignoring taxes, which action is correct?

- A) Debit Retained Earnings—PPA $11,200 and credit Inventory $11,200 on February 15, Year 3.  
- B) Debit Inventory $11,200 and credit Retained Earnings—PPA $11,200 on February 15, Year 3.  
- C) No correcting journal entry in Year 3; the subsequent measurement schedule shows Inventory and RE already correct after Year 2 self-correction; restate comparative statements presented.  
- D) Debit Cost of Goods Sold $11,200 and credit Inventory $11,200 in Year 3 as a current-period catch-up.

**Answer:** **C.** Year 1 pretax over $11,200 and Year 2 pretax under $11,200 leave RE and inventory correct by Year 3. After self-correction, **no JE**; restatement of presented periods is still required. A/B would double-correct. D improperly runs a prior error through current COGS.

---

**Question 2:**  
Which error is **noncounterbalancing** (does not self-correct within two consecutive periods solely through the BI/EI relationship)?

- A) Ending inventory understated due to a math error; next year’s count is correct.  
- B) Consigned-in goods included in ending inventory; next year’s count is correct.  
- C) FOB shipping-point goods neither recorded as a purchase nor included in EI in Year 1; both purchase and inventory recorded correctly when goods arrive in Year 2.  
- D) A merchandise purchase was debited to Equipment and is being depreciated over several years; goods were sold in the following year.

**Answer:** **D.** Equipment misclassification and multi-year depreciation do not reverse through the two-period inventory rollforward alone; a PPA and reclassification are required. A and B are classic counterbalancing EI errors. C is the special omitted-purchase-and-EI case (Year 1 COGS/income often unaffected because Purchases and EI are under by the same amount); it is not the multi-year noncounterbalancing pattern of purchase-as-equipment.
