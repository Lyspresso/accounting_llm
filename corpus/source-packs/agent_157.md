# Agent 157 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-1

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Classifying Differences Between GAAP Income and Taxable Income  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- Book-to-tax (book–tax) differences classified as **taxable temporary**, **deductible temporary**, or **permanent** (nontaxable / nondeductible)
- **Taxable temporary**: reverses by **increasing future taxable income relative to pretax GAAP** (e.g., prepaid expensed for tax when paid; excess tax depreciation; installment receivable)
- **Deductible temporary**: reverses by **decreasing future taxable income relative to pretax GAAP** (e.g., warranty accrual; estimated bad debts; deferred revenue taxed on receipt)
- **Permanent** differences **never reverse** → no deferred tax asset or liability
- Balance-sheet view: temporary difference exists when **GAAP carrying amount ≠ tax basis** of an asset or liability
- **Subsequent measurement schedule**: multi-year origin / reverse / ending **cumulative temporary difference** roll-forward
- **Initial recognition JEs** for difference-creating items; **period-end income tax adjusting JE** (current payable + DTA/DTL)
- **Settlement / maturity** of temporary differences (prepaid expires; warranty paid; deferred revenue earned)
- Classification / presentation: current vs deferred tax expense; DTA/DTL reported **noncurrent** (net within jurisdiction)

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Blue Heron Media: installment sale + deferred revenue + permanent items; classification table + multi-period schedule + settlement
**LO:** LO 18-1  
**Concept:** Classify a different set of book–tax items (installment receivable = taxable TD; customer advances = deductible TD; permanent items); subsequent measurement schedule through settlement; period-end tax adjusting JEs  
**Scenario:** **Blue Heron Media Group** has pretax GAAP income of **$200,000** in Year 1 before considering the following items already **included** in that $200,000 where noted. Tax rate **25%**. Beginning deferred tax balances are zero. Accounting and tax years end December 31.

No other differences. In Years 2 and 3, pretax GAAP income (after all items) is **$160,000** each year. Those amounts already include: municipal interest **$3,000** each year; GAAP recognition of **$13,500** of the deferred revenue each year; **no new** installment gross profit; and **no** nondeductible item. **(f) Presentation / permanent vs temporary**  
Permanent items (muni interest, nondeductible expense) **never reverse** and therefore **never** create DTA/DTL, but they **do** change the effective tax rate relative to the statutory rate; temporary items **do** create DTA/DTL that reverse on the subsequent measurement schedule and reclassify future tax effects without permanently distorting the rate once fully reversed.

**Key insight:** Revenue-side temporary differences flip the “who goes first” logic—installment GP is in GAAP **before** tax (taxable TD / DTL); customer advances are in tax **before** GAAP (deductible TD / DTA)—but both are measured the same way: **ending cumulative TD × enacted rate**.

### Q4 — MC — Classifying a book–tax difference
**LO:** LO 18-1  
**Concept:** Classification choice — taxable temporary vs deductible temporary vs permanent  
**Question:** On December 31, Year 1, **Thornwick Appliance Co.** records a product warranty accrual of $80,000 for GAAP. Warranty costs are deductible for tax only when paid. Payments are expected over the next two years. Relative to pretax GAAP income, Year 1 taxable income is higher by $80,000 because of this item, and future taxable income will be lower when claims are paid. How should Thornwick classify this difference?

- A) Taxable temporary difference (creates a deferred tax liability)  
- B) Deductible temporary difference (creates a deferred tax asset)  
- C) Permanent difference — nondeductible item (never deductible; no deferred tax)  
- D) Permanent difference — nontaxable item (never taxable; no deferred tax)

**Answer:** **B.** A warranty accrual is recognized as expense for GAAP before it is deductible for tax. The difference **originates** by making taxable income **higher** than pretax GAAP and **reverses** by making future taxable income **lower** relative to pretax GAAP when claims are paid—the definition of a **deductible temporary difference**, which creates a **deferred tax asset**. It is temporary (not permanent) because the tax deduction will eventually occur. It is not a taxable temporary difference because reversal decreases—not increases—future TI relative to GAAP.

### Q5 — MC — Prepaid expense classification
**LO:** LO 18-1  
**Concept:** Classification choice — prepaid expense deductible for tax when paid  
**Question:** **Ironclad Tooling Inc.** pays $40,000 on December 31, Year 1 for a maintenance contract covering Year 2. For GAAP the amount is Prepaid Maintenance; for tax it is deducted when paid. Which statement is correct?

- A) Deductible temporary difference; future taxable income will be lower relative to pretax GAAP when the prepaid is amortized.  
- B) Taxable temporary difference; future taxable income will be higher relative to pretax GAAP when the prepaid is amortized for GAAP.  
- C) Permanent difference because maintenance is always treated the same eventually.  
- D) No book–tax difference exists because cash left the company in Year 1 under both systems.

**Answer:** **B.** Tax deducts the full $40,000 in Year 1 while GAAP expenses it in Year 2. Origin: TI **less** than pretax GAAP. Reversal in Year 2: GAAP has maintenance expense with **no** remaining tax deduction → TI **greater** relative to pretax GAAP → **taxable temporary difference** (and a deferred tax liability). Temporary differences reverse; this is not permanent. Cash payment alone does not eliminate the difference—the **timing** of expense recognition differs.
