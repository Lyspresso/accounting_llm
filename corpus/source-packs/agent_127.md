# Agent 127 — CORE DEMO — LO 21-4

**Chapter:** 21  
**LO title:** Demonstrate the accounting for an error correction  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Identify material errors (mathematical mistakes, GAAP misapplication, misuse of facts; non-GAAP → GAAP is an error)
- **Prior-period adjustment (PPA)** journal entry: adjust Retained Earnings and the related asset/liability (ignore tax here; LO 21-5 covers tax)
- Multi-year **incorrect vs correct** measurement schedule for cumulative income effect through discovery date
- **Period-end adjusting** entries in the discovery year after books are corrected (depreciation, amortization, prepaid burn-off)
- **Settlement** of a liability that should have been accrued in a prior period (no current-period expense)
- **Counterbalancing inventory** errors: JE if discovered same year / next year before self-correction / after self-correction
- **Restatement** presentation and disclosure vs change in principle (reissue prior FS for material errors)
- Classification: error correction vs change in estimate vs change in principle

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-error prior-period adjustments, cumulative schedule, Year-6 adjusting and settlement
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of prior-period adjustment JEs for material errors; subsequent incorrect-vs-correct schedule; period-end adjusting JEs; settlement of previously unrecorded liability  
**Scenario:**  
Riverbend Tools Inc. (December 31 year-end) issued its Year 5 financial statements in early Year 6. In January of Year 6 the controller discovers the following **material** errors. All amounts are pretax; **ignore income taxes**. Record correcting entries as of **January 1, Year 6**. Straight-line depreciation/amortization; residual zero unless stated.

| # | Facts |
|---|---|
| **A** | On January 2, Year 4, building improvements costing **$48,000** were debited entirely to Maintenance Expense. The improvements should have been capitalized. Estimated life **8 years**, residual **$0**. |
| **B** | On January 2, Year 4, ordinary repairs of **$9,600** were incorrectly capitalized as Equipment and depreciated SL over **6 years**, residual **$0**. |
| **C** | On January 1, Year 4, a **3-year** insurance premium of **$10,800** was paid and debited in full to Insurance Expense (should have been Prepaid Insurance, amortized evenly). |
| **D** | At December 31, Year 5, estimated warranty costs of **$7,500** were **not accrued**. The warranty claims remain unpaid at discovery. |
| **E** | A patent purchased January 1, Year 3 for **$240,000** has been amortized over its **20-year legal life**. Economic life is only **8 years**. Amortization for Years 3–5 has already been recorded under the 20-year life. |

**Required:**  
a. For **each** error A–E, compute the prior-period adjustment amount (show incorrect cumulative expense vs correct cumulative expense through December 31, Year 5) and prepare the **January 1, Year 6 correcting journal entry**.  
b. Prepare a **schedule** for Errors **A** and **E** only: for each year from the error origin through Year 5, show incorrect expense, correct expense, and annual income (over)/understatement; roll the cumulative income effect to the PPA amount.  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 6 period-end adjusting entries** related to: (1) depreciation of the Year 4 improvements, (2) amortization of remaining prepaid insurance, and (3) correct patent amortization for Year 6.  
d. On March 15, Year 6, Riverbend pays **$7,500** cash to settle the warranty claims that should have been accrued at December 31, Year 5. Prepare the settlement entry.  
e. Briefly state how the material errors should be **reported** (restatement vs prospective; effect on beginning retained earnings in comparative statements).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Prior-period adjustment computations and initial correcting JEs**

**Error A — Capital expenditure expensed**  
Incorrect expense recognized in Year 4: **$48,000**  
Correct depreciation Years 4–5: \(48{,}000 / 8 \times 2 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\)  
Prior-period adjustment (credit to RE — prior expenses overstated): \(48{,}000 - 12{,}000 = \mathbf{\$36{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Building Improvements | 48,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Improvements | | 12,000 |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 36,000 |
| *Correct capitalization of Year 4 improvements; catch up 2 years of SL depreciation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 48,000 = Cr 12,000 + 36,000. Balanced.

**Error B — Ordinary repairs capitalized**  
Incorrect depreciation Years 4–5: \(9{,}600 / 6 \times 2 = \mathbf{\$3{,}200}\)  
Correct expense (all in Year 4): **$9,600**  
Prior-period adjustment (debit to RE — prior net expense understated / income overstated): \(9{,}600 - 3{,}200 = \mathbf{\$6{,}400}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 6,400 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 3,200 | |
| Equipment | | 9,600 |
| *Remove improperly capitalized repairs and related accumulated depreciation* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,400 + 3,200 = Cr 9,600. Balanced.

**Error C — Prepaid insurance fully expensed**  
Incorrect expense (all in Year 4): **$10,800**  
Correct expense Years 4–5: \(10{,}800 / 3 \times 2 = \mathbf{\$7{,}200}\)  
Prior-period adjustment (credit to RE): \(10{,}800 - 7{,}200 = \mathbf{\$3{,}600}\)  
Remaining prepaid at January 1, Year 6: **$3,600** (one year left)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Prepaid Insurance | 3,600 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 3,600 |
| *Restore unexpired prepaid insurance at discovery date* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 3,600. Balanced.

**Error D — Failure to accrue warranty**  
Correct Year 5 warranty expense: **$7,500**; recorded: **$0**  
Prior-period adjustment (debit to RE): **$7,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 7,500 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 7,500 |
| *Record omitted Year 5 warranty accrual* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 7,500. Balanced.

**Error E — Patent amortization (unrealistic / wrong life)**  
Incorrect amortization Years 3–5: \(240{,}000 / 20 \times 3 = \mathbf{\$36{,}000}\)  
Correct amortization Years 3–5: \(240{,}000 / 8 \times 3 = \mathbf{\$90{,}000}\)  
Prior-period adjustment (debit to RE — prior expense understated): \(90{,}000 - 36{,}000 = \mathbf{\$54{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 54,000 | |
| Patent | | 54,000 |
| *Catch up understated amortization (direct write-down of patent carrying amount)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 54,000. Balanced.

**Net effect on Retained Earnings (credit positive):**  
\(36{,}000 - 6{,}400 + 3{,}600 - 7{,}500 - 54{,}000 = \mathbf{\$(28{,}300)}\) net decrease in beginning RE.

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — Errors A and E (through Dec 31, Year 5)**

**Error A — Building improvements ($48,000; 8-year SL)**

| Year | Incorrect expense | Correct expense | Income (over)/under |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 48,000 | 6,000 | (42,000) understated income |
| 5 | 0 | 6,000 | 6,000 overstated income |
| **Cum. through Y5** | **48,000** | **12,000** | **(36,000)** net understated income → **+36,000 PPA to RE** |

**Error E — Patent ($240,000; correct 8-year vs incorrect 20-year)**

| Year | Incorrect amort. | Correct amort. | Income (over)/under |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 12,000 | 30,000 | 18,000 overstated income |
| 4 | 12,000 | 30,000 | 18,000 overstated income |
| 5 | 12,000 | 30,000 | 18,000 overstated income |
| **Cum. through Y5** | **36,000** | **90,000** | **54,000** net overstated income → **−54,000 PPA to RE** |

**c. December 31, Year 6 — period-end adjusting entries (after correction)**

(1) Depreciation on improvements: \(48{,}000 / 8 = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Improvements | 6,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Improvements | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 6,000. Ending net CV of improvements after Y6: \(48{,}000 - 12{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000}\).

(2) Amortize remaining prepaid insurance (final year of 3-year policy): **$3,600**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Insurance Expense | 3,600 | |
| Prepaid Insurance | | 3,600 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 3,600. Prepaid ending balance = 0.

(3) Patent amortization Year 6 (correct remaining life):  
Carrying amount after PPA at Jan 1, Y6: \(240{,}000 - 90{,}000 = \mathbf{\$150{,}000}\)  
Remaining economic life: \(8 - 3 = 5\) years  
Year 6 amort: \(150{,}000 / 5 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000}\) (same as \(240{,}000 / 8\))

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense—Patent | 30,000 | |
| Patent | | 30,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 30,000. Ending patent CV: \(150{,}000 - 30{,}000 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}\).

**d. Settlement of previously unrecorded warranty (March 15, Year 6)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 7,500 | |
| Cash | | 7,500 |
| *Settle Year 5 warranty that was corrected via PPA; do **not** re-expense in Year 6* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 7,500. Balanced.

**e. Classification / presentation / disclosure**  
- Material errors discovered after issuance → **restatement and reissuance** of prior-period financial statements (as soon as practicable), **not** prospective treatment.  
- Cumulative income effect for periods not presented adjusts **beginning retained earnings** of the earliest period presented (shown as a prior-period adjustment in the RE statement).  
- Disclose nature of the error, effect on each line item and EPS for each prior period presented, and cumulative effect on beginning RE.  
- Distinct from a change in principle: error correction requires restatement/reissuance; voluntary principle changes are applied retrospectively at the next reporting date without “Big R” reissuance terminology.

**Key insight:** A material error correction is booked as a **prior-period adjustment to Retained Earnings** plus the related BS accounts so cumulative income is correct as of discovery; current-year income only reflects **correct going-forward** measurements (and settlements that no longer hit expense). Counterbalancing is handled in Q3.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Prior-period adjustments and Year-4 continuing measurement
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial PPA recognition JEs, cumulative schedule, period-end adjusting JEs after correction  
**Scenario:**  
Harborline Packaging LLC (December 31 year-end) discovers the following **material** errors on **January 1, Year 4**. Ignore income taxes.

| # | Facts |
|---|---|
| **1** | On January 1, Year 2, equipment improvements of **$36,000** were charged to Repairs Expense. Should have been capitalized; **6-year** life; residual **$0**; SL. |
| **2** | On January 1, Year 2, a **5-year** insurance premium of **$15,000** was expensed in full. |
| **3** | Interest payable of **$5,400** was not accrued at December 31, Year 3 (still unpaid at discovery). |
| **4** | On January 1, Year 1, land improvements costing **$50,000** were debited to Land. Life **10 years**, residual **$0**, SL. No depreciation has ever been recorded on these improvements. |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare January 1, Year 4 correcting entries for errors 1–4 (show PPA computations).  
b. Schedule for error 1 only: incorrect vs correct expense for Years 2 and 3 and the cumulative PPA.  
c. Prepare December 31, Year 4 adjusting entries for: depreciation of the Year 2 improvements, insurance amortization for Year 4, and depreciation of the land improvements.  
d. On February 10, Year 4, Harborline pays the **$5,400** interest that should have been accrued at December 31, Year 3. Record settlement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Correcting entries January 1, Year 4**

**Error 1**  
Correct dep Years 2–3: \(36{,}000 / 6 \times 2 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\)  
PPA credit to RE: \(36{,}000 - 12{,}000 = \mathbf{\$24{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment Improvements | 36,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Improvements | | 12,000 |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 24,000 |

**Check:** 36,000 = 12,000 + 24,000.

**Error 2**  
Correct expense Years 2–3: \(15{,}000 / 5 \times 2 = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\)  
Remaining prepaid Jan 1, Year 4: \(15{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$9{,}000}\)  
PPA credit to RE: **$9,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Prepaid Insurance | 9,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 9,000 |

**Check:** 9,000 = 9,000.

**Error 3**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 5,400 | |
| Interest Payable | | 5,400 |

**Check:** 5,400 = 5,400.

**Error 4**  
Correct depreciation Years 1–3: \(50{,}000 / 10 \times 3 = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\)  
PPA debit to RE: **$15,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Land Improvements | 50,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | 15,000 | |
| Land | | 50,000 |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Land Improvements | | 15,000 |

**Check:** Dr 50,000 + 15,000 = Cr 50,000 + 15,000. Balanced.

**Net RE effect (credit positive):** \(24{,}000 + 9{,}000 - 5{,}400 - 15{,}000 = \mathbf{\$12{,}600}\) net increase in beginning RE.

**b. Schedule — Error 1**

| Year | Incorrect expense | Correct expense | Income (over)/under |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 36,000 | 6,000 | (30,000) understated income |
| 3 | 0 | 6,000 | 6,000 overstated income |
| **Cum.** | **36,000** | **12,000** | **(24,000)** → **+24,000 PPA to RE** |

**c. December 31, Year 4 period-end adjusting entries**

(1) Improvements dep: \(36{,}000 / 6 = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Improvements | 6,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Improvements | | 6,000 |

(2) Insurance (3 years remain at start of Y4 → \(9{,}000 / 3 = \mathbf{\$3{,}000}\) per year, or \(15{,}000 / 5\)):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Insurance Expense | 3,000 | |
| Prepaid Insurance | | 3,000 |

(3) Land improvements dep: \(50{,}000 / 10 = \mathbf{\$5{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense—Land Improvements | 5,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Land Improvements | | 5,000 |

**Checks:** Each JE Dr = Cr.  
Ending CV improvements: \(36{,}000 - 12{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$18{,}000}\).  
Ending prepaid: \(9{,}000 - 3{,}000 = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\).  
Ending CV land improvements: \(50{,}000 - 15{,}000 - 5{,}000 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000}\).

**d. Settlement of omitted interest (February 10, Year 4)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 5,400 | |
| Cash | | 5,400 |

**Check:** 5,400 = 5,400. No Year 4 interest expense for this amount.

**Key insight:** Same error-correction mechanics as Q1 with all new numbers — capitalize what was expensed (or reverse improper capitalization), restore prepaid, reclass Land → depreciable asset, and settle omitted accruals against the liability (not current expense).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Counterbalancing inventory error: schedule, discovery timing, restatement presentation
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Counterbalancing inventory errors — income-effect schedule; JE only if discovered before self-correction; restatement even when no JE after self-correction  
**Scenario:**  
Cedarline Retail Co. uses a perpetual inventory system adjusted to physical count at each December 31. Purchases and sales are otherwise correct. The December 31, **Year 1** physical count **understated ending inventory by $22,000**. Year 2 ending inventory is correct. Pretax data as recorded (with the error) appear below.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beginning inventory | $40,000 | $58,000 (understated) |
| Net purchases | 180,000 | 195,000 |
| Ending inventory | 58,000 (understated) | 70,000 |
| COGS (as recorded) | 162,000 | 183,000 |
| Net sales | 250,000 | 280,000 |
| Other expenses | 40,000 | 45,000 |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare a **corrected income-effect schedule** for Years 1 and 2 showing COGS, gross profit, and pretax income as recorded vs as corrected, and prove the error is **counterbalancing**.  
b. Record any necessary journal entry if the error is discovered:  
 (1) in Year 1 before books are closed;  
 (2) in Year 2 before the Year 1 error has self-corrected through COGS;  
 (3) in Year 3 after self-correction.  
c. Assume comparative Year 2 statements are presented and the error is material. State beginning RE and Year 1 pretax income **as restated**, given that Year 1 RE beginning was **$90,000** and no dividends were declared in Year 1 or Year 2. (Use pretax amounts; ignore tax.)  
d. List the **minimum ASC 250 disclosures** when restating for the inventory error.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Income-effect schedule**

**As recorded (with error)**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| BI | 40,000 | 58,000 |
| + Purchases | 180,000 | 195,000 |
| − EI | (58,000) | (70,000) |
| **COGS** | **162,000** | **183,000** |
| Sales | 250,000 | 280,000 |
| Gross profit | 88,000 | 97,000 |
| Other expenses | 40,000 | 45,000 |
| **Pretax income** | **48,000** | **52,000** |

**Corrected** (add $22,000 to Y1 EI and to Y2 BI)

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| BI | 40,000 | **80,000** |
| + Purchases | 180,000 | 195,000 |
| − EI | **(80,000)** | (70,000) |
| **COGS** | **140,000** | **205,000** |
| Sales | 250,000 | 280,000 |
| Gross profit | 110,000 | 75,000 |
| Other expenses | 40,000 | 45,000 |
| **Pretax income** | **70,000** | **30,000** |

| Effect | Year 1 | Year 2 | Two-year total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| COGS (over)/under | 22,000 over | 22,000 under | 0 |
| Pretax income (over)/under | 22,000 under | 22,000 over | **0** |

**Proof of counterbalancing:** Two-year combined pretax income recorded = \(48{,}000 + 52{,}000 = 100{,}000\); corrected = \(70{,}000 + 30{,}000 = 100{,}000\). Combined RE at end of Year 2 is correct even without a correcting entry after self-correction.

**b. Journal entries by discovery timing**

**(1) Discovered in Year 1 (year of error) — correct inventory and COGS**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 22,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | | 22,000 |

**Check:** 22,000 = 22,000.

**(2) Discovered in Year 2 before self-correction — prior-period adjustment**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 22,000 | |
| Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment | | 22,000 |
| *Restore understated beginning inventory / RE; Year 2 COGS will then be measured correctly* | | |

**Check:** 22,000 = 22,000.

**(3) Discovered in Year 3 after self-correction — **no journal entry****  
The Year 1 understatement of income reversed through Year 2 COGS. Combined RE is already correct. **However**, if comparative statements include Year 1 and/or Year 2, those periods are **restated** for presentation.

**c. Restatement presentation (material error; comparative)**  
- Restated Year 1 pretax income: **$70,000** (not $48,000).  
- Restated Year 1 ending RE / Year 2 beginning RE: \(90{,}000 + 70{,}000 = \mathbf{\$160{,}000}\) (as recorded Y1 ending RE was \(90{,}000 + 48{,}000 = 138{,}000\); difference = **$22,000** PPA if shown in Y2).  
- Restated Year 2 pretax income: **$30,000** (not $52,000).  
- Year 2 ending RE (no dividends): \(160{,}000 + 30{,}000 = \mathbf{\$190{,}000}\) (same as uncorrected path \(138{,}000 + 52{,}000 = 190{,}000\)).

**d. Disclosure (ASC 250-10-50-7 / 50-8)**  
1. Statement that previously issued FS have been **restated** and description of the **nature** of the error.  
2. Effect of the correction on **each FS line item** and any **per-share** amounts for each prior period presented.  
3. **Cumulative effect** on retained earnings (or other equity) as of the beginning of the earliest period presented.  
4. Effects (gross and net of tax, when tax applies) disclosed in the annual report for the year adjustments are recorded and in interim reports after the adjustment date. Disclosures need not be repeated in subsequent periods.

**Key insight:** Counterbalancing inventory errors reverse within two periods; a **PPA JE is needed only if discovered before self-correction**. Materiality still drives **restatement of prior FS** even when the books have already self-corrected and no entry is made in Year 3.

### Q4 — MC — Error correction vs accounting change classification
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Classification — error correction (PPA/restatement) vs change in estimate (prospective) vs change in principle (retrospective)  
**Question:**  
Which of the following is accounted for as a **correction of an error** (prior-period adjustment and restatement if material), rather than as a change in accounting estimate or a change in accounting principle?

- A) Switching from the straight-line to the double-declining-balance depreciation method because management believes DDB better matches usage (asset remains properly depreciable; prior estimates were good-faith).  
- B) Revising the useful life of machinery from 10 years to 7 years based on new engineering information that became available this year.  
- C) Discovering that last year’s ending inventory was overstated by $40,000 because of a clerical addition error in the physical count.  
- D) Voluntarily changing from the weighted-average inventory method to FIFO because FIFO is preferable.

**Answer:** **C.**  
A clerical mistake in applying the inventory count is an **error in previously issued financial statements** (mathematical mistake). Material errors are corrected by restating prior periods and recording a prior-period adjustment when the books have not yet self-corrected.  
- **A** is a change in depreciation **method** treated as a change in estimate effected by a change in principle → **prospective**.  
- **B** is a pure **change in estimate** → **prospective**.  
- **D** is a voluntary **change in accounting principle** → generally **retrospective** (not labeled an error restatement).

### Q5 — MC — When no correcting entry is required
**LO:** LO 21-4  
**Concept:** Classification of error-correction mechanics — counterbalancing after self-correction; IS-only misclassification  
**Question:**  
On December 31, Year 1, Pinewood Co. overstated ending inventory by $15,000 (math error). Purchases were correct. Year 2 ending inventory is correct. The error is first discovered in **March of Year 3**. The error is material to Year 1 and Year 2 taken separately. Which statement is **correct**?

- A) Pinewood must debit Retained Earnings—Prior Period Adjustment and credit Inventory for $15,000 in Year 3.  
- B) Pinewood records no journal entry in Year 3, but must restate prior-period financial statements presented comparatively.  
- C) Pinewood records no journal entry and need not restate any prior financial statements because the error counterbalanced.  
- D) Pinewood treats the $15,000 as a change in accounting estimate and adjusts Year 3 COGS prospectively only.

**Answer:** **B.**  
After self-correction, combined retained earnings is already correct, so **no Year 3 correcting JE** is made. Because the error was **material**, previously issued statements are still **restated** (and disclosures provided). A is the entry used **before** self-correction (e.g., discovery in Year 2). C wrongly skips restatement. D misclassifies an error as an estimate change.
