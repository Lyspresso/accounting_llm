# Agent 364 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-12

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for debt settlement and restructuring  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Troubled debt restructuring (TDR):** creditor grants a concession it would not otherwise consider because of the debtor’s financial difficulties (ASC Glossary / ASC 470-60)
- **Settlement — transfer of assets (debtor):** remeasure assets to fair value (gain/loss on disposal), derecognize debt and assets, recognize **gain on restructuring** for excess of debt carrying amount over fair value of assets transferred
- **Settlement — transfer of equity (debtor):** record equity at fair value; **gain on restructuring** = debt carrying amount − equity fair value
- **Modification of terms — total future cash payments < debt carrying amount:** reduce carrying amount of payable to **undiscounted** sum of future cash payments (principal + stated interest); recognize **gain on restructuring**; **no** subsequent interest expense (all future payments reduce principal)
- **Modification of terms — total future cash payments > debt carrying amount:** **no** gain at restructure; keep carrying amount; compute **new effective interest rate** that equates PV of future cash payments to carrying amount; amortize with effective-interest schedule
- **Initial recognition JEs:** settlement/restructure-date entries (asset revaluation, equity issuance, note rewrite, gain)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** effective-interest amortization when restructured CFs exceed carrying amount
- **Period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis):** accrue effective interest between payment dates (or at year-end when payment is the next day)
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JEs:** full settlement by assets or equity; final principal payoff at maturity after TDR

### Q1 — CORE — Debtor TDR: asset settlement, equity settlement, modification (both CF cases), amort schedule
**LO:** LO 16-12  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of troubled debt settlement and restructuring (assets, equity, modification); subsequent effective-interest schedule when restructured cash flows exceed carrying amount; maturity payoff  
**Scenario:**

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end effective-interest accruals after TDR (emphasis)
**LO:** LO 16-12  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JEs for effective interest after a CF > CV restructuring; subsequent measurement schedule; January payment entries; maturity settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Redwood Outfitters Corp.** has a **December 31** year-end. On its books at **December 31, Year 1**, Redwood reports:
- Note Payable (face) **$800,000**
- Interest Payable **$80,000** (full year of **10%** interest on the note; cash interest was due **January 1, Year 2** and was **not** paid)

On **January 1, Year 2**, North Cascades Lender agrees to a **troubled debt restructuring**:
- Accrued interest is folded into the restructured payable (forgiven as a separate interest claim)
- Face remains **$800,000**, due **January 1, Year 5**
- Stated interest reduced to **6%**, payable **each January 1** for Year 3, Year 4, and Year 5 (three payments of **$48,000** on Jan 1 Y3, Jan 1 Y4, and Jan 1 Y5)
- Principal **$800,000** also paid on **January 1, Year 5** (with the final interest payment)

Carrying amount at restructure: \(\mathbf{\$880{,}000}\).

**Required:**  
(a) Compute total restructured cash flows; confirm the **CF > CV** case; compute the new effective annual rate (4 decimal places as a percent).  
(b) Prepare the **3-year effective-interest amortization schedule** (payment dates = each January 1 beginning Y3).  
(c) Record the **January 1, Year 2 initial recognition** restructure entry.  
(d) **Emphasis — period-end adjusting:** Record the **December 31, Year 2**, **December 31, Year 3**, and **December 31, Year 4** adjusting entries to accrue effective interest (credit **Interest Payable**).  
(e) Record the **January 1, Year 3** and **January 1, Year 4** cash payments (settle interest payable + reduce note).  
(f) Record the **January 1, Year 5** final interest payment and **maturity settlement** of principal (combined or separate clear entries).  
(g) Briefly explain why Redwood recognizes **interest expense** after this restructuring but would **not** if total future cash payments had been **less than** $880,000.

**(g) Why interest expense exists here**

Total future cash payments (**$944,000**) **exceed** the pre-restructure carrying amount (**$880,000**). The **$64,000** difference is recognized as **interest expense** over the remaining term via the new effective rate. **Key insight (emphasis):** After a CF > CV TDR, year-end **adjusting entries** still accrue **effective** interest on the **carrying amount**—not the old contractual 10% and not automatically the new 6% stated rate. The stated cash payment on the following January 1 then clears the accrual and reduces the note for the residual.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of debtor accounting for term modification
**LO:** LO 16-12  
**Concept:** Classify the correct debtor measurement when only terms are modified in a troubled debt restructuring  
**Question:**  
On the date of a troubled debt restructuring involving **only a modification of terms** (no asset or equity transfer), the debtor’s total **future cash payments** specified by the new terms (undiscounted, including amounts labeled interest and principal) are **less than** the **carrying amount** of the payable. The debtor should:

- A) Leave the carrying amount unchanged and compute a new effective interest rate for prospective interest recognition  
- B) Reduce the carrying amount of the payable to the total future cash payments and recognize a **gain on restructuring**; recognize **no** interest expense in future periods  
- C) Reduce the carrying amount to the **present value** of future cash payments discounted at the original effective rate and recognize a gain  
- D) Recognize a gain equal to the reduction in face value only, and continue accruing interest at the original contractual rate  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Under ASC 470-60, when total future cash payments (undiscounted) are less than the carrying amount of the payable, the debtor reduces the carrying amount to those total payments and recognizes a gain on restructuring equal to the reduction. All subsequent payments reduce the payable; there is no interest expense after the restructuring. Choice A is the treatment when total future cash payments **exceed** carrying amount. Choice C incorrectly uses discounted PV / original rate (creditor impairment logic, not the debtor modification model in this LO). Choice D misstates both the gain measurement and subsequent interest.

### Q5 — MC — Settlement by assets: two gain components
**LO:** LO 16-12  
**Concept:** Distinguish gain on disposal of assets from gain on restructuring of debt in a full asset settlement  
**Question:**  
A debtor settles a note payable (carrying amount **$500,000**, including accrued interest) by transferring equipment with carrying amount **$320,000** and fair value **$410,000**. Ignoring taxes, the debtor’s income effects in the period of settlement are:

- A) Gain on restructuring only of $180,000  
- B) Gain on disposal of equipment $90,000 and gain on restructuring of debt $90,000  
- C) Gain on disposal of equipment $90,000 and gain on restructuring of debt $180,000  
- D) Loss on disposal $90,000 and gain on restructuring $180,000  

**Answer:** **B.**  
First remeasure the asset to FV: gain on disposal \(410{,}000 - 320{,}000 = \$90{,}000\). Then settle the debt: gain on restructuring \(500{,}000 - 410{,}000 = \$90{,}000\). Total pretax income effect \(180{,}000\), but it is **two** gains (disposal vs. restructuring), not a single restructuring gain of $180,000 (A) or mismatched amounts (C, D).
