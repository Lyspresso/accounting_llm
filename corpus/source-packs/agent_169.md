# Agent 169 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-7

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Prepare the operating cash flow section of the statement of cash flows using the direct method  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Convert accrual **sales** to **cash collected from customers** (AR T-account / rollforward; write-offs and recoveries are noncash)
- Convert **COGS** to **cash paid to suppliers** via purchases (inventory schedule) and accounts payable
- Convert operating expenses to **cash paid to employees / other suppliers** (exclude bad debt; adjust prepaids and accruals)
- Convert interest and tax expense to **cash paid for interest** and **cash paid for taxes** (premium/discount amortization; payables)
- **Subsequent measurement schedules:** AR, inventory/AP, prepaid/salaries, interest, and tax conversion schedules that roll beginning balances to ending balances
- Direct-method operating section reports **major classes** of gross receipts and payments; **same net OCF** as the indirect method
- Required **reconciliation of net income to operating cash flows** when the direct method is used (ASC 230)
- Noncash items (depreciation, amortization, gains/losses) **do not appear** as direct-method operating lines
- Bond interest: **cash coupon** under the direct method; discount/premium amortization appears only in the reconciling schedule
- Maturity/settlement of bonds: principal is **financing**; periodic cash interest remains **operating**

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Bond discount amortization schedule, direct-method interest, maturity settlement, and classification
**LO:** LO 22-7  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement amortization schedule for bonds issued at a discount; cash paid for interest under the direct method; period-end interest JE; maturity settlement JE and SCF classification  
**Scenario:**  
On **January 1, 2024**, **Cascade Ridge Corp.** issues **$150,000** face, **4-year** bonds dated that day. Stated (coupon) rate is **4%** payable annually each **December 31**. Market (effective) rate is **6%**. Issue proceeds equal the present value of the bond cash flows at 6%:

\[
PV = \$150{,}000 \times PVF(6\%,4) + \$6{,}000 \times PVA(6\%,4)
\]

Using \(PVF(6\%,4) = 0.792094\) and \(PVA(6\%,4) = 3.465106\):

\[
PV = \$150{,}000 \times 0.792094 + \$6{,}000 \times 3.465106 = \$118{,}814 + \$20{,}791 = \mathbf{\$139{,}605}
\]

Cascade Ridge uses the **effective-interest method** and presents operating cash flows under the **direct method**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **effective-interest amortization schedule** (subsequent measurement) for the full four-year life (cash interest, interest expense, discount amortization, carrying value). Round interest expense to the nearest dollar so the final carrying value equals face.  
b. For **2024**, state the amount reported as **cash paid for interest** in the direct-method operating section. Prepare the **December 31, 2024** compound journal entry for interest.  
c. On **December 31, 2027**, Cascade Ridge pays the bonds at maturity (and pays the final coupon the same day). Prepare the **settlement / maturity JE(s)**. Classify each cash flow on the 2027 statement of cash flows (operating vs financing).  
d. **Classification / presentation:** (1) List the minimum major classes of operating cash receipts and payments under the direct method (ASC 230). (2) State what supporting schedule is required when the direct method is used. (3) Briefly contrast how 2024 interest appears under the direct method vs the reconciling (indirect) schedule.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Effective-interest amortization schedule (subsequent measurement)**

Cash interest each year = \(150{,}000 \times 4\% = \mathbf{\$6{,}000}\).

| Date | Cash (4%) | Interest expense (6% × CV) | Discount amortization | Carrying value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, 2024 | — | — | — | 139,605 |
| Dec 31, 2024 | 6,000 | 8,376 | 2,376 | 141,981 |
| Dec 31, 2025 | 6,000 | 8,519 | 2,519 | 144,500 |
| Dec 31, 2026 | 6,000 | 8,670 | 2,670 | 147,170 |
| Dec 31, 2027 | 6,000 | 8,830 | 2,830 | 150,000 |
| **Totals** | **24,000** | **34,395** | **10,395** | |

Checks:  
- Y1: \(139{,}605 \times 0.06 = 8{,}376.30 \rightarrow 8{,}376\); amort \(8{,}376 - 6{,}000 = 2{,}376\); CV \(139{,}605 + 2{,}376 = 141{,}981\).  
- Y2: \(141{,}981 \times 0.06 = 8{,}518.86 \rightarrow 8{,}519\); amort \(2{,}519\); CV \(144{,}500\).  
- Y3: \(144{,}500 \times 0.06 = 8{,}670\); amort \(2{,}670\); CV \(147{,}170\).  
- Y4: plug amort \(150{,}000 - 147{,}170 = 2{,}830\); interest expense \(6{,}000 + 2{,}830 = 8{,}830\).  
- Total amort \(2{,}376 + 2{,}519 + 2{,}670 + 2{,}830 = 10{,}395 = 150{,}000 - 139{,}605\).

**b. 2024 direct-method cash interest and period-end JE**

**Cash paid for interest (direct method) = $6,000** (the cash coupon only). Discount amortization is **noncash** and does **not** appear as a direct-method operating line.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 8,376 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 2,376 |
| Cash | | 6,000 |
| *2024 effective-interest entry; cash coupon under direct method* | | |

**Check:** Dr 8,376 = Cr \(2{,}376 + 6{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**c. Maturity settlement (December 31, 2027)**

Final interest (same day as maturity):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 8,830 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 2,830 |
| Cash | | 6,000 |
| *Final period interest and last discount amortization* | | |

**Check:** Dr 8,830 = Cr \(2{,}830 + 6{,}000\). **Balanced.** Discount balance after entry: \(10{,}395 - 10{,}395 = 0\); CV = face $150,000.

Retirement of principal:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 150,000 | |
| Cash | | 150,000 |
| *Pay bonds at maturity* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 150,000.

**2027 SCF classification of cash:**
- Cash paid for interest **$6,000** → **operating** outflow (direct method).
- Cash paid to retire bonds **$150,000** → **financing** outflow.
- Discount amortization is **noncash** (no separate cash line).

**d. Classification / presentation / disclosure**

1. **Minimum major classes (ASC 230-10-45-25):**  
   (a) cash collected from customers; (b) interest and dividends received; (c) other operating cash receipts; (d) cash paid to employees and other suppliers of goods or services; (e) interest paid; (f) income taxes paid; (g) other operating cash payments.

2. **Required supporting schedule:** reconciliation of **net income to net cash flow from operating activities** (indirect-method equivalent). Direct-method and reconciling totals must agree.

3. **2024 interest contrast:**  
   - **Direct method:** report cash paid for interest **$(6,000)**.  
   - **Reconciliation / indirect:** start from net income (which already deducted interest expense **$8,376**) and **add back** noncash discount amortization **$2,376**, so the net effect on OCF is still a **$6,000** cash outflow.

**Key insight:** The amortization schedule is the subsequent measurement tool that splits each period’s interest into **cash coupon** (direct-method operating) and **noncash amortization** (reconciling item only). At maturity, only the **principal** cash settlement is financing; the final coupon remains operating.

### Q4 — MC (classification under the direct method)
**LO:** LO 22-7  
**Concept:** Classification of items in the direct-method operating section versus noncash reconciling items  

**Question 1:**  
Riverbend Components (from Q1) prepares its statement of cash flows using the **direct method**. Which item appears as a **separate line** in the **operating activities** section?

- A) Depreciation expense of $75,000  
- B) Gain on sale of land of $15,000  
- C) Cash collected from customers of $920,000  
- D) Amortization of bond premium of $3,000  

**Answer:** **C.** The direct method reports major classes of **gross cash** receipts and payments. Depreciation, gains/losses, and premium amortization are noncash (or non-operating cash) reconciling items; they appear in the **reconciliation schedule**, not as direct-method operating lines. Cash collected from customers is a required major class.

**Question 2:**  
When a company elects the **direct method** for operating cash flows, which statement is correct?

- A) No reconciliation of net income to operating cash flows is required  
- B) A reconciliation of net income to net cash from operating activities must be provided in a separate schedule  
- C) Investing and financing sections may be prepared using the indirect method  
- D) Bad debt expense is reported as an operating cash outflow under the direct method  

**Answer:** **B.** ASC 230 requires a reconciliation of net income to net operating cash flow when the direct method is used (non-NFP). Investing and financing always report gross cash flows (not an “indirect method”). Bad debt expense is noncash and is excluded from direct-method cash payments.
