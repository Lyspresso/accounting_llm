# Agent 167 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-4

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Prepare the financing activities section of the statement of cash flows  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Financing section: gross cash inflows (debt proceeds, equity issuances) and outflows (debt principal, dividends, treasury stock, debt retirements, debt issue costs)
- **Subsequent measurement schedules** (emphasis): T-account / rollforward schedules for bonds (discount), installment notes, finance lease liabilities, and dividends payable that feed the financing section
- Initial recognition JEs for cash equity issuance and new borrowing (financing inflows)
- Settlement JE for early bond retirement (cash paid = financing outflow; gain/loss = operating under indirect method)
- Period-end adjusting JE: amortization of remaining bond discount (operating interest effect — not a financing line)
- Finance lease / installment note: principal portion = financing outflow; interest portion = operating
- Dividends declared vs cash dividends paid (dividends payable roll-forward)
- Multi-period installment amortization schedule mapped to SCF financing lines
- Classification/presentation: financing vs operating vs noncash investing/financing disclosure
- Number-variant twin with all facts and amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Northvale Industrial: financing section with subsequent measurement schedules
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement / rollforward schedules for bonds, installment note, finance lease, and dividends payable; stock issuance JE; bond retirement settlement JE; discount amortization adjusting JE; prepare cash flows from financing activities  
**Scenario:**  
**Northvale Industrial Group** prepares a statement of cash flows for the year ended **December 31, 20X5**. Selected comparative balances and facts follow.

| Account | Dec 31, 20X4 | Dec 31, 20X5 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | $275,000 | $148,000 |
| Installment note payable (nontrade) | 80,000 | 58,000 |
| Bonds payable | 400,000 | 250,000 |
| Discount on bonds payable | 12,000 | 6,000 |
| Finance lease liability | 75,000 | 54,000 |
| Common stock, $10 par | 200,000 | 260,000 |
| Additional paid-in capital | 95,000 | 155,000 |
| Treasury stock (at cost) | 0 | 40,000 |
| Dividends payable | 15,000 | 9,000 |
| Retained earnings | 310,000 | 428,000 |

**Additional information for 20X5:**
1. Net income was **$180,000**.
2. On **January 15, 20X5**, Northvale retired bonds with face value **$150,000** by paying **$147,500** cash. Unamortized discount related to the retired bonds was **$4,200**. No other bonds were issued or retired during the year.
3. No new installment borrowings occurred. Cash paid on the installment note totaled **$27,200**, of which **$5,200** was interest; the remainder reduced principal.
4. No new finance leases were entered. Cash payments on the existing finance lease totaled **$28,500**, of which **$7,500** was interest; the remainder reduced principal.
5. Common stock was issued **for cash** only (no stock-for-asset exchanges).
6. Northvale purchased treasury stock for cash of **$40,000** (cost method); no treasury reissuances.
7. All changes in dividends payable relate to cash dividends declared and paid (no stock dividends).
8. Year-end amortization of discount on the **remaining** bonds is recorded on December 31, 20X5 (straight-line remainder for this problem).

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **initial recognition** journal entry for the common stock issuance (cash financing inflow).  
(b) Record the **settlement** journal entry for the January 15 bond retirement. Identify the amount that appears in the financing section and the amount that affects the operating section under the **indirect** method.  
(c) Prepare a **subsequent measurement / rollforward schedule** for: (i) bonds payable and related discount (including retirement and year-end amortization of remaining discount), (ii) installment note principal, (iii) finance lease liability, (iv) dividends declared and **cash dividends paid**.  
(d) Record the **December 31, 20X5 period-end adjusting entry** to amortize discount on the remaining bonds.  
(e) Prepare the **Cash flows from financing activities** section of the 20X5 statement of cash flows.  
(f) Classify: (i) bond retirement loss, (ii) installment interest of $5,200, (iii) finance lease interest of $7,500, (iv) commencement of a new finance lease (none this year — state SCF treatment if one had been signed with no cash at commencement).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Common stock issuance — initial recognition (cash)**

Par increase = $260,000 − $200,000 = **$60,000** → 6,000 shares × $10.  
APIC increase = $155,000 − $95,000 = **$60,000**.  
Cash proceeds = $60,000 + $60,000 = **$120,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 120,000 | |
| Common stock, $10 par | | 60,000 |
| Additional paid-in capital | | 60,000 |
| *Issue 6,000 shares for cash* | | |

**Check:** Dr 120,000 = Cr 120,000. **Balanced.**  
→ **Financing inflow: Proceeds from issuance of common stock $120,000**.

**(b) Bond retirement — settlement JE (January 15, 20X5)**

Carrying amount of retired bonds = $150,000 − $4,200 = **$145,800**.  
Loss on retirement = $147,500 − $145,800 = **$1,700**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds payable | 150,000 | |
| Loss on bond retirement | 1,700 | |
| Discount on bonds payable | | 4,200 |
| Cash | | 147,500 |
| *Retire $150,000 face bonds for cash $147,500* | | |

**Check:** Dr 151,700 = Cr 151,700. **Balanced.**

- **Financing outflow:** Payment to retire bonds **$(147,500)** (cash paid).  
- **Operating (indirect method):** Add back loss on bond retirement **$1,700**.  
Bonds payable check: $400,000 − $150,000 = **$250,000** ending.

**(c) Subsequent measurement / rollforward schedules (emphasis)**

**(i) Bonds payable and discount on bonds payable**

| Bonds payable | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning balance | $400,000 |
| Less: face retired | (150,000) |
| Ending balance | **$250,000** |

| Discount on bonds payable | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning balance | $12,000 |
| Less: discount written off on retirement | (4,200) |
| Less: year-end amortization of remaining discount (period-end adj.) | (1,800) |
| Ending balance | **$6,000** |

Amortization of remaining discount = $12,000 − $4,200 − $6,000 = **$1,800**.  
*(Discount amortization increases interest expense / reduces net income; under the indirect method it is added back if already deducted through interest expense that is not a separate cash line — see part d. It is **not** a financing cash flow.)*

**(ii) Installment note payable**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning balance | $80,000 |
| New borrowings | 0 |
| Cash paid on note | $27,200 |
| Less: interest portion (operating) | (5,200) |
| Principal reduction (financing) | **(22,000)** |
| Ending balance | **$58,000** |

Check: $80,000 − $22,000 = $58,000 ✓ (also equals beginning − ending balance with no new borrowings).

**(iii) Finance lease liability**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning balance | $75,000 |
| New leases | 0 |
| Cash lease payments | $28,500 |
| Less: interest portion (operating) | (7,500) |
| Principal reduction (financing) | **(21,000)** |
| Ending balance | **$54,000** |

Check: $75,000 − $21,000 = $54,000 ✓

**(iv) Dividends payable / retained earnings**

Dividends declared:  
\[
310{,}000 + 180{,}000 - \text{dividends declared} = 428{,}000 \implies \text{dividends declared} = \mathbf{\$62{,}000}
\]

| Dividends Payable | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | | 15,000 |
| Dividends declared | | 62,000 |
| Cash dividends paid | 68,000 | |
| End. bal. | | **9,000** |

Check: $15,000 + $62,000 − $68,000 = $9,000 ✓  
→ **Cash dividends paid = $68,000** (financing outflow).

**(d) December 31, 20X5 — Period-end discount amortization (remaining bonds)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest expense | 1,800 | |
| Discount on bonds payable | | 1,800 |
| *Amortize discount on remaining $250,000 face bonds* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,800 = Cr 1,800. **Balanced.**  
SCF effect: **not** a financing line. Under the **indirect** method, amortization of discount is already in interest expense (reducing NI); if the company starts from NI, the cash interest paid may differ — the amortization itself is a noncash portion of interest expense. *(Do not list $1,800 as a financing outflow.)*

**(e) Cash flows from financing activities — 20X5**

```
Cash flows from financing activities
  Proceeds from issuance of common stock .......... $ 120,000
  Payment to retire bonds .........................  (147,500)
  Principal payments on installment note ..........   (22,000)
  Principal payments on finance lease .............   (21,000)
  Cash dividends paid .............................   (68,000)
  Purchase of treasury stock ......................   (40,000)
  Net cash used by financing activities ........... $(178,500)
```

Cross-check: $120,000 − $147,500 − $22,000 − $21,000 − $68,000 − $40,000 = **$(178,500)**.

**(f) Classification notes**

| Item | SCF classification |
|---|---|
| (i) Loss on bond retirement $1,700 | **Operating** (add back under indirect method); cash paid for retirement is **financing** |
| (ii) Installment interest $5,200 | **Operating** (interest); principal $22,000 is **financing** |
| (iii) Finance lease interest $7,500 | **Operating** (interest); principal $21,000 is **financing** |
| (iv) New finance lease at commencement (ROU ↔ lease liability, no cash) | **Noncash investing and financing** disclosure only — **not** on the face of financing cash flows until principal is paid |

**Key insight:** Financing activities report **cash** changes in nontrade debt and equity, **cash** dividends, and treasury stock. **Subsequent measurement schedules** (bonds/discount, note, lease, dividends payable) reconstruct each line: report **gross** inflows and outflows; split installment/lease payments into interest (operating) vs principal (financing); bond retirement cash is financing even when a loss hits net income.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Harborline Marine: multi-period installment schedule → financing outflows
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Installment note life cycle — initial recognition JE; multi-year subsequent measurement (amortization) schedule; period-end payment JEs; principal as financing outflow each year; SCF financing presentation  
**Scenario:**  
On **January 1, Year 1**, **Harborline Marine LLC** borrows **$100,000** cash by signing a **3-year installment note**. The note bears **6%** interest and requires **three equal annual payments** of **$37,411** each on **December 31** of Years 1–3 (each payment includes interest and principal). Harborline’s year-end is December 31. Ignore debt issue costs. No other financing activity occurs in Years 1–3 except these note payments (no dividends, no equity issues).

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **initial recognition** journal entry on January 1, Year 1. State the SCF financing line for Year 1 related to the borrowing.  
(b) Prepare a **subsequent measurement (amortization) schedule** for Years 1–3 with columns: year, beginning carrying amount, interest expense (6%), cash payment, principal reduction, ending carrying amount.  
(c) Record the **December 31, Year 1 payment journal entry** (interest + principal).  
(d) Record the **December 31, Year 2 payment journal entry**.  
(e) Record the **December 31, Year 3 final payment journal entry** (show zero ending balance).  
(f) For each of Years 1–3, show the **Cash flows from financing activities** line(s) related to this note (and, briefly, how interest affects the operating section under the indirect method).  
(g) Prove that cumulative principal reductions over three years equal the original **$100,000** borrowing.

**Answer key:**

**(a) January 1, Year 1 — Initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 100,000 | |
| Installment note payable | | 100,000 |
| *Borrow $100,000 on 3-year 6% installment note* | | |

**Check:** Dr 100,000 = Cr 100,000. **Balanced.**  
**Year 1 SCF financing:** Proceeds from issuance of installment note **$100,000** (cash inflow).

**(b) Subsequent measurement / amortization schedule (emphasis)**

Interest each year = beginning carrying amount × 6%.  
Principal reduction = cash payment − interest.  
Payment given: **$37,411** per year.

| Year | Beginning CA | Interest (6%) | Cash payment | Principal reduction | Ending CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $100,000 | $6,000 | $37,411 | $31,411 | $68,589 |
| 2 | 68,589 | 4,115 | 37,411 | 33,296 | 35,293 |
| 3 | 35,293 | 2,118 | 37,411 | 35,293 | **0** |

**Schedule math checks:**  
- Y1: $100,000 × 0.06 = **$6,000**; principal = $37,411 − $6,000 = **$31,411**; end = $100,000 − $31,411 = **$68,589**.  
- Y2: $68,589 × 0.06 = **$4,115** (rounded to nearest dollar); principal = $37,411 − $4,115 = **$33,296**; end = $68,589 − $33,296 = **$35,293**.  
- Y3: Interest = $37,411 − $35,293 = **$2,118** (or $35,293 × 0.06 ≈ $2,118); principal = **$35,293**; end = **$0**.  
- Cumulative principal: $31,411 + $33,296 + $35,293 = **$100,000** ✓

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — Payment JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest expense | 6,000 | |
| Installment note payable | 31,411 | |
| Cash | | 37,411 |
| *Year 1 installment payment* | | |

**Check:** Dr 37,411 = Cr 37,411. **Balanced.**

**(d) December 31, Year 2 — Payment JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest expense | 4,115 | |
| Installment note payable | 33,296 | |
| Cash | | 37,411 |
| *Year 2 installment payment* | | |

**Check:** Dr 37,411 = Cr 37,411. **Balanced.**

**(e) December 31, Year 3 — Final payment JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest expense | 2,118 | |
| Installment note payable | 35,293 | |
| Cash | | 37,411 |
| *Year 3 final installment; note paid in full* | | |

**Check:** Dr 37,411 = Cr 37,411. **Balanced.** Ending note balance = **$0**.

**(f) SCF presentation by year (this note only)**

| Year | Financing activities | Operating (indirect method notes) |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Proceeds from installment note **$100,000**; Principal payment on installment note **$(31,411)**; Net financing related to note **$68,589** provided | Interest expense $6,000 reduces NI; cash interest paid is operating (not a separate financing line) |
| 2 | Principal payment on installment note **$(33,296)** | Interest expense $4,115 in NI / operating |
| 3 | Principal payment on installment note **$(35,293)** | Interest expense $2,118 in NI / operating |

Illustrative Year 1 financing section excerpt:

```
Cash flows from financing activities
  Proceeds from issuance of installment note ...... $ 100,000
  Principal payments on installment note ..........   (31,411)
  Net cash provided by financing activities ....... $  68,589
```

**(g) Life-cycle principal proof**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Original financing inflow (borrowing) | $100,000 |
| Y1 principal outflow | (31,411) |
| Y2 principal outflow | (33,296) |
| Y3 principal outflow | (35,293) |
| Cumulative principal outflows | **$(100,000)** |
| Net financing cash over life related to principal | **$0** |

Total cash paid over life = 3 × $37,411 = **$112,233** = principal $100,000 + total interest ($6,000 + $4,115 + $2,118) = **$12,233**.

**Key insight:** The **amortization schedule is the subsequent measurement engine** for installment debt. Only the **principal** column hits financing activities each period; interest is operating. Over the note’s life, cumulative financing principal outflows reverse the original borrowing inflow.

### Q4 — MC — Classify items for the financing section
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Classification of cash flows — financing vs operating vs investing vs noncash  
**Question:**  
Which of the following is reported as a **cash outflow in the financing activities** section of the statement of cash flows?

- A) Cash paid for interest on a long-term installment note  
- B) Cash paid to purchase equipment  
- C) Cash paid for the principal portion of a finance lease payment  
- D) Acquisition of a building by signing a long-term note (no cash exchanged at signing)

**Answer:** **C.**  
Under ASC 230 / LO 22-4, principal payments on finance leases and other nontrade borrowings are **financing** outflows. Interest (A) is **operating**. Equipment purchase (B) is **investing**. Signing a note for a building with no cash (D) is a **noncash investing and financing** disclosure, not a face financing cash flow.

### Q5 — MC — Compute net cash from financing activities
**LO:** LO 22-4  
**Concept:** Aggregate mixed transactions into net cash flows from financing activities  
**Question:**  
**Foxcroft Media Inc.** reports the following cash transactions in the current year:

| Transaction | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Issued common stock for cash | $210,000 |
| Paid cash dividends | 48,000 |
| Retired long-term bonds for cash | 175,000 |
| Paid interest on bonds | 12,000 |
| Borrowed on a 90-day nontrade bank note (cash) | 40,000 |
| Principal repayment on finance lease | 22,000 |
| Purchased treasury stock for cash | 30,000 |
| Sold equipment for cash | 55,000 |
| Borrowed cash on a short-term **trade** note to a supplier for inventory | 18,000 |

What is **net cash provided (used) by financing activities**?

- A) $(7,000) used  
- B) $(25,000) used  
- C) $(37,000) used  
- D) $15,000 provided  

**Answer:** **B. $(25,000) used.**  

Financing items only:  
+ Stock issuance $210,000  
+ Nontrade bank note proceeds $40,000  
− Dividends $(48,000)  
− Bond retirement $(175,000)  
− Lease principal $(22,000)  
− Treasury stock $(30,000)  

Net = $210,000 + $40,000 − $48,000 − $175,000 − $22,000 − $30,000 = **$(25,000)**.

**Excluded:** interest on bonds (operating); equipment sale (investing); trade note for inventory (operating, not financing).
