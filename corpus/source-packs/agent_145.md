# Agent 145 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-4

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for Bonds Issued at a Discount  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** bonds sold when stated rate < market rate → proceeds < face; Dr Cash, Dr Discount on Bonds Payable (contra-liability), Cr Bonds Payable (face)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (effective interest — emphasis):** Interest expense = beginning carrying amount × market rate (per period); cash interest = face × stated rate (per period); discount amortization = interest expense − cash interest; carrying amount rises toward face
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** when reporting date ≠ interest payment date, accrue prorated interest expense, discount amortization, and Interest Payable
- **Disposal / maturity settlement JE:** final interest + amortization entry; then Dr Bonds Payable, Cr Cash for face (discount fully amortized)
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** Discount on Bonds Payable is a **direct deduction** from Bonds Payable (net carrying amount); discount amortization is **interest expense** (not a separate operating item)
- **Straight-line interest method (GAAP only if not materially different):** constant discount amortization each period = total discount ÷ number of periods
- **Number-variant twin:** same effective-interest lifecycle with all new face, rates, term, and proceeds

### Q1 — CORE — Discount bonds: issuance, effective-interest schedule, accrual, maturity, presentation
**LO:** LO 16-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition at a discount; full effective-interest amortization schedule; semiannual interest JEs; month-end accrual; maturity settlement; balance-sheet net presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Forge Co.** (calendar year-end) authorizes and issues **$200,000** face of **4%** interest-bearing bonds on **January 1, Year 1**. The bonds mature **December 31, Year 3** and pay cash interest **semiannually** on **June 30** and **December 31**. The bonds are sold to yield a market (effective) rate of **6%**. Meridian amortizes the bond discount using the **effective interest method**. Amounts are rounded to the nearest dollar.

Relevant period rates and cash flow:
- Semiannual market rate = 6% ÷ 2 = **3%**
- Semiannual periods = 3 × 2 = **6**
- Semiannual cash interest = 4% ÷ 2 × $200,000 = **$4,000**
- Issue proceeds (PV of remaining cash flows at 3% for 6 periods) = **$189,166**
- Initial discount = $200,000 − $189,166 = **$10,834**

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE**.  
b. Prepare the complete **effective-interest amortization schedule** for all 6 semiannual periods (cash interest, interest expense, discount amortization, unamortized discount, carrying amount).  
c. Prepare the **June 30, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 1** journal entries for cash interest and discount amortization.  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet** presentation of the bonds and the **Year 1 income statement** effect (interest expense).  
e. On **January 31, Year 2**, Meridian prepares monthly financial statements. Record the **period-end adjusting JE** for one month of interest accrual and discount amortization (use 1/6 of the next semiannual period’s amounts from the schedule).  
f. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** entries for the **final interest payment** (including amortization) and **maturity settlement** of the bonds (assume no Interest Payable balance remains).

**Key insight:** At a discount, total interest expense over the life ($34,834) **exceeds** total cash coupons ($24,000) by the initial discount ($10,834). Effective interest produces **rising** interest expense each period as carrying amount grows toward face.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Straight-line discount schedule, interest JEs, maturity
**LO:** LO 16-4  
**Concept:** Straight-line interest method for discount amortization (constant amort. per period); interest JE pattern; maturity when discount fully amortized; contrast with effective interest  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Brewing Co.** issues **$100,000** face of **8%** annual interest bonds on **January 1, Year 1**. Interest is paid **annually** each **December 31**. The bonds mature **December 31, Year 3**. Bonds are sold for **$95,026** (market yield **10%** at issuance). Cascade amortizes the discount using the **straight-line interest method** (assume results are not materially different from effective interest for this illustration).

- Initial discount = $100,000 − $95,026 = **$4,974**
- Annual cash interest = 8% × $100,000 = **$8,000**
- Straight-line discount amortization per year = $4,974 ÷ 3 = **$1,658**
- Annual interest expense = $8,000 + $1,658 = **$9,658**

**Required:**  
a. January 1, Year 1 issuance JE.  
b. Straight-line amortization schedule for Years 1–3.  
c. December 31, Year 1 and Year 2 interest/amortization JEs.  
d. December 31, Year 3 final interest JE and maturity settlement JE.  
e. **Classification / method note:** Under GAAP (ASC 835-30), when is straight-line acceptable relative to the effective interest method? Where is the unamortized discount reported?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Issuance — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 95,026 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 4,974 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 100,000 |

**Check:** Dr 95,026 + 4,974 = 100,000 = Cr 100,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Straight-line amortization schedule**

| Date | Cash interest | Interest expense | Discount amort. | Unamortized discount | Carrying amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan. 1, Year 1 | — | — | — | $4,974 | $95,026 |
| Dec. 31, Year 1 | $8,000 | $9,658 | $1,658 | 3,316 | 96,684 |
| Dec. 31, Year 2 | 8,000 | 9,658 | 1,658 | 1,658 | 98,342 |
| Dec. 31, Year 3 | 8,000 | 9,658 | 1,658 | 0 | 100,000 |
| **Totals** | **$24,000** | **$28,974** | **$4,974** | | |

**Rollforward check:** Constant amort. $1,658 × 3 = $4,974; total interest $28,974 = cash $24,000 + discount $4,974. Ends at face. **Schedule OK.**

|---|---:|---:|

*Final interest*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 9,658 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,658 |
| Cash | | 8,000 |

**Check:** Dr 9,658 = Cr 1,658 + 8,000. **Balanced.** Discount fully amortized.

*Settlement*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 100,000 | |
| Cash | | 100,000 |

**Check:** Dr 100,000 = Cr 100,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification / method**

- **GAAP preference:** Effective interest method is required. Straight-line may be used **only if** results are **not materially different** from effective interest (ASC 835-30-55-2).  
- **Balance sheet:** Unamortized Discount on Bonds Payable is a **contra-liability** deducted from Bonds Payable (net carrying amount), not a deferred charge asset.  
- **Income statement:** Discount amortization is part of **interest expense**.  
- **Contrast:** Straight-line → **constant** interest expense each period; effective interest on a discount → **increasing** interest expense each period. Total interest expense over the bond life is the **same** under both methods ($28,974 here).

**Key insight:** Straight-line spreads the discount evenly; effective interest ties expense to carrying amount × market rate. Exam focus for LO 16-4 is usually **effective interest schedules and JEs**, with straight-line as the allowed materiality exception.

### Q4 — MC — Classification / presentation of discount and preferred method
**LO:** LO 16-4  
**Concept:** Balance-sheet classification of Discount on Bonds Payable and GAAP amortization method  
**Question:** On December 31, after several periods of amortization, a company has Bonds Payable of $200,000 and unamortized Discount on Bonds Payable of $7,434 related to those bonds. Which statement is correct under U.S. GAAP?

- A) Report Discount on Bonds Payable as a long-term asset of $7,434 and Bonds Payable at $200,000.  
- B) Report Bonds payable, net of $192,566 (face $200,000 less unamortized discount $7,434); amortize the discount using the effective interest method unless straight-line is not materially different.  
- C) Report Bonds Payable at the original issue proceeds forever; never amortize the discount if the bonds are held to maturity.  
- D) Report interest expense each period equal only to cash coupon payments; discount is closed to Additional Paid-in Capital at issuance.

**Answer:** B.  
Discount is a **direct deduction** from the face of the related bonds (net liability presentation). Amortization is recognized as **interest expense** over the term. GAAP requires the **effective interest method**; straight-line is acceptable only when not materially different. A treats the discount as an asset (incorrect under ASC 835-30). C ignores subsequent measurement. D understates interest expense and misclassifies the discount.
