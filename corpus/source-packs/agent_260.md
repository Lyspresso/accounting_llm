# Agent 260 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-6

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Utilize a worksheet to prepare the statement of cash flows  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Cash flow worksheet (optional tool):** tracks every balance-sheet account change; routes cash effects to operating, investing, or financing in the lower half
- **Reconstructed (worksheet-only) entries:** explain BS changes; **not** posted to the general ledger; each entry must **Dr = Cr**
- **Lower-half convention:** SCF **additions = debits**; SCF **deductions = credits** (indirect method)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** equipment/AD rollforward → depreciation plug; patent amortization schedule; bond premium (or discount) amortization schedule feeding worksheet add-backs/deductions
- **Initial recognition worksheet JEs:** cash purchase of equipment; cash issuance of common stock
- **Period-end working-capital adjusting worksheet JEs:** AR (gross ± allowance), inventory, prepaid, AP, salaries payable, interest/tax payable
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement worksheet JEs:** sale of equipment; early bond retirement (remove face and unamortized premium; cash paid financing; gain/loss operating); mortgage principal payment
- **Classification / presentation:** transfer lower-half activity sections to a formal SCF (indirect operating); reconcile beginning cash to ending cash
- **Number-variant twin:** same worksheet process with all amounts changed

### Q4 — MC — Worksheet lower-half convention and subsequent measurement classification
**LO:** LO 22-6  
**Concept:** Classification — lower-half debit/credit convention; correct reconstructed entry for bond premium amortization (subsequent measurement)  

**Question 1:**  
On a cash flow worksheet prepared under the **indirect method**, which statement is correct?

- A) An increase in inventory is entered as a **debit** in the operating section of the lower half.  
- B) Depreciation expense is entered as a **credit** in the operating section of the lower half.  
- C) Amortization of bond **premium** is entered as a **credit** in the operating section of the lower half (deduction from net income).  
- D) Net income is entered as a **credit** in the operating section of the lower half.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Worksheet convention: **additions = debits**, **deductions = credits** in the lower half. Premium amortization reduces interest expense (boosts NI) without a corresponding cash benefit relative to the coupon → **deduct** from NI → **credit** in operating.  
- A is wrong: inventory increase is a **deduction** → **credit**.  
- B is wrong: depreciation is an **add-back** → **debit**.  
- D is wrong: net income is the starting **addition** → **debit** (credit to RE).

**Question 2:**  
**Copperfield Labs** amortized **$2,400** of bond **discount** during the year. Which reconstructed **worksheet** entry is correct?

- A)  
  Dr Discount on Bonds Payable 2,400  
  Cr Amortization of Bond Discount—Operating 2,400  

- B)  
  Dr Amortization of Bond Discount—Operating 2,400  
  Cr Discount on Bonds Payable 2,400  

- C)  
  Dr Interest Payable 2,400  
  Cr Discount on Bonds Payable 2,400  

- D)  
  Dr Amortization of Bond Discount—Financing 2,400  
  Cr Discount on Bonds Payable 2,400  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Subsequent measurement of discount: credit Discount (reduces the contra-liability), and **debit** the operating lower-half line because discount amortization is an **add-back** to NI (noncash interest).  
- A reverses the entry and treats amortization as a deduction.  
- C confuses the worksheet reconstruction with an interest-payable WC entry.  
- D misclassifies the add-back as financing.

**Key insight:** Premium amortizations are **operating credits** (deductions); discount amortizations are **operating debits** (add-backs)—both are subsequent-measurement amounts that never touch the investing or financing sections.
