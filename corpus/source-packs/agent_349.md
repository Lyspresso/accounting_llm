# Agent 349 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-2

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Determine the initial and subsequent measurement of finite life intangible assets  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial measurement:** finite-life intangibles recognized at **cash-equivalent cost** (purchase price + transfer/legal fees + other costs to ready the asset for intended use)
- **Noncash consideration:** cost = cash paid + FV of noncash given (or FV of asset acquired if more reliably measurable); zero-interest notes recorded at **present value** using market rate
- **Basket purchase:** allocate total cost to individual intangibles on **relative fair value** (no goodwill in an asset acquisition)
- **Subsequent measurement:** amortize cost less residual over the **shorter of legal life or estimated useful life**; straight-line unless another systematic pattern is more appropriate
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** Dr Amortization Expense; Cr intangible asset (most common) or Accumulated Amortization
- **Successful patent defense:** capitalize incremental legal costs into the patent; amortize revised carrying amount over **remaining** useful life
- **Carrying value rollforward:** Cost − cumulative amortization (+ capitalized defense/subsequent costs) = carrying amount
- **Derecognition (lifecycle close-out):** amortize **to date of disposal**, remove carrying amount, recognize gain/loss vs net proceeds
- **Number-variant twin:** same LO path with all companies and amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Cascade Biotech: patent purchase, period-end amort, defense costs, note alternative, mid-year disposal

**LO:** LO 13-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition at cost; **period-end amortization adjusting JEs** (emphasis); capitalize successful defense costs; subsequent-measurement schedule / carrying value; noncash note initial measurement; disposal JE after amortizing to sale date  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Biotech Corp.** has a calendar year-end. On **January 1, 20X1**, Cascade purchased a **patent** for **$57,000 cash**. The patent’s **20-year legal life** began on **January 1, 20X0** (one year already elapsed). Cascade estimates residual value **$0** and uses the **remaining legal life** as the useful life. Amortization is **straight-line**. Cascade credits amortization directly to the **Patent** account (no separate accumulated amortization account).

On **January 15, 20X2**, Cascade **successfully defends** the patent in an infringement suit. Legal costs of **$5,400** are paid in cash and are capitalizable.

**Additional facts for alternate / extended parts:**  
- **Part (c):** Instead of paying cash on January 1, 20X1, Cascade issued a **zero-interest note payable** with face **$66,000** due in **2 years**. The market rate for similar financing is **10%**. The fair value of the patent itself is uncertain.  
- **Part (d):** On **June 30, 20X3**, Cascade sells the patent (from the cash-purchase path in parts a–b) for **$58,000 cash**. Amortize through the sale date before derecognizing.

**Required:**  
(a) Record journal entries for: (1) purchase of the patent on January 1, 20X1; (2) **December 31, 20X1 period-end amortization AJ**; (3) capitalization of defense costs on January 15, 20X2; (4) **December 31, 20X2 period-end amortization AJ**. Prove each JE balances.  
(b) Prepare a **subsequent-measurement / carrying-value schedule** through December 31, 20X2. State the carrying amount of the patent on December 31, 20X2.  
(c) Record the **January 1, 20X1 initial recognition JE** if the patent is acquired with the zero-interest note described above (PV measurement). Prove the entry balances.  
(d) Record: (1) amortization of the patent for the **six months** ended June 30, 20X3; (2) the **disposal JE** on June 30, 20X3. Prove each balances and compute gain or loss.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Journal entries — cash path**

(1) January 1, 20X1 — purchase of patent:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 57,000 | |
| Cash | | 57,000 |
| *Record purchase of patent at cash cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 57,000 = Cr 57,000. **Balanced.**

Remaining useful life at acquisition = 20 − 1 = **19 years**.  
Year 1 amortization = $57,000 / 19 = **$3,000**.

(2) December 31, 20X1 — **period-end adjusting JE** (emphasis):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 3,000 | |
| Patent | | 3,000 |
| *Period-end amortization: $57,000 / 19* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,000 = Cr 3,000. **Balanced.**

(3) January 15, 20X2 — successful defense costs capitalized:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 5,400 | |
| Cash | | 5,400 |
| *Capitalize legal costs to successfully defend patent* | | |

**Check:** Dr 5,400 = Cr 5,400. **Balanced.**

Carrying amount just before defense = $57,000 − $3,000 = **$54,000**.  
Carrying amount after defense = $54,000 + $5,400 = **$59,400**.  
Remaining useful life at start of 20X2 = **18 years**.  
Year 2 amortization = $59,400 / 18 = **$3,300**.

(4) December 31, 20X2 — **period-end adjusting JE** (emphasis):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 3,300 | |
| Patent | | 3,300 |
| *Period-end amortization: ($57,000 − $3,000 + $5,400) / 18* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,300 = Cr 3,300. **Balanced.**

**(b) Subsequent-measurement schedule / carrying value**

| Date / event | Patent carrying amount |
|---|---:|
| Jan 1, 20X1 — acquisition cost | $57,000 |
| Dec 31, 20X1 — amortization | (3,000) |
| **Dec 31, 20X1 carrying amount** | **$54,000** |
| Jan 15, 20X2 — defense costs capitalized | 5,400 |
| **Carrying amount after defense** | **$59,400** |
| Dec 31, 20X2 — amortization | (3,300) |
| **Dec 31, 20X2 carrying amount** | **$56,100** |

Proof: $57,000 − $3,000 + $5,400 − $3,300 = **$56,100**.

**(c) Initial recognition with zero-interest note (noncash financing)**

Present value of $66,000 due in 2 years at 10%:  
PV = $66,000 / (1.10)² = $66,000 / 1.21 = **$54,545** (rounded to nearest dollar).  
Discount on note payable = $66,000 − $54,545 = **$11,455**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 54,545 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | 11,455 | |
| Note Payable | | 66,000 |
| *Record patent at PV of zero-interest note; market rate 10%* | | |

**Check:** Dr 54,545 + 11,455 = 66,000 = Cr 66,000. **Balanced.**  
*(Patent cost is the more reliably measurable PV of the note when the patent’s FV is uncertain.)*

**(d) Mid-year amortization and disposal (cash-path asset from parts a–b)**

After Dec 31, 20X2, remaining useful life = 19 − 2 = **17 years**.  
Full-year 20X3 amortization = $56,100 / 17 = **$3,300**.  
Six months (Jan 1–June 30, 20X3) = $3,300 × 6/12 = **$1,650**.

(1) June 30, 20X3 — amortize to date of disposal:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 1,650 | |
| Patent | | 1,650 |
| *Amortize patent for 6 months to sale date* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,650 = Cr 1,650. **Balanced.**

Carrying amount at disposal = $56,100 − $1,650 = **$54,450**.  
Cash proceeds $58,000 − carrying amount $54,450 = **Gain $3,550**.

(2) June 30, 20X3 — disposal:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 58,000 | |
| Patent | | 54,450 |
| Gain on Sale of Patent | | 3,550 |
| *Derecognize patent; record gain on disposal* | | |

**Check:** Dr 58,000 = Cr 54,450 + 3,550 = 58,000. **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Finite-life intangibles start at cost, are **amortized by period-end AJ** over remaining legal/useful life (shorter), and successful defense costs **increase carrying amount** then are amortized over the **remaining** life. Noncash notes use **PV**. On disposal, **amortize first**, then remove carrying amount and book G/L.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Northvale Foods: patent cost, amort schedule, defense, note, year-end disposal

**LO:** LO 13-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin of Q1 — initial recognition JE, **period-end amortization AJs**, defense capitalization, carrying-value schedule, PV of zero-interest note, full-year amort then disposal  
**Scenario:**  
**Northvale Foods Inc.** (calendar year-end) purchased a **patent** for **$48,000 cash** on **January 1, 20X4**. At acquisition, **4 years** of the patent’s **20-year legal life** had already expired; Northvale uses the **remaining legal life (16 years)** as useful life, residual **$0**, **straight-line**, credit amortization **directly to Patent**.

On **February 1, 20X5**, Northvale pays **$4,500 cash** to **successfully defend** the patent (capitalize).

**Additional facts:**  
- **Part (c):** Alternatively, on January 1, 20X4 Northvale acquires the patent by issuing a **zero-interest note** face **$55,000** due in **2 years**; market rate **12%**; patent FV uncertain.  
- **Part (d):** On **December 31, 20X6**, after recording 20X6 amortization, Northvale sells the patent (cash-purchase path) for **$40,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
(a) Record: (1) Jan 1, 20X4 purchase; (2) Dec 31, 20X4 period-end amort AJ; (3) Feb 1, 20X5 defense costs; (4) Dec 31, 20X5 period-end amort AJ. Prove balances.  
(b) Carrying-value schedule through Dec 31, 20X5; state carrying amount at that date.  
(c) Initial recognition JE for the zero-interest note alternative (round PV to nearest dollar). Prove balance.  
(d) Record Dec 31, 20X6 amortization AJ and the disposal JE. Compute gain or loss. Prove balances.

Remaining life at acquisition = **16 years**.

|---|---:|---:|

(2) December 31, 20X4 — period-end AJ:

| Amortization Expense | 3,000 | |
| Patent | | 3,000 |
| *Period-end amortization: $48,000 / 16* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,000 = Cr 3,000. **Balanced.**

(3) February 1, 20X5 — defense costs:

| Account | Debit | Credit |

Carrying after defense = $48,000 − $3,000 + $4,500 = **$49,500**.

(4) December 31, 20X5 — period-end AJ:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 3,300 | |

| Date / event | Patent carrying amount |
|---|---:|

Remaining life after 20X5 = 16 − 2 = **14 years**.  
20X6 amortization = $46,200 / 14 = **$3,300**.

(1) December 31, 20X6 — period-end AJ:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 3,300 | |

**Check:** Dr 3,300 = Cr 3,300. **Balanced.**

Carrying amount at disposal = $46,200 − $3,300 = **$42,900**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — period-end AJ emphasis: mid-year patents + basket purchase allocation

### Q4 — MC — amortization method when benefit pattern is not reliable

**LO:** LO 13-2  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement method for finite-life intangibles — straight-line default when consumption pattern cannot be reliably determined  
**Question:**  
Under U.S. GAAP (ASC 350), a recognized **finite-life** intangible asset shall be amortized over its useful life. The amortization method shall reflect the pattern in which the economic benefits are consumed. **If that pattern cannot be reliably determined**, which method shall be used?

- A) Units-of-production based on units sold in the first year only  
- B) Double-declining-balance accelerated amortization in all cases  
- C) Straight-line amortization  
- D) No amortization until the pattern becomes known

**Answer:** **C.** ASC 350-30-35-6 requires the amortization method to reflect the pattern of economic benefit consumption; **if that pattern cannot be reliably determined, straight-line amortization shall be used.** Finite-life intangibles are amortized (not held unamortized). Accelerated methods are allowed only when they better reflect consumption, not as a default.

### Q5 — MC — initial measurement of finite-life intangible acquired for noncash consideration

**LO:** LO 13-2  
**Concept:** Initial measurement — cost when acquired for equity or other noncash consideration (more reliably measurable FV)  
**Question:**  
On January 1, **Pinnacle Devices** acquires a **finite-life patent** by issuing **2,000 shares** of its common stock. The stock’s **fair value is reliably measurable at $18 per share**. Separate evidence of the patent’s fair value is weak. Transfer taxes and recording fees paid in cash total **$1,200**. At what amount should Pinnacle initially measure the patent?

- A) $0, because internally the patent had no book basis before issuance  
- B) $36,000 only (FV of shares), with the $1,200 expensed immediately  
- C) $37,200 (FV of shares issued + cash acquisition costs)  
- D) $1,200 only (cash paid), with the shares ignored until sold

**Answer:** **C.** Initial cost = cash-equivalent consideration: FV of shares issued ($2,000 × $18 = $36,000) **plus** cash expenditures to acquire and ready the asset ($1,200) = **$37,200**. When equity issued has established FV and the patent’s FV is less reliable, use the stock’s FV. Direct acquisition costs are capitalized, not expensed.
