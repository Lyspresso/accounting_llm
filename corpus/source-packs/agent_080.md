# Agent 80 — CORE DEMO — LO 12-4

**Chapter:** 12  
**LO title:** Account for changes in estimate as they relate to depreciation  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize depreciable PPE at cost (cash/purchase); establish depreciable base = cost − residual value
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** straight-line depreciation over estimated useful life; accumulated depreciation and carrying amount roll-forward
- **Change in estimate (useful life and/or residual value):** treat **prospectively** — no restatement of prior periods; no cumulative-effect entry
- **At date of change:** recompute carrying amount; allocate (CA − new residual) over **remaining** useful life
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** depreciation expense → accumulated depreciation using the **updated** annual amount after the change
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** remove cost and accumulated depreciation; recognize gain or loss vs cash/proceeds
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** change in estimate affects current and future depreciation only; prior financial statements unchanged; often disclosed if material (useful-life / residual revisions)

### Q1 — CORE — Purchase, SL schedule, change in life & residual, disposal (emphasis: initial recognition JE)
**LO:** LO 12-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of PPE; multi-year SL schedule; prospective change in useful life and residual value; period-end dep JE after change; disposal JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Ridgeway Packaging LLC** (calendar year) acquires a sealing machine on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase cost | **$85,000** |
| Original estimated residual value | **$5,000** |
| Original estimated useful life | **5 years**, straight-line |
| Depreciation convention | Full year in year of acquisition |
| **January 1, Year 4** change in estimate | Total useful life revised to **7 years**; residual revised to **$1,000** |
| Disposal | Sold for **$22,000** cash on **December 31, Year 5** (after Year 5 depreciation is recorded) |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry for the purchase.  
b. Compute original annual depreciation. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** depreciation adjusting entry. Build a **depreciation schedule** for Years 1–3 (dep expense, accum. dep., carrying amount).  
c. On **January 1, Year 4**, compute carrying amount, **remaining** useful life, and **updated annual depreciation**. Prepare the **December 31, Year 4** depreciation entry.  
d. Extend the **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 4–7 (confirm residual is reached).  
e. Prepare the **December 31, Year 5 disposal** journal entry (after Year 5 depreciation).  
f. State how the Year 4 change in estimate is **presented** (prospective vs retrospective; effect on prior years).

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—Sealing Machine | 85,000 | |
| Cash | | 85,000 |
| *Capitalize PPE at acquisition cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 85,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Original depreciation and Years 1–3 schedule**

Depreciable base = $85,000 − $5,000 = **$80,000**  
Original annual SL depreciation = $80,000 / 5 = **$16,000**

*December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 16,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Sealing Machine | | 16,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 16,000.

| Year | Depreciation expense | Accumulated depreciation (EOY) | Carrying amount (EOY) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 16,000 | 16,000 | 69,000 |
| 2 | 16,000 | 32,000 | 53,000 |
| 3 | 16,000 | 48,000 | 37,000 |

**Roll-forward check:** $85,000 − $48,000 = **$37,000** carrying amount at December 31, Year 3 / January 1, Year 4.

**c. Change in estimate — prospective (January 1, Year 4)**

Carrying amount at date of change = **$37,000**  
Revised total useful life = 7 years; years already used = 3 → **remaining life = 4 years**  
Revised residual = **$1,000**  
Updated annual depreciation = ($37,000 − $1,000) / 4 = **$9,000**

*No journal entry* is made solely for the estimate change. Prior accum. dep. is **not** restated.

*December 31, Year 4 — period-end adjusting JE (new amount)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 9,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Sealing Machine | | 9,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 9,000.

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule (after change)**

| Year | Depreciation expense | Accumulated depreciation (EOY) | Carrying amount (EOY) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 9,000 | 57,000 | 28,000 |
| 5 | 9,000 | 66,000 | 19,000 |
| 6 | 9,000 | 75,000 | 10,000 |
| 7 | 9,000 | 84,000 | **1,000** (= residual) |

**Roll-forward check:** $48,000 + 4 × $9,000 = $84,000 AD; $85,000 − $84,000 = **$1,000** residual. Depreciable base after change $36,000 fully allocated over 4 years.

**e. December 31, Year 5 — disposal (after Year 5 depreciation of $9,000)**

Accumulated depreciation at sale = $48,000 + $9,000 + $9,000 = **$66,000**  
Carrying amount = $85,000 − $66,000 = **$19,000**  
Cash proceeds = **$22,000**  
**Gain on disposal** = $22,000 − $19,000 = **$3,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 22,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Sealing Machine | 66,000 | |
| Equipment—Sealing Machine | | 85,000 |
| Gain on Disposal of Equipment | | 3,000 |
| *Remove asset and AD; recognize gain* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,000 + 66,000 = **88,000**; Cr 85,000 + 3,000 = **88,000**. **Balanced.**

**f. Presentation / disclosure**  
Revisions of **useful life** and **residual value** are **changes in accounting estimate**. They are applied **prospectively** to current and remaining periods only. Years 1–3 reported amounts (including the $16,000 annual depreciation) are **not** restated. No cumulative-effect adjustment is recorded. Material changes are typically **disclosed** (nature and effect on current-period depreciation/net income).

**Key insight:** At the change date, lock in **carrying amount**, subtract the **new residual**, and spread the remainder over the **remaining** life. Never reverse prior depreciation for a pure estimate change.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Life extended, residual cut, disposal with loss

**Concept:** Number-variant twin: initial PPE recognition; SL schedule; prospective change in useful life and residual; updated period-end dep; disposal with loss

**Coastal Glassworks Inc.** (calendar year) places a tempering furnace into service on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase cost | **$240,000** |
| Original residual value | **$24,000** |
| Original useful life | **8 years**, straight-line |
| **January 1, Year 5** change in estimate | Total useful life revised to **12 years**; residual revised to **$12,000** |
| Disposal | Sold for **$95,000** cash on **December 31, Year 6** (after Year 6 depreciation) |

d. Schedule Years 5–12 (spot-check that residual is reached at end of Year 12).  
e. Disposal JE on December 31, Year 6.  
f. One-sentence classification: is this a change in principle, error correction, or estimate?

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment—Tempering Furnace | 240,000 | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 240,000.

Carrying amount = **$132,000**

| Year | Depreciation expense | Accumulated depreciation (EOY) | Carrying amount (EOY) |

AD at sale = $108,000 + $15,000 + $15,000 = **$138,000**  
CA = $240,000 − $138,000 = **$102,000**  
Proceeds = **$95,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |

**f. Classification**  
This is a **change in accounting estimate** (useful life and residual), applied **prospectively** — not a change in principle and not an error correction.

**Key insight:** Extending life and lowering residual both change the annual charge, but the **mechanics are identical**: (carrying amount − new residual) ÷ remaining years.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Shortened useful life (obsolescence) and residual cut
**LO:** LO 12-4  
**Concept:** Prospective treatment when useful life is **shortened** (higher remaining depreciation); period-end JE; scrap disposal; presentation of accelerated estimate change  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Trail Outfitters** owns a specialty embroidery machine purchased **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cost | **$72,000** |
| Original residual | **$8,000** |
| Original useful life | **8 years**, straight-line |
| **January 1, Year 4** revision | Competitive technology makes obsolescence likely; total useful life now only **5 years**; residual revised to **$4,000** |
| Disposal | Scrapped/sold for **$3,800** cash on **December 31, Year 5** (after Year 5 depreciation) |

**Required:**  
a. Original annual depreciation; carrying amount at January 1, Year 4.  
b. Compute **updated** annual depreciation for Years 4–5. Explain in one sentence why expense **increases**.  
c. Prepare **December 31, Year 4** and **Year 5** depreciation adjusting entries.  
d. Prepare a mini **schedule** from acquisition through disposal date (Years 1–5).  
e. Prepare the **disposal** journal entry on December 31, Year 5.  
f. Disclosure/classification: contrast this prospective estimate change with what would be done if Years 1–3 depreciation had been an **error** (wrong method applied intentionally misstated facts — error vs estimate).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Original amounts through Year 3**

Original annual dep = ($72,000 − $8,000) / 8 = **$8,000**  
AD after 3 years = 3 × $8,000 = **$24,000**  
Carrying amount January 1, Year 4 = $72,000 − $24,000 = **$48,000**

**b. Updated depreciation**

Remaining life = 5 − 3 = **2 years**  
Updated annual dep = ($48,000 − $4,000) / 2 = **$22,000**

Expense **increases** because the same (or larger) remaining depreciable base must be allocated over a **shorter** remaining life after the obsolescence-driven revision.

**c. Period-end adjusting JEs**

*December 31, Year 4*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 22,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Embroidery Machine | | 22,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 22,000.

*December 31, Year 5*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 22,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Embroidery Machine | | 22,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 22,000.

**d. Mini schedule Years 1–5**

| Year | Depreciation expense | Accumulated depreciation (EOY) | Carrying amount (EOY) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 8,000 | 8,000 | 64,000 |
| 2 | 8,000 | 16,000 | 56,000 |
| 3 | 8,000 | 24,000 | 48,000 |
| *Change* | — | — | — |
| 4 | 22,000 | 46,000 | 26,000 |
| 5 | 22,000 | 68,000 | **4,000** (= revised residual) |

**e. December 31, Year 5 — disposal**

CA = **$4,000**; cash = **$3,800**; **Loss** = **$200**  
AD = **$68,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 3,800 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Embroidery Machine | 68,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 200 | |
| Equipment—Embroidery Machine | | 72,000 |

**Check:** Dr 3,800 + 68,000 + 200 = **72,000**; Cr = **72,000**. **Balanced.**

**f. Estimate vs error**  
A good-faith revision of useful life/residual is a **change in estimate** → **prospective** only (current and future periods). An **error** (e.g., arithmetic mistake or failure to depreciate in prior years when required) is corrected **retrospectively** (restate prior periods / adjust beginning retained earnings as appropriate). Shortening life because new technology emerged is classic **estimate** accounting, not an error.

**Key insight:** Shortened life **accelerates** remaining depreciation prospectively; prior years stay as originally reported.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of change in residual / useful life
**LO:** LO 12-4  
**Concept:** Classification — prospective treatment of changes in depreciation estimates (life/residual)  
**Question:**  
On January 1, Year 1, **Blue Mesa Logistics** acquired equipment for $120,000 with a $12,000 residual and a 6-year straight-line life. On January 1, Year 3, management revises total useful life to 10 years and residual to $8,000. How should Blue Mesa account for this change?

- A) Restate Years 1–2 depreciation retrospectively so the new life and residual appear as if always used  
- B) Record a cumulative-effect adjustment to beginning retained earnings for the difference between old and new depreciation for Years 1–2  
- C) Leave prior periods unchanged; compute Year 3 (and later) depreciation from carrying amount at the change date using the new residual and remaining life  
- D) Treat the revision as a change in accounting principle applied retrospectively under ASC 250 for voluntary principle changes  

**Answer:** **C.**  
Revisions of useful life and residual value are **changes in accounting estimate**, applied **prospectively**. Carrying amount at January 1, Year 3 = $120,000 − 2 × [($120,000 − $12,000)/6] = $120,000 − 2 × $18,000 = **$84,000**. Updated annual depreciation = ($84,000 − $8,000) / (10 − 2) = **$9,500**. No restatement of Years 1–2; no cumulative-effect entry.

### Q4b — MC — Identify the correct post-change depreciation amount
**LO:** LO 12-4  
**Concept:** Measurement — compute updated annual depreciation after change in life and residual  
**Question:**  
Using the **Blue Mesa Logistics** facts in Q4, what is **Year 3 depreciation expense** after the January 1, Year 3 estimate change?

- A) $10,800  
- B) $9,500  
- C) $18,000  
- D) $11,200  

**Answer:** **B. $9,500.**  
Original annual dep = ($120,000 − $12,000) / 6 = $18,000. After 2 years, CA = $120,000 − $36,000 = $84,000. Remaining life = 8 years; new residual $8,000 → ($84,000 − $8,000) / 8 = **$9,500**.  
(A is ($120,000 − $12,000)/10 — incorrectly reapplies original residual over full new life from day one. C is the **old** annual amount. D is ($120,000 − $8,000)/10 — ignores accumulated depreciation already taken.)
