# Agent 360 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-5

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Describe accounting for subsequent events and contingencies including litigation, warranties, and other contingencies  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Loss contingency criteria (ASC 450):** accrue only if **probable** and **reasonably estimable**; disclose if at least reasonably possible and not both conditions met (or exposure above minimum accrued); remote → generally neither (except certain guarantees)
- **Range of loss:** accrue the **better estimate** within the range; if no amount is better, accrue the **minimum** and disclose exposure above the minimum
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** year-end litigation accrual; year-end warranty accrual; year-end remeasurement using recognized subsequent-event evidence available before FS issuance
- **Initial recognition JE:** debit loss/expense, credit estimated litigation liability or warranty liability (assurance-type)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** multi-year **warranty liability rollforward** (beginning + accruals − claims = ending)
- **Settlement / maturity JE:** pay claims against warranty liability; settle litigation (difference vs carrying amount → Year-of-settlement gain/loss if after FS issuance, or Year-1 remeasurement if recognized subsequent event)
- **Recognized subsequent events:** post–balance-sheet evidence of conditions existing at year-end → **adjust** year-end amounts (period-end / issuance-date measurement)
- **Nonrecognized subsequent events:** conditions arising after year-end → **disclose** if needed; do not accrue in prior year
- **Assurance-type warranty:** loss contingency; full estimated cost accrued in year of sale via period-end AJ
- **Service-type warranty:** separate performance obligation; cash → deferred revenue; period-end AJ recognizes revenue over coverage; costs expensed as incurred (not a loss contingency)
- **Gain contingencies:** **never accrued**; may disclose carefully

### Q1 — CORE — Period-end AJs for litigation (best estimate + recognized subsequent event) and multi-year assurance warranty
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JEs for probable/estimable litigation; recognized subsequent-event remeasurement dated year-end; settlement JE; assurance-type warranty period-end accruals and subsequent measurement liability schedule  
**Scenario:**  
**Ironwood Consumer Products** is a calendar-year company. Year 1 financial statements are **issued March 10, Year 2**. Ignore income taxes and COGS.

**Litigation**  
In **October Year 1**, a delivery-vehicle accident injured a third party. A lawsuit seeking **$350,000** was filed in **December Year 1**. Counsel concludes a loss is **probable**. At December 31, Year 1, the reasonably estimable range is **$90,000 to $280,000**, and counsel’s **better estimate** within the range is **$120,000**.

On **February 18, Year 2** (before FS issuance), the parties agree to settle for **$145,000** cash, payable **May 1, Year 2**.

**Assurance-type warranty**  
Ironwood’s products include a **two-year assurance-type warranty** against manufacturing defects (not sold separately). Estimated warranty cost = **2.0% of product sales**. Actual claims are paid in cash. Accruals are recorded as **period-end adjusting entries** each December 31.

| Year | Product sales (cash) | Actual warranty claims paid |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $3,200,000 | $18,000 |
| 2 | 3,600,000 | 52,000 |
| 3 | 4,000,000 | 61,000 |

Warranty rate remains 2.0% of that year’s sales each year.

**Required:**  
a. **(Emphasis)** Record the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting entry** to accrue the litigation contingency using the **better estimate** (ignore the February settlement for this part only).  
b. **(Emphasis)** Record the **recognized subsequent event** remeasurement as a **December 31, Year 1 adjusting entry** after the February 18 settlement agreement.  
c. Record the **May 1, Year 2 settlement** payment.  
d. Record Year 1 **product sales**, the **December 31, Year 1 period-end warranty accrual**, and **Year 1 claims** settlement.  
e. Prepare the complete **warranty liability subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–3 (beginning, accrual, claims, ending).  
f. **(Emphasis)** Record the **December 31, Year 2 period-end adjusting entry** for the Year 2 warranty accrual.  
g. In one sentence: if at December 31 Year 1 the litigation loss were only **reasonably possible** (estimate still $120,000), what period-end reporting would apply?

**a. Period-end adjusting JE — litigation better estimate**

Better estimate within range = **$120,000**.

Also disclose possible additional loss above $120,000 up to the top of the range if material.

**b. Recognized subsequent event — Year 1 remeasurement (period-end AJ)**

Underlying accident/obligation existed at December 31 Year 1. Settlement **before FS issuance** is a **recognized subsequent event** → adjust Year 1 measurement to **$145,000**.  
Additional accrual = \(145{,}000 - 120{,}000 = \$25{,}000\).

*December 31, Year 1 — Adjust litigation accrual to settlement evidence*

**c. Settlement JE (maturity of contingency)**

*May 1, Year 2 — Pay settlement*

|---|---:|---:|

No Year 2 gain or loss (cash equals carrying amount).

**d. Year 1 sales, period-end warranty AJ, and claims**

*Year 1 — Product sales*

**Check:** Dr 3,200,000 = Cr 3,200,000. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 1 — Period-end warranty accrual (emphasis)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 64,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 64,000 |
| *Accrue probable and reasonably estimable assurance-type warranty* | | |

**Check:** Dr 64,000 = Cr 64,000. **Balanced.**

*Year 1 — Settle warranty claims*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 18,000 | |
| Cash | | 18,000 |
| *Pay actual warranty claims against estimated liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Subsequent measurement schedule — warranty liability**

| Year | Beginning liability | + Accrual (2.0% × sales) | − Claims paid | Ending liability |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 64,000 | 18,000 | **46,000** |
| 2 | 46,000 | 72,000 | 52,000 | **66,000** |
| 3 | 66,000 | 80,000 | 61,000 | **85,000** |
| **Totals** | | **216,000** | **131,000** | |

- Y2 accrual = \(\$3{,}600{,}000 \times 0.02 = \$72{,}000\)  
- Y3 accrual = \(\$4{,}000{,}000 \times 0.02 = \$80{,}000\)  
- Y2 end: \(46{,}000 + 72{,}000 - 52{,}000 = 66{,}000\)  
- Y3 end: \(66{,}000 + 80{,}000 - 61{,}000 = 85{,}000\)

*December 31, Year 2 — Accrue warranty*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 72,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 72,000 |

**Check:** Dr 72,000 = Cr 72,000. **Balanced.**

**g. Reasonably possible only**  
**No period-end accrual**; **disclose** the nature of the contingency and an estimate of possible loss ($120,000 or the range) — accrual requires both probable **and** reasonably estimable.

**Key insight:** **Period-end adjusting entries** are the control point for contingencies: litigation is accrued (or remeasured) at year-end using **all information available through FS issuance** for recognized subsequent events; assurance warranties load estimated cost into **expense of the sale year** even when cash repairs lag. The warranty **schedule** must roll forward: beg + period-end accruals − claims = end.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end contingency decisions, service-type warranty AJ, post-issuance settlement, nonrecognized SE
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting vs disclose-only vs no entry across likelihood grid; service-type warranty period-end revenue recognition; settlement after FS issuance (change in estimate); nonrecognized subsequent event; gain contingency  
**Scenario:**  
**Tidalwave Fitness Equipment** is a calendar-year company. Year 1 financial statements are **issued February 28, Year 2**. Ignore taxes.

**Part A — Period-end decisions at December 31, Year 1** (independent situations unless noted)

| Item | Facts at December 31, Year 1 |
|---|---|
| 1 | Lawsuit from a **Year 1** gym-equipment injury. Loss is **probable**. Better estimate within range $40,000–$110,000 is **$70,000**. |
| 2 | Separate **Year 1** contract dispute. Loss is **reasonably possible** (not probable). Estimated loss **$40,000**. |
| 3 | Frivolous **Year 1** claim; counsel assesses loss as **remote**. |
| 4 | Possible environmental fine; **no claim asserted**. Management concludes it is **not probable** a claim will be asserted. |
| 5 | **Service-type** extended warranties: on **January 1, Year 1**, customers paid cash **$12,000** for **3-year** extended warranties sold separately (stand-alone). Actual cash service costs in Year 1: **$2,100**. Revenue recognized **straight-line**. |
| 6 | Attorneys believe it is **highly probable** Tidalwave will **win** a countersuit and collect **$200,000** in Year 2. |

**Part B — Subsequent developments**

| Item | Facts |
|---|---|
| 7 | Item 1 is settled **August 15, Year 2** (after FS issuance) for **$78,000** cash. |
| 8 | On **January 12, Year 2**, an uninsured warehouse **flood** (no Year 1 condition) destroys inventory with carrying amount **$55,000**. FS still issued February 28 Year 2. |

**Required:**  
a. **(Emphasis)** For items 1–6, state the **December 31, Year 1** accounting: prepare any **period-end adjusting JE** (balanced), or state **no entry** and whether **disclosure** is required.  
b. For item 5, also record the **January 1, Year 1** cash collection for extended warranties and the **Year 1 service-cost** entry (in addition to the period-end revenue AJ in part a).  
c. Record the **August 15, Year 2 settlement** for item 1.  
d. Classify item 8 as **recognized** or **nonrecognized** subsequent event for Year 1; state Year 1 accounting.  
e. Briefly contrast why item 1 was accrued at year-end but item 6 was not.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Period-end decisions (emphasis)**

**Item 1 — Probable + better estimate → accrue $70,000**

*December 31, Year 1 — Period-end litigation accrual*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation | 70,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 70,000 |
| *Accrue better estimate of probable, estimable loss contingency* | | |

**Check:** Dr 70,000 = Cr 70,000. **Balanced.**  
Disclose exposure above $70,000 within the range if material.

**Item 2 — Reasonably possible → no accrual; disclose**  
**No period-end JE.** Disclose nature of contingency and estimate of possible loss (**$40,000**).

**Item 3 — Remote → neither**  
**No period-end JE.** Generally **no disclosure** (not a guarantee of the type requiring remote disclosure).

**Item 4 — Unasserted; assertion not probable → neither**  
**No period-end JE** and **no disclosure** (assertion of the claim is not probable).

**Item 5 — Service-type warranty period-end revenue AJ**  
Deferred revenue at Jan 1 = **$12,000**. Annual revenue (straight-line over 3 years) = \(12{,}000 / 3 = \$4{,}000\).

*December 31, Year 1 — Period-end recognition of warranty revenue*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Warranty Revenue | 4,000 | |
| Warranty Revenue | | 4,000 |
| *Satisfy performance obligation on straight-line basis* | | |

**Check:** Dr 4,000 = Cr 4,000. **Balanced.**  
(Service costs are **not** a period-end contingency accrual — see part b.)

**Item 6 — Gain contingency → no accrual**  
**No period-end JE.** May **disclose** carefully without implying certain realization. Gain contingencies are **never accrued**.

**b. Service-type warranty — initial cash and costs incurred**

*January 1, Year 1 — Collect extended warranty cash*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 12,000 | |
| Deferred Warranty Revenue | | 12,000 |

*Year 1 — Warranty service costs as incurred*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 2,100 | |
| Cash | | 2,100 |
| *Expense service-type warranty costs as incurred (not reduction of a loss-contingency liability)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,100 = Cr 2,100. **Balanced.**

**c. Settlement after FS issuance (change in estimate)**

Carrying amount of liability = **$70,000**; cash paid = **$78,000**; additional Year 2 loss = **$8,000**.

*August 15, Year 2 — Settle litigation*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated Litigation Liability | 70,000 | |

| Cash | | 78,000 |
| *Settlement after issuance: difference is Year 2 change in estimate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 70,000 + 8,000 = Cr 78,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Nonrecognized subsequent event**  
The flood arose from conditions **after** the balance sheet date → **nonrecognized** subsequent event. **Do not** record a Year 1 inventory write-down or loss. **Disclose** the nature of the event and estimate of financial effect (**$55,000**) if needed to keep Year 1 FS from being misleading. Record the loss in **Year 2** when the flood occurs.

**e. Loss vs gain**  
Item 1 is a **loss** contingency that is **probable and reasonably estimable**, so a **period-end accrual** is required. Item 6 is a **gain** contingency; under ASC 450, contingent gains are **never accrued** (conservatism / realization), though careful disclosure may be appropriate.

**Key insight:** The **period-end adjusting process** is a decision tree: probable + estimable → **AJ**; reasonably possible → **disclose only**; remote / unasserted-not-probable → **neither**; service-type warranties use **period-end revenue AJs** (deferred revenue), not warranty-liability contingency accruals; post-issuance settlement differences hit **current-period** earnings; new post–year-end casualties are **nonrecognized** for the prior year.

### Q4 — MC — Period-end adjusting entry for recognized subsequent event
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Identify which post–balance-sheet event requires a period-end (Year 1) adjusting JE  
**Question:**  
**Oakline Packaging Co.** has a December 31, Year 1 year-end. Financial statements are issued **March 5, Year 2**. Which of the following requires a **Year 1 period-end adjusting journal entry** (or remeasurement of a Year 1 accrual)?

- A) On January 20, Year 2, Oakline issues $1,500,000 of common stock for cash.  
- B) On February 10, Year 2, Oakline settles a lawsuit arising from a **Year 1** workplace injury for $95,000 cash; at year-end the loss was probable and a $60,000 liability had been accrued.  
- C) On January 5, Year 2, a tornado (new post–year-end casualty) destroys finished goods inventory; uninsured carrying amount $28,000.  
- D) On February 28, Year 2, the market price of Oakline’s publicly traded stock declines 15%.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Settlement of litigation whose **underlying events existed at the balance sheet date**, occurring **before FS issuance**, is a **recognized subsequent event**. Oakline must **adjust** the December 31 Year 1 estimated litigation liability from $60,000 to **$95,000** (additional Year 1 loss $35,000) as a period-end / pre-issuance remeasurement.  
A and C are **nonrecognized** subsequent events (disclose if material); D is neither recognized nor typically disclosed as a subsequent-event note item.

### Q5 — MC — Amount of period-end warranty accrual
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Compute the December 31 period-end adjusting amount for an assurance-type warranty loss contingency  
**Question:**  
**Westfork Outdoor Co.** sold merchandise for **$2,500,000** cash during Year 1. Products include a two-year **assurance-type** warranty (not sold separately). Estimated warranty costs are **1.2% of sales**. Actual warranty claims paid in cash during Year 1 were **$8,000**. Westfork records warranty expense only via a **December 31 period-end adjusting entry** and reduces the liability when claims are paid. What is the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting entry** amount for Warranty Expense / Warranty Liability, and what is the **ending Warranty Liability** after claims?

- A) Accrue $30,000; ending liability $30,000.  
- B) Accrue $30,000; ending liability $22,000.  
- C) Accrue $22,000; ending liability $22,000.  
- D) Accrue $8,000; ending liability $0.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Period-end accrual = \(\$2{,}500{,}000 \times 0.012 = \$30{,}000\) (full estimated cost of the loss contingency in the year of sale). Claims reduce the liability: ending liability = \(30{,}000 - 8{,}000 = \$22{,}000\). Claims are **not** the period-end accrual amount; assurance warranties do not defer revenue for the warranty component.
