# Agent 131 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-8

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for internally developed software and cloud computing arrangements  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Software for sale (ASC 985-20):** expense all costs as R&D until **technological feasibility**; capitalize subsequent coding/testing/product-master costs until product release
- **Initial recognition JE:** Dr R&D Expense / Cr Cash (pre-feasibility); Dr Software Intangible Asset / Cr Cash (post-feasibility, pre-release)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** amortize capitalized software costs product-by-product using the **greater of** the **revenue method** or **straight-line**; amortize only after marketing begins
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** record annual software amortization (greater of revenue vs SL)
- **Internal-use software (ASC 350-40):** expense preliminary-project stage; capitalize application-development stage; expense training always; amortize SL when substantially complete and ready for use
- **Cloud / hosting arrangement:** if customer lacks possession rights (or feasibility to run elsewhere) → **service contract**; capitalize development-stage implementation/setup costs; expense subscription fees; amortize implementation over hosting term
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** cloud implementation asset and amortization presented in same lines as hosting fees/prepaid hosting; R&D vs intangible classification of software costs
- **Disposal / write-off JE:** remove remaining unamortized software cost when product is withdrawn or abandoned
- **Number-variant twin:** same structure, all amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Software for sale: capitalize after tech feasibility, multi-year amortization schedule, write-off
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of software-for-sale costs (expense vs capitalize at technological feasibility); subsequent amortization using greater of revenue method vs straight-line (multi-year schedule); period-end amortization JEs; disposal write-off of remaining book value  
**Scenario:**  
**ApexForge Analytics Corp.** (calendar-year) developed sports analytics software **to be sold** to third parties. All outlays were paid in cash.

**Facts:**
1. **2022–2023 (combined):** Incurred **$2,400,000** planning, designing, coding, and testing costs that established a **working model** (technological feasibility) at **December 31, 2023**.
2. **2024:** Incurred **$720,000** after technological feasibility for final coding, testing, debugging, and production of **product masters**. The product was **completed and available for general release on January 1, 2025**.
3. Expected **economic life** from release: **4 years** (through December 31, 2028). Residual value is zero.
4. **Total anticipated product revenue** over the life (estimated at release): **$4,500,000**.
5. Actual and remaining revenue estimates:
   - **2025** product revenue: **$900,000**
   - **2026** product revenue: **$1,350,000** (total anticipated life revenue estimate remains **$4,500,000** for the revenue-method formula, consistent with Demo 13-8)
   - **2027** product revenue: **$1,125,000**
6. On **January 1, 2028**, management withdraws the product from the market due to a superior competitor platform. Remaining unamortized software cost is written off. (No 2028 sales.)

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **initial recognition JE(s)** for 2022–2023 development costs and for **2024** post-feasibility costs.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement amortization schedule** for **2025, 2026, and 2027**. For each year, compute amortization under the **revenue method** and the **straight-line method**, select the **greater** amount, and show beginning book value, amortization, and ending book value.  
c. Prepare the **period-end adjusting JEs** for software amortization at December 31, **2025**, **2026**, and **2027**.  
d. Prepare the **disposal (write-off) JE** on January 1, **2028**.  
e. **Classification / presentation:** For 2025, state (i) how the software asset is classified on the balance sheet and (ii) where amortization appears on the income statement relative to R&D expense. What amount of software cost is **expensed as R&D** vs **capitalized** over the project life through 2024?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*2022–2023 — pre-technological feasibility (R&D)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Research and Development Expense | 2,400,000 | |
| Cash | | 2,400,000 |
| *Expense software costs incurred before technological feasibility* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,400,000 = Cr 2,400,000. **Balanced.**

*2024 — after technological feasibility, before product release (capitalize)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Intangible Asset | 720,000 | |
| Cash | | 720,000 |
| *Capitalize post-feasibility coding/testing/product masters* | | |

**Check:** Dr 720,000 = Cr 720,000. **Balanced.**

Capitalized cost available for amortization beginning 2025: **$720,000**.

**b. Subsequent measurement amortization schedule (emphasis)**

Straight-line (each full year of economic life, using original capitalized cost per Demo 13-8):  
\[
\text{SL} = \frac{1}{4} \times \$720{,}000 = \$180{,}000
\]

Revenue method (each year):  
\[
\text{Revenue method} = \frac{\text{Current product revenue}}{\text{Total anticipated product revenue}} \times \$720{,}000
\]

| Year | Current revenue | Revenue method | SL | **Amortization (greater)** | Beg. BV | End. BV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2025 | $900,000 | \(900{,}000/4{,}500{,}000 \times 720{,}000 = \$144{,}000\) | $180,000 | **$180,000** (SL) | $720,000 | $540,000 |
| 2026 | $1,350,000 | \(1{,}350{,}000/4{,}500{,}000 \times 720{,}000 = \$216{,}000\) | $180,000 | **$216,000** (Rev) | $540,000 | $324,000 |
| 2027 | $1,125,000 | \(1{,}125{,}000/4{,}500{,}000 \times 720{,}000 = \$180{,}000\) | $180,000 | **$180,000** (tie → either) | $324,000 | $144,000 |
| **Totals 2025–27** | | | | **$576,000** | | **$144,000 remaining** |

Math checks:
- 2025: \(900{,}000 \div 4{,}500{,}000 = 0.20\); \(0.20 \times 720{,}000 = 144{,}000\); greater of 144k and 180k = **180,000**
- 2026: \(1{,}350{,}000 \div 4{,}500{,}000 = 0.30\); \(0.30 \times 720{,}000 = 216{,}000\); greater of 216k and 180k = **216,000**
- 2027: \(1{,}125{,}000 \div 4{,}500{,}000 = 0.25\); \(0.25 \times 720{,}000 = 180{,}000\); greater of 180k and 180k = **180,000**
- Ending BV Dec 31, 2027: \(720{,}000 - 180{,}000 - 216{,}000 - 180{,}000 = 144{,}000\)

**c. Period-end adjusting JEs**

*December 31, 2025*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Amortization Expense | 180,000 | |
| Software Intangible Asset | | 180,000 |
| *Amortize software (greater of revenue $144k vs SL $180k)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 180,000. **Balanced.**

*December 31, 2026*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Amortization Expense | 216,000 | |
| Software Intangible Asset | | 216,000 |
| *Amortize software (greater of revenue $216k vs SL $180k)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 216,000. **Balanced.**

*December 31, 2027*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Amortization Expense | 180,000 | |
| Software Intangible Asset | | 180,000 |
| *Amortize software (revenue method = SL = $180k)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 180,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Disposal / write-off JE (January 1, 2028)**

Remaining book value = **$144,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Software Write-Off (or Software Amortization Expense) | 144,000 | |
| Software Intangible Asset | | 144,000 |
| *Write off remaining unamortized software when product withdrawn* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 144,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification / presentation / disclosure**

(i) **Balance sheet:** Capitalized software costs are reported as an **intangible asset** (finite-life), typically net of accumulated amortization (or with direct credit to the asset as in Demo 13-8).  
(ii) **Income statement:** **Amortization of capitalized software** is **not** R&D expense; it is operating amortization (or cost of sales / operating expense as policy requires). Costs before technological feasibility are **R&D expense**. After product release, ongoing maintenance/support costs are **non-R&D operating expenses**.

Project cost split through 2024:
- **Expensed as R&D:** $2,400,000  
- **Capitalized as software intangible:** $720,000  
- **Total project cash outlays:** $3,120,000

**Key insight:** For software **sold or leased**, capitalize only after **technological feasibility** until **product release**. Each period, amortize the **greater of** revenue-method and straight-line amounts. Withdrawing the product triggers write-off of any remaining book value.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Software for sale twin (all numbers changed)
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: software-for-sale R&D vs capitalization at technological feasibility; multi-year amortization schedule (greater of revenue vs SL); period-end amortization JEs  
**Scenario:**  
**CascadeLogic Systems Inc.** (calendar-year) developed inventory-routing software for **external sale**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare Year 1 and Year 2 **initial recognition JEs**.  
b. Prepare the **amortization schedule** for Year 3 and Year 4 (revenue method, SL, greater amount, beginning/ending BV).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Internal-use software and cloud hosting service contract
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Internal-use software (preliminary vs application development; training expensed); cloud computing hosting as service contract — capitalize implementation, expense subscription, amortize over hosting term; period-end adjusting JEs; classification/presentation of cloud implementation costs  
**Scenario:**  
**HarborPoint Retail Group** (calendar-year) has two software initiatives in **2025**.

**Part A — Internal-use software**  
HarborPoint developed cash-management software **solely for internal use** (not sold or marketed). Cash costs:
1. Preliminary project stage (needs assessment, vendor evaluation, technology selection): **$18,000**
2. Application development stage (coding, configuration, testing, installation to ready-for-use): **$240,000** employee and contractor costs
3. Training employees on the new system (incurred during and after development): **$12,000**
4. Software was **substantially complete and ready for intended use on July 1, 2025**. Estimated useful life: **4 years**, residual value zero, amortize **straight-line**. HarborPoint records full-month amortization starting in July.

**Part B — Cloud computing arrangement**  
On **January 1, 2025**, HarborPoint entered a **four-year** cloud hosting arrangement for an ERP module. HarborPoint **does not** have a contractual right to take possession of the software without significant penalty (and cannot practically run it on its own hardware). Arrangement cash terms:
1. Implementation / setup costs paid January 1, 2025: **$80,000** (development-stage implementation costs)
2. Annual subscription (hosting) fee paid each **December 31**: **$96,000**
3. Implementation is complete and ready for intended use on January 1, 2025; amortize capitalized implementation costs **straight-line over the 4-year hosting term**.

**Required:**  
a. **Internal-use:** Compute the amount **initially capitalized**. Prepare JEs for (i) preliminary costs, (ii) application development costs, and (iii) training costs.  
b. **Internal-use:** Prepare the **2025 period-end amortization JE** and state December 31, 2025 **carrying amount**. Show a brief **amortization schedule** for 2025–2028 (full years after 2025).  
c. **Cloud:** Is the arrangement a **service contract** or a **software purchase**? Explain using the possession criteria.  
d. **Cloud:** Prepare the **January 1, 2025** JE for implementation costs, the **December 31, 2025** JE for the subscription fee, and the **December 31, 2025** amortization adjusting JE.  
e. Prepare a **cloud subsequent measurement schedule** for the implementation asset over the four-year term (beginning BV, amortization, ending BV each year).  
f. **Classification / presentation:** How should HarborPoint present (i) the capitalized cloud implementation costs on the **balance sheet**, (ii) amortization of those costs on the **income statement**, and (iii) related cash flows on the **statement of cash flows**?

**f. Classification / presentation / disclosure (ASC 350-40-45)**

(i) **Balance sheet:** Present capitalized implementation costs in the **same line item** as a **prepayment of fees** for the associated hosting arrangement (not necessarily as a separate software intangible line if the arrangement is a service contract).  
(ii) **Income statement:** Present amortization of implementation costs in the **same income-statement line** as the **hosting subscription expense**.  
(iii) **Cash flows:** Classify cash paid for capitalized implementation costs in the **same manner** as cash paid for the hosting fees (typically operating, consistent with the service nature of the arrangement).

**Key insight:** Internal-use software capitalizes only the **application development** stage (not preliminary or training). Cloud hosting without possession rights is a **service contract**: expense subscriptions; **capitalize and amortize** development-stage **implementation** costs over the hosting term, with presentation tied to hosting fees.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of software and cloud costs
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Classification — expense vs capitalize for software-for-sale, internal-use, and cloud service-contract costs  

**Question 1:**  
Meridian Labs is developing software it will **sell** to customers. Which cost is **capitalized as an intangible asset**?

- A) Costs to establish technological feasibility (planning, designing, and coding that produce the first working model)  
- B) Costs after technological feasibility to produce product masters, incurred **before** the product release date  
- C) Employee training costs after the product is released for sale  
- D) Routine post-release customer support costs  

**Answer:** **B.** After technological feasibility and until product release, coding/testing/product-master costs are capitalized. Costs to establish technological feasibility are **R&D expense** (A). After release, training and support are **non-R&D operating expenses** (C, D), not software assets.

**Question 2:**  
Oakline Corp. has a **cloud hosting service contract** (no right to take possession). Which item is **capitalized**?

- A) Monthly subscription fee for access to the hosted software  
- B) Costs of evaluating alternative cloud vendors in the preliminary stage  
- C) Implementation and setup costs incurred in the development stage before the system is ready for intended use  
- D) Post-implementation security and maintenance fees paid to the vendor  

**Answer:** **C.** Under a service-contract hosting arrangement, development-stage **implementation/setup** costs are capitalized and amortized over the hosting term. Subscription, preliminary evaluation, and post-implementation maintenance/security fees are **expensed** as incurred (A, B, D).
