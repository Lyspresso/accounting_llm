# Agent 227 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-3

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Record accruals for payroll, compensated absences, bonuses, and defined contribution plans  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE — employee payroll:** Salaries Expense for gross pay; credit statutory/voluntary withholdings and Salaries Payable (net)
- **Initial recognition JE — employer payroll taxes:** Payroll Tax Expense; FICA (employer), FUTA, SUTA payables
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-year Accrued Compensation roll-forward (beginning balance, accruals earned, usage/settlement, ending balance); wage-rate change as change in estimate
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** accrue vested/accumulating compensated absences, unpaid bonuses earned, and defined contribution employer match
- **Settlement / remittance JE:** pay net salaries; remit withholdings and employer taxes; extinguish bonus and pension liabilities when paid to employees/trustee
- **Classification:** accrue vacation when ASC 710 four criteria met (including *probable*); do **not** accrue sick pay when payment is only *possible*; employee-related accruals are current liabilities
- **Defined contribution plan:** Pension Expense = required employer contribution for the service period (not investment returns)

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-year compensated absences schedule + payroll lifecycle (emphasis: subsequent measurement schedule)
**LO:** LO 15-3  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for Accrued Compensation over three years; initial payroll/tax recognition; period-end bonus and DC accruals; settlement of liabilities  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Ridge Fabricators Inc.** (calendar-year) tracks payroll and employee-related liabilities as follows.

**Part A — Weekly payroll, week ended December 26, Year 1**  
Gross wages: **$48,000**

| Item | Amount / rate |
|---|---|
| Federal income tax withheld | $11,200 |
| State income tax withheld | $2,400 |
| FICA (employee) | 7.65% of gross |
| Employee voluntary retirement contributions | $1,440 |
| Employee health-care premiums | $1,200 |
| Employer FICA | 7.65% of gross |
| FUTA | 0.6% of full gross (all wages within wage base for simplicity) |
| SUTA | 5.4% of full gross |

**Part B — Compensated absences (subsequent measurement emphasis)**  
Vacation rights vest, relate to services already rendered, payment is probable, and amounts are reasonably estimable.

| Date / period | Event | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Dec 31, Year 1 | Accrue unused vacation earned in Year 1 | **$54,000** |
| During Year 2 | Employees take vacation with prior-accrual cost | **$41,000** |
| During Year 2 | Wage rates for that vacation time are **$3,000 higher** than the Year-1 accrual (change in estimate); credit Cash for cash paid | |
| Dec 31, Year 2 | Accrue additional unused vacation earned in Year 2 | **$48,000** |
| During Year 3 | Employees take vacation with prior-accrual cost | **$53,000** |
| During Year 3 | Wage rates for that vacation time are **$2,500 higher** than prior accrual | |
| Dec 31, Year 3 | Accrue additional unused vacation earned in Year 3 | **$36,000** |

No vacation liability exists before December 31, Year 1.

**Part C — Year 1 other period-end accruals and early Year 2 settlement**  
1. Board-approved cash bonus for Year 1 services: **$28,000**, payable January 20, Year 2.  
2. Employer defined contribution match for Year 1: **$7,200**, payable January 15, Year 2 to the plan trustee.  
3. On **January 2, Year 2**, Meridian (i) pays employees their December 26 net salaries and (ii) remits **all** December 26 employee withholdings and employer payroll taxes in one combined remittance entry.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **December 26, Year 1** journal entries for (1) employee payroll and withholdings and (2) employer payroll taxes (**initial recognition**).  
b. Prepare a complete **subsequent measurement schedule** for Accrued Compensation for **Years 1–3** showing beginning balance, vacation earned (accrued), vacation used (at prior rates), and ending balance.  
c. Record the **period-end adjusting entries** on December 31, Year 1 for (1) compensated absences, (2) bonus, and (3) defined contribution plan.  
d. Record the **Year 2** entry when vacation is taken (including the wage-rate increase).  
e. Record the **Year 3** entry when vacation is taken (including the wage-rate increase).  
f. Record the **settlement** entries: (1) January 2, Year 2 payment of net salaries; (2) January 2 remittance of withholdings and employer payroll taxes; (3) January 15 payment of DC match; (4) January 20 payment of bonus.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end classification adjustments + settlement of prior accruals
**LO:** LO 15-3  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JEs only; classify vacation (probable) vs sick pay (possible only); settle prior bonus and DC liability; presentation as current liabilities  
**Scenario:**  
**Silverbrook Dental Group LLC** is preparing **December 31, Year 2** adjusting entries. Selected information:

| Item | Facts |
|---|---|
| Vested vacation | Rights vest; attributable to services already rendered; payment **probable**; reasonably estimated at **$95,000** (no prior Accrued Compensation balance) |
| Sick pay | Rights **do not vest** but accumulate; attributable to services rendered; reasonably estimated at **$40,000**; payment is only **possible** (not probable) |
| Bonus | Board approved **$18,500** for Year 2 services on December 20; payable January 18, Year 3 |
| Defined contribution | Employer match for Year 2 service = **$14,200**, to be remitted to the trustee on January 10, Year 3 |
| Prior Year 1 bonus | Accrued Dec 31, Year 1 for **$16,000**; paid in full on **January 18, Year 2** |
| Prior Year 1 DC match | Accrued Dec 31, Year 1 for **$11,800**; remitted to trustee on **January 12, Year 2** |

**Required:**  
a. Record **only** the December 31, Year 2 **period-end adjusting** journal entries that are required under GAAP (vacation, sick pay if any, bonus, DC plan).  
b. State the **total employee-related liability** Silverbrook reports from items in (a) on the December 31, Year 2 balance sheet, and classify as current or noncurrent.  
c. Record the **settlement** entries on January 18, Year 2 (prior bonus) and January 12, Year 2 (prior DC).  
d. In one or two sentences, explain why sick pay is treated differently from vacation pay in this fact pattern.

**Answer key:**  

**a. December 31, Year 2 — period-end adjusting entries**

*(1) Vacation — accrue (all four ASC 710 criteria met)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Salaries Expense | 95,000 | |
| Accrued Compensation | | 95,000 |

**Check:** 95,000 = 95,000.

*(2) Sick pay — **no entry***  
Payment is only **possible**, not **probable**. Criterion (c) of ASC 710-10-25-1 fails → no liability or expense for the $40,000 estimate. Disclosure may be appropriate, but no accrual.

*(3) Bonus*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Salaries Expense | 18,500 | |
| Salaries Payable (Bonuses Payable) | | 18,500 |

**Check:** 18,500 = 18,500.

*(4) Defined contribution*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pension Expense | 14,200 | |
| Accrued Pension Liability | | 14,200 |

**Check:** 14,200 = 14,200.

**b. Balance sheet presentation (Dec 31, Year 2)**  
Total liability from (a) = \(95{,}000 + 18{,}500 + 14{,}200 = \mathbf{\$127{,}700}\), all **current liabilities** (expected settlement within one year / operating cycle). Sick pay **$0** liability.

**c. Settlement of prior-year accruals (Year 2 cash payments)**

*January 18, Year 2 — pay Year 1 bonus*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Salaries Payable (Bonuses Payable) | 16,000 | |
| Cash | | 16,000 |

**Check:** 16,000 = 16,000.

*January 12, Year 2 — remit Year 1 DC match*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accrued Pension Liability | 11,800 | |
| Cash | | 11,800 |

**Check:** 11,800 = 11,800.

**d. Why sick vs vacation differs**  
ASC 710 requires **probable** payment. Vested vacation is almost always accrued; nonvested sick pay that is only *possible* (employees may never use it) fails the probability criterion, so no liability is recognized even if the amount is estimable.

**Key insight:** Period-end work is a **gatekeeping** exercise: same “estimate” field does not always produce a JE—**probability** (and vest/accumulate) decide whether compensated absences hit the books. Settlement of prior accruals never re-expenses the original amount; it only clears the liability with cash.

### Q4 — MC — Compensated absences criteria
**LO:** LO 15-3  
**Concept:** When to accrue a liability for compensated absences (ASC 710 four criteria)  
**Question:**  
Which of the following fact patterns requires **accrual** of a liability for compensated absences at year-end under U.S. GAAP (ASC 710)?

- A) Employees earn nonvested sick days that accumulate; payment is **possible** but not probable; amount reasonably estimated at $50,000.  
- B) Employees have vested unused vacation attributable to services already rendered; payment is **probable**; amount cannot be reasonably estimated.  
- C) Employees have vested unused vacation attributable to services already rendered; payment is **probable**; amount reasonably estimated at $50,000.  
- D) Employees will be granted a discretionary holiday next year that has **not** yet been earned by services rendered this year; amount estimated at $50,000.

**Answer:** **C.**  
All four criteria are met: (1) obligation attributable to services already rendered, (2) rights vest (or accumulate), (3) payment probable, (4) amount reasonably estimable.  
A fails **probable**. B fails **reasonably estimable** (disclosure only). D fails **services already rendered** / earned obligation.

### Q5 — MC — Defined contribution measurement
**LO:** LO 15-3  
**Concept:** Measurement of defined contribution pension expense vs investment risk  
**Question:**  
Under a **defined contribution** retirement plan, the employer’s Pension Expense for the period equals:

- A) The actuarial present value of future retirement benefits promised to employees.  
- B) The required employer contribution for the period attributable to employee services (paid or payable).  
- C) The fair-value change in plan assets during the period.  
- D) Employee elective deferrals withheld from gross pay only (employer match is never expensed).

**Answer:** **B.**  
For a defined contribution plan, the employer’s obligation is satisfied when the required contribution for the service period is made (or accrued as payable). Investment performance risk rests with the employee; the employer does **not** measure expense using projected benefit obligations (that is defined **benefit** accounting, Ch. 23). Employee elective deferrals are withholdings from salaries (liabilities to the plan), separate from employer Pension Expense for the match/contribution.
