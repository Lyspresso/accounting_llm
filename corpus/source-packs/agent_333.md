# Agent 333 — CORE DEMO — LO 11-6

**Chapter:** 11  
**LO title:** Account for property, plant, and equipment related costs after acquisition  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Ordinary repairs & maintenance:** costs that keep an asset in operating condition without extending original useful life or productivity — **expense** in the period incurred
- **Accident / neglect damage:** repair after mishap does not increase value above pre-mishap condition — recognize as a **loss** (not capitalized)
- **Improvements / replacements:** major component upgrade that increases useful life or productivity — **capitalize**; method depends on facts:
  - **Substitution:** remove old component (cost + accum. dep.) and record loss/gain, then capitalize new (use when old carrying amount is known)
  - **Capitalization:** debit asset for new cost without removing old (use when old is significantly depreciated or old cost unknown)
  - **Reduction of accumulated depreciation:** debit Accum. Dep. for expenditure (use when life is extended but improved quality is not the primary result)
- **Additions:** extensions/enlargements capitalized; depreciate over the **shorter** of the addition’s own life or remaining life of the related asset
- **Rearrangements:** material reinstallations that clearly benefit future periods — capitalize and depreciate over periods benefited; otherwise expense
- **No accrue-in-advance** for planned major maintenance (ASC 360-10-25-5)
- **Initial recognition JE** of subsequent capital expenditures (Building / Equipment)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** revise depreciable base and remaining life; partial-year depreciation from placement / expenditure date
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** year-end depreciation on structure + new components after subsequent costs
- **Disposal / removal JE:** substitution method removes old component (and separate asset replacement/disposal)

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)

**LO:** LO 11-6  
**Concept:** Classify subsequent PPE costs (capitalize vs expense vs loss) and select the appropriate improvement method  

**Question 1:**  
Which expenditure is **expensed** as incurred rather than capitalized as a post-acquisition PPE cost?

- A) Installation of a fire sprinkler system that expands building safety capacity for future periods  
- B) Annual lubrication, cleaning, and minor parts for production equipment that does not extend original useful life  
- C) Construction of an extra wing that enlarges an existing building  
- D) Material rearrangement of factory equipment that will improve efficiency for the next five years  

**Answer:** **B.** Maintenance and ordinary repairs keep assets in operating condition without prolonging original useful life or productivity and are expensed. A, C, and D are capital expenditures (addition, addition, and rearrangement with multi-period benefit).

**Question 2:**  
A company replaces a major building component. The original component’s cost and accumulated depreciation **are known**. The replacement significantly improves the building. The **most appropriate** accounting approach is:

- A) Expense the entire cost of the new component as repairs  
- B) Debit Accumulated Depreciation only (reduction method) and never remove the old component’s cost  
- C) **Substitution:** remove the old component’s cost and accum. dep. (recognize gain/loss), then capitalize the new component  
- D) Accrue the replacement cost evenly over the two years before the work is performed  

**Answer:** **C.** When the old component’s carrying amount is available, **substitution** removes the old component and capitalizes the new one (Demo 11-6). The reduction-of-accum.-dep. method is aimed at life extension without quality improvement / when that presentation is elected; accrue-in-advance for planned maintenance is prohibited; ordinary expense treatment is wrong for a major improvement.
