# Agent 312 — CORE DEMO — LO 9-3

**Chapter:** 9  
**LO title:** Demonstrate specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO in a periodic inventory system  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE** (periodic): Purchases (temporary) and Accounts Payable/Cash at acquisition; permanent Inventory not updated until period end
- **Disposal / settlement at sale** (periodic): revenue only at sale; no Inventory/COGS relief until the period-end cost-flow allocation
- **COGAS / subsequent measurement schedule:** BI + purchases layers; physical count → EI units; allocate COGAS under each cost-flow method
- **Specific identification (periodic):** EI and COGS at actual costs of identified lots
- **Average cost (periodic):** weighted-average unit cost × EI units and sold units
- **FIFO (periodic):** EI from most recent purchases; COGS from earliest layers
- **LIFO (periodic):** EI from earliest layers; COGS from most recent purchases
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** Dr Inventory (EI) + Dr COGS; Cr Purchases + Cr Inventory (BI)—only the EI/COGS split changes by method
- Rising-price comparison: FIFO highest EI / lowest COGS; LIFO lowest EI / highest COGS; average typically in between
- Classification/presentation: Inventory (BS current asset); COGS (IS); method is a cost-flow assumption (except specific ID matches physical identity)

### Q1 — CORE — Periodic four methods: purchases, sale, schedules, period-end adjusting JE (emphasis)

**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Initial purchase recognition under periodic; sale (revenue only); COGAS schedule; EI/COGS under specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO; **period-end adjusting JE** under average cost (emphasis)  
**Scenario:**  
**Clearwater Industrial Supply Co.** uses a **periodic inventory system**. Fiscal month is **March**. Beginning inventory on March 1 consists of **200 units at $10** each (**$2,000**). No purchase discounts, returns, freight-in, or allowances this month. Unit purchase costs are **rising**.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Mar 5 | Purchased **300 units at $12** on account (**$3,600**). |
| Mar 16 | Purchased **400 units at $14** on account (**$5,600**). |
| Mar 25 | Purchased **100 units at $16** on account (**$1,600**). |
| Mar (various) | Sold **700 units** on account for total sales revenue of **$14,000**. |
| Mar 31 | Physical inventory count: **300 units** on hand. |

For **specific identification**, ending inventory is identified as: **50 units from March 1**, **75 units from March 5**, **100 units from March 16**, and **75 units from March 25**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare **initial recognition journal entries** for the three purchases and the aggregate **sale** entry (periodic).  
b. Prepare the **cost of goods available for sale** schedule (units and dollars).  
c. Compute **ending inventory** and **cost of goods sold** under each of: (1) specific identification, (2) average cost, (3) FIFO, (4) LIFO. Show layer detail for EI (and COGS layers where helpful).  
d. Prepare the **March 31 period-end adjusting entry** assuming the company uses the **average cost** method (**emphasis**).  
e. State March 31 **Inventory** (balance sheet) and **Cost of goods sold** (income statement) under average cost. Rank the four methods by ending inventory (highest to lowest) given rising costs.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition and sale (periodic)**

*March 5 — Purchase (initial recognition)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 3,600 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 3,600 |
| *Record purchase at invoice cost; Inventory permanent account not updated in periodic* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 3,600.

*March 16 — Purchase*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 5,600 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 5,600 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 5,600.

*March 25 — Purchase*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 1,600 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 1,600 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,600.

Purchases total: \(3{,}600 + 5{,}600 + 1{,}600 = \mathbf{\$10{,}800}\).

*March (aggregate) — Sale (periodic: revenue only; disposal of inventory quantity only—no cost relief yet)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 14,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 14,000 |
| *No COGS or Inventory relief at sale under periodic* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 14,000.

**b. Cost of goods available for sale**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1 beginning inventory | 200 | $10 | $2,000 |
| Mar 5 purchase | 300 | 12 | 3,600 |
| Mar 16 purchase | 400 | 14 | 5,600 |
| Mar 25 purchase | 100 | 16 | 1,600 |
| **Goods available for sale** | **1,000** | | **$12,800** |

Units sold = 700; units in EI = \(1{,}000 - 700 = \mathbf{300}\).  
COGAS check: BI \( \$2{,}000 \) + Purchases \( \$10{,}800 \) = **$12,800**.

**c. Ending inventory and COGS — four methods**

**(1) Specific identification — Ending inventory**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1 | 50 | $10 | $500 |
| Mar 5 | 75 | 12 | 900 |
| Mar 16 | 100 | 14 | 1,400 |
| Mar 25 | 75 | 16 | 1,200 |
| **EI** | **300** | | **$4,000** |

COGS = COGAS − EI = \(12{,}800 - 4{,}000 = \mathbf{\$8{,}800}\).

*(Optional sold-layer check)*  
Mar 1: \(150 \times \$10 = \$1{,}500\); Mar 5: \(225 \times \$12 = \$2{,}700\); Mar 16: \(300 \times \$14 = \$4{,}200\); Mar 25: \(25 \times \$16 = \$400\).  
Units sold \(150 + 225 + 300 + 25 = 700\); COGS \(= 1{,}500 + 2{,}700 + 4{,}200 + 400 = \mathbf{\$8{,}800}\).

**(2) Average cost (weighted average, periodic)**

Weighted-average unit cost = \( \$12{,}800 \div 1{,}000 \) units = **$12.80** per unit.

| | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Ending inventory | \(300 \times \$12.80\) | **$3,840** |
| Cost of goods sold | \(700 \times \$12.80\) | **$8,960** |

Check: \(3{,}840 + 8{,}960 = 12{,}800\).

**(3) FIFO — Ending inventory (most recent layers first)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 25 | 100 | $16 | $1,600 |
| Mar 16 | 200 | 14 | 2,800 |
| **EI** | **300** | | **$4,400** |

COGS = \(12{,}800 - 4{,}400 = \mathbf{\$8{,}400}\).

*(COGS layers — earliest first)*  
Mar 1: \(200 \times \$10 = \$2{,}000\); Mar 5: \(300 \times \$12 = \$3{,}600\); Mar 16: \(200 \times \$14 = \$2{,}800\).  
Total 700 units; COGS = **$8,400**.

**(4) LIFO — Ending inventory (earliest layers first)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mar 1 | 200 | $10 | $2,000 |
| Mar 5 | 100 | 12 | 1,200 |
| **EI** | **300** | | **$3,200** |

COGS = \(12{,}800 - 3{,}200 = \mathbf{\$9{,}600}\).

*(COGS layers — most recent first)*  
Mar 25: \(100 \times \$16 = \$1{,}600\); Mar 16: \(400 \times \$14 = \$5{,}600\); Mar 5: \(200 \times \$12 = \$2{,}400\).  
Total 700 units; COGS = **$9,600**.

**Summary (rising unit costs)**

| Method | Ending inventory | COGS |
|---|---:|---:|
| Specific identification | $4,000 | $8,800 |
| Average cost | 3,840 | 8,960 |
| FIFO | **4,400** (highest EI) | **8,400** (lowest COGS) |
| LIFO | **3,200** (lowest EI) | **9,600** (highest COGS) |

**d. March 31 — Period-end adjusting entry (average cost) — emphasis**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 3,840 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 8,960 | |
| Purchases | | 10,800 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 2,000 |
| *Close Purchases and BI; record EI at weighted average; residual = COGS* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(3{,}840 + 8{,}960 = \mathbf{12{,}800}\); Cr \(10{,}800 + 2{,}000 = \mathbf{12{,}800}\). **Balanced.**  
Also: EI + COGS = BI + Purchases = COGAS.

**e. Presentation (average cost) and ranking**  
- Balance sheet — Inventory (Mar 31): **$3,840**  
- Income statement — Cost of goods sold: **$8,960**  
- EI ranking (highest → lowest): **FIFO ($4,400) > specific ID ($4,000) > average ($3,840) > LIFO ($3,200)**.

**Key insight:** In a **periodic** system, purchases go to temporary **Purchases**; sales do not relieve Inventory or record COGS. After the physical count, cost-flow methods allocate **COGAS** between EI and COGS. The **period-end adjusting JE** always closes Purchases and BI and records EI and COGS—only the **dollar split** depends on the method. With rising prices, FIFO assigns newer costs to EI; LIFO leaves older costs in EI.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Periodic four methods twin

**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin—periodic specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO; initial purchase/sale JEs; **period-end adjusting JE under LIFO** (emphasis)  
**Scenario:**  
**Ironclad Fasteners Inc.** uses a **periodic inventory system**. Fiscal month is **October**. Beginning inventory on October 1: **200 units at $25** each (**$5,000**).

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Oct 4 | Purchased **250 units at $28** cash (**$7,000**). |
| Oct 14 | Purchased **350 units at $30** on account (**$10,500**). |
| Oct 25 | Purchased **200 units at $32** on account (**$6,400**). |
| Oct (various) | Sold **650 units** on account for total sales of **$32,500**. |
| Oct 31 | Physical count: **350 units** on hand. |

**Specific identification** of ending inventory: **60 units from Oct 1**, **90 units from Oct 4**, **120 units from Oct 14**, and **80 units from Oct 25**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare journal entries for the three purchases and the aggregate sale.  
b. Compute COGAS (units and dollars).  
c. Compute EI and COGS under specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO.  
d. Prepare the October 31 **period-end adjusting entry under LIFO** (emphasis).  
e. Briefly rank EI amounts from highest to lowest and relate the ranking to the price trend.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Journal entries**

*Oct 4 — Purchase for cash*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 7,000 | |
| Cash | | 7,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 7,000.

*Oct 14 — Purchase on account*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 10,500 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 10,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,500.

*Oct 25 — Purchase on account*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Purchases | 6,400 | |
| Accounts Payable | | 6,400 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 6,400.

Purchases total = \(7{,}000 + 10{,}500 + 6{,}400 = \mathbf{\$23{,}900}\).

*Sale (periodic)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 32,500 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 32,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 32,500.

**b. COGAS**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct 1 beginning | 200 | $25 | $5,000 |
| Oct 4 | 250 | 28 | 7,000 |
| Oct 14 | 350 | 30 | 10,500 |
| Oct 25 | 200 | 32 | 6,400 |
| **Available** | **1,000** | | **$28,900** |

EI units = \(1{,}000 - 650 = \mathbf{350}\). COGAS = \(5{,}000 + 23{,}900 = \mathbf{\$28{,}900}\).

**c. Four methods**

**(1) Specific identification — EI**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct 1 | 60 | $25 | $1,500 |
| Oct 4 | 90 | 28 | 2,520 |
| Oct 14 | 120 | 30 | 3,600 |
| Oct 25 | 80 | 32 | 2,560 |
| **EI** | **350** | | **$10,180** |

COGS = \(28{,}900 - 10{,}180 = \mathbf{\$18{,}720}\).

*(Sold-layer check)*  
Oct 1: \(140 \times \$25 = \$3{,}500\); Oct 4: \(160 \times \$28 = \$4{,}480\); Oct 14: \(230 \times \$30 = \$6{,}900\); Oct 25: \(120 \times \$32 = \$3{,}840\).  
Units \(140 + 160 + 230 + 120 = 650\); COGS \(= 3{,}500 + 4{,}480 + 6{,}900 + 3{,}840 = \mathbf{\$18{,}720}\).

**(2) Average cost**  
WA unit cost = \( \$28{,}900 \div 1{,}000 = \mathbf{\$28.90} \).  
EI = \(350 \times \$28.90 = \mathbf{\$10{,}115}\).  
COGS = \(650 \times \$28.90 = \mathbf{\$18{,}785}\).  
Check: \(10{,}115 + 18{,}785 = 28{,}900\).

**(3) FIFO — EI (most recent)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct 25 | 200 | $32 | $6,400 |
| Oct 14 | 150 | 30 | 4,500 |
| **EI** | **350** | | **$10,900** |

COGS = \(28{,}900 - 10{,}900 = \mathbf{\$18{,}000}\).  
*(COGS layers)* \(200 \times \$25 + 250 \times \$28 + 200 \times \$30 = 5{,}000 + 7{,}000 + 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$18{,}000}\).

**(4) LIFO — EI (earliest)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct 1 | 200 | $25 | $5,000 |
| Oct 4 | 150 | 28 | 4,200 |
| **EI** | **350** | | **$9,200** |

COGS = \(28{,}900 - 9{,}200 = \mathbf{\$19{,}700}\).  
*(COGS layers)* \(200 \times \$32 + 350 \times \$30 + 100 \times \$28 = 6{,}400 + 10{,}500 + 2{,}800 = \mathbf{\$19{,}700}\).

**Summary**

| Method | EI | COGS |
|---|---:|---:|
| Specific identification | $10,180 | $18,720 |
| Average cost | 10,115 | 18,785 |
| FIFO | 10,900 | 18,000 |
| LIFO | 9,200 | 19,700 |

**d. Oct 31 — Period-end adjusting entry (LIFO) — emphasis**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 9,200 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 19,700 | |
| Purchases | | 23,900 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 5,000 |
| *LIFO allocation of COGAS; close temporary Purchases and BI* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(9{,}200 + 19{,}700 = \mathbf{28{,}900}\); Cr \(23{,}900 + 5{,}000 = \mathbf{28{,}900}\). **Balanced.**

**e. Ranking and price trend**  
Unit costs are **rising** ($25 → $32). EI ranking highest to lowest: **FIFO ($10,900) > specific ID ($10,180) > average ($10,115) > LIFO ($9,200)**. FIFO matches newest costs to inventory; LIFO leaves oldest costs in inventory. Average cost falls between FIFO and LIFO when prices trend monotonically; specific identification depends on which lots remain.

**Key insight:** Changing only the unit costs and quantities preserves the same mechanics: build COGAS, apply each cost-flow rule to the physical count, then close Purchases and BI into EI and COGS with one balanced **period-end adjusting entry**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end adjusting JEs under all four methods (emphasis)

**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** **Period-end adjusting JEs** for specific identification, average cost, FIFO, and LIFO from the same COGAS; subsequent comparison schedule; BS/IS presentation and rising-price pretax income effects  
**Scenario:**  
**Northvale Outdoor Gear Inc.** uses a **periodic inventory system**. For **November**, the company has already recorded purchases totaling **$30,400** in the Purchases account (initial recognition complete). Beginning inventory on November 1 was **$6,000**. No purchase returns, allowances, freight-in, or discounts apply. Sales revenue of **$39,000** for 520 units sold has already been recorded (no COGS entry yet).

Unit data (same for all methods):

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nov 1 beginning inventory | 150 | $40 | $6,000 |
| Nov 7 purchase | 250 | 44 | 11,000 |
| Nov 18 purchase | 300 | 48 | 14,400 |
| Nov 28 purchase | 100 | 50 | 5,000 |
| **Available** | **800** | | **$36,400** |

Physical count on November 30: **280 units** remaining (thus **520 units** sold).

For **specific identification**, ending inventory is: **30 units from Nov 1**, **70 units from Nov 7**, **100 units from Nov 18**, and **80 units from Nov 28**.

**Required:**  
a. Compute ending inventory and COGS under **specific identification**, **average cost**, **FIFO**, and **LIFO** (show layers where applicable).  
b. Prepare the **November 30 period-end adjusting entry** under each of the four methods (four separate entries—**emphasis**).  
c. Prepare a **comparison schedule** of Inventory (BS) and COGS (IS) under all four methods.  
d. Compute the difference in pretax income between FIFO and LIFO (Sales already $39,000; ignore taxes). State which method reports higher pretax income when costs are rising and why.  
e. Confirm that for every method, Dr = Cr on the adjusting entry and EI + COGS = COGAS.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Allocations**

**(1) Specific identification — EI**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nov 1 | 30 | $40 | $1,200 |
| Nov 7 | 70 | 44 | 3,080 |
| Nov 18 | 100 | 48 | 4,800 |
| Nov 28 | 80 | 50 | 4,000 |
| **EI** | **280** | | **$13,080** |

COGS = \(36{,}400 - 13{,}080 = \mathbf{\$23{,}320}\).

**(2) Average cost**  
WA unit cost = \( \$36{,}400 \div 800 = \mathbf{\$45.50} \).  
EI = \(280 \times \$45.50 = \mathbf{\$12{,}740}\).  
COGS = \(520 \times \$45.50 = \mathbf{\$23{,}660}\).  
Check: \(12{,}740 + 23{,}660 = 36{,}400\).

**(3) FIFO — EI (most recent 280 units)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nov 28 | 100 | $50 | $5,000 |
| Nov 18 | 180 | 48 | 8,640 |
| **EI** | **280** | | **$13,640** |

COGS = \(36{,}400 - 13{,}640 = \mathbf{\$22{,}760}\).  
*(COGS check — earliest)* \(150 \times \$40 + 250 \times \$44 + 120 \times \$48 = 6{,}000 + 11{,}000 + 5{,}760 = \mathbf{\$22{,}760}\).

**(4) LIFO — EI (earliest 280 units)**

| Layer | Units | Unit cost | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nov 1 | 150 | $40 | $6,000 |
| Nov 7 | 130 | 44 | 5,720 |
| **EI** | **280** | | **$11,720** |

COGS = \(36{,}400 - 11{,}720 = \mathbf{\$24{,}680}\).  
*(COGS check — most recent)* \(100 \times \$50 + 300 \times \$48 + 120 \times \$44 = 5{,}000 + 14{,}400 + 5{,}280 = \mathbf{\$24{,}680}\).

**b. November 30 period-end adjusting entries (emphasis — all four methods)**

Structure for every method (only EI and COGS amounts change):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory (ending) | EI | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | COGS | |
| Purchases | | 30,400 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 6,000 |

*Specific identification*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 13,080 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 23,320 | |
| Purchases | | 30,400 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(13{,}080 + 23{,}320 = 36{,}400\); Cr \(30{,}400 + 6{,}000 = 36{,}400\). **Balanced.**

*Average cost*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 12,740 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 23,660 | |
| Purchases | | 30,400 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(12{,}740 + 23{,}660 = 36{,}400\); Cr \(30{,}400 + 6{,}000 = 36{,}400\). **Balanced.**

*FIFO*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 13,640 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 22,760 | |
| Purchases | | 30,400 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(13{,}640 + 22{,}760 = 36{,}400\); Cr \(30{,}400 + 6{,}000 = 36{,}400\). **Balanced.**

*LIFO*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending) | 11,720 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 24,680 | |
| Purchases | | 30,400 |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(11{,}720 + 24{,}680 = 36{,}400\); Cr \(30{,}400 + 6{,}000 = 36{,}400\). **Balanced.**

**c. Comparison schedule**

| Method | Inventory (BS) | COGS (IS) | Gross profit (Sales $39,000 − COGS) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Specific identification | $13,080 | $23,320 | $15,680 |
| Average cost | 12,740 | 23,660 | 15,340 |
| FIFO | **13,640** | **22,760** | **16,240** |
| LIFO | **11,720** | **24,680** | **14,320** |

**d. FIFO vs LIFO pretax income (rising costs)**  
Difference in pretax income = FIFO GP − LIFO GP = \(16{,}240 - 14{,}320 = \mathbf{\$1{,}920}\).  
Reconciliation: FIFO EI − LIFO EI = \(13{,}640 - 11{,}720 = \mathbf{\$1{,}920}\) = LIFO COGS − FIFO COGS \(= 24{,}680 - 22{,}760\).  

When unit costs are **rising**, **FIFO** reports **higher ending inventory**, **lower COGS**, and **higher pretax income** than LIFO because older, cheaper costs flow to COGS. These are **cost-flow assumptions**; physical goods often move FIFO even when LIFO is elected for accounting/tax.

**e. Universal checks**  
For every method: EI + COGS = **$36,400** = COGAS; period-end JE Dr = Cr = **$36,400**. The adjusting entry **settles** the temporary Purchases account and replaces beginning inventory with ending inventory while recognizing COGS for the period’s “disposal” of units sold.

**Key insight:** The period-end adjusting entry has the **same structure** under every cost-flow method—only the **split** between EI and COGS changes. FIFO vs LIFO pretax income differences equal the EI difference. Specific identification and average cost produce intermediate results here, but specific ID can fall outside FIFO–LIFO depending on which lots remain.

### Q4 — MC — Method effects under rising prices (periodic)

**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Classification of cost-flow method effects on EI and COGS when acquisition costs are rising  
**Question:**  
A company uses a **periodic inventory system**. Acquisition costs of inventory **rose steadily** during the year. Ending unit quantity is positive and the same under all methods. Which method produces the **highest ending inventory** and the **lowest cost of goods sold**?

- A) LIFO  
- B) Average cost  
- C) FIFO  
- D) Specific identification  

**Answer:** **C.** Under rising costs, FIFO assigns the **most recent (highest)** unit costs to ending inventory and the **earliest (lowest)** costs to COGS, so EI is highest and COGS is lowest among the assumption methods. LIFO does the reverse. Average cost typically falls between FIFO and LIFO. Specific identification depends on which units remain and need not produce the highest EI.

### Q5 — MC — Timing of COGS under periodic cost-flow methods

**LO:** LO 9-3  
**Concept:** Classification of when COGS is measured under periodic cost-flow assumptions  
**Question:**  
Under a **periodic** inventory system that applies FIFO (or LIFO, average cost, or specific identification) after a physical count, when is **cost of goods sold** first recorded in the accounts?

- A) At the moment of each sale, when Inventory is credited for the cost of units sold  
- B) Continuously after every purchase, when a moving-average unit cost is recomputed  
- C) Only at **period end**, in the adjusting entry that records ending inventory and closes Purchases and beginning inventory  
- D) Only when cash is collected from customers  

**Answer:** **C.** In a **periodic** system, sales entries record **revenue only**. COGS is measured after the physical count using the elected cost-flow method and is recorded in the **period-end adjusting entry** (along with EI). Continuous COGS at each sale is a **perpetual** system feature (LO 9-5), not periodic.
