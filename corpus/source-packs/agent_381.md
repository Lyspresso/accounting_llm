# Agent 381 — CORE DEMO — LO 20-5

**Chapter:** 20  
**LO title:** Compute EPS given share issuances, buybacks, dividends, and splits  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Basic EPS formula with LO 20-5 adjustments:** (net income − preferred dividend claim) ÷ weighted-average common shares outstanding
- **Preferred dividends (numerator):** noncumulative — subtract only dividends **declared**; cumulative — subtract **one year’s claim** whether or not declared (current year only; no double-count of arrears)
- **Common cash dividends:** no effect on the EPS numerator (distributions of earnings already in net income / RE)
- **Initial recognition JE:** mid-period common stock issuance (and related equity accounts) that changes shares outstanding for the WA denominator
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** multiperiod **weighted-average common shares** schedule (time-weight issuances and buybacks); basic EPS computation schedule
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** year-end AJs that finalize pretax income, income tax, and **net income** before the EPS numerator is computed; year-end preferred dividend declaration that fixes the preferred claim when declared
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** treasury stock buyback (cost method); preferred (and optional common) cash dividend payment/settlement
- **Stock dividends and stock splits (denominator):** apply a **retroactive restatement factor** as if the split/dividend occurred at the **beginning of the earliest period presented** (unlike cash issuances/buybacks, which are time-weighted only from the change date)
- **Large stock dividend JE:** capitalize par of shares issued from retained earnings to common stock

### Q1 — CORE — Issuance, buyback, cumulative preferred, period-end AJs that finalize NI (emphasis)
**LO:** LO 20-5  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for mid-year common issuance; treasury buyback settlement JE; period-end adjusting JEs that finalize net income (emphasis); preferred dividend declaration/settlement; weighted-average shares schedule with time-weighting; basic EPS with cumulative preferred subtraction  
**Scenario:**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|

\]

*Check:* \(217{,}500 \times \$2.00 = \$435{,}000\).

*Note:* EPS is a **presentation** measure; there is no journal entry that books basic EPS itself.

**Key insight:** Under LO 20-5, the **numerator** is net income reduced by the **preferred** claim (for cumulative preferred, one year’s dividend even if undeclared—here it was also declared). **Common cash dividends do not** affect EPS. The **denominator** time-weights **issuances and buybacks** only for the fraction of the year the shares were outstanding. **Period-end adjusting JEs** finalize depreciation, interest, and tax so the EPS numerator is complete before division.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — 100% stock dividend retroactive restatement; issuance; buyback; noncumulative preferred
**LO:** LO 20-5  
**Concept:** Weighted-average shares with **retroactive restatement** for a large stock dividend; mid-year issuance and buyback JEs; large stock dividend capitalization JE; noncumulative preferred dividends (subtract only when declared); basic EPS schedule

**Lakeshore Fiber Optics Ltd.** is a calendar-year public company. No convertibles, options, or warrants. No noncontrolling interest.

**Beginning of Year 1 (already outstanding):**

25,000 + 30,000 + 27,500 + 27,500 = **110,000**.

Noncumulative preferred: subtract only the **$30,000 declared** (if none had been declared, **no** preferred subtraction).  
Net income available to common = $360,000 − $30,000 = **$330,000**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net income | $360,000 |
| Less: Preferred dividends declared (noncumulative) | (30,000) |
| **Net income available to common stockholders** | **$330,000** |

\]

*Check:* \(110{,}000 \times \$3.00 = \$330{,}000\).

**Why preferred is subtracted / how stock dividend differs from cash issuance:**  
Noncumulative preferred reduces the numerator only for dividends **declared** this period ($30,000). The **100% stock dividend** does not bring in capital and is treated as if it occurred at the **beginning of the year** (factor 2.0 applied to all pre-dividend share counts), whereas the **April cash issuance** is weighted only from April 1 (then restated by the stock-dividend factor for pre-October periods).

**Key insight:** Stock dividends and stock splits change the **unit of ownership**, not the capital available for the year; GAAP therefore **restates** all pre-change periods. Cash issuances and buybacks are weighted only for the **time outstanding**. Noncumulative preferred affects EPS only when dividends are **declared**; cumulative preferred always claims one year in the numerator.

### Q4 — MC — Retroactive treatment of stock splits vs time-weighting of cash issuances
**LO:** LO 20-5  
**Concept:** Classification of how stock splits (vs cash share issuances) enter the weighted-average common shares denominator  
**Question:**  
When computing **basic EPS** for the current year, how should a **2-for-1 common stock split** that occurs on **September 1** of the current year be reflected in the **weighted-average common shares** denominator, relative to a **cash issuance** of common shares that also occurs on September 1?

- A) Both the stock split and the cash issuance are weighted only for the 4/12 of the year after September 1; neither affects pre-September share counts.  
- B) The stock split is treated as if it occurred at the **beginning of the earliest period presented** (restating pre-September share counts), while the cash issuance is weighted only for the portion of the year the new shares were outstanding.  
- C) Both the stock split and the cash issuance are treated as if they occurred at the beginning of the year.  
- D) The cash issuance is restated retroactively for all periods, while the stock split is weighted only from September 1.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Stock dividends and stock splits are applied **retroactively** to the beginning of the earliest period presented (they do not raise new capital). Shares issued for cash (or reacquired) are included in the weighted average only for the **fraction of the period** they were outstanding.

### Q5 — MC — Cumulative vs noncumulative preferred in the EPS numerator
**LO:** LO 20-5  
**Concept:** Classification of the preferred-dividend subtraction for basic EPS when preferred is cumulative and **no** dividend is declared  
**Question:**  
**Cedarline Packaging Inc.** reports Year 1 net income of **$500,000**. It has **10,000** shares of **6%, $50 par cumulative** preferred stock outstanding all year. **No** preferred dividends are declared or paid in Year 1. Weighted-average common shares are **100,000**. What is **basic EPS** for Year 1?

- A) $5.00 — no preferred adjustment because no dividend was declared  
- B) $4.70 — subtract one year’s cumulative claim of $30,000  
- C) $4.40 — subtract two years of cumulative preferred claims ($60,000) for current and prior arrears  
- D) $5.30 — add preferred dividends to net income because they were not paid  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Annual cumulative claim = 6% × 10,000 × $50 = **$30,000**. For cumulative preferred, **one year’s** claim is subtracted whether or not declared. Prior-year arrears were (or should have been) subtracted in **prior** periods and are **not** subtracted again.  
Numerator = $500,000 − $30,000 = **$470,000**; basic EPS = $470,000 / 100,000 = **$4.70**.  
(If the preferred were **noncumulative** and nothing was declared, the answer would be $5.00.)
