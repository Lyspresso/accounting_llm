# Agent 278 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-6

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Accounting for Notes Payable (Measure and record notes at issuance and after issuance)  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Measurement basis:** note payable = present value of cash to be paid, discounted at the **market rate at inception**; if face ≠ PV, record Discount (or Premium) on Note Payable
- **Initial recognition JE — cash note:** Dr Cash (± Discount / Premium) / Cr Note Payable at face (par when stated = market; discount when zero-interest or stated < market)
- **Initial recognition JE — noncash:** measure at **fair value of asset or debt, whichever is more clearly evident**; if both unclear, impute market rate on cash flows
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** effective-interest method — Interest expense = beginning carrying amount × original market rate; amort bridges expense and cash (zero cash for zero-interest notes)
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** when the reporting date is not an interest/anniversary date, accrue a pro rata share of the next period’s interest expense, discount amortization, and (if interest-bearing) Interest Payable
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JE:** pay face (carrying amount = face after full amortization); installment notes reduce Note Payable each payment until zero
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** Note payable net of unamortized discount; reclassify amount due within one year (or next installment principal) as current
- **Number-variant twin:** same LO 16-6 path with all different amounts

### Q1 — CORE — Zero-interest mid-year note (period-end emphasis), interest-bearing discount note, schedules, maturity, presentation
**LO:** LO 16-6  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of cash notes (zero-interest and interest-bearing discount); effective-interest amortization schedule; **period-end adjusting JE** when year-end falls between anniversary dates; maturity settlement; BS presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Pinecrest Logistics Inc.** (calendar-year reporter) uses the **effective interest method** and rounds present values and schedule amounts to the nearest dollar (final period plugs residual unamortized discount). Two **separate** cash-note financings:

**Note A — Zero-interest-bearing (period-end emphasis)**  
On **April 1, Year 1**, Pinecrest borrows cash by issuing a **2-year, $180,000 zero-interest-bearing** note payable (stated rate = 0%). No cash interest is paid during the term; the entire face is due **April 1, Year 3**. The market rate for similar risk is **10%**. Because anniversary dates are April 1, **December 31 year-end requires an adjusting accrual** for interest (discount amortization) since the last anniversary (or since issuance in Year 1).

**Note B — Interest-bearing issued at a discount**  
On **January 1, Year 1**, Pinecrest issues a **3-year, $100,000** note for cash, with **5%** cash interest payable **annually each December 31**. The market rate for similar risk is **8%**. Principal is due **December 31, Year 3**.

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **issue proceeds** and prepare the **April 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE** for Note A.  
b. Prepare the full **effective-interest amortization schedule** for Note A (two annual periods ending April 1, Year 2 and April 1, Year 3).  
c. **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting JE** for Note A (accrue **9/12** of the first annual period’s interest/amortization).  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1 balance sheet excerpt** for Note A (note payable, net) and **Year 1 interest expense** related to Note A.  
e. Compute the **issue proceeds** and prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE** for Note B.  
f. Prepare the full **effective-interest amortization schedule** for Note B and the **December 31, Year 1** interest payment + amortization JE.  
g. Prepare the **maturity / settlement JEs** for Note A on **April 1, Year 3** (final period interest from April 1, Year 2 through maturity, then pay face) and for Note B on **December 31, Year 3** (final interest + pay face). Assume all prior interest entries for the final annual period of Note A through April 1, Year 2 have already been recorded, and for Note B the Dec 31, Year 3 interest entry is recorded **before** the principal payment.

### Q3 — CORE — Noncash consideration (imputed rate), installment note, classification / presentation
**LO:** LO 16-6  
**Concept:** Note payable issued for **noncash consideration** (asset FV not clearly evident → impute market rate); note exchanged when asset FV is clear; **installment note** amortization and payments; **current vs noncurrent** classification of note carrying amount  
**Scenario:**

### Q4 — MC — Measurement basis and classification (notes payable)
**LO:** LO 16-6  
**Concept:** Classification of measurement approach for a note issued for noncash consideration; classification of current vs noncurrent installment principal  

**Question 1:**  
On January 1, a company purchases equipment that has **no readily determinable fair value** by issuing a 3-year note with a **stated rate substantially below** market rates for similar risk. Cash interest is paid annually and principal is due at maturity. How should the company **initially measure** the equipment and the note payable?

- A) Face amount of the note, with no discount, because a stated rate exists  
- B) Present value of the note’s future cash payments discounted at the **stated** rate  
- C) Present value of the note’s future cash payments discounted at the **prevailing market (imputed) rate** for similar risk  
- D) Undiscounted sum of all future cash interest and principal payments  

**Answer:** **C.** When the fair value of the asset (and of the note) is not clearly evident and the stated rate is unreasonable relative to market, ASC 835-30 requires measuring the transaction by discounting the note’s cash flows at the **imputed market rate**. That PV is both the equipment cost and the initial carrying amount of the note (any difference from face is Discount on Note Payable).

**Question 2:**  
At December 31, Year 1, an installment note has a carrying amount of **$58,622**. The amortization schedule shows principal reductions of **$27,916** in Year 2 and **$30,706** in Year 3. How much of the note should be reported as a **current liability** at December 31, Year 1?

- A) $0 — installment notes are always long-term until the final year  
- B) $27,916  
- C) $30,706  
- D) $58,622  

**Answer:** **B.** The **current** portion equals the principal to be paid within one year (next installment’s principal reduction, $27,916). The remaining $30,706 is noncurrent. The full carrying amount is not current while payments extend beyond one year.
