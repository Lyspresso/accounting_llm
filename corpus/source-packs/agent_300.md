# Agent 300 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-7

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Prepare the operating cash flow section of the statement of cash flows using the direct method  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Direct method** reports major classes of **gross operating cash receipts and payments** that sum to net cash provided (used) by operating activities
- Convert accrual income-statement amounts to cash using **T-accounts / schedules** for AR, inventory, AP, prepaids, payables, interest, and taxes
- **Noncash items never appear** on the direct-method operating section (depreciation, amortization, bad debt expense, gains/losses, bond premium/discount amortization as lines)
- **Period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis)** create the noncash expenses and year-end accruals that drive the accrual-to-cash conversion
- **Cash collected from customers** = Sales − ↑AR + ↓AR − write-offs + recoveries (gross AR T-account)
- **Cash paid to suppliers** = COGS ± inventory change ± accounts payable change
- **Cash paid for interest** = Interest expense + premium amortization − discount amortization ± interest payable change
- **Cash paid for taxes** = Income tax expense ± income taxes payable (and deferred tax) changes
- **Disposal / settlement:** PPE sale proceeds are **investing**; bond retirement cash is **financing**; related G/L is excluded from direct-method lines (not a cash operating class)
- **Required disclosure:** reconciliation of net income to net operating cash flows (indirect-method equivalent schedule) when the direct method is used
- **Number-variant twin:** same LO path with all companies and amounts changed

| Cash income taxes paid during 20X9 | $27,000 |

**Period-end adjusting items still needed (emphasis):**  
1. Accrue salaries of **$5,500**.  
2. Reduce prepaid rent to **$18,000** (rent expense = **$6,000**).  
3. Expire prepaid insurance **$3,600**.  
4. Record depreciation **$19,000**.  
5. Record bad debt expense so ending allowance = **$2,800** (write-offs of **$2,000** already reduced the allowance during the year).  
6. Amortize bond premium **$900** and accrue interest payable **$1,400**.  
7. Record income tax expense so that, with cash taxes paid of **$27,000**, ending income taxes payable is **$5,000**.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare **period-end adjusting JEs 1–7**. Prove each balances. For each, state whether it is **omitted entirely** from the direct-method operating section, or only affects a **conversion schedule**.  
(b) Compute **cash collected from customers** and **cash paid to suppliers**.  
(c) Compute **cash paid for salaries**, **cash paid for rent**, **cash paid for insurance**, **cash paid for interest**, and **cash paid for taxes**.  
(d) Prepare the **direct-method operating activities** section.  
(e) **Classification:** If Crestmont had also reported a **$7,000** gain on equipment sale in income, would that gain appear as a direct-method operating cash inflow? Where would the related cash proceeds be reported?

**Answer key:**

**(a) Period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis)**

(1) Salaries accrual:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Salaries Expense | 5,500 | |
| Salaries Payable | | 5,500 |

**Check:** Dr 5,500 = Cr 5,500. **Balanced.**  
→ Conversion only (↑SP). Final salaries expense = \(52{,}000 + 5{,}500 = \mathbf{\$57{,}500}\); end SP = \(4{,}000 + 5{,}500 = \mathbf{\$9{,}500}\).

(2) Rent prepaid expiration:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Rent Expense | 6,000 | |
| Prepaid Rent | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr 6,000 = Cr 6,000. **Balanced.**  
→ Conversion only. End prepaid rent = \(24{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \mathbf{\$18{,}000}\).

(3) Insurance expiration:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Insurance Expense | 3,600 | |
| Prepaid Insurance | | 3,600 |

**Check:** Dr 3,600 = Cr 3,600. **Balanced.**  
→ Conversion only. End prepaid insurance = \(4{,}800 - 3{,}600 = \mathbf{\$1{,}200}\).

(4) Depreciation:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 19,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation | | 19,000 |

**Check:** Dr 19,000 = Cr 19,000. **Balanced.**  
→ **Omitted entirely** (noncash).

(5) Bad debt expense:  
Allow after write-offs, before BDE: \(1{,}200 - 2{,}000 = \mathbf{-\$800}\) (debit).  
BDE to reach end credit $2,800: \(2{,}800 - (-800) = \mathbf{\$3{,}600}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bad Debt Expense | 3,600 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 3,600 |

**Check:** Dr 3,600 = Cr 3,600. **Balanced.**  
→ **Omitted entirely** as a cash line (noncash).

(6a) Premium amortization:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 900 | |
| Interest Expense | | 900 |

**Check:** Dr 900 = Cr 900. **Balanced.**  
→ Noncash; used only in interest **conversion**.

(6b) Interest accrual:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 1,400 | |
| Interest Payable | | 1,400 |

**Check:** Dr 1,400 = Cr 1,400. **Balanced.**  
→ Conversion only. Final interest expense = \(9{,}600 - 900 + 1{,}400 = \mathbf{\$10{,}100}\); end IP = \(1{,}100 + 1{,}400 = \mathbf{\$2{,}500}\).

(7) Income tax accrual:  
Tax expense = End ITP − Beg ITP + Cash taxes paid = \(5{,}000 - 6{,}200 + 27{,}000 = \mathbf{\$25{,}800}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 25,800 | |
| Income Taxes Payable | | 25,800 |

**Check:** Dr 25,800 = Cr 25,800. **Balanced.**  
→ Conversion only (tax expense vs cash taxes paid / ITP). Direct-method line is **cash taxes paid $27,000**.

**(b) Cash collected from customers; cash paid to suppliers**

Collections: \(31{,}000 + 410{,}000 - 2{,}000 - 34{,}500 = \mathbf{\$404{,}500}\).

Purchases: \(36{,}500 - 38{,}000 + 245{,}000 = \mathbf{\$243{,}500}\).  
Cash to suppliers: \(22{,}000 + 243{,}500 - 25{,}500 = \mathbf{\$240{,}000}\).  
Shortcut: \(245{,}000 - 1{,}500\ \text{(↓Inv)} - 3{,}500\ \text{(↑AP)} = \mathbf{\$240{,}000}\).

**(c) Other cash payments**

Salaries cash: \(4{,}000 + 57{,}500 - 9{,}500 = \mathbf{\$52{,}000}\).  
Rent cash: **$0** (no new prepayments; entire rent expense from prepaid).  
Insurance cash: **$0** (same).  
Interest cash: \(10{,}100 + 900 - 1{,}400 = \mathbf{\$9{,}600}\).  
Taxes cash: **$27,000** (given). Proof: \(6{,}200 + 25{,}800 - 27{,}000 = 5{,}000\). ✓

**(d) Direct-method operating section**

| Cash flows from operating activities | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Collections from customers | $404,500 |
| Payments to suppliers | (240,000) |
| Payments to employees (salaries) | (52,000) |
| Payments for rent | 0 |
| Payments for insurance | 0 |
| Interest payments | (9,600) |
| Tax payments | (27,000) |
| **Net cash provided by operating activities** | **$75,900** |

Proof: \(404{,}500 - 240{,}000 - 52{,}000 - 9{,}600 - 27{,}000 = \mathbf{\$75{,}900}\). ✓  

Depreciation **$19,000** and bad debt **$3,600** do **not** appear.

**(e) Classification if there were a $7,000 equipment gain**  
The gain would **not** appear as a direct-method operating cash inflow. Cash proceeds would be an **investing inflow**. In the required NI reconciliation, the gain would be **subtracted** from net income; the direct-method operating total itself is unaffected by the gain (only by actual operating cash classes).

**Key insight:** Period-end AJs are the bridge from the cash ledger to accrual statements. Under the **direct method**, drop pure noncash AJs (dep, BDE, premium as lines) and restate accrued revenues/expenses with related BS accounts to get cash classes.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)

**LO:** LO 22-7  
**Concept:** Classification/presentation — which amounts appear under the **direct method** operating section  
**Question:** Which of the following is **correct** when preparing the **operating activities** section using the **direct method**?

- A) Depreciation expense is listed as a cash outflow for operating activities.  
- B) Cash paid for interest equals interest expense whenever bonds were issued at a premium.  
- C) Collections from customers, payments to suppliers, interest paid, and income taxes paid are reported as major classes of gross operating cash flows, and depreciation does not appear as a line.  
- D) The gain on sale of equipment is reported as an operating cash inflow under the direct method.

**Answer:** **C.** The direct method reports major classes of gross operating cash receipts and payments (customers, suppliers/employees, interest, taxes, etc.). Noncash items such as depreciation do not appear.  
- A is wrong: depreciation is noncash and is omitted (it is only an add-back under the **indirect** method / reconciliation).  
- B is wrong: with a **premium**, cash interest paid = interest expense **+** premium amortization ± change in interest payable (cash **exceeds** interest expense before the IP adjustment).  
- D is wrong: the gain is noncash relative to operating classes; **full proceeds** are **investing**.

### Q5 — MC (period-end AJ → interest paid)

**LO:** LO 22-7  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE for bond **discount** amortization and the correct direct-method interest paid  
**Question:** On December 31, a company records this period-end adjusting entry related to its bonds:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 1,200 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,200 |

Interest expense for the year (including this AJ) is **$15,000**. Interest payable increased by **$500** during the year. Under the **direct method**, cash paid for interest is:

- A) $15,000  
- B) $16,200  
- C) $13,300  
- D) $14,300  

**Answer:** **C.** Cash paid for interest = Interest expense − discount amortization − increase in interest payable = \(15{,}000 - 1{,}200 - 500 = \mathbf{\$13{,}300}\).  
- A ignores both noncash discount amortization and the IP increase.  
- B incorrectly **adds** the discount amortization (that treatment is for a **premium**).  
- D subtracts only the discount amortization and ignores the $500 IP increase.
