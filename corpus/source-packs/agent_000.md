# Agent 0 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-8

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for internally developed software and cloud computing arrangements  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Expense software R&D until technological feasibility; capitalize product-master costs after feasibility until release
- Amortize capitalized software-to-be-sold using the **greater of** revenue method and straight-line
- Multi-period amortization schedule and carrying-value rollforward
- Internal-use software stages: preliminary (expense), application development (capitalize), postimplementation (expense; training always expensed)
- Cloud hosting: service contract vs software license (possession criteria); capitalize implementation costs; expense subscription fees
- Period-end adjusting entries for amortization
- Derecognition / abandonment of capitalized software
- Classification and presentation (income statement line items; capitalized implementation costs with hosting prepayments)

### Q1 — CORE — Software developed for sale: initial recognition, amortization, and two-year schedule
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of software-to-be-sold costs; greater-of revenue vs straight-line amortization  
**Scenario:**  
NorthPeak Analytics Inc. developed inventory-optimization software to sell to third parties. All amounts were paid in cash.

- During Year 1, total project expenditures were **$2,400,000**. Technological feasibility (working model complete) was established when **35%** of total Year 1 costs remained to be incurred (i.e., **35%** of costs occurred **after** technological feasibility).
- The product masters were completed and the software was **available for sale on January 1, Year 2**. Estimated economic life is **4 years**. Residual value is zero.
- Year 2 product revenues: **$1,500,000**.  
- At the start of Year 2, total anticipated product revenues over the full economic life (including Year 2): **$10,000,000**.
- Year 3 product revenues: **$2,800,000**. At the start of Year 3, management’s revised total of **current Year 3 revenue plus remaining anticipated future revenues** is **$8,500,000** (so remaining anticipated after Year 3 is **$5,700,000**).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the Year 1 journal entries to account for development costs (expense vs capitalize).  
b. Compute Year 2 amortization under **both** the revenue method and the straight-line method; prepare the Year 2 amortization journal entry using the **greater** amount.  
c. Prepare a two-year amortization schedule (Year 2 and Year 3) showing: revenue-method amount, SL amount, amount recorded, and ending carrying value. Apply the greater-of test each year to **unamortized** capitalized cost: revenue method = current revenue ÷ (current + remaining anticipated future revenues) × beginning unamortized cost; SL = beginning unamortized cost ÷ remaining useful life (years).  
d. On December 31, Year 3, after amortization, NorthPeak abandons the product (no further sales; no residual recovery). Prepare the disposal/write-off entry.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1 — initial recognition**  
Capitalizable portion = $2,400,000 × 35% = **$840,000**  
Expense portion = $2,400,000 × 65% = **$1,560,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Research and Development Expense | 1,560,000 | |
| Cash | | 1,560,000 |
| *To expense costs incurred before technological feasibility* | | |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Intangible Asset | 840,000 | |
| Cash | | 840,000 |
| *To capitalize costs after technological feasibility until product masters / release readiness* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,560,000 + 840,000 = Cr 2,400,000. Balanced.

**b. Year 2 amortization (greater of)**  
Revenue method (textbook form on original capitalized cost for Year 2 start):  
\[
\frac{\$1{,}500{,}000}{\$10{,}000{,}000} \times \$840{,}000 = \$126{,}000
\]  
Straight-line:  
\[
\frac{1}{4} \times \$840{,}000 = \$210{,}000
\]  
**Greater = $210,000** (straight-line).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Amortization Expense | 210,000 | |
| Software Intangible Asset | | 210,000 |
| *Year 2 amortization — greater of revenue ($126,000) and SL ($210,000)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 210,000. Ending CV after Year 2 = 840,000 − 210,000 = **$630,000**.

**c. Two-year schedule**  

| Year | Beg. CV | Current rev | Current + remaining future rev | Revenue method | SL method | **Recorded amort.** | End. CV |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 840,000 | 1,500,000 | 10,000,000 | 126,000 | 210,000 | **210,000** | 630,000 |
| 3 | 630,000 | 2,800,000 | 2,800,000 + 5,700,000 = 8,500,000 | 2,800,000/8,500,000 × 630,000 = **207,529** | 630,000 / 3 = **210,000** | **210,000** | 420,000 |

Year 3 JE:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Amortization Expense | 210,000 | |
| Software Intangible Asset | | 210,000 |

**d. Abandonment / disposal at Dec 31, Year 3 (after Y3 amort)**  
Carrying amount written off = **$420,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Disposal of Software Intangible | 420,000 | |
| Software Intangible Asset | | 420,000 |
| *Write off remaining carrying amount upon abandonment; no proceeds* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 420,000.

**Key insight:** Costs before technological feasibility are R&D expense; costs after feasibility until release are capitalized. Annual amortization is the **greater of** the revenue ratio and straight-line. Abandonment derecognizes any remaining CV with a loss in continuing operations.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Software for sale: initial recognition and Year-1 marketing amortization
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin of software-to-be-sold initial recognition and greater-of amortization  
**Scenario:**  
Cascade Logic LLC developed a new CRM analytics package for sale. All cash.

- Year 1 total expenditures: **$3,600,000**, of which **40%** occurred **after** technological feasibility (detailed program design complete).
- Product available for sale **January 1, Year 2**; expected economic life **5 years**; residual value zero.
- Year 2 revenues: **$4,800,000**. Total anticipated life revenues: **$20,000,000**.

**Required:**  
a. Year 1 journal entries (expense and capitalize).  
b. Year 2 amortization computation (both methods) and journal entry.  
c. Carrying amount of the software intangible at December 31, Year 2.

**Answer key:**  

**a.**  
Capitalize = $3,600,000 × 40% = **$1,440,000**  
Expense = $3,600,000 × 60% = **$2,160,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Research and Development Expense | 2,160,000 | |
| Cash | | 2,160,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Intangible Asset | 1,440,000 | |
| Cash | | 1,440,000 |

**Check:** 2,160,000 + 1,440,000 = 3,600,000.

**b.**  
Revenue method:  
\[
\frac{\$4{,}800{,}000}{\$20{,}000{,}000} \times \$1{,}440{,}000 = \$345{,}600
\]  
Straight-line:  
\[
\frac{1}{5} \times \$1{,}440{,}000 = \$288{,}000
\]  
**Greater = $345,600** (revenue method).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Amortization Expense | 345,600 | |
| Software Intangible Asset | | 345,600 |

**c.** Ending CV = 1,440,000 − 345,600 = **$1,094,400**.

**Key insight:** When early-year sales are a large fraction of total anticipated revenue, the revenue method can exceed straight-line and drive amortization.

### Q3 — CORE — Internal-use software stages + cloud hosting service contract (period-end adjusting)
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Internal-use software stage accounting; cloud CCA as service contract with capitalized implementation costs  
**Scenario:**  

**Part A — Internal-use software**  
Harbor Retail Group developed software solely for internal cash-management use (not sold or marketed). Cash costs in Year 1:

| Cost item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Preliminary project stage — evaluate functionality requirements, vendor selection meetings | $28,000 |
| Application development — coding, configuration, testing, installation by internal staff and contractors | 210,000 |
| Training of end-user employees (during application development) | 12,000 |
| Postimplementation — routine data conversion support and minor bug fixes after go-live | 9,500 |

The software was substantially complete and ready for intended use on **October 1, Year 1**. Useful life **3 years**; residual zero; amortize **straight-line** (monthly for partial year). Fiscal year-end **December 31**.

**Part B — Cloud computing arrangement**  
On **January 1, Year 2**, Harbor entered a **4-year** cloud hosting arrangement for a SaaS warehouse module. Harbor **does not** have the contractual right to take possession of the software without significant penalty (and cannot run it on its own hardware). Cash paid:

- Implementation / setup costs (development stage, substantially complete and ready Jan 1, Year 2): **$80,000** on January 1, Year 2  
- Annual subscription fee: **$96,000**, paid each **December 31**

**Required:**  
a. Compute the amount initially capitalized for internal-use software; prepare the compound (or separate) Year 1 cash entries for all four cost buckets.  
b. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entry to amortize internal-use software (partial year).  
c. Classify the cloud arrangement (service contract vs software purchase) and explain the two possession criteria.  
d. Record January 1, Year 2 implementation costs and the December 31, Year 2 entries for subscription fee and amortization of implementation costs.  
e. State how capitalized implementation costs and related amortization are **presented** (balance sheet / income statement) under ASC 350-40 for a hosting service contract.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Internal-use capitalization**  
Capitalize application development only: **$210,000**.  
Expense: preliminary $28,000 + training $12,000 + postimplementation $9,500 = **$49,500**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Internal-Use Software Expense (preliminary) | 28,000 | |
| Cash | | 28,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Internal-Use Software Intangible Asset | 210,000 | |
| Cash | | 210,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Training Expense | 12,000 | |
| Cash | | 12,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Maintenance / Operating Expense (postimplementation) | 9,500 | |
| Cash | | 9,500 |

**Checks:** Each entry balances; total cash out 28k+210k+12k+9.5k = **$259,500**.

**b. Period-end adjusting — partial-year amortization**  
Ready Oct 1 → **3 months** in Year 1.  
Annual amort = 210,000 / 3 = **$70,000**  
Year 1 amort = 70,000 × 3/12 = **$17,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Software Amortization Expense | 17,500 | |
| Internal-Use Software Intangible Asset (or Accumulated Amortization) | | 17,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 17,500. Ending CV Dec 31 Y1 = 210,000 − 17,500 = **$192,500**.

**c. Cloud classification**  
Accounted for as a **service contract** (not a software license/purchase). ASC 350-40 internal-use software guidance for the hosted software itself applies only if **both**:  
1. Customer has contractual right to take possession anytime during the hosting period **without significant penalty**, and  
2. It is feasible to run the software on the customer’s own hardware **or** with an unrelated third-party host.  

Harbor fails (at least) criterion 1 → **service contract**. Subscription fees expensed; **implementation costs** in the development stage are still capitalized and amortized over the **hosting term**.

**d. Year 2 cloud JEs**

Jan 1, Year 2 — capitalize implementation:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Capitalized Implementation Costs (Cloud Hosting) | 80,000 | |
| Cash | | 80,000 |

Dec 31, Year 2 — subscription fee:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cloud Subscription / Hosting Expense | 96,000 | |
| Cash | | 96,000 |

Dec 31, Year 2 — amortize implementation (SL over 4 years):  
80,000 / 4 = **$20,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cloud Hosting Expense (amortization of implementation) | 20,000 | |
| Capitalized Implementation Costs (Cloud Hosting) | | 20,000 |

**Checks:** All balance. Ending capitalized implementation CV = 80,000 − 20,000 = **$60,000**.

**e. Presentation (service-contract hosting)**  
- **Balance sheet:** Present capitalized implementation costs in the **same line item** as a prepayment of fees for the associated hosting arrangement.  
- **Income statement:** Present amortization of implementation costs in the **same line item** as the expense for hosting/subscription fees.  
- **Cash flows:** Classify cash paid for capitalized implementation costs in the **same manner** as cash flows for the hosting fees.

**Key insight:** Internal-use software capitalizes only application-development costs (training always expensed). Cloud arrangements without possession rights are service contracts: expense the fee, capitalize and amortize setup over the term, and co-present with hosting fees.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of software / cloud costs
**LO:** LO 13-8  
**Concept:** Classification of costs by software stage and cloud possession criteria  

**Question 1:**  
Which cost is **capitalized** as an intangible asset under U.S. GAAP for software **developed to be sold** to customers?

- A) Coding and testing costs incurred **before** technological feasibility is established  
- B) Costs of producing product masters incurred **after** technological feasibility and **before** the product is available for general release  
- C) Customer training costs incurred after the software is released for sale  
- D) Marketing campaign costs in the first year the product is sold  

**Answer:** **B.** After technological feasibility and before release, costs to obtain product masters are capitalized (ASC 985-20). Pre-feasibility costs are R&D expense (A). Post-release training and marketing are operating expenses, not software capitalization (C, D).

**Question 2:**  
Pine & Oak Co. enters a three-year SaaS hosting arrangement. It **cannot** take possession of the software without significant penalty. It pays $45,000 of implementation costs at inception and $50,000 per year for access. How should Pine & Oak account for these amounts?

- A) Capitalize the full $45,000 + $150,000 of fees as a software license intangible  
- B) Expense implementation immediately; capitalize subscription fees as prepaid over three years only if paid in advance — treat as software purchase  
- C) Treat as a **service contract**: capitalize implementation $45,000 and amortize over three years; expense each $50,000 subscription fee as incurred  
- D) Expense all costs as research and development  

**Answer:** **C.** Failing the possession criteria makes the arrangement a service contract. Development-stage implementation costs are capitalized and amortized over the hosting term; subscription fees are period expense (not R&D).
