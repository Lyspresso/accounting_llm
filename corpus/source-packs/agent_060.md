# Agent 60 — CORE DEMO — LO 10-4

**Chapter:** 10  
**LO title:** Demonstrate the gross profit method to estimate inventory  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Gross profit method two-step process: (1) express gross profit as a **percentage of sales**; (2) estimate COGS and ending inventory
- Convert **markup on cost** to markup on sales: GP%_sales = markup_on_cost ÷ (1 + markup_on_cost)
- Estimation schedule: Beginning inventory + Net purchases = GAS − Estimated COGS = Estimated ending inventory
- Casualty / disaster application: estimate inventory on hand at loss date, subtract **salvage / undamaged** residual → inventory loss
- **Initial recognition JE** for casualty: remove destroyed inventory, reclassify salvage, recognize loss
- Insurance **settlement JE** when recovery is received (and optional receivable when recovery is probable)
- **Period-end adjusting JE** for interim reporting under the periodic system using estimated EI
- Theft / shrinkage reasonableness test: estimated EI vs physical count
- Uses and limitations; multi-class application when markups differ (classification / method choice)

### Q1 — CORE — Meridian Hardware: fire loss, markup conversion, GP schedule, casualty & insurance JEs
**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Convert markup on cost; gross profit estimation schedule; initial recognition of inventory casualty loss and salvage; insurance settlement JE; presentation of remaining inventory  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Hardware Co.** uses a **periodic** inventory system. On **September 14, Year 1**, a fire destroyed inventory in the main warehouse. Undamaged merchandise that can still be sold was moved to a temporary location and counted at **cost of $52,000**. Accounting records show:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Inventory, September 1 | $540,000 |
| Net purchases, September 1–14 | 295,000 |
| Net sales, September 1–14 | 720,000 |

Past experience shows a consistent **markup of 25% of cost**. Meridian will pursue insurance; recovery of the book inventory loss is considered **probable and reasonably estimable**. On **October 5**, the insurer pays **$195,000** cash as final settlement of the claim (the company had recognized an insurance receivable equal to the expected recovery of **$195,000** on September 14).

**Required:**  
(a) Convert the markup on cost to **gross profit as a percentage of sales**.  
(b) Prepare the **gross profit method schedule** estimating inventory on hand at the fire date (before salvage).  
(c) Compute the **inventory loss** from the fire.  
(d) Prepare the **September 14 journal entry** to reclassify salvage inventory, write off destroyed inventory, and recognize the casualty loss (**before** insurance).  
(e) Prepare the **September 14 journal entry** to recognize the **insurance receivable** for the expected recovery, reducing the net casualty loss.  
(f) Prepare the **October 5 journal entry** for the cash insurance settlement.  
(g) Show the **balance sheet presentation** of inventory immediately after the September 14 entries (before the insurance cash is received).  
(h) State **one use** and **one limitation** of the gross profit method.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Markup conversion**  
\[
\text{GP\% of sales} = \frac{0.25}{1 + 0.25} = \frac{0.25}{1.25} = \mathbf{0.20\ (20\%)}
\]  
Cost percentage of sales = \(1 - 0.20 = \mathbf{80\%}\).

**(b) Gross profit method schedule — inventory at fire date**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory, Sept 1 | $540,000 |
| Add: Net purchases, Sept 1–14 | 295,000 |
| **Cost of goods available for sale** | **835,000** |
| Subtract: Cost of goods sold, estimated \($720{,}000 \times 0.80\) | (576,000) |
| **Ending inventory, estimated (at fire date)** | **$259,000** |

**(c) Inventory loss**  
\[
\text{Loss} = \$259{,}000 - \$52{,}000\ \text{salvage} = \mathbf{\$207{,}000}
\]

**(d) September 14 — initial recognition of casualty (write-off; no insurance yet)**  
```
Dr Inventory—Salvage ........................ 52,000
Dr Loss from Fire .......................... 207,000
   Cr Inventory .................................... 259,000
```
**Check:** Dr \(52{,}000 + 207{,}000 = 259{,}000\) = Cr \(259{,}000\). Balanced.

**(e) September 14 — recognize probable insurance recovery**  
```
Dr Insurance Receivable .................... 195,000
   Cr Loss from Fire ............................... 195,000
```
**Check:** Dr = Cr = 195,000. Balanced.  
**Net casualty loss** after expected recovery = \(207{,}000 - 195{,}000 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\).

**(f) October 5 — insurance settlement (settlement JE)**  
```
Dr Cash .................................... 195,000
   Cr Insurance Receivable ......................... 195,000
```
**Check:** Dr = Cr = 195,000. Balanced. (Settlement equals the receivable; no additional gain/loss.)

**(g) Balance sheet — inventory presentation after Sept 14 write-off**  

| | |
|---|---:|
| Inventory—Salvage (undamaged goods at cost) | $52,000 |
| **Total inventory reported** | **$52,000** |

(Insurance receivable of $195,000 is a **receivable**, not inventory.)

**(h) Use and limitation**  
- **Use (examples):** estimate inventory destroyed by fire/flood/theft for accounting and insurance claims; estimate interim ending inventory when a count is impractical; test reasonableness of a physical count or perpetual records; budgeting.  
- **Limitation (examples):** historical GP rate may not hold for the current period; different markups by product line or **sales-mix shifts** change the average GP rate and impair reliability—apply the method **separately by inventory class** when markups differ.

**Key insight:** The gross profit method first estimates **total inventory at the casualty date**; salvage is a residual that **reduces** the loss. Converting markup **on cost** to a sales percentage is required before estimating COGS. The casualty **write-off JE must balance** (salvage + loss = estimated inventory removed). Insurance recovery is recognized separately when probable, then settled when cash is received.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Cascade Tools twin (same structure, all numbers changed)
**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Markup-on-cost conversion; GP estimation schedule; casualty write-off and insurance settlement (number-variant twin)  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Tools Inc.** uses a periodic inventory system. On **March 22**, a flood destroyed warehouse inventory. Undamaged goods salvaged and still salable are valued at **cost of $30,000**. Records:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Inventory, March 1 | $480,000 |
| Net purchases, March 1–22 | 320,000 |
| Net sales, March 1–22 | 700,000 |

Historical **markup is 25% of cost**. Expected insurance recovery of the book loss is **probable** at **$200,000**. On **April 18**, the insurer pays **$200,000** cash in full settlement (equal to the receivable previously recognized).

**Required:**  
(a) Convert markup on cost to GP% of sales.  
(b) Prepare the GP method schedule estimating inventory at the flood date.  
(c) Compute inventory loss after salvage.  
(d) Journal entry March 22 to reclassify salvage, write off destroyed inventory, and record the loss (before insurance).  
(e) Journal entry March 22 to record the insurance receivable and reduce the loss.  
(f) Journal entry April 18 for cash settlement.  
(g) Net casualty loss after expected insurance recovery.

**Answer key:**

**(a)** \(\text{GP\% of sales} = 0.25 / 1.25 = \mathbf{20\%}\). Cost % of sales = **80%**.

**(b) Schedule**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory, March 1 | $480,000 |
| Add: Net purchases, March 1–22 | 320,000 |
| **Goods available for sale** | **800,000** |
| Subtract: Est. COGS \($700{,}000 \times 0.80\) | (560,000) |
| **Ending inventory, estimated** | **$240,000** |

**(c)** Loss = \(240{,}000 - 30{,}000 = \mathbf{\$210{,}000}\).

**(d) March 22 — casualty write-off**  
```
Dr Inventory—Salvage ........................ 30,000
Dr Loss from Flood ......................... 210,000
   Cr Inventory .................................... 240,000
```
**Check:** Dr \(30{,}000 + 210{,}000 = 240{,}000\) = Cr. Balanced.

**(e) March 22 — insurance receivable**  
```
Dr Insurance Receivable .................... 200,000
   Cr Loss from Flood .............................. 200,000
```
**Check:** Dr = Cr = 200,000. Balanced.

**(f) April 18 — settlement**  
```
Dr Cash .................................... 200,000
   Cr Insurance Receivable ......................... 200,000
```
**Check:** Dr = Cr = 200,000. Balanced.

**(g)** Net casualty loss = \(210{,}000 - 200{,}000 = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\).

**Key insight:** Same structure as Q1 with every amount changed: convert markup → estimate EI → subtract salvage → balance the write-off JE → layer insurance receivable and cash settlement.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Brightwater Sporting Goods: interim period-end GP estimate + year-end theft test
**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Gross profit method for interim ending inventory; period-end adjusting JE under periodic inventory; theft/shrinkage estimate vs physical count; multi-class reminder when markups differ  
**Scenario:**  
**Brightwater Sporting Goods** uses a **periodic** inventory system and takes a physical count only at year-end (**December 31**). For **interim (June 30)** reporting, management estimates ending inventory with the gross profit method. Historical gross profit has been a steady **30% of net sales**.

**First-half and annual data:**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Inventory, January 1 (from physical count) | $120,000 |
| Net purchases, January 1 – June 30 | 340,000 |
| Net sales, January 1 – June 30 | 450,000 |
| Net purchases, full year (Jan 1 – Dec 31) | 680,000 |
| Net sales, full year | 900,000 |
| Physical inventory count, December 31 | 148,000 |

The Inventory control account still shows only the **January 1 balance** of $120,000; purchases for the year have been recorded in **Purchases**. (Ignore income taxes.)

**Additional fact for part (e):** Brightwater also stocks a small **accessories** line with a **45% of sales** markup, separate from the main **equipment** line at **30% of sales**. Management is debating whether one company-wide GP rate is acceptable for interim estimates.

**Required:**  
(a) Using the gross profit method, **estimate inventory at June 30**.  
(b) Prepare the **June 30 period-end adjusting (closing-style) entry** to recognize estimated cost of goods sold and to set ending inventory for interim reporting (debit ending Inventory and COGS; credit beginning Inventory and Purchases for the first half).  
(c) Using full-year data and the same 30% of sales GP rate, estimate what December 31 inventory **should be**, and compute the estimated **cost of missing inventory** (theft/shrinkage) given the physical count.  
(d) Prepare the **December 31 entry** to write off the missing inventory after the physical count has been booked at actual cost (assume books have already been adjusted so Inventory is at the physical-count amount of $148,000 via normal periodic closing to estimated or actual EI—**simplifying approach:** books currently reflect Inventory at the **GP-estimated** year-end amount before considering the shortage; write Inventory down from estimated EI to physical count and recognize a shortage loss).  
(e) In two to three sentences, explain whether Brightwater should apply the gross profit method **separately** to equipment vs accessories, and what limitation this illustrates.

**Answer key:**

**(a) June 30 estimated inventory**  
Est. COGS (H1) = \(450{,}000 \times (1 - 0.30) = 450{,}000 \times 0.70 = \mathbf{\$315{,}000}\).  
GAS (H1) = \(120{,}000 + 340{,}000 = \mathbf{\$460{,}000}\).  
Est. EI, June 30 = \(460{,}000 - 315{,}000 = \mathbf{\$145{,}000}\).

**(b) June 30 period-end adjusting entry (interim)**  
```
Dr Inventory (ending, estimated) ........... 145,000
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ...................... 315,000
   Cr Inventory (beginning) ........................ 120,000
   Cr Purchases .................................... 340,000
```
**Check:** Dr \(145{,}000 + 315{,}000 = 460{,}000\); Cr \(120{,}000 + 340{,}000 = 460{,}000\). Balanced.

**(c) Full-year estimate vs physical — missing inventory**  
Est. COGS (year) = \(900{,}000 \times 0.70 = \mathbf{\$630{,}000}\).  
GAS (year) = \(120{,}000 + 680{,}000 = \mathbf{\$800{,}000}\).  
Est. EI, Dec 31 = \(800{,}000 - 630{,}000 = \mathbf{\$170{,}000}\).  
Physical count = **$148,000**.  
Estimated missing inventory (theft/shrinkage) = \(170{,}000 - 148{,}000 = \mathbf{\$22{,}000}\).

**(d) December 31 — write down from GP-estimated EI to physical count**  
```
Dr Inventory Shortage Loss (or COGS) ....... 22,000
   Cr Inventory .................................... 22,000
```
**Check:** Dr = Cr = 22,000. Balanced.  
Ending Inventory after shortage = **$148,000** (physical).

*(Alternative full year-end compound entry from BI + Purchases if books were not yet closed, incorporating physical EI and shortage:*  
```
Dr Inventory (physical ending) ............. 148,000
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ...................... 630,000
Dr Inventory Shortage Loss ................. 22,000
   Cr Inventory (beginning) ........................ 120,000
   Cr Purchases .................................... 680,000
```  
Check: Dr \(148{,}000 + 630{,}000 + 22{,}000 = 800{,}000\); Cr \(120{,}000 + 680{,}000 = 800{,}000\). Balanced.)*

**(e) Multi-class / limitation**  
If equipment and accessories have **materially different markups** (30% vs 45% of sales), Brightwater should estimate each class **separately** and **sum** the class estimates. A single blended GP rate is unreliable when product mix shifts toward the higher- or lower-margin class—this is a core textbook **limitation** of the gross profit method and a classification/method-application rule, not a mere computational preference.

**Key insight:** Interim reporting uses the same two-step GP engine as casualty estimates, but the deliverable is a **period-end adjusting JE** that records estimated COGS and EI. At year-end, comparing GP-estimated EI to a physical count is a **reasonableness / theft** application of the same method.

### Q4 — MC (method / conversion classification)
**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Convert markup on cost and select estimated ending inventory under the gross profit method  
**Question:**  
Oakridge Merchandising reports beginning inventory of **$45,000**, net purchases of **$280,000**, and sales of **$180,000**. Markup on cost has remained constant at **34%**. What is the estimated cost of ending inventory under the gross profit method (rounded to the nearest dollar)?

- A) $108,209  
- B) $263,800  
- C) $190,671  
- D) $145,000  

**Answer:** C.  
GP% of sales = \(0.34 / 1.34 \approx 0.253731\).  
Est. COGS = \(180{,}000 \times (1 - 0.34/1.34) = 180{,}000 \times (1/1.34) = 180{,}000 / 1.34 \approx \mathbf{\$134{,}328}\).  
GAS = \(45{,}000 + 280{,}000 = \$325{,}000\).  
Est. EI = \(325{,}000 - 134{,}328 = \mathbf{\$190{,}672}\) (nearest dollar; option C rounds to **$190,671**).  
Distractors: A mishandles the conversion or multiplies sales by GP%; B is GAS − sales × GP-like error without proper cost %; D is GAS − sales (treats sales as COGS).

### Q5 — MC (when to apply multi-class)
**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Identify when the gross profit method must be applied separately by inventory class  
**Question:**  
Which situation best requires applying the gross profit method **separately to each inventory class** (then summing class estimates) rather than using one company-wide average GP rate?

- A) The company uses a perpetual system and can count inventory monthly.  
- B) Product lines have **widely different markup rates**, and the sales mix can shift across lines.  
- C) Markup is given as a percentage of **cost** rather than sales (single product line).  
- D) Ending inventory is needed only for an internal budget draft with no financial-statement use.  

**Answer:** B.  
Textbook limitation: different markup rates and **sales-mix shifts** change the average GP rate and impair reliability of a single blended rate. Separate class estimates are required; then sum for total inventory. A is about when estimation may be unnecessary; C only requires converting cost markup to sales markup for that one line; D does not create a multi-class requirement.
