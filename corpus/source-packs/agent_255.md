# Agent 255 — CORE DEMO — LO 21-1

**Chapter:** 21  
**LO title:** Demonstrate accounting for change in accounting estimate including a change in accounting estimate effected by change in accounting principle  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize PPE/intangible at cost; **no** catch-up/restatement entry when estimates later revise
- **Change in accounting estimate** (useful life, residual/salvage): **prospective** only — recompute depreciation/amortization from **carrying amount at change date** over **remaining life** to revised residual; **no** prior-period restatement
- **Change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle** (e.g., depreciation method change): treated as a **change in estimate** — **prospective** from carrying amount at method-change date (ASC 250-10-45-18)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-year depreciation/amortization rollforward (beginning CA, expense, accumulated, ending CA) **before and after** the estimate (or method) change
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** record revised depreciation/amortization in the year of change and later years
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** remove cost and accumulated depreciation/amortization; gain/loss = proceeds − carrying amount after revised depreciation
- **Disclosure:** effect on income from continuing operations, net income, and EPS when the change affects several future periods and is material (ASC 250-10-50-4); ordinary recurring estimates need disclosure only if material

### Q1 — CORE — Useful life and residual revision: schedule emphasis through disposal
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Prospective change in useful life and residual; multi-year subsequent measurement schedule before/after change; period-end adjusting JEs; disposal  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Precision Inc.** (calendar year-end) purchased specialized packaging equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$450,000 cash**. Original estimates: **10-year** useful life, residual value **$30,000**, **straight-line** depreciation (full years; no partial-year convention issues).

On **January 1, Year 4**, after three full years of use, management revises estimates based on new throughput data: total useful life from acquisition is now **8 years**, and residual value is **$12,000**. Depreciation method is unchanged (still SL).

On **December 31, Year 6**, after recording Year 6 depreciation under the revised estimates, Harborline sells the equipment for **$140,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE**.  
b. Compute original annual SL depreciation and prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule for Years 1–3** (beginning carrying amount, depreciation expense, end-of-year accumulated depreciation, ending carrying amount).  
c. Compute the **carrying amount on January 1, Year 4** and the **revised annual depreciation**. State whether any retrospective or cumulative catch-up entry is recorded.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 4 period-end adjusting JE**.  
e. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule for Years 4–6** under the revised estimates.  
f. Prepare the **December 31, Year 6 disposal JE** (after Year 6 depreciation).  
g. If material, what **disclosure** is required for Year 4 regarding the estimate change? Quantify the Year 4 pretax income effect relative to continuing the old estimates.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 450,000 | |
| Cash | | 450,000 |
| *Capitalize equipment at cash acquisition cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 450,000 = Cr 450,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Original SL and Years 1–3 subsequent measurement schedule**

Original depreciable base = $450,000 − $30,000 = **$420,000**  
Original annual SL depreciation = $420,000 ÷ 10 = **$42,000**

| Year | Beg. carrying amount | Depreciation expense | End. accum. dep. | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $450,000 | $42,000 | $42,000 | $408,000 |
| 2 | 408,000 | 42,000 | 84,000 | 366,000 |
| 3 | 366,000 | 42,000 | 126,000 | 324,000 |

*Illustrative period-end JE each of Years 1–3:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 42,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 42,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 42,000 each year. **Balanced.**

**c. Carrying amount at change date and revised annual depreciation**

Carrying amount, January 1, Year 4 = $450,000 − $126,000 = **$324,000**  
Revised total useful life = 8 years; elapsed = 3 years → **remaining life = 5 years**  
Revised residual = **$12,000**  
Revised annual depreciation = ($324,000 − $12,000) ÷ 5 = **$62,400**

**Prospective application only** — **no** journal entry to restate prior Accumulated Depreciation or Retained Earnings; **no** pro forma restatement of prior financial statements.

**d. December 31, Year 4 — Period-end adjusting JE (year of change)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 62,400 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 62,400 |
| *Prospective depreciation using revised life and residual* | | |

**Check:** Dr 62,400 = Cr 62,400. **Balanced.**

**e. Subsequent measurement schedule — Years 4–6 (revised estimates)**

| Year | Beg. carrying amount | Depreciation expense | End. accum. dep. | End. carrying amount |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | $324,000 | $62,400 | $188,400 | $261,600 |
| 5 | 261,600 | 62,400 | 250,800 | 199,200 |
| 6 | 199,200 | 62,400 | 313,200 | 136,800 |

Accumulated depreciation check, Dec 31 Year 6:  
$126,000 (pre-change) + 3 × $62,400 = $126,000 + $172,800 = **$313,200**  
Carrying amount: $450,000 − $313,200 = **$136,800** ✓

Year 5 and Year 6 period-end JEs are identical in form to Year 4 ($62,400 each year).

**f. December 31, Year 6 — Disposal (settlement) JE**

Cash proceeds $140,000 − carrying amount $136,800 = **gain $3,200**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 140,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 313,200 | |
| Equipment | | 450,000 |
| Gain on Disposal of Equipment | | 3,200 |
| *Remove cost and accum. dep.; record cash and gain* | | |

**Check:** Dr 140,000 + 313,200 = **453,200**; Cr 450,000 + 3,200 = **453,200**. **Balanced.**

**g. Disclosure (Year 4)**  
- **Type:** Change in **accounting estimate** (useful life and residual).  
- **Treatment:** **Prospective** only (ASC 250-10-45-17).  
- **Disclosure:** If material and the change affects several future periods, disclose the **effect on income from continuing operations, net income, and related per-share amounts** for the current period (ASC 250-10-50-4).  
- Year 4 depreciation under new estimates **$62,400** vs old rate **$42,000** → pretax income lower by **$20,400** relative to continuing the pre-change estimates.

**Key insight:** The control document is the **subsequent measurement schedule**. At the change date, take **book value**, subtract the **new residual**, and allocate over **remaining** life only. Prior years stay as reported; disposal uses the **post-revision** carrying amount after that year’s depreciation.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Useful life and residual revision with schedule and disposal
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: prospective life/residual revision; subsequent measurement schedules; period-end JE; disposal with loss  
**Scenario:**  
**Copperleaf Assembly LLC** (calendar year-end) purchased production machinery on **January 1, Year 1** for **$480,000 cash**. Original estimates: **10-year** useful life, residual value **$30,000**, **straight-line** depreciation.

On **January 1, Year 5**, after four full years, management revises estimates: total useful life from acquisition is now **8 years**, residual value **$20,000**. Method remains SL.

On **December 31, Year 7**, after recording Year 7 depreciation under the revised estimates, Copperleaf sells the machinery for **$85,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
a. January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE.  
b. Original annual SL amount and **Years 1–4 subsequent measurement schedule**.  
c. Carrying amount January 1, Year 5 and revised annual depreciation.  
d. December 31, Year 5 period-end adjusting JE.  
e. **Subsequent measurement schedule for Years 5–7**.  
f. December 31, Year 7 disposal JE.  
g. Classify the change (estimate vs principle vs error) and state prospective vs retrospective.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 480,000 | |
| Cash | | 480,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 480,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Original SL and Years 1–4 schedule**

Depreciable base = $480,000 − $30,000 = **$450,000**  
Annual SL = $450,000 ÷ 10 = **$45,000**

| Year | Beg. CA | Dep. expense | End. accum. dep. | End. CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $480,000 | $45,000 | $45,000 | $435,000 |
| 2 | 435,000 | 45,000 | 90,000 | 390,000 |
| 3 | 390,000 | 45,000 | 135,000 | 345,000 |
| 4 | 345,000 | 45,000 | 180,000 | 300,000 |

**c. Change date amounts**

CA January 1, Year 5 = $480,000 − $180,000 = **$300,000**  
Remaining life = 8 − 4 = **4 years**  
Revised residual = **$20,000**  
Revised annual depreciation = ($300,000 − $20,000) ÷ 4 = **$70,000**

No restatement / no cumulative catch-up entry.

**d. December 31, Year 5 period-end JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 70,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 70,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 70,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Subsequent measurement schedule — Years 5–7**

| Year | Beg. CA | Dep. expense | End. accum. dep. | End. CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | $300,000 | $70,000 | $250,000 | $230,000 |
| 6 | 230,000 | 70,000 | 320,000 | 160,000 |
| 7 | 160,000 | 70,000 | 390,000 | 90,000 |

Check: pre-change accum. $180,000 + 3 × $70,000 = $180,000 + $210,000 = **$390,000**; CA = $480,000 − $390,000 = **$90,000** ✓

**f. December 31, Year 7 — Disposal JE**

Proceeds $85,000 − CA $90,000 = **loss $5,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 85,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | 390,000 | |
| Loss on Disposal of Equipment | 5,000 | |
| Equipment | | 480,000 |

**Check:** Dr 85,000 + 390,000 + 5,000 = **480,000**; Cr **480,000**. **Balanced.**

**g. Classification**  
- **Change in accounting estimate** (useful life and residual).  
- **Prospective** application only — current and future periods; prior statements not restated.

**Key insight:** Same structure as Q1 with all numbers changed. The schedule after the revision is what drives period-end expense and the eventual gain/loss on disposal.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Depreciation method change as estimate effected by principle
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Change from SL to DDB treated as change in estimate effected by change in principle; prospective DDB subsequent measurement schedule; period-end JEs; income comparison disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Mariner Forge Co.** (calendar year-end) purchased equipment on **January 1, Year 1** for **$400,000 cash**. Estimated useful life **10 years**, residual value **$0**, **straight-line** depreciation.

On **January 1, Year 3**, after two full years under SL, management concludes that the pattern of economic benefits is more front-loaded and changes the depreciation method from **straight-line to double-declining-balance (DDB)**. Useful life and residual remain 10 years and $0 from acquisition (8 years remaining). The change is inseparable from the revised estimate of the consumption pattern.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE**.  
b. Compute annual SL depreciation and the **Years 1–2 subsequent measurement schedule**.  
c. Compute carrying amount on **January 1, Year 3** and the **DDB rate** based on remaining useful life. Compute **Year 3 DDB depreciation**.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3 period-end adjusting JE**.  
e. Prepare a **DDB subsequent measurement schedule for Years 3–5** (beginning CA, rate, depreciation expense, accum. dep., ending CA).  
f. How much higher is Year 3 depreciation under DDB than under continuing SL? Is that difference reported as a cumulative catch-up or restatement? What disclosure applies if material?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 400,000 | |
| Cash | | 400,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 400,000. **Balanced.**

**b. SL history — Years 1–2**

Annual SL = ($400,000 − $0) ÷ 10 = **$40,000**

| Year | Beg. CA | Dep. expense | End. accum. dep. | End. CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $400,000 | $40,000 | $40,000 | $360,000 |
| 2 | 360,000 | 40,000 | 80,000 | 320,000 |

**c. Prospective switch to DDB at January 1, Year 3**

Carrying amount January 1, Year 3 = $400,000 − $80,000 = **$320,000**  
Remaining useful life = **8 years**  
Straight-line rate on remaining life = 1/8 = **0.125**  
DDB rate = 2 × 0.125 = **0.25**  
Year 3 DDB depreciation = $320,000 × 0.25 = **$80,000**

**Classification:** Change in accounting **estimate effected by a change in accounting principle** (ASC 250-10-45-18) — accounted for **prospectively** as a change in estimate. **No** retrospective restatement; **no** cumulative effect through retained earnings.

**d. December 31, Year 3 period-end JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Depreciation Expense | 80,000 | |
| Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment | | 80,000 |
| *Year 3 DDB depreciation on carrying amount at method-change date* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 80,000. **Balanced.**

**e. DDB subsequent measurement schedule — Years 3–5**

| Year | Beg. CA | Rate | Dep. expense | End. accum. dep. | End. CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | $320,000 | 0.25 | **$80,000** | $160,000 | $240,000 |
| 4 | 240,000 | 0.25 | **60,000** | 220,000 | 180,000 |
| 5 | 180,000 | 0.25 | **45,000** | 265,000 | 135,000 |

Accum. dep. check Year 5: pre-change $80,000 + $80,000 + $60,000 + $45,000 = **$265,000**; CA = $400,000 − $265,000 = **$135,000** ✓

Year 4 JE: Dr Depreciation Expense 60,000 / Cr Accumulated Depreciation 60,000.  
Year 5 JE: Dr Depreciation Expense 45,000 / Cr Accumulated Depreciation 45,000.

**f. Income comparison and disclosure**  
Year 3 DDB $80,000 − continuing SL $40,000 = **$40,000 higher** depreciation (lower pretax income by $40,000).  
This difference is **not** reported as a cumulative catch-up or prior-period restatement; it is simply the **current-year** effect of the new estimate/method applied prospectively.  
If material and the change affects several future periods, disclose the effect on **income from continuing operations, net income, and per-share amounts** for the current period (ASC 250-10-50-4).

**Key insight:** A switch in depreciation (amortization/depletion) method is **not** a retrospective principle change. Apply the **new method to the existing carrying amount** over remaining life. The multi-year **DDB schedule** is the subsequent measurement engine after the change date.

### Q4 — CORE — Patent amortization: prospective useful-life revision and write-off
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Intangible initial recognition; subsequent amortization schedule; prospective life revision; period-end JE; abandonment/disposal write-off  
**Scenario:**  
**Brightspan IP Holdings** purchased a patent on **January 1, Year 1** for **$120,000 cash**. The patent was amortized on a straight-line basis over an estimated **10-year** useful life with **zero** residual value.

Until December 31, Year 5, amortization used the original 10-year life. At the **start of Year 6**, management determined that the **total** probable useful life from acquisition is **8 years** (remaining life 3 years).

On **December 31, Year 7**, after recording Year 7 amortization, Brightspan abandons the patent (no residual recovery, no sale proceeds).

**Required:**  
a. January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE.  
b. Original annual amortization and **Years 1–5 subsequent measurement schedule** (or cumulative summary with ending CA at Dec 31, Year 5).  
c. Revised annual amortization for Year 6 and the **Years 6–7 schedule**.  
d. December 31, Year 6 period-end adjusting JE.  
e. December 31, Year 7 abandonment (disposal) JE after Year 7 amortization.  
f. Classify the change and state treatment (prospective vs retrospective).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — January 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 120,000 | |
| Cash | | 120,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 120,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Original amortization and CA at change date**

Original annual amortization = $120,000 ÷ 10 = **$12,000**  
Accumulated amortization after 5 years = 5 × $12,000 = **$60,000**  
Carrying amount January 1, Year 6 = $120,000 − $60,000 = **$60,000**

| Year | Beg. CA | Amort. expense | End. accum. amort. | End. CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $120,000 | $12,000 | $12,000 | $108,000 |
| 2 | 108,000 | 12,000 | 24,000 | 96,000 |
| 3 | 96,000 | 12,000 | 36,000 | 84,000 |
| 4 | 84,000 | 12,000 | 48,000 | 72,000 |
| 5 | 72,000 | 12,000 | 60,000 | 60,000 |

**c. Revised amortization and Years 6–7 schedule**

Remaining life = 8 − 5 = **3 years**  
Revised annual amortization = $60,000 ÷ 3 = **$20,000**

| Year | Beg. CA | Amort. expense | End. accum. amort. | End. CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | $60,000 | $20,000 | $80,000 | $40,000 |
| 7 | 40,000 | 20,000 | 100,000 | 20,000 |

**d. December 31, Year 6 period-end JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 20,000 | |
| Accumulated Amortization—Patent | | 20,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 20,000. **Balanced.**

(Year 7 amortization JE is the same amount: Dr 20,000 / Cr Accum. Amort. 20,000.)

**e. December 31, Year 7 — Abandonment / disposal after amortization**

Remaining carrying amount = **$20,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Abandonment of Patent | 20,000 | |
| Accumulated Amortization—Patent | 100,000 | |
| Patent | | 120,000 |
| *Write off remaining carrying amount; no proceeds* | | |

**Check:** Dr 20,000 + 100,000 = **120,000**; Cr **120,000**. **Balanced.**

**f. Classification**  
- **Change in accounting estimate** (useful life of amortizable intangible).  
- **Prospective** only — no restatement of Years 1–5 amortization.

**Key insight:** Patent life revisions work exactly like PPE: **remaining book value ÷ remaining life**. Abandonment after the revised amortization schedule writes off whatever CA remains.

### Q5 — MC (classification / method of application)
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Classification — pure change in estimate vs estimate effected by principle vs retrospective principle change; prospective application  
**Question:**  
On January 1, Year 4, a company changes the depreciation method for a machine from sum-of-the-years’-digits to straight-line because management’s revised assessment of the pattern of future benefits is more even over the remaining life. Useful life and residual are unchanged. How should the company account for this change?

- A) As a voluntary change in accounting principle applied **retrospectively**, with a cumulative effect adjustment to beginning retained earnings  
- B) As a **change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle**, applied **prospectively** to the carrying amount over remaining life  
- C) As a correction of an error, with restatement of all prior periods presented  
- D) As a change in estimate only if the difference in Year 4 expense is material; otherwise ignore the method change  

**Answer:** **B.** A change in depreciation (amortization/depletion) method is treated as a **change in estimate effected by a change in principle** and is applied **prospectively** (ASC 250-10-45-18). Prior financial statements are not restated, and there is no retrospective RE catch-up of the type used for most voluntary principle changes (e.g., inventory methods). A is the typical voluntary principle-change treatment but is **not** used for depreciation-method changes. C confuses method revision with error correction (unrealistic estimates or math mistakes). D invents a materiality gate that does not change the CORE prospective classification rule.

### Q6 — MC (prospective pure estimate — no catch-up)
**LO:** LO 21-1  
**Concept:** Prospective application of pure change in estimate — no restatement or cumulative catch-up  
**Question:**  
Equipment cost $300,000; original life 10 years; residual $0; SL depreciation for three years. On January 1, Year 4, total useful life is revised to 8 years (residual still $0). Which statement is correct?

- A) Debit Retained Earnings and credit Accumulated Depreciation for the difference between three years of old vs new rates  
- B) Restate Years 1–3 comparative depreciation as if the 8-year life had always been used  
- C) Compute Year 4 depreciation as (carrying amount at Jan 1, Year 4 − residual) ÷ remaining life of 5 years; no prior-period adjustment  
- D) Apply the new life only to newly acquired similar assets; continue the original rate for this asset  

**Answer:** **C.** Pure changes in useful life/residual are **changes in estimate** applied **prospectively** from the carrying amount at the start of the change period over remaining life (ASC 250-10-45-17). No RE catch-up (A), no restatement of prior years (B), and no “grandfathering” of the old rate for existing assets (D).
