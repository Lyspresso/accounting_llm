# Agent 219 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-3

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for impairment and derecognition of finite life intangible assets  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** record finite-life intangible (patent) at cash-equivalent cost
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-year amortization roll-forward of carrying amount before and after impairment; new cost basis amortized over remaining useful life
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** year-end amortization expense; two-step impairment (recoverability then measurement) when indicator present
- **Disposal / derecognition JE:** amortize to disposal date, remove carrying amount, recognize gain or loss
- **Classification:** recoverability uses undiscounted cash flows; impairment loss uses fair value; subsequent reversal prohibited
- **Number-variant twin:** same impairment + revised amortization path with all amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Purchase, multi-year amortization schedule, year-end impairment, revised schedule
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for a finite-life patent; recoverability test and impairment measurement; post-impairment amortization over remaining life  
**Scenario:**  
**Vespera Analytics Co.** purchased a **patent** for cash on **January 1, Year 1** for **$80,000**. The patent has an estimated **useful life of 8 years**, residual value **$0**, and is amortized **straight-line**. Vespera credits the **Patent** account directly for amortization and impairment (no Accumulated Amortization contra). The fiscal year ends **December 31**.

At **December 31, Year 3**, after recording Year 3 amortization, a competitor’s product creates an **indicator** that the patent’s carrying amount may not be recoverable. Management estimates:

| Estimate | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Undiscounted future net cash inflows from use of the patent | $44,000 |
| Fair value of the patent (PV of future net cash flows) | $35,000 |

Remaining useful life after any impairment write-down: **5 years** (through end of Year 8). Residual remains **$0**. Subsequent reversal of impairment is prohibited under U.S. GAAP.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1, Year 1** initial recognition journal entry.  
b. Prepare the **subsequent measurement amortization schedule** for Years 1–3 **before** impairment testing (show annual amortization and end-of-year carrying amount).  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 3** period-end amortization adjusting entry.  
d. Perform the **recoverability test** and, if required, the **impairment test**. Compute any impairment loss and record the impairment journal entry on December 31, Year 3.  
e. Prepare the **revised subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 4–8 under the new cost basis after impairment.  
f. State the patent’s **carrying amount on December 31, Year 4**.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Initial recognition — Jan 1, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 80,000 | |
| Cash | | 80,000 |
| *Acquire finite-life patent at cash cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 80,000.

**(b) Subsequent measurement schedule — Years 1–3 (pre-impairment)**  
Annual amortization = \(80{,}000 / 8 = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\)

| Year | Beg. CA | Amortization | End CA (pre-impairment) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $80,000 | $10,000 | $70,000 |
| 2 | 70,000 | 10,000 | 60,000 |
| 3 | 60,000 | 10,000 | **50,000** |

Accumulated amortization through Year 3 = \(10{,}000 \times 3 = \mathbf{\$30{,}000}\).  
Carrying amount Dec 31, Year 3 before impairment = \(80{,}000 - 30{,}000 = \mathbf{\$50{,}000}\).

**(c) Period-end amortization — Dec 31, Year 3**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 10,000 | |
| Patent | | 10,000 |
| *SL amort \(80{,}000/8\)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,000.

**(d) Impairment analysis and JE — Dec 31, Year 3**

*Step 1 — Indicator:* Competitor product → perform recoverability test.

*Step 2 — Recoverability test:*  
Undiscounted future net cash inflows **$44,000** **<** carrying amount **$50,000** → carrying amount **not recoverable** → proceed to impairment measurement.

*Step 3 — Impairment test (measurement):*  
Impairment loss = carrying amount − fair value = \(50{,}000 - 35{,}000 = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Impairment Loss | 15,000 | |
| Patent | | 15,000 |
| *Write patent down to fair value; new cost basis $35,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 15,000.  
New accounting basis = **$35,000**. Subsequent reversal is **prohibited**.

**(e) Revised subsequent measurement schedule — Years 4–8**  
Post-impairment annual amortization = \(35{,}000 / 5 = \mathbf{\$7{,}000}\)

| Year | Beg. CA | Amortization | End CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | $35,000 | $7,000 | $28,000 |
| 5 | 28,000 | 7,000 | 21,000 |
| 6 | 21,000 | 7,000 | 14,000 |
| 7 | 14,000 | 7,000 | 7,000 |
| 8 | 7,000 | 7,000 | **0** |

**(f) Carrying amount Dec 31, Year 4** = **$28,000**.

**Key insight:** Finite-life intangibles are amortized first; impairment uses a **two-step** process (undiscounted recoverability, then fair-value measurement). The post-impairment carrying amount becomes the **new cost basis** and is amortized over the **remaining** useful life on the subsequent measurement schedule.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Same path, all numbers changed

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Derecognition with amortization to disposal date
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Derecognition of a finite-life intangible — amortize through disposal date, remove carrying amount, recognize gain or loss  
**Scenario:**  
**Clearbrook Instruments Inc.** purchased a **patent** on **January 1, Year 1** for **$54,000** cash. Useful life **6 years**, residual **$0**, straight-line; amortization credited directly to Patent. No impairment has ever been recognized.

On **April 1, Year 4**, Clearbrook **sells** the patent for **$28,000 cash**. Books are otherwise up to date only through **December 31, Year 3** (Year 4 amortization has not yet been recorded).

**Required:**  
a. Compute the carrying amount of the patent on **January 1, Year 4** (after three full years of amortization).  
b. Record the **amortization entry** needed to update the patent to the **April 1, Year 4** disposal date.  
c. Compute the **adjusted carrying amount** at the sale date and the **gain or loss** on disposal.  
d. Record the **disposal (derecognition) journal entry** on April 1, Year 4.  
e. Briefly state why amortization is required **before** recording the sale.

**Answer key:**

**(a) CA on Jan 1, Year 4**  
Annual amortization = \(54{,}000 / 6 = \mathbf{\$9{,}000}\).  
Amortization for Years 1–3 = \(9{,}000 \times 3 = \mathbf{\$27{,}000}\).  
Carrying amount Jan 1, Year 4 = \(54{,}000 - 27{,}000 = \mathbf{\$27{,}000}\).

**(b) Amortize to disposal date (3 months of Year 4)**  
Amortization Jan 1–Apr 1 = \(9{,}000 \times 3/12 = \mathbf{\$2{,}250}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 2,250 | |
| Patent | | 2,250 |
| *Update carrying amount to disposal date* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 2,250.

**(c) Adjusted CA and gain/loss**  
Adjusted CA at sale = \(27{,}000 - 2{,}250 = \mathbf{\$24{,}750}\).  
Proceeds = **$28,000**.  
**Gain on sale** = \(28{,}000 - 24{,}750 = \mathbf{\$3{,}250}\).

**(d) Derecognition JE — Apr 1, Year 4**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 28,000 | |
| Patent | | 24,750 |
| Gain on Sale of Patent | | 3,250 |
| *Remove CA; recognize gain as part of income from continuing operations* | | |

**Check:** Dr 28,000 = Cr \(24{,}750 + 3{,}250\). Balanced.

**(e)** Amortization must be recorded **through the disposal date** so the carrying amount removed equals the asset’s book value at the moment of sale; otherwise the gain or loss is misstated.

**Key insight:** Derecognition is a two-move process: (1) amortize to the disposal date, then (2) remove the updated carrying amount against proceeds and plug gain or loss.

### Q4 — MC — Recoverability vs. impairment measurement
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Classification of the two-step impairment model for finite-life intangibles (undiscounted recoverability vs. fair-value measurement)  
**Question:**  
On December 31, **Pinnacle Ridge Co.** holds a finite-life patent with a **carrying amount of $48,000**. An impairment **indicator** is present. Estimated **undiscounted** future net cash inflows from the patent are **$52,000**. Estimated **fair value** is **$40,000**. What amount of **impairment loss**, if any, should Pinnacle Ridge recognize on December 31?

- A) $0  
- B) $8,000  
- C) $12,000  
- D) $48,000  

**Answer:** **A**. The recoverability test compares **undiscounted** future net cash inflows to carrying amount. Because \(52{,}000 > 48{,}000\), the carrying amount **is recoverable**, so the company **does not** proceed to the fair-value impairment measurement. No impairment loss is recognized even though fair value is below carrying amount.

### Q5 — MC — Post-impairment basis and reversal
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** New cost basis after impairment; subsequent reversal prohibited  
**Question:**  
**Lakewood Design** wrote a finite-life trademark down for impairment at year-end. Immediately after the write-down, the trademark’s new carrying amount is **$22,000**, remaining useful life is **4 years**, residual **$0**. Two years later, fair value has recovered to **$30,000**. Under U.S. GAAP for this finite-life intangible held and used, which statement is correct?

- A) Reverse $8,000 of the prior impairment and resume amortization on a $30,000 basis.  
- B) Reverse the entire original impairment loss when fair value recovers.  
- C) Do not reverse the impairment; continue amortizing the $22,000 new cost basis over the remaining life.  
- D) Reclassify the trademark as indefinite-life so amortization stops and annual fair-value testing begins.  

**Answer:** **C**. After an impairment loss, the adjusted carrying amount is the **new accounting basis**. **Subsequent reversal** of a previously recognized impairment loss on a finite-life intangible (held and used) is **prohibited**. Amortization continues on the reduced basis over remaining useful life (\(22{,}000 / 4 = \$5{,}500\) per year if unchanged).

### QL — CORE long multi-period — Full life cycle: acquire → amortize → impair → amortize → dispose
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Integrated subsequent measurement schedule across acquisition, amortization, impairment, post-impairment amortization, and cash disposal  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Optics Corp.** acquired a **process patent** on **January 1, Year 1** for **$96,000** cash. Useful life **8 years**, residual **$0**, straight-line; credits Patent directly. Year-end **December 31**.

- **December 31, Year 3:** After Year 3 amortization, an adverse legal/business change is an **indicator**. Undiscounted future net cash inflows = **$52,000**; fair value = **$48,000**. Remaining life after impairment = **5 years**.  
- **October 1, Year 6:** Meridian **sells** the patent for **$18,500 cash**. Year 6 amortization has not yet been recorded for any months.

**Required:**  
a. Full **subsequent measurement schedule** from Year 1 through Year 5 (include impairment row at end of Year 3 and post-impairment amortization).  
b. All **journal entries** for: (1) acquisition; (2) Year 3 amortization; (3) Year 3 impairment; (4) Year 4 amortization; (5) amortization to Oct 1, Year 6; (6) disposal on Oct 1, Year 6.  
c. Compute **gain or loss** on disposal.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Subsequent measurement schedule**

Pre-impairment annual amort = \(96{,}000 / 8 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\).

| Date / Year | Beg. CA | Amort. | Impairment | End CA | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Y1 | $96,000 | $12,000 | — | $84,000 | |
| Y2 | 84,000 | 12,000 | — | 72,000 | |
| Y3 (amort) | 72,000 | 12,000 | — | 60,000 | CA before impairment test |
| Y3 (impair) | 60,000 | — | **12,000** | **48,000** | Recover: \(52k < 60k\); loss \(60k-48k\) |
| Y4 | 48,000 | 9,600 | — | 38,400 | \(48{,}000/5 = 9{,}600\) |
| Y5 | 38,400 | 9,600 | — | **28,800** | |

Post-impairment amort/year = \(48{,}000 / 5 = \mathbf{\$9{,}600}\).

**(b) Journal entries**

*(1) Jan 1, Year 1 — Acquisition*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 96,000 | |
| Cash | | 96,000 |

**Check:** 96,000 = 96,000.

*(2) Dec 31, Year 3 — Amortization*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 12,000 | |
| Patent | | 12,000 |

**Check:** 12,000 = 12,000.

*(3) Dec 31, Year 3 — Impairment*  
Recoverability fails (\(52{,}000 < 60{,}000\)). Loss = \(60{,}000 - 48{,}000 = \mathbf{\$12{,}000}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Impairment Loss | 12,000 | |
| Patent | | 12,000 |

**Check:** 12,000 = 12,000. New basis **$48,000**.

*(4) Dec 31, Year 4 — Amortization*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 9,600 | |
| Patent | | 9,600 |

**Check:** 9,600 = 9,600.

*(5) Oct 1, Year 6 — Amortize 9 months of Year 6*  
CA Jan 1, Year 6 (end of Year 5) = **$28,800**.  
Year 6 amort through Oct 1 = \(9{,}600 \times 9/12 = \mathbf{\$7{,}200}\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 7,200 | |
| Patent | | 7,200 |

**Check:** 7,200 = 7,200.  
Adjusted CA at sale = \(28{,}800 - 7{,}200 = \mathbf{\$21{,}600}\).

*(6) Oct 1, Year 6 — Disposal*  
Adjusted CA **$21,600** − proceeds **$18,500** = **loss \(21{,}600 - 18{,}500 = \$3{,}100\)**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 18,500 | |
| Loss on Sale of Patent | 3,100 | |
| Patent | | 21,600 |
| *Remove CA; recognize loss as part of income from continuing operations* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(18{,}500 + 3{,}100 = \mathbf{21{,}600}\); Cr **21,600**. Balanced.

**(c) Loss on disposal** = **$3,100**.

**Proof roll-forward of CA at disposal:**  
\(96{,}000 - 12{,}000\times3 - 12{,}000 - 9{,}600\times2 - 7{,}200 = 96{,}000 - 36{,}000 - 12{,}000 - 19{,}200 - 7{,}200 = \mathbf{\$21{,}600}\). ✓

**Key insight:** The subsequent measurement schedule is the spine of LO 13-3: amortize → test recoverability when indicated → write down to fair value → amortize the new basis → on disposal, amortize to date and clear the residual carrying amount against proceeds.
