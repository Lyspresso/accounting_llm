# Agent 12 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-2

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** The issue price of bonds depends on the relation between the market and stated rates (Measure and record bonds at issuance)  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** initial_recognition_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- Issue price = PV of principal + PV of cash interest, both discounted at the **market (effective) rate** at issuance
- Stated rate = market rate → sell at **face (par)**
- Market rate > stated rate → sell at a **discount** (Discount on Bonds Payable)
- Market rate < stated rate → sell at a **premium** (Premium on Bonds Payable)
- Initial recognition JE: Cash; Discount or Premium; Bonds Payable at face
- Bonds sold **between interest dates**: proceeds include accrued interest (Interest Payable)
- Subsequent measurement: effective-interest amortization schedule and period interest JE
- Period-end **adjusting** accrual of interest/amortization between payment dates
- Maturity/settlement JE (final interest + repay face)
- Classification/presentation: bonds payable net of unamortized discount (or plus unamortized premium)

### Q1 — CORE — Cascade Ridge Manufacturing: par, discount, and premium at issuance
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition — compute bond issue price from market vs stated rate and record issuance JE  
**Scenario:** On January 1 of Year 1, Cascade Ridge Manufacturing Inc. authorizes and issues **$200,000** face of **6%** interest-bearing bonds. The bonds mature on December 31 of Year 5 (5-year term) and pay interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. Semiannual cash interest is contractual at the **stated** rate. Treat the following as **three separate** market-rate scenarios at issuance (each independent).

**Required:**  
(a) For each scenario, state whether the bonds sell at par, discount, or premium and why (relation of market rate to stated rate).  
(b) Compute the issue price (present value of principal + present value of interest annuity) rounded to the nearest cent. Show the semiannual market rate, number of periods, and cash interest payment used.  
(c) Record the January 1, Year 1 issuance journal entry for each scenario.  
(d) For the **discount** scenario only, state the initial carrying amount of the bonds and how Discount on Bonds Payable is presented relative to Bonds Payable.

**Scenarios:**  
1. Market (effective) rate = **6%**  
2. Market (effective) rate = **8%**  
3. Market (effective) rate = **4%**

**Answer key:**  
Common inputs for all scenarios:  
- Face (Bonds Payable) = $200,000  
- Semiannual cash interest (PMT) = $200,000 × 6% × 1/2 = **$6,000**  
- Semiannual periods (n) = 5 × 2 = **10**  
- Issue price = PV(principal at market) + PV(annuity of $6,000 at market)

**(a)–(c) Scenario 1 — Market 6% (par)**  
Market rate = stated rate (6%) → bonds sell at **face (par)**.  
Semiannual market rate = 3%; n = 10; PMT = $6,000.  
PV = $200,000 (when rates are equal, issue price equals face).  

January 1, Year 1 — Bonds sold at face value  
| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 200,000.00 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 200,000.00 |
| **Totals** | **200,000.00** | **200,000.00** |

**(a)–(c) Scenario 2 — Market 8% (discount)**  
Market 8% > stated 6% → bonds sell at a **discount**.  
Semiannual market rate = 4%; n = 10; PMT = $6,000.  
- PV of principal = $200,000 / (1.04)^10 = $135,112.8338… → component $135,112.83  
- PV of interest = $6,000 × [1 − (1.04)^−10] / 0.04 = $48,665.3747… → component $48,665.37  
- **Issue price (single PV of all cash flows)** = **$183,778.21**  
- Discount = $200,000.00 − $183,778.21 = **$16,221.79**

January 1, Year 1 — Bonds sold at a discount  
| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 183,778.21 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 16,221.79 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 200,000.00 |
| **Totals** | **200,000.00** | **200,000.00** |

**(a)–(c) Scenario 3 — Market 4% (premium)**  
Market 4% < stated 6% → bonds sell at a **premium**.  
Semiannual market rate = 2%; n = 10; PMT = $6,000.  
- PV of principal = $200,000 / (1.02)^10 = $164,069.6600… → $164,069.66  
- PV of interest = $6,000 × [1 − (1.02)^−10] / 0.02 = $53,895.5100… → $53,895.51  
- **Issue price** = **$217,965.17**  
- Premium = $217,965.17 − $200,000.00 = **$17,965.17**

January 1, Year 1 — Bonds sold at a premium  
| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 217,965.17 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | | 17,965.17 |
| Bonds Payable | | 200,000.00 |
| **Totals** | **217,965.17** | **217,965.17** |

**(d) Discount scenario presentation**  
Initial carrying amount = issue price = **$183,778.21** (= face $200,000 − unamortized discount $16,221.79). On the balance sheet, Discount on Bonds Payable is a **contra-liability** deducted from Bonds Payable.

**Key insight:** The stated rate sets the contractual cash coupons; the **market rate alone** prices the bond. Higher market rate → lower PV → discount; lower market rate → higher PV → premium; equal rates → par.

**Scenario:** On July 1 of Year 2, Cedar Peak Logistics Corp. issues **$500,000** face of **4%** bonds. The bonds mature in **3 years** (June 30 of Year 5) and pay interest **semiannually** on December 31 and June 30. Consider three **separate** market-rate cases at issuance.

(b) Compute issue price to the nearest cent (show n, i, PMT).  
(c) Record the July 1, Year 2 issuance JE for each case.  
(d) For the premium case only, state the initial carrying amount.

**Cases:**  
1. Market rate = **4%**  
2. Market rate = **6%**  
3. Market rate = **2%**

**Answer key:**  
Common: Face = $500,000; PMT = $500,000 × 4% × 1/2 = **$10,000**; n = 3 × 2 = **6**.

Market = stated → **par**. Issue price = **$500,000.00**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Harborline Tools: issued between interest payment dates at par
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Bonds issued between interest dates — proceeds include accrued interest; first interest payment splits Interest Payable and Interest Expense  
**Scenario:** Harborline Tools Co. has authorized **$300,000** of **5%** bonds that pay interest **semiannually** on **January 1** and **July 1**. The bonds are dated January 1 of Year 1. Because of registration delays, the bonds are actually issued at **face value** on **March 1 of Year 1** when the market rate equals the stated rate of 5%. Harborline’s fiscal year ends December 31.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute accrued interest collected from investors on March 1 and the total cash proceeds.  
(b) Record the March 1, Year 1 issuance entry.  
(c) Record the July 1, Year 1 cash interest payment (bonds outstanding March–June = 4 months).  
(d) In one sentence, explain why investors pay accrued interest at purchase even though the bonds are at par.

**Answer key:**  

**(a)** Accrued interest (Jan 1–Mar 1 = 2 months)  
$300,000 × 5% × 2/12 = **$2,500.00**  
Cash proceeds = face + accrued interest = $300,000 + $2,500 = **$302,500.00**  
(Bond **price** at par excludes accrued interest; **proceeds** include it.)

**(b)** March 1, Year 1 — Bonds sold at par between interest dates  
| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 302,500.00 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 300,000.00 |
| Interest Payable | | 2,500.00 |
| **Totals** | **302,500.00** | **302,500.00** |

**(c)** July 1, Year 1 — Semiannual cash interest payment  
Cash interest for 6 months = $300,000 × 5% × 6/12 = **$7,500.00**  
Interest expense for 4 months outstanding = $300,000 × 5% × 4/12 = **$5,000.00**  
Clear Interest Payable $2,500.00  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 2,500.00 | |
| Interest Expense | 5,000.00 | |
| Cash | | 7,500.00 |
| **Totals** | **7,500.00** | **7,500.00** |

**(d)** Investors prepay interest already accrued since the last coupon date so that on the next full coupon date they can receive the standard full-period cash interest; net cash interest equals only the period they held the bonds.

**Key insight:** Between-date issuances separate **bond price** (PV / face when at par) from **proceeds** (price + accrued interest). Interest Payable is a temporary liability wiped out on the next interest payment date.

### Q5 — MC (classification) — Market vs stated rate at issuance
**LO:** LO 16-2  
**Concept:** Classification of bond issue price from the relation of market rate to stated rate  

**Question 5A:** On the issuance date, which relation causes a company to record **Premium on Bonds Payable**?  
- A) Market rate equals stated rate  
- B) Market rate exceeds stated rate  
- C) Market rate is less than stated rate  
- D) Stated rate is zero and market rate is positive  

**Answer:** **C.** When the market (effective) rate is **less than** the stated (coupon) rate, investors pay more than face; the excess is Premium on Bonds Payable (adjunct liability). A is par; B is discount; D describes deep-discount/zero-coupon style pricing at a discount, not a premium.

---

**Question 5B:** Northline Dairy issues bonds at a **discount**. Immediately after issuance, which statement is correct?  
- A) Carrying amount equals face value  
- B) Carrying amount equals face value plus unamortized discount  
- C) Carrying amount equals face value minus unamortized discount  
- D) Bonds Payable is credited only for the cash proceeds  

**Answer:** **C.** Discount is a contra-liability; net carrying amount = face − unamortized discount = issue price. Bonds Payable is credited for **face**, not proceeds (A false at discount; B describes premium; D confuses face credit with cash debit).
