# Agent 220 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-4

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for change in estimate in finite life intangible assets  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** finite-life intangible (patent / license / customer list) at cash-equivalent cost
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-year straight-line amortization roll-forward of beginning CA, amortization expense, and ending CA; credit often direct to the intangible
- **Change in estimate (prospective):** revise useful life and/or residual value; do **not** restate prior periods or adjust beginning retained earnings
- New periodic amortization = **(carrying amount at revision date − revised residual) ÷ revised remaining useful life**
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** amortization expense after the revision using the updated amount
- **Disposal JE:** amortize through disposal date if needed; remove carrying amount; gain or loss = proceeds − carrying amount
- Classification: change in estimate (or estimate effected by principle for method change) → **prospective** only

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-year amortization schedule with prospective life extension (emphasis: subsequent measurement schedule)
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for a finite-life patent; prospective revision of useful life; period-end amortization after change; disposal with gain  
**Scenario:**  
**Meridian Vale Biotech** (calendar-year company) acquires a finite-life **patent** on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase price of patent | **$90,000** |
| Original estimated useful life | **6 years** |
| Original residual value | **$0** |
| Amortization method | Straight-line; company credits **Patent** directly |
| Revision date | **January 1, Year 3** (after two full years of amortization) |
| Revised total useful life from acquisition | **8 years** (**6 years** remaining at the revision date) |
| Revised residual value | Still **$0** |
| Disposal | **December 31, Year 5**, sold for **$38,000** cash (after Year-5 amortization has been recorded) |

No impairment indicators arise at any date.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1, Year 1** journal entry for **initial recognition** of the patent.  
b. Compute original annual amortization and the **carrying amount on January 1, Year 3** (immediately before the estimate revision).  
c. Prepare a complete **subsequent-measurement amortization schedule** for **Years 1–5** showing beginning carrying amount, amortization expense, and ending carrying amount. Apply the revised estimate beginning Year 3.  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 3** period-end adjusting entry for amortization under the revised remaining life.  
e. Record the **December 31, Year 5** disposal entry (after Year-5 amortization).  
f. In one or two sentences, explain why Years 1–2 amortization is **not** restated when the useful life is revised.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1 — Purchase patent*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 90,000 |
| *Record finite-life patent at cash-equivalent cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Pre-revision amortization and carrying amount**

Original annual amortization = \(\$90{,}000 / 6 = \$15{,}000\) per year.

Accumulated amortization, Years 1–2 = \(2 \times \$15{,}000 = \$30{,}000\).

Carrying amount, January 1, Year 3 = \(\$90{,}000 - \$30{,}000 = \$60{,}000\).

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — prospective revision at start of Year 3**

At revision: remaining useful life = \(8 - 2 = 6\) years; residual = $0.  
Revised annual amortization (Years 3–8) = \(\$60{,}000 / 6 = \$10{,}000\).

| Year | Beginning CA | Amortization expense | Ending CA | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 90,000 | 15,000 | 75,000 | Original estimate |
| 2 | 75,000 | 15,000 | 60,000 | Original estimate |
| 3 | 60,000 | **10,000** | **50,000** | **Revised life (prospective)** |
| 4 | 50,000 | 10,000 | 40,000 | Revised life |
| 5 | 40,000 | 10,000 | **30,000** | Revised life; sold at YE |
| **Total through Y5** | | **60,000** | | |

**Roll-forward check:** Cost − total amort through Y5 = \$90,000 − \$60,000 = **\$30,000** ending CA.

**d. Period-end adjusting entry after change in estimate**

*December 31, Year 3 — Amortization under revised remaining life*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 10,000 | |
| Patent | | 10,000 |

**Check:** Dr 10,000 = Cr 10,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Disposal (after Year-5 amortization)**

Carrying amount on December 31, Year 5 (after amort) = **\$30,000**.  
Cash proceeds = **\$38,000**.  
Gain on disposal = \$38,000 − \$30,000 = **\$8,000**.

*December 31, Year 5 — Sell patent*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 38,000 | |
| Patent | | 30,000 |
| Gain on Sale of Patent | | 8,000 |

**Check:** Dr 38,000 = Cr 30,000 + 8,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Prospective treatment**  
A change in estimated useful life of a finite-life intangible is a **change in accounting estimate**. Prior periods are **not** restated and beginning retained earnings is **not** adjusted; the remaining carrying amount is amortized over the **revised remaining** useful life only.

**Key insight:** The **subsequent measurement schedule** is the core deliverable: lock historical amortization for completed years, then recompute only from the revision-date **carrying amount** over the **new remaining life**. Extending total useful life **decreases** post-revision periodic amortization.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — License shortened life, schedule, disposal with loss
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: subsequent measurement schedule for a finite-life license; prospective useful-life shortening; period-end JE; disposal with loss  
**Scenario:**  
**Glacier Peak Industrial** (calendar year) purchases an exclusive technology **license** (finite-life intangible) on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase price | **$150,000** |
| Original estimated useful life | **10 years** |
| Original residual value | **$0** |
| Amortization | Straight-line; credit **License** directly |
| Revision date | **January 1, Year 5** (after four full years) |
| Revised total useful life from acquisition | **8 years** (**4 years** remaining) |
| Revised residual | Still **$0** |
| Disposal | **January 1, Year 7**, sold for **$40,000** cash (Year-6 amortization already recorded) |

**Required:**  
a. Record January 1, Year 1 **initial recognition**.  
b. Compute carrying amount on January 1, Year 5 before the revision.  
c. Complete amortization schedule Years 1–6 (apply revision from Year 5).  
d. Record December 31, Year 5 amortization.  
e. Record January 1, Year 7 disposal.  
f. Compute **total amortization expense** recognized over the holding period (Years 1–6) and reconcile to cost − disposal carrying amount removed.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| License | 150,000 | |
| Cash | | 150,000 |

**Check:** Dr 150,000 = Cr 150,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Carrying amount at revision date**

Original annual amort = \(\$150{,}000 / 10 = \$15{,}000\).  
Accumulated amort (4 years) = \(4 \times \$15{,}000 = \$60{,}000\).  
CA, January 1, Year 5 = \(\$150{,}000 - \$60{,}000 = \$90{,}000\).

**c. Schedule (emphasis: roll-forward through disposal)**

Revised remaining life = \(8 - 4 = 4\) years; revised annual amort = \(\$90{,}000 / 4 = \$22{,}500\).

| Year | Beginning CA | Amortization expense | Ending CA | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 150,000 | 15,000 | 135,000 | Original estimate |
| 2 | 135,000 | 15,000 | 120,000 | Original estimate |
| 3 | 120,000 | 15,000 | 105,000 | Original estimate |
| 4 | 105,000 | 15,000 | 90,000 | Original estimate |
| 5 | 90,000 | **22,500** | **67,500** | **Revised life (prospective)** |
| 6 | 67,500 | 22,500 | **45,000** | Revised life |
| **Total Y1–Y6** | | **105,000** | | |

**Check:** \$150,000 − \$105,000 = **\$45,000** ending CA.

**d. December 31, Year 5**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 22,500 | |
| License | | 22,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 22,500.

**e. Disposal with loss**

CA = **\$45,000**; proceeds = **\$40,000**.  
Loss on disposal = \$45,000 − \$40,000 = **\$5,000**.

*January 1, Year 7 — Sell license*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 40,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of License | 5,000 | |
| License | | 45,000 |

**Check:** Dr 40,000 + 5,000 = Cr 45,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Holding-period reconciliation**  
Total amortization expense Years 1–6 = **\$105,000**.  
Cost − CA removed at disposal = \$150,000 − \$45,000 = **\$105,000**. Matches.

**Key insight:** Same schedule mechanics as Q1 with **all numbers changed**. Shortening total useful life **increases** post-revision periodic amortization (here from \$15,000 to \$22,500); cash proceeds **below** updated carrying amount produce a **loss**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Dual revision (life + residual), mid-year disposal
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement with dual prospective revision of useful life and residual; period-end amortization; mid-year disposal with amortization update  
**Scenario:**  
**Thornfield Apparel Brands** (calendar year) acquires a finite-life **customer list** on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash cost of customer list | **$80,000** |
| Original useful life | **5 years** |
| Original residual value | **$5,000** |
| Amortization | Straight-line; credit **Customer List** directly |
| **Revision (January 1, Year 3)** after two full years | Total useful life revised to **8 years** from acquisition (**6 years** remaining); residual revised to **$2,000** |
| Disposal | **July 1, Year 5** sold for **$27,500** cash |

Amortization is recorded annually on December 31, except Year 5 requires **half-year** amortization through the July 1 disposal date.

**Required:**  
a. Record January 1, Year 1 initial recognition.  
b. Build the **Years 1–2** amortization schedule under the original estimate; compute carrying amount on January 1, Year 3 before revision.  
c. Compute **revised annual amortization** beginning Year 3; record December 31, Year 3 period-end amortization.  
d. Prepare the post-revision schedule excerpt through disposal; record (1) the July 1, Year 5 amortization update and (2) the disposal.  
e. **Classification / presentation:** State (i) whether the dual revision is retrospective or prospective, (ii) whether beginning retained earnings is adjusted at the revision date, and (iii) where amortization expense and the disposal gain/loss appear in the income statement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Customer List | 80,000 | |
| Cash | | 80,000 |

**Check:** Dr 80,000 = Cr 80,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Original schedule and CA at revision**

Original depreciable base = \$80,000 − \$5,000 = **\$75,000**.  
Original annual amort = \$75,000 / 5 = **\$15,000**.

| Year | Beginning CA | Amortization expense | Ending CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 80,000 | 15,000 | 65,000 |
| 2 | 65,000 | 15,000 | **50,000** |

CA, January 1, Year 3 = **\$50,000**.

**c. Dual revision math and Year 3 period-end JE**

Revised remaining life = \(8 - 2 = 6\) years.  
Revised residual = **\$2,000**.  
Revised annual amortization = \((\$50{,}000 - \$2{,}000) / 6 = \$48{,}000 / 6 = \$8{,}000\).

*December 31, Year 3*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 8,000 | |
| Customer List | | 8,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 8,000.

CA, December 31, Year 3 = \$50,000 − \$8,000 = **\$42,000**.  
CA, December 31, Year 4 (after another \$8,000) = **\$34,000**.

**d. Mid-year disposal, Year 5**

Partial Year-5 amortization (½ year) = \(\$8{,}000 \times 6/12 = \$4{,}000\).

*July 1, Year 5 — Update amortization to disposal date*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 4,000 | |
| Customer List | | 4,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 4,000.

Carrying amount at sale = \$34,000 − \$4,000 = **\$30,000**.  
Proceeds = **\$27,500**.  
Loss = \$30,000 − \$27,500 = **\$2,500**.

*July 1, Year 5 — Sell customer list*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 27,500 | |
| Loss on Sale of Customer List | 2,500 | |
| Customer List | | 30,000 |

**Check:** Dr 27,500 + 2,500 = Cr 30,000. **Balanced.**

**Post-revision schedule excerpt (through disposal)**

| Period | Beginning CA | Amortization | Ending CA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1 Y3 (post-revision base) | 50,000 | — | 50,000 |
| Year 3 | 50,000 | 8,000 | 42,000 |
| Year 4 | 42,000 | 8,000 | 34,000 |
| Jan 1–Jun 30 Y5 | 34,000 | 4,000 | **30,000** (sold) |

**e. Classification / presentation**  
(i) **Prospective** only (current and remaining periods).  
(ii) **No** beginning retained earnings adjustment at the revision date.  
(iii) Amortization expense is reported in **income from continuing operations** (typically operating expense); the loss on sale is also a component of income from continuing operations.

**Key insight:** When **both** remaining life and residual change, recompute only once at the revision date: **(CA − new residual) ÷ new remaining life**. Lengthening life and/or lowering residual interact—here the net effect cut annual amortization from \$15,000 to \$8,000. Mid-period disposal still requires amortization **through the sale date**.

### Q4 — MC — Prospective treatment of useful-life revision
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Classification of accounting treatment for change in estimated useful life of a finite-life intangible  
**Question:**  
On January 1, Year 1, a company purchased a franchise right for \$48,000 and amortized it straight-line over 8 years with zero residual value. On January 1, Year 5, management revises the franchise’s **total** useful life to 6 years from acquisition (2 years remaining). Which statement is correct?

- A) Restate Years 1–4 amortization as if the 6-year life had always been used, with an offset to beginning retained earnings.  
- B) Leave Years 1–4 unchanged; amortize the January 1, Year 5 carrying amount of \$24,000 over the remaining 2 years (\$12,000 per year).  
- C) Immediately expense the entire remaining carrying amount of \$24,000 as an impairment loss because the life was shortened.  
- D) Debit retained earnings and credit the franchise for the cumulative difference between old and new amortization for Years 1–4 only (no Year-5 amortization until year-end).

**Answer:** **B.**  
A change in estimated useful life is a **change in accounting estimate** applied **prospectively**. CA at revision = \$48,000 − 4 × (\$48,000/8) = \$24,000; new amort = \$24,000 / 2 = \$12,000. Prior periods are not restated (rejects A and D). Shortening life alone is not an automatic impairment write-off (rejects C).

### Q5 — MC — Change in amortization method
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Change in amortization method as estimate effected by principle (prospective)  
**Question:**  
A company has amortized a finite-life patent using the straight-line method. On January 1 of the current year it switches to an accelerated method that better reflects the pattern of economic benefits for the **remaining** life. Under U.S. GAAP, how is this change treated?

- A) Retrospective restatement of all prior periods presented.  
- B) Prospective application from the carrying amount at the change date (change in estimate effected by a change in accounting principle).  
- C) Cumulative catch-up through current-period amortization expense equal to the entire difference for all prior years.  
- D) Cumulative catch-up through beginning retained earnings, with no effect on current amortization.

**Answer:** **B.**  
A change in amortization (or depreciation) method is a **change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle** and is applied **prospectively**—same orientation as a pure life/residual revision. No full retrospective restatement and no RE catch-up for prior years’ amortization.
