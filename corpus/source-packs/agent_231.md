# Agent 231 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-10

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Describe financing disclosures and analyses using leverage ratios  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** issue long-term bonds at discount or premium; record face in Bonds Payable with Discount (contra) or Premium (adjunct)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period effective-interest amortization table — beginning carrying value, interest expense, cash coupon, discount/premium amortization, ending carrying value; amounts roll forward
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** accrue bond interest and amortize discount/premium when cash interest date falls after year-end; reclassify **current maturities of LTD**
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JE:** pay accrued cash coupon; early extinguishment (remove face, unamortized discount/premium, cash paid; gain/loss); installment principal payment
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure (Demo 16-10A core):** five-year principal maturities and sinking-fund requirements (ASC 470-10-50); nature of debt, rates, maturities, call provisions; assets pledged as collateral; fair value of debt and FV hierarchy level (ASC 825); unused long-term financing / unused lines of credit (ASC 470-10-50-6); SCF — debt proceeds/retirements = financing; cash interest = operating
- **Leverage ratios (Demo 16-10B core):** total liabilities-to-equity; total liabilities-to-total assets; times interest earned (income before taxes and interest ÷ interest expense)
- **Number-variant twin:** premium bond issue with all amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Effective-interest schedule (emphasis), disclosures, and leverage ratios
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of bonds at a discount; multi-period subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis); period-end interest adjusting JE; current-maturity reclass; five-year debt maturity disclosure; collateral / FV / unused credit disclosures; three leverage ratios  
**Scenario:**  
**Crestwood Precision Inc.** is a calendar-year manufacturer preparing notes and ratio analysis for its December 31, **Year 1** financial statements.

**Long-term debt outstanding at December 31, Year 1** (after Year 1 activity below):

| Instrument | Terms |
|---|---|
| **1. Unsecured bank term note** | Face **$1,200,000**; **4%** annual interest paid each **December 31**; **entire principal due December 31, Year 4**. Outstanding all of Year 1. |
| **2. Warehouse installment note (secured)** | Remaining principal **$360,000**; **5.5%** interest on unpaid principal paid each **December 31**; **principal installments of $45,000** due each December 31 for **Year 2 through Year 9** (8 remaining payments). Warehouse assets with carrying amount **$420,000** are pledged as collateral. |
| **3. Bonds payable** | On **July 1, Year 1**, Crestwood issued **$2,500,000** face, **6%** bonds dated July 1, Year 1, maturing **June 30, Year 9** (8 years). Interest is payable **semiannually on January 1 and July 1**. Bonds were issued for cash of **$2,380,000** (discount **$120,000**). Effective interest method; **effective rate 7% annual (3.5% per semiannual period)**. Cash coupon each period = **$75,000**. |

**Additional disclosure facts (December 31, Year 1):**
- Estimated **fair value** of the bonds (Level 2 inputs): **$2,450,000**. Carrying amounts of the bank note and installment note approximate fair value.
- Unused committed revolving line of credit: **$3,000,000** (commitment fee **0.25%** per year on unused portion); no amounts drawn at year-end.
- Bonds are callable at **101** after Year 3 (disclose call feature). No conversion privileges.

**December 31, Year 1 statement amounts (after recording all Year 1 interest and bond amortization):**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total assets | $14,000,000 |
| Total liabilities | 8,400,000 |
| Total stockholders’ equity | 5,600,000 |
| Income before taxes and interest expense (Year 1) | 1,890,000 |

Year 1 interest expense includes: bank term note **$48,000**; installment note **$19,800**; and the effective interest on the bonds for the half-year from July 1–December 31 (from the required schedule).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **July 1, Year 1 initial recognition journal entry** for the bond issuance.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (amortization) schedule** for the **first four semiannual periods** (Period 1: Jul 1–Dec 31 Year 1; Period 2: Jan 1–Jun 30 Year 2; Period 3: Jul 1–Dec 31 Year 2; Period 4: Jan 1–Jun 30 Year 3). Columns: beginning carrying value, interest expense, cash interest, discount amortization, ending carrying value. Round interest expense to the nearest dollar (half-up).  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting entry** for bond interest and discount amortization.  
d. Prepare the **period-end reclassification entry** for the **current maturity** of long-term debt at December 31, Year 1.  
e. Prepare a **five-year principal maturity table** (Years 2–6 and thereafter) for all three debt instruments and draft the **note disclosure sentence** for aggregate maturities (use **face/principal** amounts, ASC 470-10-50).  
f. List the **key financing disclosures** required (beyond the maturity table) given the facts.  
g. Compute **(1)** total liabilities-to-equity, **(2)** total liabilities-to-total assets, and **(3)** times interest earned for Year 1. Briefly interpret leverage and interest coverage.

**Answer key:**  

**a. July 1, Year 1 — initial recognition of bonds at discount**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 2,380,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 120,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 2,500,000 |
| *Issue $2,500,000 face bonds for $2,380,000; record discount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,380,000 + 120,000 = Cr 2,500,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule — effective interest (emphasis) (3.5% × beginning CV)**

Cash interest each period = $2,500,000 × 3% = **$75,000**.

| Period | Beg. carrying value | Interest expense (3.5%) | Cash interest | Discount amort. | End. carrying value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (Jul–Dec Y1) | $2,380,000 | $83,300 | $75,000 | $8,300 | $2,388,300 |
| 2 (Jan–Jun Y2) | 2,388,300 | 83,591 | 75,000 | 8,591 | 2,396,891 |
| 3 (Jul–Dec Y2) | 2,396,891 | 83,891 | 75,000 | 8,891 | 2,405,782 |
| 4 (Jan–Jun Y3) | 2,405,782 | 84,202 | 75,000 | 9,202 | 2,414,984 |

**Schedule checks (roll-forward):**  
Period 1: $2,380,000 × 0.035 = **$83,300**; $83,300 − $75,000 = **$8,300**; $2,380,000 + $8,300 = **$2,388,300**.  
Period 2: $2,388,300 × 0.035 = $83,590.50 → **$83,591**; amort **$8,591**; end **$2,396,891**.  
Period 3: $2,396,891 × 0.035 = $83,891.185 → **$83,891**; amort **$8,891**; end **$2,405,782**.  
Period 4: $2,405,782 × 0.035 = $84,202.37 → **$84,202**; amort **$9,202**; end **$2,414,984**.  

**Cumulative check through Period 1 (Year 1):** Unamortized discount at Dec 31 Year 1 = $120,000 − $8,300 = **$111,700**; CV = $2,500,000 − $111,700 = **$2,388,300**.

**c. December 31, Year 1 — period-end adjusting JE (bond interest)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 83,300 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 8,300 |
| Interest Payable | | 75,000 |
| *Accrue semi-annual effective interest; first cash coupon due Jan 1 Year 2* | | |

**Check:** Dr 83,300 = Cr 8,300 + 75,000. **Balanced.**

**d. December 31, Year 1 — reclassify current maturities of LTD**

Only the installment note has principal due within one year: **$45,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable—Long-Term (Installment) | 45,000 | |
| Current Maturities of Long-Term Debt | | 45,000 |
| *Reclassify principal due within one year as current liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 45,000. **Balanced.**

(After reclass: current portion $45,000; noncurrent installment principal $315,000; unsecured term note $1,200,000 all noncurrent until Year 4; bonds all noncurrent until Year 9.)

**e. Five-year principal maturity table (December 31, Year 1)**

| Debt | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Year 5 | Year 6 | Thereafter |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unsecured bank term note | $0 | $0 | $1,200,000 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Secured installment note | 45,000 | 45,000 | 45,000 | 45,000 | 45,000 | 135,000 |
| Bonds payable (face) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2,500,000 |
| **Total** | **$45,000** | **$45,000** | **$1,245,000** | **$45,000** | **$45,000** | **$2,635,000** |

**Table checks:** Installment thereafter = $360,000 − 5 × $45,000 = **$135,000**. Year 4 total = $1,200,000 + $45,000 = **$1,245,000**. Thereafter = $135,000 + $2,500,000 = **$2,635,000**. Grand total of all columns = $1,200,000 + $360,000 + $2,500,000 = **$4,060,000**.

**Sample note disclosure:**  
*At December 31, Year 1, aggregate principal maturities of long-term debt are approximately $45,000, $45,000, $1,245,000, $45,000, and $45,000 for Year 2 through Year 6, respectively, and $2,635,000 thereafter.*

**f. Other required financing disclosures (given facts)**
- Nature of each debt instrument; **interest rates**; **maturity dates**; **call provision** (callable at 101 after Year 3).
- **Assets pledged as collateral** — warehouse CA **$420,000** securing the installment note.
- **Fair value of financial instruments** — bonds FV **$2,450,000** (Level 2); notes approximate carrying amount; hierarchy level disclosed (ASC 825-10-50).
- **Unused long-term / revolving financing** — unused committed LOC **$3,000,000**, terms and commitment fee 0.25% (ASC 470-10-50-6).
- Creditor restrictions/covenants if any (none given beyond collateral and call feature).

**g. Leverage ratios — Year 1**

Total interest expense Year 1 = $48,000 + $19,800 + $83,300 = **$151,100**.

| Ratio | Formula | Computation | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Total liabilities-to-equity | TL ÷ Equity | $8,400,000 ÷ $5,600,000 | **1.50** |
| Total liabilities-to-total assets | TL ÷ TA | $8,400,000 ÷ $14,000,000 | **0.60** (60%) |
| Times interest earned | EBIT ÷ Interest expense | $1,890,000 ÷ $151,100 | **12.51 times** |

**Interpretation:** Creditors finance **60%** of assets; liabilities are **1.50×** equity (moderate leverage). Times interest earned of **~12.5×** indicates operating earnings cover interest many times — capacity to service debt from current earnings appears strong despite material long-term borrowings.

**Key insight:** The **subsequent measurement schedule** is the analytical bridge between issuance proceeds and year-end carrying value used on the balance sheet and in fair-value comparisons. Financing analysis then pairs that CV with **note disclosures** (five-year maturities by **principal** payment date, collateral, FV, unused credit) and **leverage ratios**. Maturity tables use **face/principal**, not amortized cost.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Premium bond issue, amort schedule, disclosures, leverage ratios
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition of bonds at a **premium**; multi-period effective-interest subsequent measurement schedule; period-end adj; current maturity reclass; five-year maturities; unused credit and FV disclosure; leverage ratios with all new amounts

**Oakmont Drive Components Corp.** (calendar-year) has the following long-term debt at December 31, **Year 1**:

Income before taxes and interest **$2,500,000**.

a. **July 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE** for the bond issue.  
b. **Amortization schedule** for the first **four** semiannual periods (premium amortization; round IE to nearest dollar, half-up).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Coupon settlement, early extinguishment, SCF, ratio and maturity impact
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Settlement JEs (coupon payment and early extinguishment); update amortization to retirement date using subsequent measurement amounts; financing vs operating cash-flow classification; recompute leverage ratios after debt retirement; maturity disclosure change  
**Scenario:**  
Continue **Crestwood Precision Inc.** from Q1. Bond carrying value at **December 31, Year 1** = **$2,388,300** (discount remaining **$111,700**). Interest payable of **$75,000** is on the books for the January 1, Year 2 coupon.

On **January 1, Year 2**, Crestwood pays the scheduled cash interest on the full bond issue.

On **March 1, Year 2**, Crestwood retires the **entire** bond issue on the open market at **99** (pays **$2,475,000** for principal) **plus accrued interest** for two months. Use the **effective-interest** amounts for **Period 2** from Q1 (full-issue Period 2 interest expense **$83,591**; discount amort **$8,591**; cash coupon **$75,000**). Allocate **2/6** of those Period-2 amounts for the two months from January 1 to March 1.

**Immediately before the March 1 retirement** (after updating books for two months of interest as you record in part b), Crestwood’s simplified balances for ratio analysis are:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total assets (before paying retirement cash and accrued interest) | $14,200,000 |
| Total liabilities (before removing retired debt and related accruals) | 8,520,000 |
| Stockholders’ equity | 5,680,000 |

(These balances already include the January 1 coupon payment’s effect on cash and interest payable for the full issue, and include two months’ accrual as you record in part b.)

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 2 settlement JE** for the full-issue cash coupon payment.  
b. Prepare the **March 1, Year 2 updating JE** for two months’ effective interest on the bonds.  
c. Prepare the **March 1, Year 2 extinguishment JE** for retirement of $2,500,000 face at 99 (exclude accrued interest from this entry if already accrued in b; show cash paid for principal). Also settle the related interest payable.  
d. **Classification / SCF:** Classify each cash flow—(i) January 1 coupon, (ii) March 1 accrued interest paid, (iii) March 1 cash paid to retire principal at 99—as **operating** or **financing**.  
e. Compute **cash paid on March 1** for principal + accrued interest. Compute **liabilities-to-equity** and **liabilities-to-assets** **after** the March 1 retirement entries (b–c and payment of accrued interest), starting from the “before” totals given.  
f. How does the **five-year maturity disclosure** change after retiring all bonds?

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 2 — settle full coupon (Interest Payable from Dec 31)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 75,000 | |
| Cash | | 75,000 |
| *Pay semiannual cash interest on $2,500,000 face* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 75,000. **Balanced.**

**b. March 1, Year 2 — update books for 2 months of Period 2**

Full-issue Period 2: IE $83,591; amort $8,591; coupon $75,000.  
× 2/6 of period:  
- Interest expense = $83,591 × 2/6 = **$27,864** (rounded)  
- Discount amortization = $8,591 × 2/6 = **$2,864** (rounded)  
- Accrued cash interest = $75,000 × 2/6 = **$25,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 27,864 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 2,864 |
| Interest Payable | | 25,000 |
| *Update effective interest for 2 months to retirement date* | | |

**Check:** Dr 27,864 = Cr 2,864 + 25,000. **Balanced.**

Carrying value at Dec 31 Year 1 = **$2,388,300**.  
CV at March 1 Year 2 = $2,388,300 + $2,864 = **$2,391,164**.  
Unamortized discount = $2,500,000 − $2,391,164 = **$108,836**.

**c. March 1, Year 2 — extinguish $2,500,000 face at 99**

Cash paid for principal = $2,500,000 × 0.99 = **$2,475,000**.  
Loss = cash paid − carrying value retired = $2,475,000 − $2,391,164 = **$83,836**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 2,500,000 | |
| Loss on Early Extinguishment of Debt | 83,836 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 108,836 |
| Cash | | 2,475,000 |
| *Remove entire issue; loss = reacquisition price − CV* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,500,000 + 83,836 = **2,583,836**; Cr 108,836 + 2,475,000 = **2,583,836**. **Balanced.**

Also settle accrued interest:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Payable | 25,000 | |
| Cash | | 25,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 25,000. **Balanced.**

**d. SCF classification / disclosure**

| Cash flow | Amount | SCF section |
|---|---:|---|
| (i) Jan 1 coupon (full issue) | $75,000 | **Operating** (cash paid for interest) |
| (ii) Mar 1 accrued interest | $25,000 | **Operating** |
| (iii) Mar 1 principal retirement at 99 | $2,475,000 | **Financing** (outflow to retire long-term debt) |

Noncash / income effects related to debt: discount amortization increases interest expense above cash interest; **loss on early extinguishment** is included in net income (indirect-method presentation coordinates with financing cash for the reacquisition price).

**e. Ratios after March 1 retirement**

Cash outflows March 1 = $2,475,000 + $25,000 = **$2,500,000**.

Starting “before” totals already reflect Jan 1 coupon and the part-b accrual. After extinguishment + paying $25,000 interest:

- **Assets:** $14,200,000 − $2,475,000 − $25,000 = **$11,700,000**  
- **Liabilities:** $8,520,000 − $2,391,164 (CV removed) − $25,000 (interest payable paid) = **$6,103,836**  
- **Equity:** $5,680,000 − $83,836 (loss) = **$5,596,164**  

**Check:** Assets $11,700,000 = Liabilities $6,103,836 + Equity $5,596,164.

| Ratio | After retirement |
|---|---:|
| Liabilities-to-equity | $6,103,836 ÷ $5,596,164 = **1.09** |
| Liabilities-to-total assets | $6,103,836 ÷ $11,700,000 = **0.52** |

**Interpretation:** Early retirement **deleverages** the balance sheet (L/E falls to about **1.09**; L/A to **52%**), at the cost of cash and a recognized **loss**.

**f. Maturity disclosure after full bond retirement**

Bond face **$2,500,000** is removed from the **thereafter** column.  
Thereafter becomes installment thereafter only = **$135,000** (was $2,635,000). Near-term Years 2–6 totals for the term note and installment note are **unchanged** ($45k / $45k / $1,245k / $45k / $45k). Aggregate long-term principal remaining at Dec 31 Year 1 terms would be $1,200,000 + $360,000 = **$1,560,000** if bonds had not existed; after March 1 Year 2 retirement the same remaining notes continue on their schedules.

**Key insight:** Settlement and extinguishment entries **remove** balance-sheet leverage and change **note maturity tables**; cash **interest** stays in **operating** activities while cash to **retire principal** is **financing**—the financing disclosure / cash-flow mapping in LO 16-10.

### Q4 — MC — Five-year debt maturity disclosure
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Classification / disclosure — ASC 470 five-year aggregate maturities requirement  
**Question:**  
Which of the following must be disclosed in the notes for long-term borrowings under U.S. GAAP (ASC 470-10-50)?

- A) Only the total long-term debt carrying value; year-by-year maturities are optional  
- B) The combined aggregate amount of maturities and sinking-fund requirements for each of the **five years** following the balance sheet date  
- C) Fair value of debt only if the company elected the fair value option  
- D) Unused lines of credit only when amounts have been drawn  

**Answer:** **B**. ASC 470-10-50-1 requires disclosure of the combined aggregate amount of maturities and sinking-fund requirements for all long-term borrowings for **each of the five years** following the latest balance sheet date. (A is wrong—near-term maturities are required. C is wrong—FV of financial instruments is generally disclosed when practicable under ASC 825, not only under the FV option. D is wrong—**unused** commitments and lines must be disclosed, including terms and fees.)

### Q5 — MC — Times interest earned and leverage interpretation
**LO:** LO 16-10  
**Concept:** Times interest earned ratio definition and leverage analysis  
**Question:**  
Tallgrass Industrial reports income before taxes and interest of **$960,000**, interest expense of **$160,000**, total liabilities of **$4,800,000**, total assets of **$8,000,000**, and equity of **$3,200,000**. Which statement is correct?

- A) Times interest earned = $960,000 ÷ $4,800,000 = 0.20; the company cannot cover interest  
- B) Times interest earned = $960,000 ÷ $160,000 = **6.0**; liabilities-to-assets = **0.60**; interest coverage is adequate despite 60% creditor financing  
- C) Times interest earned = ($960,000 − $160,000) ÷ $160,000 = 5.0; liabilities-to-equity = 1.0  
- D) Liabilities-to-equity = $8,000,000 ÷ $3,200,000 = 2.5; times interest earned is not used for leverage analysis  

**Answer:** **B**. Times interest earned = income before taxes and interest ÷ interest expense = $960,000 ÷ $160,000 = **6.0 times**. Liabilities-to-total assets = $4,800,000 ÷ $8,000,000 = **0.60**. A 6× coverage indicates earnings can cover interest six times—generally comfortable coverage—even though creditors finance 60% of assets. (A uses the wrong denominator. C incorrectly subtracts interest from the numerator and misstates L/E: L/E = 4,800,000 ÷ 3,200,000 = **1.5**, not 1.0. D uses assets in the L/E numerator and wrongly dismisses TIE.)
