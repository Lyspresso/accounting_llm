# Agent 149 — CORE DEMO — LO 16-8

**Chapter:** 16  
**LO title:** Account for conversion of debt into equity  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** issue convertible bonds as **straight debt** — entire proceeds to liability (Bonds Payable ± Discount/Premium); **no allocation** of proceeds to equity for the conversion feature (ASC 470-20-25-12)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** amortize discount or premium (straight-line when specified) and roll forward carrying value; cash coupon vs interest expense
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** record interest expense and discount/premium amortization (and cash interest if on payment date) **through the conversion date** before derecognizing debt
- **Disposal / settlement JE (conversion under terms):** remove Bonds Payable and remaining unamortized discount/premium; credit Common Stock (shares × par) and APIC for the **plug to carrying amount**; **no gain or loss** in net income (book-value method); market price of stock is **not** used to measure equity issued
- **Induced conversion:** expense equal to fair value of **excess consideration** offered beyond original conversion terms (e.g., cash sweetener); still no gain/loss on the debt-to-equity exchange itself
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** convertible debt reported as a **liability** until converted; conversion privileges disclosed in notes; induced conversion expense on the income statement; equity issued increases common stock and APIC
- **Number-variant twin:** same skill path with all face, premium/discount, rates, shares, and dates changed

### Q1 — CORE — Convertible bonds: issue at discount, amort schedule, interest JEs, partial conversion (book value)
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of convertible debt as straight debt; straight-line subsequent measurement schedule; period-end interest and amort JEs; partial conversion settlement at carrying amount (no gain/loss); presentation of conversion feature  
**Scenario:**  
**Crestline Dynamics Inc.** is a calendar-year company. On **January 1, Year 1**, Crestline issues **$400,000** face of **5%**, **4-year** convertible bonds for cash proceeds of **$384,000**. Interest is payable **annually each December 31**. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible at any time into **20** shares of Crestline’s **$1** par common stock. Crestline amortizes bond discount using the **straight-line method**. The conversion feature is not required to be accounted for as a derivative and does not create a substantial premium subject to special equity allocation.

**Additional facts:**
- On **December 31, Year 2**, immediately after recording Year 2 interest and amortization, holders of **60%** of the bonds convert under the original conversion terms. Crestline’s common stock trades at **$48** per share on that date (for context only).
- No cash inducement is offered.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition JE**. Explain briefly why no equity is recognized for the conversion feature at issuance.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (amortization) schedule** for the **full 4-year life** (columns: beginning carrying value, cash interest, discount amortization, interest expense, ending carrying value).  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 2** period-end interest / amortization JEs.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 2 conversion JE** for the 60% converted (after the Year 2 interest entry in part c).  
e. **Classification / presentation:** (1) How is the convertible debt classified on the balance sheet **before** conversion? (2) Is a gain or loss recognized on conversion under the original terms? (3) What note disclosure is typically required regarding the conversion feature?

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition (straight debt)**

Discount = $400,000 − $384,000 = **$16,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 384,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | 16,000 | |
| Bonds Payable | | 400,000 |
| *Issue $400,000 face 5% convertible bonds for $384,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr 384,000 + 16,000 = Cr 400,000. **Balanced.**

**Why no equity at issuance:** Under ASC 470-20, an embedded conversion feature is generally **not** bifurcated; the instrument is accounted for **in its entirety as a liability**. There is no objective market basis to allocate proceeds between the debt host and the conversion option, so **none of the proceeds is credited to equity** at issuance (unless derivative or certain premium guidance applies — not here).

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (straight-line)**

Annual cash interest = $400,000 × 5% = **$20,000**  
Annual discount amortization = $16,000 ÷ 4 years = **$4,000**  
Annual interest expense = $20,000 + $4,000 = **$24,000**

| Year | Beg. carrying value | Cash interest | Discount amort. | Interest expense | End. carrying value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $384,000 | $20,000 | $4,000 | $24,000 | $388,000 |
| 2 | 388,000 | 20,000 | 4,000 | 24,000 | 392,000 |
| 3 | 392,000 | 20,000 | 4,000 | 24,000 | 396,000 |
| 4 | 396,000 | 20,000 | 4,000 | 24,000 | 400,000 |
| **Totals** | | **80,000** | **16,000** | **96,000** | |

**Schedule checks:** discount fully amortized ($16,000); ending CV at maturity = face $400,000; total interest expense = total cash + total amort = $80,000 + $16,000 = $96,000.

**c. Period-end interest and amortization JEs**

*December 31, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 24,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 4,000 |
| Cash | | 20,000 |
| *Record annual interest and SL discount amortization* | | |

**Check:** Dr 24,000 = Cr 4,000 + 20,000. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 2* (same pattern; recorded **before** conversion)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 24,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 4,000 |
| Cash | | 20,000 |
| *Record annual interest and SL discount amortization* | | |

**Check:** Dr 24,000 = Cr 4,000 + 20,000. **Balanced.**

After Year 2 entry: unamortized discount remaining = $16,000 − $4,000 − $4,000 = **$8,000**; carrying value = **$392,000**.

**d. December 31, Year 2 — partial conversion (60%) under original terms**

Face converted = $400,000 × 60% = **$240,000**  
Unamortized discount allocated to converted portion = $8,000 × 60% = **$4,800**  
Carrying amount converted = $240,000 − $4,800 = **$235,200**  
Shares issued = ($240,000 / $1,000) × 20 = **4,800** shares  
Common stock at par = 4,800 × $1 = **$4,800**  
APIC = $235,200 − $4,800 = **$230,400**

Market price ($48) is **ignored** under the book-value method.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 240,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 4,800 |
| Common Stock ($1 par) | | 4,800 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 230,400 |
| *Convert 60% of bonds into 4,800 common shares at carrying amount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 240,000 = Cr 4,800 + 4,800 + 230,400. **Balanced.**  
**No gain or loss** is recognized.

**Remaining after conversion:** Bonds Payable $160,000; Discount $3,200; carrying value $156,800.

**e. Classification / presentation / disclosure**

1. **Before conversion:** reported as a **long-term liability** (Bonds Payable net of Discount), not as equity — the conversion feature does not reclassify the instrument to equity while debt is outstanding.  
2. **On conversion under original terms:** **no gain or loss** in net income; the carrying amount of the debt becomes the basis of the equity issued.  
3. **Disclosure:** note disclosure of **conversion privileges** (conversion rate/price, shares issuable, any conditions or windows) is typical; also outstanding convertible debt amounts and related terms.

**Key insight:** Convertible debt is booked like ordinary debt. At conversion under the indenture, update amort to the conversion date, remove the **carrying amount** of the debt, and replace it with common stock + APIC — **never** mark equity to the stock’s market price, and **never** report conversion G/L under the original terms.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Convertible bonds at premium: full schedule, full conversion, no inducement
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: issue convertible bonds at a premium as straight debt; SL amort schedule; interest JEs; full conversion at carrying amount with remaining unamortized premium  
**Scenario:**  
**Northfork Holdings Corp.** (calendar year) issues **$750,000** face of **7%**, **5-year** convertible bonds on **January 1, Year 1** for cash of **$765,000**. Interest is paid **annually on December 31**. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **15** shares of Northfork’s **$5** par common stock. Straight-line amortization of premium. On **December 31, Year 3**, after recording Year 3 interest and amortization, **all** bondholders convert under the original terms. Common stock market price on conversion date: **$62** per share (context only).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, Year 1 issuance JE**.  
b. Prepare the **5-year subsequent measurement schedule** (beg CV, cash interest, premium amort, interest expense, end CV).  
c. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** interest / amortization JE.  
d. Prepare the **December 31, Year 3 conversion JE** for 100% of the bonds.  
e. Briefly state the effect of conversion on the **liabilities** and **equity** sections of the balance sheet.

**Answer key:**  

**a. January 1, Year 1 — issuance at premium**

Premium = $765,000 − $750,000 = **$15,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 765,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | | 15,000 |
| Bonds Payable | | 750,000 |
| *Issue $750,000 face 7% convertible bonds for $765,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr 765,000 = Cr 15,000 + 750,000. **Balanced.**  
No equity credited for the conversion feature.

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (straight-line)**

Annual cash interest = $750,000 × 7% = **$52,500**  
Annual premium amortization = $15,000 ÷ 5 = **$3,000**  
Annual interest expense = $52,500 − $3,000 = **$49,500**

| Year | Beg. carrying value | Cash interest | Premium amort. | Interest expense | End. carrying value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $765,000 | $52,500 | $3,000 | $49,500 | $762,000 |
| 2 | 762,000 | 52,500 | 3,000 | 49,500 | 759,000 |
| 3 | 759,000 | 52,500 | 3,000 | 49,500 | 756,000 |
| 4 | 756,000 | 52,500 | 3,000 | 49,500 | 753,000 |
| 5 | 753,000 | 52,500 | 3,000 | 49,500 | 750,000 |
| **Totals** | | **262,500** | **15,000** | **247,500** | |

**Schedule checks:** total premium amortized $15,000; CV at maturity = face $750,000; total interest expense = total cash − total amort = $262,500 − $15,000 = $247,500.

**c. December 31, Year 3 — interest and amortization**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 49,500 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 3,000 | |
| Cash | | 52,500 |
| *Record annual interest and SL premium amortization* | | |

**Check:** Dr 49,500 + 3,000 = Cr 52,500. **Balanced.**

After Year 3 entry: unamortized premium = $15,000 − $9,000 = **$6,000**; carrying value = **$756,000**.

**d. December 31, Year 3 — full conversion under original terms**

Shares issued = ($750,000 / $1,000) × 15 = **11,250** shares  
Common stock at par = 11,250 × $5 = **$56,250**  
APIC = carrying amount − CS par = $756,000 − $56,250 = **$699,750**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 750,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 6,000 | |
| Common Stock ($5 par) | | 56,250 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 699,750 |
| *Convert all bonds into 11,250 common shares at carrying amount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 750,000 + 6,000 = Cr 56,250 + 699,750. **Balanced.**  
Market price $62 is **not** used. **No gain or loss.**

**e. Balance sheet effect**

- **Liabilities decrease** by the carrying amount of the converted debt (**$756,000** net).  
- **Equity increases** by the same amount: Common Stock **$56,250** + APIC **$699,750**.  
- Total assets unchanged by the conversion itself (no cash exchanged).

**Key insight:** Premium remaining at conversion **increases** the equity credited (debt carrying amount is higher). The twin of Q1’s discount case: unamortized premium is closed **with** Bonds Payable into equity accounts.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Mid-period conversion update + induced conversion
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Period-end/mid-period adjusting JE to update interest and amortization to conversion date; conversion settlement JE; induced conversion expense for cash sweetener; disclosure of inducement  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborlight Technologies LLC** has outstanding **$300,000** face of **6%** convertible bonds. Interest is payable **annually on December 31**. Each **$1,000** bond is convertible into **12** shares of Harborlight’s **$2** par common stock.

**Part A — Conversion mid-year (update then convert)**  
At **January 1, Year 4**, unamortized discount is **$9,000**. Remaining term is **3 years**. Harborlight uses **straight-line** amortization (annual amort = $9,000 ÷ 3 = **$3,000**). On **July 1, Year 4**, all bondholders convert under the **original** conversion terms. No cash inducement. Harborlight has not yet recorded any Year 4 interest.

**Part B — Induced conversion (separate fact set)**  
On an interest payment date, Harborlight has **$200,000** face of **8%** convertible bonds with **unamortized premium of $6,000** (carrying amount **$206,000**). Each **$1,000** bond converts into **10** shares of **$10** par common. To encourage prompt conversion, Harborlight offers **$40 cash per bond** if converted within a limited window that qualifies as an **induced conversion** under ASC 470-20. All holders accept. Market price of common is **$55** per share (context only).

**Required:**  
a. **Part A:** Prepare the **July 1, Year 4 adjusting JE** to update interest expense and discount amortization for six months.  
b. **Part A:** Prepare the **July 1, Year 4 conversion JE**.  
c. **Part B:** Prepare the **induced conversion JE**.  
d. **Part B:** How is the cash inducement reported, and why is the transaction **not** treated as a debt extinguishment for the inducement cost under the chapter’s induced-conversion model?  
e. **Classification:** After either conversion, what happens to the convertible debt liability and to equity?

**Answer key:**  

**a. July 1, Year 4 — update interest and amortization (6/12 of annual)**

Six-month cash interest (accrued) = $300,000 × 6% × 6/12 = **$9,000**  
Six-month discount amortization = $3,000 × 6/12 = **$1,500**  
Interest expense = $9,000 + $1,500 = **$10,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 10,500 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 1,500 |
| Interest Payable | | 9,000 |
| *Update interest and SL discount amort to conversion date* | | |

**Check:** Dr 10,500 = Cr 1,500 + 9,000. **Balanced.**

After update: unamortized discount = $9,000 − $1,500 = **$7,500**; carrying value = $300,000 − $7,500 = **$292,500**.

**b. July 1, Year 4 — conversion under original terms**

Shares issued = ($300,000 / $1,000) × 12 = **3,600** shares  
Common stock at par = 3,600 × $2 = **$7,200**  
APIC = $292,500 − $7,200 = **$285,300**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 300,000 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 7,500 |
| Common Stock ($2 par) | | 7,200 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 285,300 |
| *Convert all bonds into 3,600 common shares at updated carrying amount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 300,000 = Cr 7,500 + 7,200 + 285,300. **Balanced.** **No gain or loss.**

(Note: Interest Payable $9,000 remains until paid; conversion does not eliminate accrued interest unless the indenture so provides — here interest was accrued separately.)

**c. Part B — induced conversion JE**

Cash inducement = 200 bonds × $40 = **$8,000** = **Debt Conversion Expense**  
Shares issued = 200 × 10 = **2,000** shares  
Common stock at par = 2,000 × $10 = **$20,000**  
APIC from debt carrying amount = ($200,000 + $6,000) − $20,000 = **$186,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bonds Payable | 200,000 | |
| Debt Conversion Expense | 8,000 | |
| Premium on Bonds Payable | 6,000 | |
| Common Stock ($10 par) | | 20,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 186,000 |
| Cash | | 8,000 |
| *Induced conversion: remove debt at carrying amount; expense cash sweetener* | | |

**Check:** Dr 200,000 + 8,000 + 6,000 = Cr 20,000 + 186,000 + 8,000 → 214,000 = 214,000. **Balanced.**

APIC is based on **debt carrying amount**, not market value of shares. The **$8,000** is **not** charged to APIC; it is an **expense**.

**d. Reporting of inducement**

- Debt conversion expense **$8,000** is recognized in **net income** (expense for excess consideration over original conversion privileges, measured at FV of consideration transferred — here cash).  
- Under induced-conversion accounting (when criteria are met: limited-time offer, form/amount within original instrument framework, reasonably possible exercise), the **inducement cost is expensed**; the equity issued is still measured from the **carrying amount of the debt** (no extinguishment gain/loss on the debt-for-stock exchange itself). The chapter treats qualifying inducements this way rather than as a full debt extinguishment remeasurement of the liability.

**e. Classification after conversion**

- Convertible **bonds payable (and related discount/premium) are fully derecognized** — no remaining long-term convertible debt for the converted portion.  
- **Equity increases** (Common Stock + APIC). Induced cash reduces assets and increases expense (reduces retained earnings via net income).

**Key insight:** Always **update amortization and interest to the conversion date** first. Then convert at **carrying amount**. A cash (or other) sweetener that qualifies as an inducement is **expensed** — it does not create conversion G/L on the debt itself and does not change the book-value equity credits for the shares.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 16-8  
**Concept:** Classification — straight-debt issuance of convertible bonds; book-value conversion (no G/L); treatment of conversion inducement  

**Question 1:**  
On January 1, **Rivermark Corp.** issues **$100,000** of convertible bonds for **$106,000**. Each bond is convertible into common stock. The conversion feature is not a freestanding equity instrument and is not required to be accounted for as a derivative. At issuance, Rivermark should:

- A) Credit Bonds Payable $100,000, credit Premium $6,000, and credit Paid-In Capital—Conversion Feature for an amount equal to the estimated value of the conversion option  
- B) Credit Bonds Payable $100,000 and credit Premium on Bonds Payable $6,000; **no** equity is recognized for the conversion feature  
- C) Credit Common Stock for the market value of shares issuable on conversion and credit Bonds Payable for the residual  
- D) Debit Unrealized Loss—Equity and credit a conversion option liability for the difference between proceeds and the pure-debt fair value  

**Answer:** **B.** Convertible debt with an embedded conversion feature is generally recorded **entirely as a liability** (straight-debt issuance). Proceeds of $106,000 are allocated to Bonds Payable $100,000 and Premium $6,000; **none** of the proceeds is allocated to equity for the conversion feature under ASC 470-20 (absent derivative/special premium rules).

---

**Question 2:**  
When convertible bonds are converted **under the original conversion privileges**, the issuer has updated interest and amortization to the conversion date. The common stock’s market price exceeds the carrying amount of the debt. The issuer should:

- A) Recognize a loss equal to market value of shares issued minus carrying amount of debt  
- B) Recognize a gain equal to carrying amount of debt minus par value of shares issued  
- C) Remove the carrying amount of the debt and credit Common Stock (par) and APIC for the residual so that **no gain or loss** is recognized; market price is not used  
- D) Credit Common Stock and APIC for the market value of shares issued and recognize gain or loss for the difference from carrying amount  

**Answer:** **C.** On conversion under the instrument’s terms, the **carrying amount** of convertible debt (face ± remaining premium/discount, reduced by any cash/assets transferred) is reclassified into capital accounts. **No gain or loss** is recognized in net income. The stock’s **market price is ignored** under this book-value approach.
