# Agent 56 — CORE DEMO — LO 9-9

**Chapter:** 9  
**LO title:** Perform inventory ratio analysis and interpretation  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Inventory turnover = Cost of goods sold ÷ Average inventory
- Average days in inventory = 365 ÷ Inventory turnover
- Gross profit percentage = Gross profit ÷ Net sales
- Average inventory = (Beginning inventory + Ending inventory) ÷ 2
- **Initial recognition** of merchandise purchases (perpetual): Inventory / Accounts Payable (and freight-in)
- **Settlement** of payables and purchase returns; removal of inventory at sale (COGS)
- **Inventory rollforward schedule** (subsequent measurement of the inventory account)
- Interpretation: higher turnover / lower days generally preferred; excess vs insufficient inventory vs industry
- Cost-flow method effects when prices rise: LIFO → higher COGS, lower inventory, higher turnover, lower gross profit % than FIFO
- Comparability: adjust LIFO amounts using the **LIFO reserve** (and period-end LIFO conversion JE) before comparing to FIFO peers
- Classification / presentation / disclosure: inventory method and LIFO reserve in notes; inventory as current asset

### Q1 — CORE — Northfork Trading Co.: purchase cycle, inventory schedule, multi-year ratios (initial recognition emphasis)
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of inventory purchases and freight; settlement of AP and returns; sale/COGS; inventory rollforward; inventory turnover, days in inventory, and gross profit % with trend interpretation  
**Scenario:**  
**Northfork Trading Co.** is a wholesale merchandiser using a **perpetual inventory system**. Fiscal year ends **December 31**. Selected balances:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Inventory — Dec 31, Year 0 | $210,000 |
| Inventory — Dec 31, Year 1 | 245,000 |
| Net sales — Year 1 | 1,800,000 |
| Cost of goods sold — Year 1 | 1,080,000 |
| Net sales — Year 2 | 2,100,000 |

**Year 2 activity (chronological summary):**  
1. Purchased merchandise on account, **$1,250,000** (invoice gross).  
2. Paid freight-in of **$35,000** cash on Year 2 purchases (f.o.b. shipping point; inventoriable).  
3. Returned defective merchandise to suppliers; accounts payable credit **$40,000** (inventory returned).  
4. Paid the remaining Year 2 accounts payable related to these purchases in cash (settlement of net payables from items 1 and 3).  
5. Sold merchandise on account for **$2,100,000**; cost of merchandise sold **$1,260,000**.  
6. Year-end physical count agrees with the perpetual records (no inventory shrink adjustment).

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the **initial recognition** journal entries for Year 2 purchases and freight-in.  
(b) Prepare the journal entries for the **purchase return** and for **settlement** of the related accounts payable.  
(c) Prepare the journal entries for the **sale** and for **cost of goods sold** (inventory disposal / derecognition).  
(d) Prepare the **inventory rollforward schedule** for Year 2 and prove ending inventory.  
(e) Compute for **Year 1 and Year 2**: (1) inventory turnover, (2) average days in inventory, (3) gross profit, and (4) gross profit percentage. Round turnover and days to **two** decimals; round percentages to **two** decimals.  
(f) Briefly **interpret** the Year 1 → Year 2 trend (efficiency and margin).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Initial recognition — Year 2 purchases and freight**  
```
Dr Inventory ................................ 1,250,000
   Cr Accounts Payable ............................... 1,250,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 1,250,000)

```
Dr Inventory ................................... 35,000
   Cr Cash .............................................. 35,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 35,000)

**(b) Purchase return and AP settlement**  
```
Dr Accounts Payable ........................... 40,000
   Cr Inventory ......................................... 40,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 40,000)

Net AP from these purchases = $1,250,000 − $40,000 = **$1,210,000**

```
Dr Accounts Payable ........................ 1,210,000
   Cr Cash ........................................... 1,210,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 1,210,000)

**(c) Sale and inventory disposal (COGS)**  
```
Dr Accounts Receivable ..................... 2,100,000
   Cr Sales Revenue .................................. 2,100,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 2,100,000)

```
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ...................... 1,260,000
   Cr Inventory ...................................... 1,260,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 1,260,000)

**(d) Inventory rollforward — Year 2**  

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory (Jan 1, Year 2) | $245,000 |
| + Purchases | 1,250,000 |
| + Freight-in | 35,000 |
| − Purchase returns | (40,000) |
| − Cost of goods sold | (1,260,000) |
| **Ending inventory (Dec 31, Year 2)** | **$230,000** |

Check: \(245{,}000 + 1{,}250{,}000 + 35{,}000 - 40{,}000 - 1{,}260{,}000 = 230{,}000\).

**(e) Ratio analysis**  

| Year | Net sales | COGS | Gross profit | GP % | Average inventory | Inventory turnover | Avg days in inventory |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 | $1,800,000 | $1,080,000 | $720,000 | **40.00%** | \((210{,}000 + 245{,}000)/2 = 227{,}500\) | \(1{,}080{,}000 / 227{,}500 = \mathbf{4.75}\) | \(365 / 4.75 = \mathbf{76.84}\) days |
| Year 2 | $2,100,000 | $1,260,000 | $840,000 | **40.00%** | \((245{,}000 + 230{,}000)/2 = 237{,}500\) | \(1{,}260{,}000 / 237{,}500 = \mathbf{5.31}\) | \(365 / 5.31 = \mathbf{68.74}\) days |

Gross profit checks:  
- Year 1: \(1{,}800{,}000 - 1{,}080{,}000 = 720{,}000\); \(720{,}000 / 1{,}800{,}000 = 40.00\%\).  
- Year 2: \(2{,}100{,}000 - 1{,}260{,}000 = 840{,}000\); \(840{,}000 / 2{,}100{,}000 = 40.00\%\).

*(Days use turnover rounded to two decimals, then \(365 \div\) turnover. Unrounded Year 2: \(1{,}260{,}000/237{,}500 = 5.30526\ldots\); \(365/5.30526 \approx 68.80\). Pack standard: two-decimal turnover first.)*

**(f) Interpretation**  
Inventory turnover **increased** (4.75 → 5.31) and average days in inventory **decreased** (76.84 → 68.74). Northfork is turning inventory faster and tying up fewer days of resources in stock relative to COGS—generally a favorable efficiency signal (subject to industry benchmarks and stockout risk). Gross profit percentage remained stable at **40%**, so margin was maintained while inventory activity improved. Users should still compare to peers and assess whether lower ending inventory risks insufficient stock for demand.

**Key insight:** Inventory ratios are only as good as the inventory and COGS amounts that feed them. Perpetual **initial recognition** of purchases (and inventoriable freight), returns, and COGS at sale produce a clean **inventory rollforward**; average inventory and COGS then drive turnover and days. Gross profit % pairs with the activity ratios to separate **margin** from **velocity**.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — LIFO vs FIFO ratios, period-end LIFO conversion JE, comparability disclosure
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Period-end LIFO conversion adjusting JE; inventory method effects on turnover, days, and GP%; LIFO-reserve adjustment for peer comparability; disclosure/presentation of method and reserve  
**Scenario:**  
**Summitline Appliances Inc.** keeps **internal (FIFO)** inventory records and converts to **LIFO** at year-end for external reporting using an **Allowance to Reduce Inventory to LIFO** (LIFO reserve). Inventory acquisition costs **rose** during Year 3. Net sales for Year 3 = **$1,400,000**.

| ($ amounts) | Dec 31, Year 2 | Dec 31, Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory on **FIFO** basis (internal) | $180,000 | $220,000 |
| Inventory on **LIFO** basis (external) | 150,000 | 170,000 |
| LIFO reserve (FIFO − LIFO) | 30,000 | 50,000 |

Year 3 **LIFO** cost of goods sold (after conversion) = **$860,000**.  
A FIFO peer, **Flatiron Home Goods Co.**, reports for Year 3: beginning inventory **$190,000**, ending inventory **$210,000**, COGS **$830,000**, net sales **$1,400,000**. Both companies sell similar product lines.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute the **increase in the LIFO reserve** for Year 3 and prepare the **period-end adjusting journal entry** that converts FIFO books to LIFO (debit COGS; credit allowance).  
(b) Compute Year 3 **FIFO COGS** implied by the LIFO reserve change.  
(c) For Summitline, compute inventory turnover, average days in inventory, gross profit, and gross profit percentage under **both LIFO (as reported)** and **FIFO (as if)**. Round turnover and days to two decimals; GP% to two decimals.  
(d) Compute Flatiron’s Year 3 inventory turnover and average days in inventory.  
(e) Explain why comparing Summitline’s **as-reported LIFO** turnover to Flatiron’s FIFO turnover is misleading, and recompute Summitline’s turnover on a **FIFO-comparable** basis for the peer comparison.  
(f) Identify two **note disclosures / presentation** items Summitline should provide so users can interpret inventory and these ratios.

**Answer key:**

**(a) LIFO reserve change and period-end converting JE**  
Increase in LIFO reserve = \(50{,}000 - 30{,}000 = \mathbf{\$20{,}000}\).

```
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ........................ 20,000
   Cr Allowance to Reduce Inventory to LIFO .......... 20,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 20,000)  
*To increase the LIFO reserve (reduce inventory carrying amount from FIFO to LIFO) and recognize the additional LIFO COGS when prices rose.*

**(b) FIFO COGS**  
\[
\text{FIFO COGS} = \text{LIFO COGS} - \text{increase in LIFO reserve} = 860{,}000 - 20{,}000 = \mathbf{\$840{,}000}
\]

**(c) Summitline ratios — LIFO vs FIFO**  

**LIFO (as reported externally)**  
Average inventory = \((150{,}000 + 170{,}000)/2 = 160{,}000\)  
Inventory turnover = \(860{,}000 / 160{,}000 = \mathbf{5.38}\)  
Average days = \(365 / 5.38 = \mathbf{67.84}\) days  
Gross profit = \(1{,}400{,}000 - 860{,}000 = \mathbf{\$540{,}000}\)  
GP% = \(540{,}000 / 1{,}400{,}000 = \mathbf{38.57\%}\)

**FIFO (as if / internal)**  
Average inventory = \((180{,}000 + 220{,}000)/2 = 200{,}000\)  
Inventory turnover = \(840{,}000 / 200{,}000 = \mathbf{4.20}\)  
Average days = \(365 / 4.20 = \mathbf{86.90}\) days  
Gross profit = \(1{,}400{,}000 - 840{,}000 = \mathbf{\$560{,}000}\)  
GP% = \(560{,}000 / 1{,}400{,}000 = \mathbf{40.00\%}\)

Summary table:

| Basis | COGS | Avg inventory | Turnover | Days | GP % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LIFO (reported) | $860,000 | $160,000 | **5.38** | **67.84** | **38.57%** |
| FIFO (as if) | $840,000 | $200,000 | **4.20** | **86.90** | **40.00%** |

**(d) Flatiron (FIFO peer)**  
Average inventory = \((190{,}000 + 210{,}000)/2 = 200{,}000\)  
Turnover = \(830{,}000 / 200{,}000 = \mathbf{4.15}\)  
Days = \(365 / 4.15 = \mathbf{87.95}\) days  

**(e) Comparability**  
On **as-reported** bases, Summitline’s LIFO turnover (**5.38**) looks far better than Flatiron’s FIFO turnover (**4.15**). That gap is **method-driven**: with rising prices, LIFO reports **higher COGS** and **lower average inventory**, both of which **inflate turnover** (and shrink days) even when physical flow is similar. For a meaningful peer comparison, restate Summitline using the LIFO reserve so both are on FIFO: Summitline FIFO turnover **4.20** vs Flatiron **4.15**—nearly identical velocity. Users should adjust LIFO companies to FIFO (or both to a common basis) **before** ranking inventory efficiency.

**(f) Presentation / disclosure**  
1. **Inventory cost-flow method(s)** in the accounting-policies note (LIFO for external reporting; any FIFO internal use is optional narrative).  
2. **LIFO reserve** (or the amount by which FIFO would exceed LIFO inventory)—so users can restate inventory and COGS for ratio analysis.  
Also: inventory as a **current asset** on the balance sheet at the LIFO carrying amount (FIFO inventory less allowance); major inventory classifications if material.

**Key insight (Demo 9-9 style):** When prices rise, **LIFO vs FIFO changes the ratios without changing physical inventory flow**. Inventory turnover is higher (days lower) and gross profit % is lower under LIFO. Cross-company analysis requires **LIFO-reserve adjustments** for comparability—exactly why the reserve and method must be **disclosed**.

### Q4 — MC — Method effect on inventory turnover (rising prices)
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Classification of cost-flow method effects on inventory turnover when acquisition costs are rising  
**Question:**  
Assuming **rising** inventory purchase prices and identical physical inventory activity, which cost-flow assumption typically produces the **highest inventory turnover** ratio?

- A) FIFO, because ending inventory is lower and COGS is higher.  
- B) LIFO, because COGS is higher and average inventory is lower.  
- C) Specific identification, because it always matches physical flow and maximizes COGS.  
- D) The average-cost method, because it always falls outside the LIFO–FIFO range.

**Answer:** **B.** With rising prices, LIFO assigns higher (recent) costs to COGS and lower (older) costs to ending inventory, so the numerator of turnover rises and the average-inventory denominator falls—**higher turnover** (and lower days). FIFO does the opposite (A is backwards). Specific identification depends on which units are sold, not a fixed “always highest COGS” rule (C). Average cost generally falls **between** LIFO and FIFO, not outside that range (D).

### Q5 — MC — Inventory ratio formulas
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Correct computation of inventory turnover and average days in inventory  
**Question:**  
Westbranch Merchants reports cost of goods sold of **$540,000**, beginning inventory of **$90,000**, and ending inventory of **$110,000**. What are inventory turnover and average days in inventory (use 365 days; round turnover to two decimals and days to the nearest whole day)?

- A) Turnover **5.40**; days **68**  
- B) Turnover **4.91**; days **74**  
- C) Turnover **6.00**; days **61**  
- D) Turnover **5.00**; days **73**

**Answer:** **A.** Average inventory = \((90{,}000 + 110{,}000)/2 = 100{,}000\).  
Turnover = \(540{,}000 / 100{,}000 = \mathbf{5.40}\).  
Days = \(365 / 5.40 = 67.59 \approx \mathbf{68}\) days.  
B uses ending inventory only (\(540{,}000/110{,}000 \approx 4.91\)). C uses beginning only. D incorrectly uses a \(100{,}000\) COGS-style misread or rounds to 5.00 without support.
