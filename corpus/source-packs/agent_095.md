# Agent 95 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-2

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Record customer deposits and advances  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** cash from customers that creates a **current liability** (not revenue) — Liability—Returnable Deposit or Deferred Revenue / Deferred Gift Card Revenue
- **Customer returnable (refundable) deposits:** held until return of property or completion of related obligation; reported current or noncurrent based on expected settlement timing
- **Return / settlement JE:** reverse deposit liability and pay cash when deposit is released
- **Forfeiture JE:** reclassify forfeited deposit liability to **Sales** (revenue); if related inventory is unrecoverable, recognize **COGS** and remove **Inventory**
- **Customer advance payments (contract liability):** Dr Cash / Cr Deferred Revenue; recognize revenue when performance obligation is satisfied
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** straight-line (or pattern-of-performance) roll-forward of deferred revenue over the service period
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** recognize earned portion of advances / gift card breakage at year-end
- **Gift cards:** sale → deferred liability; redemption → revenue; **proportional breakage** when estimable and probable no significant reversal
- **Classification / presentation:** remaining deposit and deferred revenue balances as **current liabilities** (if settlement/performance expected within one year or operating cycle)

### Q1 — CORE — Initial recognition of deposits and advances; revenue schedule; period-end adj; return/forfeiture settlement
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE for returnable deposits and customer advances; subsequent deferred-revenue schedule; period-end adjusting JE; settlement (return) and forfeiture JEs with COGS; classification on the balance sheet  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Events LLC** is a calendar-year company that rents event furniture and also sells multi-month catering-consulting packages.

**Part A — Returnable deposits (June 2025)**  
During June 2025, Cedarline collected **$12,000** cash as returnable deposits from customers renting tables, chairs, and linens (protects against damage and nonreturn).  
By June 30:
- Deposits of **$10,500** were returned in cash (equipment returned in good condition).
- Deposits of **$1,200** were **forfeited** (equipment damaged or not returned). Cedarline recovered **no usable equipment**. The cost of the forfeited equipment is **80%** of the related deposit amount. Policy: beach/event equipment is carried in **Inventory** until a deposit is forfeited.
- The remaining **$300** of deposits is still outstanding at June 30 (one customer’s claim window closes July 2; expected to be returned then).

**Part B — Customer advance for services**  
On **September 1, 2025**, Cedarline collected **$4,800** cash in advance for an **8-month** catering-consulting package (performance evenly over **September 1, 2025 – April 30, 2026**; **$600** earned per month). Fiscal year-end is **December 31**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **June summary entry** to **initially recognize** the collection of customer deposits.  
b. Prepare the June summary entry to **return** deposits of $10,500.  
c. Prepare the June entries to record **forfeiture** of $1,200 deposits (revenue and COGS/inventory).  
d. Prepare the **September 1** entry to **initially recognize** the $4,800 customer advance.  
e. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** of monthly revenue recognition and remaining deferred revenue for the full 8-month package (Sept 2025–Apr 2026).  
f. Prepare the **December 31, 2025 period-end adjusting entry** to recognize revenue earned on the advance from Sept 1 through Dec 31 (may be recorded as one summary entry).  
g. Compute the **June 30** balance of Liability—Returnable Deposit and the **December 31** balance of Deferred Revenue. Classify each on the balance sheet and briefly support the classification.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Deposits, advances, schedule, and year-end adj (twin)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — initial recognition of deposits and advances; return and forfeiture settlement; deferred-revenue schedule; period-end adjusting JE; classification  
**Scenario:**  
**Northshore Marina Inc.** (calendar year) rents dock equipment and sells prepaid winter-storage service plans.

**Part A — Returnable deposits (March 2026)**  
During March, Northshore collected **$9,000** cash deposits on rented pier equipment.  
By March 31:
- **$7,650** of deposits returned in cash (equipment OK).
- **$900** forfeited (equipment not returned / damaged; **no usable equipment** recovered). Equipment cost = **75%** of the forfeited deposit amount. Equipment is in Inventory until forfeiture.
- **$450** of deposits remain outstanding (to be settled in early April).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Gift cards (proportional breakage) and advance applied at delivery (settlement)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Gift card initial recognition, redemption (settlement of liability), period-end proportional breakage JE; advance on goods sale applied at delivery; classification of remaining contract liabilities  
**Scenario:**  

**Part A — Gift cards — GlowBox Retail Corp.** (calendar year)  
During Year 1, GlowBox sold **$10,000** of nonrefundable gift cards for cash. Customers redeemed **$6,300** of gift cards for store purchases during Year 1 (ignore COGS).  
GlowBox estimates **10%** of gift card sales will break (never be redeemed) and **meets the requirements** for the **proportional method**. It is probable that a significant reversal of recognized breakage will not occur.

**Part B — Customer advance on goods — Trailform Equipment Co.**  
On **March 28, Year 1**, Trailform received a **$50,000** cash advance on a special-order machine. On **May 1, Year 1**, Trailform delivered the machine (control transferred). Total sales price is **$200,000**; the advance is applied to the invoice and the remaining **$150,000** is due on account. Ignore COGS.

**Required:**  
a. Record the Year 1 **sale** of gift cards (initial recognition).  
b. Record Year 1 **redemptions** of gift cards.  
c. Compute and record the **December 31, Year 1 adjusting entry** for gift card **breakage** under the proportional method. Show the schedule of expected redemptions, redemption rate, and breakage recognized.  
d. Compute the **ending Deferred Gift Card Revenue** balance and classify it.  
e. Record Trailform’s **March 28** advance collection and **May 1** delivery/settlement entries.  
f. Briefly contrast a **returnable deposit** liability with **deferred revenue** from an advance or gift card (classification/presentation concept).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1 — sale of gift cards (initial recognition)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 10,000 | |
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | | 10,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,000. Balanced.

**b. Year 1 — redemption (settlement of gift card liability)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 6,300 | |
| Sales | | 6,300 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 6,300. Balanced.

**c. Proportional breakage — computation and period-end JE**

| Schedule item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Gift card sales | $10,000 |
| Estimated breakage rate | 10% |
| **Total estimated breakage** | **$1,000** |
| **Total expected redemptions** (90% × $10,000) | **$9,000** |
| Actual redemptions Year 1 | $6,300 |
| **Redemption rate** = $6,300 ÷ $9,000 | **70%** |
| **Breakage revenue to recognize** = $1,000 × 70% | **$700** |

*December 31, Year 1 — proportional gift card breakage*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 700 | |
| Gift Card Breakage Revenue | | 700 |
| *Breakage recognized in proportion to rights exercised* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 700. Balanced.

**d. Ending balance and classification**  
Deferred Gift Card Revenue ending = $10,000 − $6,300 − $700 = **$3,000**  
→ reported as a **current liability** (contract liability for expected remaining redemptions of $9,000 − $6,300 = $2,700 plus residual timing of the yet-unrecognized portion of estimated breakage of $1,000 − $700 = $300; total liability still **$3,000**).

**e. Trailform — advance and settlement at delivery**

*March 28 — initial recognition of customer advance*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 50,000 | |
| Deferred Revenue | | 50,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 50,000. Balanced.  
(At March 31, $50,000 Deferred Revenue is a **current liability** until delivery.)

*May 1 — delivery / settlement (performance satisfied; advance applied)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash (or Accounts Receivable) | 150,000 | |
| Deferred Revenue | 50,000 | |
| Sales | | 200,000 |
| *Derecognize contract liability; recognize full sales price* | | |

**Check:** Dr 150,000 + 50,000 = **200,000**; Cr = **200,000**. Balanced.

**f. Classification / presentation contrast**  
- **Returnable deposit:** liability to **refund cash** (or recognize revenue only if forfeited). Not deferred revenue from a sale of goods/services.  
- **Deferred revenue / gift card liability:** liability to **transfer goods or services** (or recognize breakage). Settled by performance/redemption, not typically by refunding the original cash (nonrefundable cards).  
Both are usually **current liabilities** when settlement or performance is expected within one year (or the operating cycle).

**Key insight:** Gift cards follow the same “cash → contract liability → revenue” pattern as other advances, with an extra **breakage** measurement under the **proportional method** (breakage × redemption rate). An advance on a **goods** sale is settled at delivery by **debiting Deferred Revenue** and recognizing **Sales** for the full contract price.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Classification of initial credit when cash is received from a customer before performance or property return  

**Question:** On August 10, Riverglen Rentals collected **$1,500** cash deposit **plus** a **$2,400** rental fee from a customer that rented banquet equipment. The deposit will be returned if equipment is returned undamaged. How should Riverglen **initially** record the **$1,500** deposit portion only?

- A) Debit Cash $1,500; credit Rental Revenue $1,500  
- B) Debit Cash $1,500; credit Liability—Returnable Deposit $1,500  
- C) Debit Cash $1,500; credit Deferred Revenue $1,500 (to be earned ratably over the rental period)  
- D) Debit Accounts Receivable $1,500; credit Liability—Returnable Deposit $1,500  

**Answer:** **B.** A returnable (refundable) deposit is a **liability to return cash** if the customer performs (returns undamaged property). It is **not** rental revenue at collection (A) and is **not** deferred service revenue that will automatically convert to revenue over time (C)—revenue arises only if the deposit is **forfeited**. Cash was received, so A/R is incorrect (D). (Separately, the $2,400 fee would be revenue or deferred revenue depending on whether the rental performance has already been satisfied.)

### Q4b — MC (proportional breakage measurement)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Classification/method — computing proportional gift card breakage revenue  

**Question:** In its first year, Summit Perk sold **$8,000** of gift cards, of which **$4,800** were redeemed. Summit estimates **20%** breakage and uses the proportional method. What amount of **Gift Card Breakage Revenue** should Summit recognize at year-end?

- A) $1,600  
- B) $640  
- C) $1,200  
- D) $3,200  

**Answer:** **C. $1,200.**  
Expected redemptions = 80% × $8,000 = **$6,400**; estimated breakage = **$1,600**.  
Redemption rate = $4,800 ÷ $6,400 = **75%**.  
Breakage revenue = $1,600 × 75% = **$1,200**.  
(A is total estimated breakage before applying the rate; B is 40% × $1,600 incorrectly; D is remaining unredeemed cards.)
