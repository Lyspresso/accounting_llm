# Agent 265 — CORE DEMO — LO 14-2

**Chapter:** 14  
**LO title:** Account for debt securities measured at FV-NI with fair value adjustments at sale date and period-end  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** trading debt securities purchased at par (including between interest dates with Interest Receivable)
- **Interest revenue:** full coupon cash received; reverse accrued Interest Receivable; recognize only interest earned while held
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** Fair Value Adjustment (FVA) true-up so carrying amount = fair value; unrealized holding G/L in **net income** (FV-NI), not OCI
- **FVA worksheet / subsequent measurement schedule:** amortized cost vs fair value; existing FVA; amount of increase (decrease) to FVA needed
- **Multi-period period-end FVA:** when an FVA balance already exists, adjust only the **change** required to reach the new required FVA
- **Discount subsequent measurement:** effective-interest amortization updates amortized cost **before** computing period-end FVA
- **Disposal (sale) under FV-NI:** (1) adjust to fair value at sale date through NI; (2) record cash, eliminate investment at amortized cost, clear associated FVA—no freestanding “realized G/L” if already at exit FV
- **Fair value option:** otherwise HTM debt measured at FV-NI with same period-end FVA mechanics as TS
- **Classification / presentation:** TS typically **current** assets; unrealized holding G/L in **other income (NI)**

### Q1 — CORE — TS purchase between interest dates, period-end FVA (emphasis), presentation, and sale-date FV-NI
**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Period-end Fair Value Adjustment (FV-NI) for trading debt securities—FVA worksheet from amortized cost to year-end fair value; initial purchase between interest dates; sale-date remeasurement and FVA elimination  
**Scenario:**  
On **October 1, Year 1**, **Valebrook Treasury Partners** purchased **at par** **$80,000** face of **Ironclad Manufacturing** **9%** bonds (bonds dated **July 1, Year 1**; mature **June 30, Year 8**). Cash interest is paid **semiannually on June 30 and December 31**. Valebrook classifies the investment as **trading securities (TS)** and measures it at **FV-NI**. Valebrook’s accounting period ends **December 31**. Brokerage fees are zero.

Additional facts:
- Fair value of the Ironclad bonds on **December 31, Year 1** is **$77,200**.
- On **February 10, Year 2**, Valebrook sells the entire holding for **$77,900** cash (treat the quoted sale price as the clean exit fair value for the investment account—no additional accrued-interest complexity beyond the post–December 31 coupon cycle).

**Required:**  
a. Record the **October 1, Year 1** purchase entry (include accrued interest).  
b. Record receipt of cash interest on **December 31, Year 1**.  
c. Prepare the **period-end FVA worksheet** and record the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting** journal entry to measure the TS at fair value through net income (**emphasis**).  
d. Show the **December 31, Year 1** balance-sheet carrying amount and Year 1 income-statement effects (interest revenue and unrealized holding G/L).  
e. Record the **February 10, Year 2** sale: first adjust to fair value using FV-NI, then record the sale and eliminate the associated FVA. Reconcile total G/L recognized over the holding period to (proceeds − amortized cost).

**Answer key:**  

**a. October 1, Year 1 — Purchase between interest dates**  
Accrued interest from July 1 to October 1 = 3 months:  
\(80{,}000 \times 0.09 \times 3/12 = \$1{,}800\)  
Cash paid = \(80{,}000 + 1{,}800 = \$81{,}800\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in TS—Ironclad Bonds | 80,000 | |
| Interest Receivable | 1,800 | |
| Cash | | 81,800 |

**Check:** Dr 81,800 = Cr 81,800. **Balanced.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Receipt of semiannual cash interest**  
Cash (full 6 months) = \(80{,}000 \times 0.09 \times 6/12 = \$3{,}600\)  
Interest revenue earned since purchase (3 months, Oct–Dec) = \(80{,}000 \times 0.09 \times 3/12 = \$1{,}800\)  
Reverse Interest Receivable \(1{,}800\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 3,600 | |
| Interest Receivable | | 1,800 |
| Interest Revenue | | 1,800 |

**Check:** Dr 3,600 = Cr 3,600. **Balanced.**

**c. December 31, Year 1 — Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis): FV-NI FVA worksheet**

| TS Investment | Amortized cost | Fair value YE | Unrealized holding G/(L) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ironclad bonds | $80,000 | $77,200 | $(2,800)$ |
| Existing FVA account balance | | | 0 |
| **Amount of increase (decrease) to FVA needed** | | | **$(2,800)$** |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income | 2,800 | |
| Fair Value Adjustment—TS | | 2,800 |

**Check:** Dr 2,800 = Cr 2,800. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount = amortized cost − credit FVA = \(80{,}000 - 2{,}800 = \mathbf{\$77{,}200}\) = year-end fair value.

**d. Year 1 financial statement presentation**

**Balance sheet (December 31, Year 1) — Current assets**  
Investment in trading securities …… **$77,200**  
*(Investment in TS $80,000 less Fair Value Adjustment—TS credit $2,800)*

**Income statement (year ended December 31, Year 1) — Other revenues (expenses)**  
Interest revenue ……………………………… **$1,800**  
Unrealized holding loss on trading securities … **$(2,800)**

**e. February 10, Year 2 — Sale (adjust to FV at sale date, then eliminate FVA)**

Required FVA at sale date\* = \(77{,}900 - 80{,}000 = \$(2{,}100)\) (credit)  
Less existing FVA account balance = \((2{,}800)\) credit  
**Amount of increase (decrease) to FVA needed** = **$700** (debit FVA / credit Unrealized G/L—Income)

\*$77,900 fair value − $80,000 amortized cost.

*Adjust investment to fair value at sale date (FV-NI):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fair Value Adjustment—TS | 700 | |
| Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income | | 700 |

**Check:** Dr 700 = Cr 700. **Balanced.** FVA now credit $2,100; carrying amount = \(80{,}000 - 2{,}100 = \$77{,}900\).

*Record sale and eliminate associated FVA (no separate realized G/L—already in earnings via FV-NI):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 77,900 | |
| Fair Value Adjustment—TS | 2,100 | |
| Investment in TS—Ironclad Bonds | | 80,000 |

**Check:** Dr 80,000 = Cr 80,000. **Balanced.**

**Holding-period G/L reconciliation:**  
Unrealized loss in Year 1 income …… $(2,800)  
Unrealized gain in Year 2 income …… 700  
**Net loss over ownership period** …… **$(2,100)**  
= Cost $80,000 − proceeds $77,900. ✓

**Key insight:** The **period-end adjusting JE** is the core of FV-NI: compute required FVA = fair value − amortized cost, then adjust from the **existing** FVA balance so the investment reports at fair value and the change hits **net income**. Sale-date remeasurement uses the same worksheet logic before clearing FVA against cash and amortized cost.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — TS between interest dates, period-end FVA, sale

**Concept:** Number-variant twin: period-end FV-NI adjusting JE for trading debt (FVA worksheet); purchase between interest dates; sale-date adjust and FVA elimination  
**Scenario:**  
On **November 1, Year 1**, **Silverline Capital Group** purchased **at par** **$120,000** face of **Redwood Transit** **6%** bonds (dated **July 1, Year 1**; mature **June 30, Year 9**). Cash interest is paid **semiannually on June 30 and December 31**. Classified as **TS (FV-NI)**. Year-end is **December 31**. Brokerage fees are zero.

- Fair value on **December 31, Year 1** is **$123,600**.  
- On **January 20, Year 2**, Silverline sells the bonds for **$122,400** cash (clean exit price for the investment account).

**Required:**  
a. Record the **November 1, Year 1** purchase (include accrued interest).  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 1** interest receipt.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Discount TS: effective-interest schedule, multi-year period-end FVA, disposal
**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule (effective-interest amortized cost) plus multi-period period-end FVA adjusting JEs (existing FVA balance) and sale under FV-NI  
**Scenario:**  
On **January 1, Year 1**, **Pinehaven Investments LLC** purchases **$75,000** face of **Blueharbor Shipping** **6%** bonds for **$70,030.63**. The bonds pay **cash interest annually each December 31** and mature on **December 31, Year 4** (four years remaining). The market (effective) rate on the purchase date is **8%**. Pinehaven classifies the bonds as **trading securities** and uses the **effective interest method**. Year-end is December 31.

Additional facts:
- Fair value on **December 31, Year 1** is **$72,000**.  
- Fair value on **December 31, Year 2** is **$71,500**.  
- On **January 1, Year 3**, Pinehaven sells the entire investment for **$71,800** cash.

**Required:**  
a. Verify (show computation) that the purchase price is the present value of the bonds at 8%. Record the January 1, Year 1 purchase.  
b. Prepare the **Years 1–2 rows** of the amortization (subsequent measurement) schedule and record the December 31, Year 1 and December 31, Year 2 interest entries.  
c. Record the **December 31, Year 1 period-end FVA adjusting** entry (worksheet using **amortized cost after amortization**).  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 2 period-end FVA adjusting** entry, explicitly rolling from the **existing** Year 1 FVA balance (**emphasis**—multi-period period-end adjust).  
e. Record the January 1, Year 3 sale (FV-NI adjust, then sale with FVA elimination). Reconcile cumulative unrealized G/L to (proceeds − amortized cost at sale).  
f. Compute pretax net income effects from this investment in Year 1 and Year 2 (interest + unrealized G/L only).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Purchase price verification and entry**  
Annual cash interest = \(75{,}000 \times 0.06 = \$4{,}500\)  
\[
PV = 75{,}000 \times (1.08)^{-4} + 4{,}500 \times \frac{1-(1.08)^{-4}}{0.08}
\]  
\((1.08)^4 = 1.36048896\)  
\(PV_{\text{principal}} = 75{,}000 / 1.36048896 = 55{,}126.06\)  
\(PVAF(8\%,4) = [1 - (1.08)^{-4}] / 0.08 = 3.312127\)  
\(PV_{\text{interest}} = 4{,}500 \times 3.312127 = 14{,}904.57\)  
**Combined PV = $70,030.63** ✓

### Q4 — CORE — Fair value option period-end adjusting JE and classification/presentation
**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Period-end FV-NI adjusting JE under the fair value option for otherwise HTM debt; classification of unrealized G/L in net income; contrast with pure amortized-cost HTM  
**Scenario:**  
On **July 1, Year 1**, **Stonewell Holdings** purchases **at par** **$150,000** face of **Prairie Wind Energy** **5%** bonds that mature on **June 30, Year 7**. Cash interest is paid **semiannually on June 30 and December 31**. Management has **positive intent and ability** to hold the bonds to maturity and therefore **classifies** them as **held-to-maturity (HTM)**. On the **acquisition date**, Stonewell **irrevocably elects the fair value option** for this investment under ASC 825 (subsequent measurement is **FV-NI**, same mechanics as trading securities). Interest for the second half of Year 1 has already been recorded properly.

On **December 31, Year 1**, fair value of the Prairie Wind bonds is **$146,250**. Beginning FVA related to this investment is zero.

**Required:**  
a. Record the July 1, Year 1 purchase.  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting** entry under the fair value option (**emphasis**).  
c. State the **carrying amount** on the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet and whether the unrealized holding loss is reported in **net income** or **OCI**.  
d. Briefly state **two** disclosure/election points management should address for the fair value option.  
e. **Contrast:** If Stonewell had **not** elected the fair value option and kept pure HTM amortized-cost accounting, what year-end entry (if any) would be made for the $3,750 unrealized decline?

**Answer key:**  

**a. July 1, Year 1 — Purchase at par**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Debt Securities—Prairie Wind (HTM, FVO) | 150,000 | |
| Cash | | 150,000 |

**Check:** Dr 150,000 = Cr 150,000. **Balanced.**

**b. December 31, Year 1 — Period-end adjusting JE (fair value option / FV-NI)**  
Unrealized holding loss = \(150{,}000 - 146{,}250 = \$3{,}750\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income | 3,750 | |
| Fair Value Adjustment—Fair Value Option | | 3,750 |

**Check:** Dr 3,750 = Cr 3,750. **Balanced.**

**c. Presentation**  
- **Carrying amount:** **$146,250** (amortized cost $150,000 − FVA credit $3,750).  
- Unrealized holding loss of **$3,750** is recognized in **net income** (other income/expense), **not** OCI—because the fair value option produces **FV-NI** measurement (same income treatment as trading securities).

**d. Disclosure / election points (examples)**  
1. Management **elected the fair value option** for this specific debt investment **on the acquisition date**; the election is **generally irrevocable** and applied **instrument-by-instrument**.  
2. The investment is subsequently measured at **fair value with changes in fair value recognized in earnings**; disclose reasons for the election and how fair value is determined (as applicable).

**e. Contrast without FVO (pure HTM)**  
**No year-end fair value entry.** HTM debt remains at amortized cost; unrealized fair value changes are **not** recognized in the accounts (footnote fair-value disclosure only). No FVA; no unrealized G/L in NI or OCI.

**Key insight:** The fair value option is **not** a “second coat” on TS (already FV-NI), but it **is** the LO 14-2 path that puts otherwise amortized-cost HTM debt onto **FV-NI**. The **period-end adjusting JE** mirrors TS: credit FVA for holding losses, debit Unrealized G/L—**Income**.

### Q5 — MC — Period-end FVA adjustment amount given existing balance
**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Computing the period-end FV-NI adjusting entry when an existing FVA balance is present  
**Question:** **Northfork Asset Desk** holds a single trading debt security with **amortized cost $100,000** at year-end. The related **Fair Value Adjustment—TS** account has a **debit balance of $2,000** from the prior year-end. Year-end fair value is **$97,000**. Northfork adjusts trading securities to fair value through net income at each period-end. Which **period-end adjusting entry** is correct?

- A) Debit Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income $3,000; credit Fair Value Adjustment—TS $3,000.  
- B) Debit Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income $5,000; credit Fair Value Adjustment—TS $5,000.  
- C) Debit Fair Value Adjustment—TS $3,000; credit Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income $3,000.  
- D) Debit Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income $2,000; credit Fair Value Adjustment—TS $2,000 (only reverse the prior-year FVA).  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Required FVA at year-end = \(97{,}000 - 100{,}000 = \$(3{,}000)\) **credit**.  
Existing FVA = **$2,000 debit**.  
Decrease to FVA needed = \(-3{,}000 - (+2{,}000) = \$(5{,}000)\):  
Dr Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income **$5,000**; Cr Fair Value Adjustment—TS **$5,000**.  
After the entry, FVA is a $3,000 credit and carrying amount = $97,000.  
Option A records only the **required** FVA as if existing FVA were zero. C records a gain. D only clears the old debit and leaves carrying amount wrong ($100,000 − $0 FVA after reverse of $2,000 debit without building the $3,000 credit, or incomplete true-up).

### Q6 — MC — Classification of unrealized holding G/L (TS / FV-NI vs AFS)
**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Classification—where period-end unrealized holding gains/losses on FV-NI debt securities are reported  
**Question:** **Granite Peak Fund** holds two debt portfolios at year-end (no sales during the year):  
- Portfolio T: debt securities classified as **trading** (measured at **FV-NI**).  
- Portfolio A: debt securities classified as **available-for-sale** (measured at **FV-OCI**).  

During Year 1, Portfolio T’s fair value rose **$12,000** above amortized cost. Portfolio A’s fair value rose **$12,000** above amortized cost. Ignoring interest and taxes, which statement is **correct**?

- A) Both portfolios report the $12,000 unrealized gain in **net income**.  
- B) Portfolio T reports $12,000 in **net income**; Portfolio A reports $12,000 in **other comprehensive income**.  
- C) Portfolio T reports $12,000 in **OCI**; Portfolio A reports $12,000 in **net income**.  
- D) Neither portfolio recognizes unrealized gains until sale.  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Trading / FV-NI debt (LO 14-2) recognizes unrealized holding gains and losses **in earnings** each period via the period-end FVA adjusting entry. Available-for-sale debt (LO 14-3) recognizes unrealized holding gains and losses **in OCI** until realized (subject to impairment rules). Option A confuses AFS with TS; C reverses the categories; D describes neither US GAAP model for TS/AFS subsequent measurement.
