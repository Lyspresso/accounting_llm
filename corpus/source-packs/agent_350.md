# Agent 350 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-3

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for impairment and derecognition of finite life intangible assets  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** capitalize finite-life intangible (patent, customer list, franchise) at cash-equivalent cost
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** straight-line amortization over useful life (≤ legal life); residual typically zero; roll-forward of carrying amount
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** year-end amortization (Dr Amortization Expense / Cr intangible); when an impairment indicator is present, **after** updating amortization, perform recoverability then measurement and record impairment AJ
- **Two-step impairment (ASC 360-10 / ASC 350-30):** (1) recoverability — undiscounted future net cash inflows vs carrying amount; (2) if not recoverable, loss = carrying amount − fair value; new cost basis; **subsequent reversal prohibited**
- **Post-impairment amortization:** amortize revised carrying amount over **remaining** useful life
- **Derecognition / disposal JE:** amortize **to date of disposal**, remove carrying amount, gain/loss = net proceeds − adjusted carrying amount (income from continuing operations)
- **No-impairment path:** if undiscounted CF ≥ carrying amount, stop — no write-down even if FV is lower
- **Number-variant twin:** same skill path with all companies and amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Patent: initial recognition, multi-year schedule, **period-end amort + impairment AJs** (emphasis), post-impairment amort
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of finite-life patent; subsequent measurement schedule; **period-end adjusting JEs** for amortization and two-step impairment; post-impairment amortization on new cost basis  
**Scenario:**  
**Ridgeway Process Systems LLC** (calendar-year reporter) acquires exclusive rights to a manufacturing-process **patent**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Purchase date | **January 1, Year 1** |
| Cash purchase price (includes transfer/legal fees to ready for use) | **$72,000** |
| Estimated useful life (also remaining legal life) | **6 years**, straight-line, **zero** residual |
| Year-end | December 31 |
| Amortization / impairment policy | Credit **Patent** directly (no separate Accumulated Amortization) |
| **Impairment indicator (Year 2):** competitor releases a superior substitute | Present before year-end Year 2 |
| Estimated **undiscounted** future net cash inflows from the patent (Dec 31, Year 2) | **$40,000** |
| **Fair value** of the patent (PV of expected cash flows) at Dec 31, Year 2 | **$27,000** |
| Remaining useful life after any impairment write-down | **4 years** (through Dec 31, Year 6) |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1, Year 1 initial recognition** journal entry.  
b. Compute annual pre-impairment amortization. Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–2 (before impairment).  
c. **(Emphasis)** Record the **December 31, Year 1** period-end amortization adjusting entry.  
d. **(Emphasis)** Record the **December 31, Year 2** period-end amortization adjusting entry (update carrying amount **before** impairment testing).  
e. On **December 31, Year 2**, after Year-2 amortization, perform the **recoverability test** and, if required, the **impairment test**. Record any **period-end impairment adjusting entry**.  
f. Compute **Year 3** amortization on the new basis. **(Emphasis)** Record the **December 31, Year 3** period-end amortization adjusting entry.  
g. State the patent’s **carrying amount** on December 31, Year 2 (after impairment) and December 31, Year 3. Note income-statement classification of the impairment loss.

**a. January 1, Year 1 — initial recognition**

**d. December 31, Year 2 — period-end amortization AJ before impairment (emphasis)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 12,000 | |
| Patent | | 12,000 |
| *Update amortization before impairment testing* | | |

**Check:** Dr 12,000 = Cr 12,000. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount after this entry = **$48,000**.

**e. Impairment analysis and period-end impairment AJ — December 31, Year 2**

*Indicator:* competitor’s superior product → perform recoverability test.

*Step 1 — Recoverability test:*  
Undiscounted future net cash inflows **$40,000** **<** carrying amount **$48,000** → carrying amount **not recoverable** → proceed to impairment measurement.

*Step 2 — Impairment test (measurement):*  
Impairment loss = carrying amount − fair value = $48,000 − $27,000 = **$21,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Impairment Loss | 21,000 | |
| Patent | | 21,000 |
| *Write patent down to fair value; new cost basis $27,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr 21,000 = Cr 21,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Post-impairment amortization — Year 3 period-end AJ (emphasis)**

New annual amortization = $27,000 / 4 remaining years = **$6,750**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 6,750 | |
| Patent | | 6,750 |
| *Amortize new cost basis over remaining 4-year life* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,750 = Cr 6,750. **Balanced.**

**g. Balance-sheet carrying amounts and classification**  
- **December 31, Year 2** (after impairment): Patent **$27,000**  
- **December 31, Year 3**: $27,000 − $6,750 = **$20,250**  
- Impairment loss is reported in **income from continuing operations** (not as a prior-period restatement).

**Key insight:** At period-end, **first** complete amortization, **then** test for impairment when an indicator exists. Recoverability uses **undiscounted** cash flows; the loss is measured using **fair value**. The written-down amount is a **new cost basis** amortized over the remaining useful life; recovery is prohibited.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Two assets at period-end (impair vs no impair) + mid-year **derecognition**
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Period-end impairment testing on two finite-life intangibles (one fails recoverability, one passes); amortize to disposal date and record sale gain/loss  
**Scenario:**  
**Northshore Packaging Co.** (calendar year-end) holds two finite-life intangibles acquired for cash on **January 1, Year 1**. Amortization is straight-line, residual **$0**, credited directly to each asset account.

| Asset | Cost | Useful life | Annual amortization |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Patent Alpha** | $60,000 | 5 years | $12,000 |
| **Franchise Bravo** | $90,000 | 10 years | $9,000 |

At **December 31, Year 2**, after recording Year 2 amortization, indicators of possible impairment exist for **both** assets. Management estimates:

| Asset | Undiscounted future net cash inflows | Fair value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent Alpha | **$42,000** | $28,000 |
| Franchise Bravo | **$50,000** | $48,000 |

**Additional disposal facts (Year 3):**  
On **April 1, Year 3**, Northshore sells **Patent Alpha** (after any Year-2 impairment and after amortizing through the sale date) for **$22,000 cash**.

**Required:**  
a. Compute the **carrying amount of each asset on December 31, Year 2 after amortization but before impairment testing**.  
b. For each asset, perform the recoverability test and, if required, the impairment test. Compute any impairment loss.  
c. **(Emphasis)** Record **all December 31, Year 2 period-end adjusting entries**: amortization for each asset and any impairment entries. Prove each balances.  
d. For **Franchise Bravo only**, state the new annual amortization for Year 3 (if impaired) or confirm the original amount (if not).  
e. Record **April 1, Year 3** entries for Patent Alpha: (1) amortization to the disposal date; (2) **derecognition / sale** JE. Compute gain or loss.

**Answer key:**

**a. Carrying amounts Dec 31, Year 2 after amortization (pre-impairment)**

| Asset | Cost | Amort. Y1–Y2 | CA before impairment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Patent Alpha | $60,000 | $12,000 × 2 = $24,000 | **$36,000** |
| Franchise Bravo | 90,000 | $9,000 × 2 = $18,000 | **$72,000** |

**b. Impairment analysis**

**Patent Alpha**  
Recoverability: $42,000 **≥** $36,000 → **recoverable** → **stop**.  
Impairment loss = **$0** (even though FV $28,000 < CA $36,000 — recoverability is not failed, so no write-down).

**Franchise Bravo**

**c. December 31, Year 2 period-end AJs (emphasis)**

*(1) Amortize Patent Alpha*

| Patent Alpha | | 12,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 9,000 | |
| Franchise Bravo | | 9,000 |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.**

*(3) Impairment — Franchise Bravo only*

|---|---:|---:|

CA at sale date = $36,000 − $3,000 = **$33,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 22,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Patent | 11,000 | |
| Patent Alpha | | 33,000 |
| *Remove CA; recognize loss vs cash proceeds* | | |

**Key insight:** Recoverability is a **gate**: if undiscounted CF ≥ CA, **no impairment** even when FV is lower. On disposal, **always amortize first** through the disposal date, then remove the updated carrying amount and recognize gain or loss.

### Q4 — MC — Recoverability vs measurement amounts
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Distinguish recoverability test (undiscounted cash flows) from impairment measurement (fair value)  
**Question:**  
On December 31, after recording amortization, **LumenBridge Softworks** holds a finite-life patent with carrying amount **$55,000**. An impairment indicator is present. Estimated undiscounted future net cash inflows are **$50,000**. Fair value is **$38,000**. What amount of impairment loss should LumenBridge recognize?

- A) $0  
- B) $5,000  
- C) $17,000  
- D) $12,000  

**Answer:** **C.**  
Recoverability fails because $50,000 < $55,000, so the company proceeds to measurement. Impairment loss = CA − FV = $55,000 − $38,000 = **$17,000**.  
- A is wrong (would apply only if recoverability passed).  
- B is the difference between CA and undiscounted CF (not the measurement amount).  
- D is the difference between undiscounted CF and FV (not used).

### Q5 — MC — New cost basis; subsequent recovery prohibited
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** After impairment, adjusted carrying amount is the new accounting basis; subsequent reversal of impairment is prohibited  
**Question:**  
In Year 2, **CedarPeak Instruments** recognized a **$14,000** impairment loss on a finite-life customer list, writing the asset down to fair value of **$42,000**. In Year 3, before amortization, fair value recovers to **$50,000**. Under U.S. GAAP, what amount of impairment **recovery** (gain) may CedarPeak recognize in Year 3?

- A) $14,000  
- B) $8,000  
- C) $0  
- D) $42,000  

**Answer:** **C.**  
After an impairment loss is recognized, the adjusted carrying amount is the **new cost basis**. **Subsequent reversal of a previously recognized impairment loss is prohibited** (ASC 350-30-35-14 / ASC 360-10). CedarPeak continues to amortize the $42,000 basis (less residual) over the remaining useful life; it does not write the asset back up.

### QL — Long multi-period life cycle: acquire → amortize → impair → amortize → dispose
**LO:** LO 13-3  
**Concept:** Full finite-life intangible life cycle with period-end AJs, impairment, revised schedule, and mid-year derecognition  
**Scenario:**  
**Summitvale Analytics Corp.** purchases a **developed technology** intangible for **$80,000 cash** on **January 1, Year 1**. Useful life **8 years**, residual **$0**, SL amortization credited directly to the asset. Calendar year-end.

| Event | Details |
|---|---|
| Dec 31, Year 1–2 | Normal amortization only (no indicators) |
| Dec 31, Year 3 | Indicator present. Undiscounted CF = **$44,000**; FV = **$35,000**. Remaining life after write-down = **5 years** |
| June 30, Year 5 | Sells the technology for **$18,000 cash** |

**Required:**  
a. Subsequent measurement schedule Years 1–3 pre-impairment; Dec 31, Year 3 amortization AJ and impairment AJ.  
b. Post-impairment schedule for Years 4–5 through June 30, Year 5 (partial year).  
c. June 30, Year 5 amortization-to-date and disposal JEs.  
d. Prove total amortization + impairment + CA removed at disposal reconcile to original cost.

**Answer key:**

**a. Pre-impairment and Year 3 period-end AJs**  
Annual amort pre-impairment = $80,000 / 8 = **$10,000**

| Year | Beg. CA | Amort. | End CA pre-impair |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $80,000 | $10,000 | $70,000 |
| 2 | 70,000 | 10,000 | 60,000 |
| 3 | 60,000 | 10,000 | **$50,000** |

*Dec 31, Year 3 — amortization AJ*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 10,000 | |
| Developed Technology | | 10,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,000. **Balanced.**

Recoverability: $44,000 < $50,000 → fail.  
Impairment = $50,000 − $35,000 = **$15,000**.

*Dec 31, Year 3 — impairment AJ*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Impairment Loss | 15,000 | |
| Developed Technology | | 15,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 15,000. **Balanced.** New basis = **$35,000**.

**b. Post-impairment**  
Annual amort = $35,000 / 5 = **$7,000**

| Period | Beg. CA | Amort. | End CA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 4 (full) | $35,000 | $7,000 | $28,000 |
| Year 5 (Jan 1–Jun 30 = 6/12) | 28,000 | **$3,500** | **$24,500** |

**c. June 30, Year 5 — amortize then dispose**

*(1) Amortization to disposal date*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 3,500 | |
| Developed Technology | | 3,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 3,500. **Balanced.**

*(2) Sale*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 18,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Intangible | 6,500 | |
| Developed Technology | | 24,500 |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 + 6,500 = 24,500 = Cr. **Balanced.**  
Loss = $24,500 − $18,000 = **$6,500**.

**d. Reconciliation to original cost**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Amortization Years 1–3 | $30,000 |
| Impairment Year 3 | 15,000 |
| Amortization Year 4 | 7,000 |
| Amortization Year 5 (½) | 3,500 |
| CA removed at sale | 24,500 |
| **Total** | **$80,000** |

**Check:** $30,000 + $15,000 + $7,000 + $3,500 + $24,500 = **$80,000** = original cost. ✓

**Key insight:** Over the full life cycle, every dollar of original cost is either **amortized**, **impaired**, or **derecognized** (via gain/loss on disposal). Period-end AJs keep the carrying amount current before each test or sale.
