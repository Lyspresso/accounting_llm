# Agent 295 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-1

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Identify operating, investing, and financing activities, and the statement of cash flows format  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Classify cash inflows/outflows as **operating**, **investing**, or **financing** (ASC 230 / U.S. GAAP)
- **Initial recognition JEs** for cash events that create SCF lines (stock issue, bond issue, PPE purchase, asset disposal, debt principal settlement)
- **Subsequent cash-section schedule:** net O + net I + net F = change in cash; roll beginning cash to ending cash
- **Period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis):** interest accrual and dividend declaration that affect the books but produce **no cash** on the SCF; contrast with cash interest paid and cash dividends paid
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JEs:** PPE sale (investing inflow = **gross proceeds**); bond principal at maturity (financing outflow); finance-lease principal (financing)
- **Classification / presentation / disclosure:** SCF order O → I → F; **gross** investing and financing lines; “cash” = cash + cash equivalents + restricted cash; noncash investing/financing supplemental disclosure
- Edge traps: interest paid (O) vs principal (F); dividends received (O) vs dividends paid (F); trading securities (O) vs nontrading investments (I); trade notes (O) vs nontrade loans (I)
- **Number-variant twin:** same skills with all amounts and entity names changed

**“Cash” on the SCF** includes **cash + cash equivalents + restricted cash**. Investing and financing **inflows and outflows are reported separately** (gross), not netted.

**f. Interest paid vs accrual; dividends paid vs declaration**

Under U.S. GAAP, **interest paid** is an **operating** cash outflow because interest expense enters the determination of net income and ASC 230 classifies interest as operating. The **year-end interest accrual** records expense and a payable but involves **no cash**, so it does not appear as a cash line on the SCF (cash is reported when paid). **Dividends paid** are **financing** outflows (return of resources to owners). A **year-end dividend declaration** reduces retained earnings and creates a liability but is a **noncash** event until settlement; the financing outflow is recognized when cash is disbursed.

**Key insight:** Every **cash** movement lands in exactly one of O, I, or F. Period-end **adjusting** entries that do not move cash never appear on the face of the SCF. The three section totals must explain the **entire** change in cash (including cash equivalents and restricted cash). Investing and financing lines are shown **gross**.

**Financing:**  
Inflow: bank note $50,000  
Outflows: bond principal $80,000 + lease principal $12,000 + dividends paid $7,500 = **$99,500**  
Net cash used by financing activities = $50,000 − $99,500 = **$(49,500)**

**Change in cash:**  
$5,400 + $85,000 − $49,500 = **$40,900** increase.

**Beginning cash:**  
Ending $84,500 − increase $40,900 = **$43,600**.  
Check: $43,600 + $40,900 = $84,500.

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net cash provided by operating activities | $5,400 |
| Net cash provided by investing activities | 85,000 |
| Net cash used by financing activities | (49,500) |
| Net increase in cash | 40,900 |
| Cash at beginning of period | 43,600 |
| **Cash at end of period** | **$84,500** |

**d. Format / reconciliation**

Order on SCF: **(1) Operating → (2) Investing → (3) Financing**, then:

```
Net increase in cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash .....  40,900
Cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash, beginning ..........  43,600
Cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash, ending .............  84,500
```

Item 6 appears in a **noncash investing and financing activities** schedule/note: “Acquired land by issuing long-term note payable, $90,000.”  
Items 4 and 9 are period-end **accrual/declaration** entries only — they do not appear as cash inflows or outflows.

**e. Gross presentation**  
**False.** Except for limited items specified in ASC 230, **investing and financing cash inflows and outflows shall be reported separately** (e.g., PPE purchases separate from PPE sale proceeds; borrowings separate from repayments; stock issuances separate from treasury purchases).

**Key insight:** Split **interest paid** (operating cash) from **interest accrued** (no cash) and from **principal** (financing). Split **dividends paid** (financing cash) from **dividends declared** (no cash until paid). PPE **disposals** and **purchases** are investing on a **gross** basis. Trading securities cash flows are **operating**; noncash asset-for-debt exchanges never appear as cash inflows/outflows on the face of the SCF. Period-end, the SCF cash total is broader than “cash in bank” alone when equivalents or restricted cash exist.

### Q4 — MC — Classification choice (two items max)
**LO:** LO 22-1  
**Concept:** Classification of cash flows — interest/dividends and period-end accrual vs cash payment; PPE sale proceeds  

**Question 1:**  
**Oakridge Millworks Corp.** paid **$11,200** cash interest on its bonds during 2025 and, at December 31, 2025, accrued an additional **$2,800** of unpaid interest. Separately, it paid **$28,000** cash dividends to common shareholders. How should the **cash** amounts be classified on the 2025 statement of cash flows under U.S. GAAP, and what is the SCF effect of the accrual?

- A) Interest paid: financing outflow $11,200; dividends paid: financing outflow $28,000; interest accrual: financing outflow $2,800  
- B) Interest paid: operating outflow $11,200; dividends paid: financing outflow $28,000; interest accrual: no cash flow on the SCF  
- C) Interest paid: operating outflow $11,200; dividends paid: operating outflow $28,000; interest accrual: operating outflow $2,800  
- D) Interest paid: investing outflow $11,200; dividends paid: financing outflow $28,000; interest accrual: operating outflow $2,800  

**Answer:** **B.** Under U.S. GAAP (ASC 230), cash interest paid is classified as an **operating** activity; cash dividends paid to owners are a **financing** outflow. A year-end **interest accrual** records expense and a payable but involves **no cash**, so it does not appear as a cash line on the SCF.

**Question 2:**  
**Pinecrest Tools Co.** sold equipment for **$72,000** cash. The equipment’s book value was **$65,000**, so a **$7,000** gain was included in net income. On the statement of cash flows, the **$72,000** cash is reported as:

- A) An operating inflow of $72,000  
- B) An operating inflow of $7,000 and an investing inflow of $65,000  
- C) An investing inflow of $72,000  
- D) An investing inflow of $65,000 only (gain is noncash)  

**Answer:** **C.** The **full cash proceeds** from sale of PPE are an **investing inflow**. The $7,000 gain is part of net income (and would be **deducted** in the **indirect** operating section under LO 22-2), but it does not split the cash proceeds into operating and investing pieces on the face of the SCF.
