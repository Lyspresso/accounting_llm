# Agent 87 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-2

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Determine the initial and subsequent measurement of finite life intangible assets  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** finite-life intangibles recorded at cash-equivalent cost (purchase price + transfer/legal/registration fees and other costs to place the asset in condition for intended use)
- Noncash acquisition: cost = PV of note / FV of consideration given or FV of asset acquired, whichever is more reliably measurable
- Basket (group) asset acquisition: allocate total cost to individual intangibles on **relative fair value**; no goodwill
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** amortize cost less residual over the **shorter** of remaining legal life and estimated useful life; straight-line unless another systematic pattern better reflects consumption
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** Dr Amortization Expense; Cr Patent (direct write-down, most common) or Accumulated Amortization
- Successful legal defense costs: capitalize to the patent; recompute amortization over **remaining** useful life
- **Disposal JE:** amortize through disposal date; remove carrying amount; recognize gain or loss vs net proceeds (income from continuing operations)
- Classification / presentation / disclosure: gross carrying amount and accumulated amortization (by major class); period amortization expense; estimated amortization for each of the next five years

### Q1 — CORE — Patent purchase, amortization schedule, defense costs, disposal (emphasis: initial recognition JE)
**LO:** LO 13-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition at cash-equivalent cost (purchase + fees); subsequent SL amortization schedule; period-end adjusting JEs; capitalize successful defense; disposal gain; presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**LumenForge Precision LLC** (calendar year) acquires a manufacturing process patent and accounts for it as a **finite-life** intangible. Company policy is to credit the **Patent** account directly for amortization (no Accumulated Amortization account). Residual value is zero unless stated.

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Acquisition date | **January 1, 2025** |
| Cash purchase price paid to seller | **$95,000** |
| Cash transfer, registration, and outside legal fees to secure title | **$5,000** |
| Remaining legal life at acquisition | 18 years |
| Estimated useful life at acquisition | **10 years** (expected technological obsolescence) |
| Residual value | **$0** |
| Amortization method | Straight-line |
| **January 5, 2026** | Successfully defends the patent in court; outside counsel fees paid in cash **$9,000** |
| **January 1, 2028** | Sells the patent for **$82,000** cash (amortization for 2025–2027 already recorded) |
| Fiscal year-end | December 31 |

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **January 1, 2025 initial recognition** journal entry for the patent (include all capitalizable acquisition costs). Show Dr = Cr.  
b. Prepare a **subsequent measurement (amortization) schedule** for 2025–2027 that incorporates the 2026 defense costs and recomputed amortization. Show carrying amount at each year-end.  
c. Prepare the **period-end adjusting entries** for amortization on December 31, 2025; December 31, 2026; and December 31, 2027.  
d. Prepare the **January 5, 2026** entry for the successful legal defense.  
e. Prepare the **January 1, 2028 disposal** journal entry. Compute gain or loss.  
f. Briefly state how the patent and amortization would be **presented/disclosed** at December 31, 2025 (balance sheet and notes).

**Answer key:**

**a. January 1, 2025 — initial recognition (cash-equivalent cost)**  
Capitalized cost = purchase price + transfer/legal fees = $95,000 + $5,000 = **$100,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 100,000 | |
| Cash | | 100,000 |
| *Record finite-life patent at cash-equivalent cost (price + acquisition costs to place in use)* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = **100,000**. Balanced.

Useful life for amortization = shorter of remaining legal life (18) and estimated useful life (10) → **10 years**.  
Annual amortization (pre-defense) = $100,000 / 10 = **$10,000**.

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (amortization roll-forward)**

| Date / event | Computation | Amount | Carrying amount |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, 2025 — capitalized cost | $95,000 + $5,000 | 100,000 | **100,000** |
| Dec 31, 2025 — amortization | $100,000 ÷ 10 | (10,000) | **90,000** |
| Jan 5, 2026 — successful defense capitalized | | +9,000 | **99,000** |
| Dec 31, 2026 — amortization | $99,000 ÷ **9** remaining years | (11,000) | **88,000** |
| Dec 31, 2027 — amortization | $99,000 ÷ 9 (same annual amount) | (11,000) | **77,000** |

*After defense, remaining useful life from Jan 1, 2026 = 9 years; revised annual amortization = ($90,000 + $9,000) / 9 = $11,000.*

**c. Period-end adjusting JEs**

*December 31, 2025 — amortization*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense—Patent | 10,000 | |
| Patent | | 10,000 |
| *SL amortization: $100,000 / 10* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 10,000.

*December 31, 2026 — amortization (after defense)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense—Patent | 11,000 | |
| Patent | | 11,000 |
| *Revised SL: ($90,000 + $9,000) / 9 remaining years* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 11,000.

*December 31, 2027 — amortization*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense—Patent | 11,000 | |
| Patent | | 11,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 11,000.  
Carrying amount Dec 31, 2027 = **$77,000**.

**d. January 5, 2026 — successful legal defense (capitalized)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 9,000 | |
| Cash | | 9,000 |
| *Capitalize successful defense costs as part of patent cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 9,000.

**e. January 1, 2028 — disposal**  
Carrying amount = **$77,000**  
Cash proceeds = **$82,000**  
**Gain on sale of patent** = $82,000 − $77,000 = **$5,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 82,000 | |
| Patent | | 77,000 |
| Gain on Sale of Patent | | 5,000 |
| *Derecognize patent at carrying amount; recognize gain* | | |

**Check:** Dr 82,000 = Cr 77,000 + 5,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Classification / presentation / disclosure (Dec 31, 2025)**  
- **Balance sheet:** Patent reported among noncurrent intangible assets at **gross carrying amount $100,000** less amortization of **$10,000** (or net **$90,000** if presented net). If the entity used Accumulated Amortization, both gross and accumulated amortization by major class would be shown.  
- **Notes:** Aggregate amortization expense for 2025 (**$10,000**); estimated amortization for each of the **next five fiscal years** (at Dec 31, 2025, still $10,000 per year for 2026–2030 before the 2026 defense revision).  
- Gain on disposal (when it occurs) is a component of **income from continuing operations**.

**Key insight:** **Initial recognition** is always at **cash-equivalent cost**, including incremental acquisition costs (transfer/legal fees). Subsequent periods **amortize** over the shorter of legal and useful life. Successful **defense costs are capitalized** and the remaining book value is amortized prospectively over the **remaining** useful life. Disposal requires removing the updated carrying amount and recognizing gain or loss.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Patent life-cycle twin (all numbers changed)

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Noncash note acquisition, basket allocation, mid-year amortization, residual value
**LO:** LO 13-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition with zero-interest note (PV); basket purchase relative-FV allocation; mid-year acquisition partial-period amortization; residual value in amort base; five-year amortization disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Circuitry Inc.** (calendar year) completes three separate finite-life intangible acquisitions in **2025**. Residual values are zero except where noted. Policy may credit the asset account or Accumulated Amortization (either is acceptable; solutions credit the asset).

**Transaction 1 — Zero-interest note (January 1, 2025)**  
Cascade acquires a patent by issuing a **zero-interest** note payable:

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Note face (due in a lump sum in **2 years**) | **$46,656** |
| Stated rate on note | **0%** |
| Market (cash-equivalent) rate for similar financing | **8%** |
| Fair value of the patent | not more reliably measurable than PV of the note |
| Remaining useful life (also ≤ remaining legal life) | **5 years** |
| Residual value | **$0** |

**Transaction 2 — Basket purchase of two intangibles (July 1, 2025)**  
Cascade pays **$240,000** cash for a group of two identifiable intangibles (asset acquisition, **not** a business combination):

| Asset | Independent fair value | Estimated useful life |
|---|---:|---|
| Customer list | $150,000 | 5 years |
| Developed technology | $90,000 | 6 years |
| **Total FV** | **$240,000** | |

(Relative fair values equal total cost in this case, so allocated costs equal FVs.)

**Transaction 3 — Mid-year franchise with residual (October 1, 2025)**  

| Item | Amount / fact |
|---|---|
| Cash cost of franchise license | **$48,000** |
| Estimated residual value at end of useful life | **$6,000** |
| Useful life | **7 years** |
| Remaining contractual/legal life | 10 years |

**Required:**  
a. **Transaction 1:** Compute the present value of the note; prepare the **initial recognition JE** for the patent and note (include Discount on Note Payable). Prepare the **December 31, 2025** amortization adjusting entry for the patent only (ignore interest accretion on the note for this LO pack).  
b. **Transaction 2:** Allocate the $240,000 basket cost; prepare the July 1, 2025 acquisition JE; prepare the **December 31, 2025** combined amortization entry for both intangibles (half-year).  
c. **Transaction 3:** Compute the amortizable base and 2025 amortization; record the Oct 1 purchase and Dec 31 amortization entries.  
d. Prepare the **note disclosure** of estimated aggregate amortization expense for **2026–2030** related only to these three 2025 acquisitions (full years for 2026 onward; Transaction 1 and 2 and 3 all outstanding for full years after 2025).  
e. One sentence on **classification**: where finite-life intangibles appear on the statement of financial position.

**Answer key:**

**a. Transaction 1 — PV and initial recognition**

\[
PV = \frac{\$46{,}656}{(1.08)^{2}} = \$40{,}000
\]

Discount on Note Payable = $46,656 − $40,000 = **$6,656**

*January 1, 2025 — purchase patent with zero-interest note*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 40,000 | |
| Discount on Note Payable | 6,656 | |
| Note Payable | | 46,656 |
| *Patent at cash-equivalent cost = PV of note; face of note separate* | | |

**Check:** Dr 40,000 + 6,656 = Cr 46,656. **Balanced.**

Annual patent amortization = $40,000 / 5 = **$8,000**

*December 31, 2025 — patent amortization*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense—Patent | 8,000 | |
| Patent | | 8,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 8,000.  
Carrying amount of patent Dec 31, 2025 = $40,000 − $8,000 = **$32,000**.

**b. Transaction 2 — basket allocation and half-year amortization**

Relative FV allocation (total FV = total cost = $240,000):

| Asset | FV | Relative % | Allocated cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Customer list | 150,000 | 150/240 = 62.5% | **150,000** |
| Developed technology | 90,000 | 90/240 = 37.5% | **90,000** |
| **Total** | **240,000** | 100% | **240,000** |

*No goodwill* in an asset (group) acquisition — cost is fully allocated on relative FV.

*July 1, 2025 — basket acquisition*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Customer List | 150,000 | |
| Developed Technology | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 240,000 |

**Check:** Dr 150,000 + 90,000 = Cr 240,000. Balanced.

Annual amortization:  
Customer list: $150,000 / 5 = **$30,000** per year → 2025 (6/12) = **$15,000**  
Developed technology: $90,000 / 6 = **$15,000** per year → 2025 (6/12) = **$7,500**  
**Total 2025 amortization** = $15,000 + $7,500 = **$22,500**

*December 31, 2025 — amortization of basket intangibles*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense—Intangibles | 22,500 | |
| Customer List | | 15,000 |
| Developed Technology | | 7,500 |

**Check:** Dr 22,500 = Cr 15,000 + 7,500. **Balanced.**

**c. Transaction 3 — residual value and partial year**

Amortizable base = cost − residual = $48,000 − $6,000 = **$42,000**  
Useful life for amort = shorter of 7 (useful) and 10 (legal) → **7 years**  
Annual amortization = $42,000 / 7 = **$6,000**  
2025 amortization (Oct 1–Dec 31 = 3/12) = $6,000 × 3/12 = **$1,500**

*October 1, 2025 — purchase franchise*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Franchise | 48,000 | |
| Cash | | 48,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 48,000.

*December 31, 2025 — franchise amortization*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense—Franchise | 1,500 | |
| Franchise | | 1,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 1,500.  
Carrying amount Dec 31, 2025 = $48,000 − $1,500 = **$46,500**.

**d. Estimated aggregate amortization expense — next five years (2026–2030)**  
Annual full-year rates while each asset remains in service:

| Source | Annual amort | Life from acquisition |
|---|---:|---|
| Patent (Transaction 1) | 8,000 | 5 years from Jan 1, 2025 → last year **2029** |
| Customer list (Transaction 2) | 30,000 | 5 years from July 1, 2025 → ends June 30, 2030 |
| Developed technology (Transaction 2) | 15,000 | 6 years from July 1, 2025 → ends June 30, 2031 |
| Franchise (Transaction 3) | 6,000 | 7 years from Oct 1, 2025 → ends Sept 30, 2032 |

**Anniversary-strict five-year disclosure schedule:**

| Year | Patent | Customer list | Dev. tech | Franchise | **Total** |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026 | 8,000 | 30,000 | 15,000 | 6,000 | **59,000** |
| 2027 | 8,000 | 30,000 | 15,000 | 6,000 | **59,000** |
| 2028 | 8,000 | 30,000 | 15,000 | 6,000 | **59,000** |
| 2029 | 8,000 | 30,000 | 15,000 | 6,000 | **59,000** |
| 2030 | **0** | 15,000 (6/12) | 15,000 | 6,000 | **36,000** |

**e. Classification / presentation**  
Finite-life intangible assets are reported as **noncurrent assets** on the balance sheet (often a single “Intangible assets, net” line with note detail by major class showing gross carrying amount and accumulated amortization).

**Key insight:** Noncash purchases use **present value (cash-equivalent cost)** for initial measurement. Basket acquisitions allocate total cost by **relative fair value** without creating goodwill. Amortization is **cost − residual**, over the shorter life, and **prorated** for partial periods. Disclosure of the next five years’ amortization is a core presentation requirement for finite-life intangibles.

### Q4 — MC — Useful life and amortization credit (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 13-2  
**Concept:** Classification of amortization life (shorter of legal vs useful) and acceptable credit account for period-end amortization  
**Question:**  
On March 1, Year 1, HarborChem Co. purchases a patent for $72,000 cash. The patent’s remaining **legal** life is 12 years. Management estimates the patent will provide economic benefits for only **8 years** and will then be worthless. HarborChem uses straight-line amortization and may credit either the Patent account or Accumulated Amortization—Patent. Which statement is **correct** for Year 1 (calendar year)?

- A) Capitalize $72,000; amortize $72,000 / 12 × 10/12 = $5,000; credit must be to Accumulated Amortization only  
- B) Capitalize $72,000; amortize $72,000 / 8 × 10/12 = $7,500; credit may be to Patent or to Accumulated Amortization—Patent  
- C) Capitalize $72,000; amortize $72,000 / 8 = $9,000 full year (no partial-year); credit Patent only  
- D) Expense $72,000 immediately because patents developed for internal use are never capitalized when purchased  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Finite-life intangibles are capitalized at cost and amortized over the **shorter** of remaining legal life (12) and estimated useful life (8) → 8 years. Partial-year amortization from March 1 is 10/12 × ($72,000 / 8) = **$7,500**. GAAP permits crediting the intangible asset **directly** or using a contra **Accumulated Amortization** account; both are acceptable (textbook notes direct credit is most common). A is wrong on the life (should not use 12 when useful life is shorter) and on “must.” C ignores partial year. D confuses purchased patents (capitalize) with **internally developed** R&D costs (generally expensed).

### Q5 — MC — Basket purchase vs business combination (classification)
**LO:** LO 13-2  
**Concept:** Classification — relative-FV cost allocation in an asset acquisition does not create goodwill  
**Question:**  
Northline Media pays $300,000 cash for a group of **identifiable** intangible assets (not a business). Independent appraisals: Trademark (finite life) FV $100,000; Customer relationships FV $150,000; Noncompete agreement FV $50,000. How should Northline record the acquisition?

- A) Debit the three intangibles for their FVs totaling $300,000; no goodwill  
- B) Debit the three intangibles for $300,000 in total using relative FV, and debit Goodwill for any excess of cost over FV  
- C) Debit Goodwill $300,000 only, because intangibles acquired outside a business combination are not recognized  
- D) Expense $300,000 as R&D because the assets lack physical substance  

**Answer:** **A.**  
In an **asset acquisition** of a group of assets, total cost is allocated to individual assets based on **relative fair values** and **does not give rise to goodwill**. Here FVs sum to cost ($300,000), so each intangible is recorded at its FV. Goodwill arises only in a **business combination** when consideration exceeds the FV of identifiable net assets. C and D contradict recognition of acquired identifiable intangibles under ASC 350 / ASC 805-50.
