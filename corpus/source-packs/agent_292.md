# Agent 292 — CORE DEMO — LO 18-5

**Chapter:** 18  
**LO title:** Explain how to record a valuation allowance and report deferred taxes  
**Critical gap LO:** yes  
**Emphasis:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- **Valuation allowance (VA):** contra-asset reducing Deferred Tax Asset (DTA) when it is **more likely than not (>50%)** that some or all of the tax benefit will **not** be realized
- **Realizability sources:** (1) future reversals of existing taxable temporary differences, (2) future taxable income exclusive of reversing temps, (3) taxable income in prior carryback years (if allowed), (4) tax-planning strategies
- **Initial recognition JE:** establish gross DTA from deductible temporary differences; establish VA when needed — Dr Income Tax Expense / Cr Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** roll DTA and VA forward; target VA = unrealizable portion of **ending** gross DTA; adjust only the **change**
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** reassess positive/negative evidence each reporting date; plug VA to target ending balance through Income Tax Expense (increase VA raises tax expense; decrease VA lowers tax expense)
- **Settlement / full reversal:** when the related DTA reverses to zero (or is written off), reduce VA so net DTA remains supportable — often full release of remaining VA
- **Classification / presentation:** for one tax-paying component and one jurisdiction, offset DTA (net of VA) and DTL → **single noncurrent** net deferred tax asset **or** liability; (DTA − VA) > DTL → noncurrent DTA, net; DTL > (DTA − VA) → noncurrent DTL, net
- **Income tax JE integration:** current tax (taxable income × rate) + deferred effects from DTA/DTL/VA changes

### Q1 — CORE — Establish DTA and VA, period-end VA adjustment schedule, full tax JEs, net BS presentation
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of DTA and valuation allowance; subsequent VA measurement schedule; **period-end adjusting JE** to reassess realizability; settlement when DTA reverses; single noncurrent net presentation of deferred taxes  
**Scenario:**  
**Redwood Trail Outfitters Inc.** is a calendar-year U.S. C corporation. Enacted tax rate is **25%** for all years (no rate change). Accounting and tax years both end December 31. Beginning balances in all deferred tax accounts are **zero** on January 1, Year 1.

**Year 1 facts**
- Taxable income: **$560,000**
- Deductible temporary difference at Dec 31, Year 1: **$64,000** warranty liability accrued for GAAP; deductible for tax when paid → cumulative future deductible amount **$64,000**
- Taxable temporary difference at Dec 31, Year 1: **$28,000** excess of tax depreciation over GAAP depreciation → cumulative future taxable amount **$28,000**
- After weighing positive and negative evidence, management concludes it is **more likely than not** that **$18,000** of the **$64,000 deductible temporary difference** will **not** reverse into realizable tax benefits (i.e., the related portion of the DTA is unrealizable). Record the valuation allowance at the enacted rate on that amount.

**Year 2 facts**
- Taxable income: **$420,000**
- During Year 2, warranty claims paid reduce the deductible temporary difference; cumulative deductible temporary difference at Dec 31, Year 2: **$40,000**
- Cumulative taxable temporary difference at Dec 31, Year 2: **$16,000** (partial reverse of depreciation difference)
- At Dec 31, Year 2 period-end reassessment, management concludes it is more likely than not that **30%** of the **ending** gross DTA will not be realized (improved outlook vs Year 1).

**Year 3 facts (settlement of remaining warranty DTA)**
- Cumulative deductible temporary difference falls to **$0** at Dec 31, Year 3 (all warranties settled for tax as paid). Ignore further DTL changes for part (f) only — focus on clearing the warranty-related DTA and related VA. Taxable income Year 3: **$300,000**. Assume the depreciation-related DTL ending balance at Dec 31, Year 3 is **$0** (fully reversed during Year 3).

**Required:**  
a. Compute Year 1 ending **gross DTA**, **DTL**, and **target VA**. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** combined income tax journal entry (current tax + deferred tax + valuation allowance).  
b. Prepare a **two-year subsequent measurement schedule** for Gross DTA, Valuation Allowance, Net DTA, Gross DTL, and Net deferred tax position (asset or liability) at Dec 31, Year 1 and Year 2.  
c. **(Emphasis)** Prepare the **December 31, Year 2 period-end adjusting** income tax journal entry (remeasure DTA, DTL, and VA to ending targets; record current tax). Show the VA T-account rollforward.  
d. Show **balance sheet presentation** of deferred taxes at Dec 31, Year 1 and Dec 31, Year 2 (single noncurrent amount).  
e. Prepare a **valuation allowance rollforward disclosure** for Year 2 (beginning balance, additions, deductions, ending balance) consistent with the Year 2 JE.  
f. **(Settlement)** Prepare the **December 31, Year 3** income tax journal entry that clears remaining Gross DTA and VA to zero and records current tax (DTL already zero). Confirm net deferred tax balances are zero.

**Answer key:**  

**Setup — rates and temporary differences**  
- Tax rate \(= 25\%\)  
- Gross DTA \(=\) cumulative deductible temp. difference \(\times 25\%\)  
- Gross DTL \(=\) cumulative taxable temp. difference \(\times 25\%\)  
- Target VA \(=\) amount of gross DTA more likely than not unrealizable (given)

---

**a. Year 1 — balances and combined income tax JE**

| Item | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Gross DTA, Dec 31 Y1 | \(64{,}000 \times 25\%\) | **$16,000** |
| Gross DTL, Dec 31 Y1 | \(28{,}000 \times 25\%\) | **$7,000** |
| Target VA, Dec 31 Y1 | \(18{,}000 \times 25\%\) (unrealizable temp. × rate) | **$4,500** |
| Net DTA | \(16{,}000 - 4{,}500\) | **$11,500** |
| Net deferred tax asset position | \(11{,}500 - 7{,}000\) | **$4,500** |

Current tax (Income Tax Payable): \(560{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$140{,}000}\)

Deferred effects Year 1 (from zero beginning balances):  
- Increase DTA: **$16,000** (decreases tax expense)  
- Increase DTL: **$7,000** (increases tax expense)  
- Increase VA: **$4,500** (increases tax expense)  

Income Tax Expense \(= 140{,}000 - 16{,}000 + 7{,}000 + 4{,}500 = \mathbf{\$135{,}500}\)

**December 31, Year 1 — To record income tax expense (current, deferred, and VA)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 135,500 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 16,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | | 7,000 |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | | 4,500 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 140,000 |
| *Record current tax, establish DTA/DTL, establish valuation allowance* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(135{,}500 + 16{,}000 = 151{,}500\); Cr \(7{,}000 + 4{,}500 + 140{,}000 = 151{,}500\). **Balanced.**

Net DTA on BS before netting with DTL: \(16{,}000 - 4{,}500 = 11{,}500\).  
Single noncurrent amount: \(11{,}500 - 7{,}000 = \mathbf{\$4{,}500}\) **noncurrent deferred tax asset, net**.

---

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule (Year 1–Year 2)**

Year 2 targets:  
- Gross DTA: \(40{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\)  
- Target VA: \(30\% \times 10{,}000 = \mathbf{\$3{,}000}\)  
- Gross DTL: \(16{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$4{,}000}\)  
- Net DTA: \(10{,}000 - 3{,}000 = 7{,}000\); Net position: \(7{,}000 - 4{,}000 = \mathbf{\$3{,}000}\) noncurrent DTA, net

| Account / metric | Dec 31, Year 1 | Change in Y2 | Dec 31, Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cumulative deductible temp. diff. | 64,000 | (24,000) | 40,000 |
| Gross Deferred Tax Asset | 16,000 | (6,000) | 10,000 |
| Valuation Allowance for DTA | (4,500) | +1,500 (debit; reduce allow.) | (3,000) |
| **Net Deferred Tax Asset** | **11,500** | | **7,000** |
| Cumulative taxable temp. diff. | 28,000 | (12,000) | 16,000 |
| Gross Deferred Tax Liability | 7,000 | (3,000) | 4,000 |
| **Net deferred tax (asset) / liability** | **(4,500) asset** | | **(3,000) asset** |

VA change needed: ending target **$3,000** − beginning **$4,500** = **decrease VA $1,500** (debit VA).

DTA change: ending **$10,000** − beginning **$16,000** = **decrease DTA $6,000** (credit DTA).  
DTL change: ending **$4,000** − beginning **$7,000** = **decrease DTL $3,000** (debit DTL).

---

**c. Period-end adjusting JE — December 31, Year 2 (emphasis)**

Current tax: \(420{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$105{,}000}\)

Deferred effects Year 2:  
- Decrease DTA \(6{,}000\) → **increases** tax expense \(+6{,}000\)  
- Decrease DTL \(3{,}000\) → **decreases** tax expense \(-3{,}000\)  
- Decrease VA \(1{,}500\) → **decreases** tax expense \(-1{,}500\)  

Income Tax Expense \(= 105{,}000 + 6{,}000 - 3{,}000 - 1{,}500 = \mathbf{\$106{,}500}\)

**December 31, Year 2 — To record income tax expense and adjust deferred tax accounts (period-end reassessment)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 106,500 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 3,000 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | 1,500 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 6,000 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 105,000 |
| *Remeasure DTA/DTL/VA to year-end targets; record current tax* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(106{,}500 + 3{,}000 + 1{,}500 = 111{,}000\); Cr \(6{,}000 + 105{,}000 = 111{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**Valuation Allowance T-account (Year 2)**

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Adj. (period-end) 1,500 | Beg. bal. 4,500 |
| | |
| | **End. bal. 3,000** |

Rollforward: \(4{,}500 - 1{,}500 = 3{,}000\). Matches \(10{,}000 \times 30\%\).

---

**d. Balance sheet presentation (single noncurrent amount)**

**December 31, Year 1**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net | **$4,500** |

Computation: \((16{,}000 - 4{,}500) - 7{,}000 = 4{,}500\) asset.  
Rule: (DTA − VA) \(11{,}500\) > DTL \(7{,}000\) → **noncurrent deferred tax asset, net**.

**December 31, Year 2**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net | **$3,000** |

Computation: \((10{,}000 - 3{,}000) - 4{,}000 = 3{,}000\) asset.

---

**e. Valuation allowance rollforward disclosure — Year 2**

| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Assets | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Balance at beginning of Year 2 | $4,500 |
| Additions charged to income tax expense | 0 |
| Deductions (reductions of allowance) | (1,500) |
| **Balance at end of Year 2** | **$3,000** |

---

**f. Settlement — December 31, Year 3 (clear DTA and VA; DTL = 0)**

Year 3 ending targets: Gross DTA **$0**, VA **$0**, DTL **$0**.  
Changes from Dec 31 Y2: decrease DTA **$10,000**; decrease VA **$3,000**; DTL already reduced to 0 during Year 3 — assume DTL was **$4,000** at start of Y3 and goes to **$0** (decrease DTL **$4,000**).

Current tax: \(300{,}000 \times 25\% = \mathbf{\$75{,}000}\)

Income Tax Expense \(= 75{,}000 + 10{,}000 - 4{,}000 - 3{,}000 = \mathbf{\$78{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 78,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 4,000 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | 3,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 10,000 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 75,000 |
| *Clear remaining DTA/VA/DTL; record current tax* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(78{,}000 + 4{,}000 + 3{,}000 = 85{,}000\); Cr \(10{,}000 + 75{,}000 = 85{,}000\). **Balanced.**  
Ending: Gross DTA 0, VA 0, DTL 0 → **no deferred tax amount** on the balance sheet.

**Key insight:** The valuation allowance is a **contra-DTA** adjusted at **each period-end** to the amount of tax benefit **more likely than not unrealizable**. Increases in VA raise income tax expense; decreases (better evidence) reduce tax expense. On the balance sheet, DTA net of VA is **offset** against DTL for the same taxpaying component/jurisdiction into **one noncurrent** net asset or liability. When the underlying temporary difference fully reverses, both the gross DTA and related VA are cleared.

### Q2 — CORE number variant twin
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — recompute gross DTA/DTL/VA, combined tax JE, period-end VA plug to new % unrealizable, net noncurrent presentation, and VA release when realizability improves fully  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Ridge Industrial Coatings LLC** (calendar-year). Enacted rate **21%** all years. Zero beginning deferred tax balances on Jan 1, Year 1.

**Year 1**
- Taxable income: **$900,000**
- Cumulative deductible temporary difference (unearned revenue taxed when cash received; recognized later for GAAP): **$100,000** → Gross DTA \(= 100{,}000 \times 21\%\)
- Cumulative taxable temporary difference (installment receivable recognized for GAAP, taxed when collected): **$60,000** → Gross DTL \(= 60{,}000 \times 21\%\)
- Management: more likely than not that **40%** of ending gross DTA will **not** be realized.

**Year 2 (period-end emphasis)**
- Taxable income: **$750,000**
- Ending cumulative deductible temp. difference: **$70,000**
- Ending cumulative taxable temp. difference: **$45,000**
- New assessment: more likely than not only **10%** of ending gross DTA will not be realized (substantial positive evidence from backlog of firm contracts).

**Required:**  
a. Year 1: compute gross DTA, target VA, gross DTL, net presentation; record Dec 31 Year 1 combined income tax JE.  
b. Year 2 schedule of ending targets and the **period-end adjusting** combined income tax JE.  
c. VA T-account for Year 2 and Year 2 balance sheet presentation of deferred taxes.  
d. Assume at Dec 31, Year 3 management concludes the **entire** remaining DTA **is** more likely than not realizable (target VA = **$0**), while Gross DTA remains **$70,000 × 21%** and Gross DTL remains **$45,000 × 21%** (no change in temps). Taxable income Year 3 **$800,000**. Prepare the Year 3 period-end JE (current tax + VA release only; DTA/DTL unchanged).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Year 1**

| Item | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Gross DTA | \(100{,}000 \times 21\%\) | **$21,000** |
| Target VA (40%) | \(21{,}000 \times 40\%\) | **$8,400** |
| Net DTA | \(21{,}000 - 8{,}400\) | **$12,600** |
| Gross DTL | \(60{,}000 \times 21\%\) | **$12,600** |
| Net deferred tax position | \(12{,}600 - 12{,}600\) | **$0** |
| Current tax payable | \(900{,}000 \times 21\%\) | **$189,000** |

Income Tax Expense \(= 189{,}000 - 21{,}000 + 12{,}600 + 8{,}400 = \mathbf{\$189{,}000}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 189,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | 21,000 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | | 12,600 |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | | 8,400 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 189,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(189{,}000 + 21{,}000 = 210{,}000\); Cr \(12{,}600 + 8{,}400 + 189{,}000 = 210{,}000\). **Balanced.**  
BS: net deferred taxes **$0** (fully offset).

---

**b. Year 2 period-end targets and JE**

| Item | Computation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Gross DTA | \(70{,}000 \times 21\%\) | **$14,700** |
| Target VA (10%) | \(14{,}700 \times 10\%\) | **$1,470** |
| Net DTA | | **$13,230** |
| Gross DTL | \(45{,}000 \times 21\%\) | **$9,450** |
| Net deferred tax asset | \(13{,}230 - 9{,}450\) | **$3,780** |
| Current tax | \(750{,}000 \times 21\%\) | **$157,500** |

Changes from Year 1 ending:  
- DTA: \(14{,}700 - 21{,}000 = \mathbf{(6{,}300)}\) credit DTA  
- VA: \(1{,}470 - 8{,}400 = \mathbf{(6{,}930)}\) debit VA (release)  
- DTL: \(9{,}450 - 12{,}600 = \mathbf{(3{,}150)}\) debit DTL  

Income Tax Expense \(= 157{,}500 + 6{,}300 - 3{,}150 - 6{,}930 = \mathbf{\$153{,}720}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 153,720 | |
| Deferred Tax Liability | 3,150 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | 6,930 | |
| Deferred Tax Asset | | 6,300 |
| Income Tax Payable | | 157,500 |

**Check:** Dr \(153{,}720 + 3{,}150 + 6{,}930 = 163{,}800\); Cr \(6{,}300 + 157{,}500 = 163{,}800\). **Balanced.**

---

**c. VA T-account Year 2 and presentation**

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Period-end release 6,930 | Beg. 8,400 |
| | **End. 1,470** |

**December 31, Year 2 BS:** Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net **$3,780**.

---

**d. Year 3 — full VA release (temps unchanged)**

Target VA = **$0**; beginning VA = **$1,470**. Release **$1,470**.  
DTA and DTL unchanged → no deferred entry except VA.  
Current tax: \(800{,}000 \times 21\% = \mathbf{\$168{,}000}\)  
Income Tax Expense \(= 168{,}000 - 1{,}470 = \mathbf{\$166{,}530}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 166,530 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | 1,470 | |
| Income Tax Payable | | 168,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(166{,}530 + 1{,}470 = 168{,}000\); Cr \(168{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**Key insight:** Same mechanics as Q1 with a different rate and percentages. Period-end VA is always a **plug to the new target**. A full release of VA (strong positive evidence) reduces current-period income tax expense even if temporary differences are unchanged.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end VA-only adjustment + classification of net deferred taxes (Demo 18-5B style)
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Standalone **period-end adjusting JE** for valuation allowance when gross DTA is already recorded; classify single noncurrent net deferred tax asset vs liability  
**Scenario:**  
**Ironwood Medical Devices Corp.** already recorded its Year-end **gross** deferred tax accounts before considering the valuation allowance. At December 31, the **pre-allowance** trial balance shows:

| Account | Balance |
|---|---:|
| Deferred Tax Asset (debit) | $85,000 |
| Deferred Tax Liability (credit) | $52,000 |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | $0 (before adjustment) |

Management’s period-end assessment: it is more likely than not that **$22,000** of the gross DTA will **not** be realized. Taxable income for the year was already used to record Income Tax Payable and the gross DTA/DTL entries; you need **only** the VA adjusting entry and the **balance sheet classification**.

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **period-end adjusting JE** to establish the valuation allowance.  
b. Compute the amount and classification of deferred taxes on the **classified balance sheet**.  
c. Next year, before any other deferred tax remeasurement, management revises the estimate: only **$8,000** of gross DTA is more likely than not unrealizable (gross DTA and DTL balances unchanged at $85,000 and $52,000). Prepare the **period-end adjusting JE** to revise the allowance.  
d. Recompute balance sheet presentation after the Year 2 revision.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Establish VA — period-end adjusting JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income Tax Expense | 22,000 | |
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | | 22,000 |
| *Establish VA for portion of DTA MLTN not realizable* | | |

**Check:** Dr 22,000 = Cr 22,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Classification — Year 1**

Net DTA \(= 85{,}000 - 22{,}000 = 63{,}000\)  
Compare to DTL \(52{,}000\): \(63{,}000 > 52{,}000\) →  

| | |
|---|---:|
| **Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net** | **$11,000** |

Computation: \(63{,}000 - 52{,}000 = 11{,}000\).

**c. Year 2 period-end VA revision (gross accounts unchanged)**

Target VA \(= 8{,}000\); beginning VA \(= 22{,}000\); **decrease VA $14,000**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset | 14,000 | |
| Income Tax Expense | | 14,000 |
| *Reduce VA after improved realizability evidence* | | |

**Check:** Dr 14,000 = Cr 14,000. **Balanced.**  
Effect: **decreases** income tax expense (increases net income) by $14,000 with **no** change in taxes currently payable.

**d. Classification — Year 2**

Net DTA \(= 85{,}000 - 8{,}000 = 77{,}000\)  
\(77{,}000 - 52{,}000 = \mathbf{\$25{,}000}\) **noncurrent deferred tax asset, net**.

**Key insight:** The period-end VA entry is often a **standalone plug**. Changing only the allowance (not the gross DTA) still moves income tax expense and changes the **net** noncurrent deferred tax amount presented. Forming a conclusion that **no** VA is needed is difficult when there is significant **negative evidence** (e.g., cumulative losses); positive evidence must be objectively supportable.

### Q4 — MC — Balance sheet classification of deferred taxes with valuation allowance
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Classification / presentation — single noncurrent net deferred tax asset or liability after offsetting DTA net of VA against DTL  
**Question:**  
Larkspur Analytics reports the following for a single tax-paying component in a single jurisdiction at year-end:

- Deferred tax assets (gross): **$140,000**  
- Valuation allowance for deferred tax assets: **$55,000**  
- Deferred tax liabilities: **$100,000**  

How should Larkspur report deferred taxes on its classified balance sheet?

- A) Noncurrent deferred tax asset $140,000 and noncurrent deferred tax liability $100,000 (no offset)  
- B) Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net $85,000  
- C) Noncurrent deferred tax liability, net $15,000  
- D) Noncurrent deferred tax asset, net $40,000 and noncurrent deferred tax liability $100,000  

**Answer:** C.  
Net DTA \(= 140{,}000 - 55{,}000 = 85{,}000\).  
DTL \(100{,}000 > 85{,}000\), so report **one noncurrent deferred tax liability, net** of \(100{,}000 - 85{,}000 = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\).  
ASC 740 requires offset of DTA, related VA, and DTL for the same taxpaying component and jurisdiction into a **single noncurrent** amount (A and D incorrectly present gross or dual amounts; B treats the net as an asset when the DTL is larger).

### Q5 — MC — Effect of period-end increase in valuation allowance
**LO:** LO 18-5  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE — increase in valuation allowance increases income tax expense  
**Question:**  
At year-end, Glacier Peak Corp. increases its Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset by **$12,000** because new negative evidence makes realization of a portion of the DTA less than more-likely-than-not. Gross DTA and DTL are otherwise unchanged. What is the immediate effect of this period-end adjusting entry alone?

- A) Decrease income tax expense $12,000; no effect on Income Tax Payable  
- B) Increase income tax expense $12,000; no effect on Income Tax Payable  
- C) Increase Income Tax Payable $12,000; no effect on income tax expense  
- D) Decrease Deferred Tax Liability $12,000  

**Answer:** B.  
JE: Dr Income Tax Expense 12,000 / Cr Valuation Allowance for Deferred Tax Asset 12,000.  
This is an **accounting estimate** change for realizability — it does **not** change taxes currently payable. Increasing the contra-asset reduces net DTA and increases tax expense (and typically the effective tax rate reconciliation line for “change in valuation allowance”).
