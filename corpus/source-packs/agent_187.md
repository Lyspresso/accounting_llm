# Agent 187 — CORE DEMO — LO 9-9

**Chapter:** 9  
**LO title:** Perform inventory ratio analysis and interpretation  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Inventory turnover = Cost of goods sold ÷ Average inventory
- Average days in inventory = 365 ÷ Inventory turnover
- Gross profit percentage = Gross profit ÷ Net sales
- Average inventory = (Beginning inventory + Ending inventory) ÷ 2
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** inventory rollforward (beg + purchases + freight − returns − COGS = end) and multi-year / multi-method ratio tables
- **Initial recognition JE** of merchandise purchases and freight-in (perpetual)
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** sale + COGS (inventory derecognition); settlement of accounts payable; purchase returns
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** LIFO conversion (increase LIFO reserve → debit COGS, credit inventory allowance)
- Method effects when prices rise: LIFO → higher COGS, lower inventory, **higher turnover**, lower days, lower GP% than FIFO
- Comparability: restate LIFO to FIFO using the **LIFO reserve** before peer ratio comparison
- Interpretation: higher turnover / lower days generally preferred; excess vs insufficient inventory vs industry; method differences ≠ physical flow differences

### Q1 — CORE — Cedarhaven Distributors: inventory rollforward schedule and multi-year ratios (subsequent measurement schedule emphasis)
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement inventory rollforward schedule; initial recognition of purchases/freight; settlement and sale/COGS disposal; multi-year inventory turnover, days in inventory, and gross profit % with interpretation  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarhaven Distributors Inc.** is a wholesale merchandiser using a **perpetual inventory system**. Fiscal year ends **December 31**. Selected balances:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Inventory — Dec 31, Year 0 | $320,000 |
| Inventory — Dec 31, Year 1 | 360,000 |
| Net sales — Year 1 | 2,400,000 |
| Cost of goods sold — Year 1 | 1,440,000 |
| Net sales — Year 2 | 2,700,000 |

**Year 2 activity (chronological summary):**  
1. Purchased merchandise on account, **$1,580,000** (invoice gross).  
2. Paid freight-in of **$42,000** cash on Year 2 purchases (f.o.b. shipping point; inventoriable).  
3. Returned defective merchandise to suppliers; accounts payable credit **$28,000** (inventory returned).  
4. Paid the remaining Year 2 accounts payable related to these purchases in cash.  
5. Sold merchandise on account for **$2,700,000**; cost of merchandise sold **$1,620,000**.  
6. Year-end physical count agrees with the perpetual records (no inventory shrink adjustment).

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the **initial recognition** journal entries for Year 2 purchases and freight-in.  
(b) Prepare the journal entries for the **purchase return** and for **settlement** of the related accounts payable.  
(c) Prepare the journal entries for the **sale** and for **cost of goods sold** (inventory disposal / derecognition).  
(d) Prepare the **inventory rollforward schedule** (subsequent measurement of the inventory account) for Year 2 and prove ending inventory.  
(e) Prepare a **ratio analysis schedule** for **Year 1 and Year 2** showing: (1) average inventory, (2) inventory turnover, (3) average days in inventory, (4) gross profit, and (5) gross profit percentage. Round turnover and days to **two** decimals; round percentages to **two** decimals. (Compute turnover to two decimals first, then divide 365 by that rounded turnover for days.)  
(f) Briefly **interpret** the Year 1 → Year 2 trend (efficiency and margin).

**Answer key:**

**(a) Initial recognition — Year 2 purchases and freight**  
```
Dr Inventory ................................ 1,580,000
   Cr Accounts Payable ............................... 1,580,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 1,580,000)

```
Dr Inventory ................................... 42,000
   Cr Cash .............................................. 42,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 42,000)

**(b) Purchase return and AP settlement**  
```
Dr Accounts Payable ........................... 28,000
   Cr Inventory ......................................... 28,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 28,000)

Net AP from these purchases = $1,580,000 − $28,000 = **$1,552,000**

```
Dr Accounts Payable ........................ 1,552,000
   Cr Cash ........................................... 1,552,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 1,552,000)

**(c) Sale and inventory disposal (COGS)**  
```
Dr Accounts Receivable ..................... 2,700,000
   Cr Sales Revenue .................................. 2,700,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 2,700,000)

```
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ...................... 1,620,000
   Cr Inventory ...................................... 1,620,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 1,620,000)

**(d) Inventory rollforward schedule — Year 2 (subsequent measurement)**  

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory (Jan 1, Year 2) | $360,000 |
| + Purchases | 1,580,000 |
| + Freight-in | 42,000 |
| − Purchase returns | (28,000) |
| − Cost of goods sold | (1,620,000) |
| **Ending inventory (Dec 31, Year 2)** | **$334,000** |

Check: \(360{,}000 + 1{,}580{,}000 + 42{,}000 - 28{,}000 - 1{,}620{,}000 = 334{,}000\).

**(e) Ratio analysis schedule**  

| Year | Net sales | COGS | Gross profit | GP % | Average inventory | Inventory turnover | Avg days in inventory |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 | $2,400,000 | $1,440,000 | $960,000 | **40.00%** | \((320{,}000 + 360{,}000)/2 = 340{,}000\) | \(1{,}440{,}000 / 340{,}000 = \mathbf{4.24}\) | \(365 / 4.24 = \mathbf{86.08}\) days |
| Year 2 | $2,700,000 | $1,620,000 | $1,080,000 | **40.00%** | \((360{,}000 + 334{,}000)/2 = 347{,}000\) | \(1{,}620{,}000 / 347{,}000 = \mathbf{4.67}\) | \(365 / 4.67 = \mathbf{78.16}\) days |

Gross profit checks:  
- Year 1: \(2{,}400{,}000 - 1{,}440{,}000 = 960{,}000\); \(960{,}000 / 2{,}400{,}000 = 40.00\%\).  
- Year 2: \(2{,}700{,}000 - 1{,}620{,}000 = 1{,}080{,}000\); \(1{,}080{,}000 / 2{,}700{,}000 = 40.00\%\).

Turnover checks (unrounded then rounded):  
- Year 1: \(1{,}440{,}000 / 340{,}000 = 4.23529\ldots \rightarrow 4.24\).  
- Year 2: \(1{,}620{,}000 / 347{,}000 = 4.66859\ldots \rightarrow 4.67\).

**(f) Interpretation**  
Inventory turnover **increased** from 4.24 to 4.67 and average days in inventory **decreased** from 86.08 to 78.16, indicating **improved inventory velocity** (fewer days of resources tied up in inventory). Gross profit percentage remained **stable at 40.00%**, so the efficiency gain did not come from sacrificing product margin. Sales and COGS both grew while average inventory rose only modestly—consistent with better inventory management relative to sales volume.

**Key insight:** The inventory rollforward is the **subsequent measurement schedule** that proves ending inventory used in average inventory; ratios are only as good as the inventory and COGS amounts that feed them. Higher turnover / lower days is generally preferred when not signaling stockouts.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Harborcrest Supply: multi-method ratio schedule, LIFO period-end adjusting JE, and peer comparability
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Period-end LIFO conversion adjusting JE; subsequent measurement ratio schedule under LIFO vs FIFO (LIFO reserve restatement); method effects when prices rise; peer comparison after adjustment  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborcrest Supply Co.** keeps **internal perpetual records on FIFO** and converts to **LIFO for external reporting** at year-end. Inventory acquisition costs have been **rising**. Selected data:

| Item | Dec 31, Year 2 | Dec 31, Year 3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (LIFO, as reported) | $480,000 | $510,000 |
| LIFO reserve (FIFO inventory − LIFO inventory) | 72,000 | 95,000 |

| Item | Year 3 |
|---|---:|
| Net sales | $4,200,000 |
| Cost of goods sold (LIFO, as reported) | 2,850,000 |

**Peer:** **Maplebridge Retailers Inc.** uses **FIFO** and reports for Year 3: beginning inventory **$560,000**, ending inventory **$590,000**, COGS **$2,750,000**.

**Required:**  
(a) Compute the **increase in the LIFO reserve** for Year 3 and prepare the **period-end adjusting journal entry** that converts FIFO books to LIFO (debit COGS; credit inventory allowance / LIFO reserve).  
(b) Compute **FIFO inventory** at Dec 31, Year 2 and Dec 31, Year 3, and **FIFO COGS** for Year 3.  
(c) Prepare a **ratio comparison schedule** for Harborcrest under **LIFO (as reported)** and **FIFO (as if)** for Year 3: average inventory, inventory turnover, average days in inventory, gross profit, and gross profit percentage (same rounding as Q1).  
(d) Compute Maplebridge’s Year 3 inventory turnover and average days in inventory.  
(e) **Interpret:** On an as-reported basis, how does Harborcrest’s turnover compare to Maplebridge’s? After restating Harborcrest to FIFO, what changes? Why does method matter?

**Answer key:**

**(a) LIFO reserve increase and period-end adjusting JE**  
Increase in LIFO reserve = $95,000 − $72,000 = **$23,000**

```
Dr Cost of Goods Sold ......................... 23,000
   Cr Inventory Allowance (LIFO reserve) ............... 23,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 23,000)

*To increase the LIFO reserve (reduce inventory carrying amount from FIFO to LIFO) and recognize the additional LIFO COGS when prices rose.*

**(b) FIFO amounts**  
- FIFO inventory Dec 31, Year 2 = $480,000 + $72,000 = **$552,000**  
- FIFO inventory Dec 31, Year 3 = $510,000 + $95,000 = **$605,000**  
- FIFO COGS Year 3 = LIFO COGS − increase in LIFO reserve = $2,850,000 − $23,000 = **$2,827,000**

**(c) Harborcrest ratio schedule — LIFO vs FIFO (Year 3)**  

| Basis | COGS | Gross profit | GP % | Average inventory | Inventory turnover | Avg days |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **LIFO (as reported)** | $2,850,000 | $1,350,000 | **32.14%** | \((480{,}000 + 510{,}000)/2 = 495{,}000\) | \(2{,}850{,}000 / 495{,}000 = \mathbf{5.76}\) | \(365 / 5.76 = \mathbf{63.37}\) |
| **FIFO (as if)** | $2,827,000 | $1,373,000 | **32.69%** | \((552{,}000 + 605{,}000)/2 = 578{,}500\) | \(2{,}827{,}000 / 578{,}500 = \mathbf{4.89}\) | \(365 / 4.89 = \mathbf{74.64}\) |

Checks:  
- GP LIFO: \(4{,}200{,}000 - 2{,}850{,}000 = 1{,}350{,}000\); \(1{,}350{,}000 / 4{,}200{,}000 = 32.1429\% \rightarrow 32.14\%\).  
- GP FIFO: \(4{,}200{,}000 - 2{,}827{,}000 = 1{,}373{,}000\); \(1{,}373{,}000 / 4{,}200{,}000 = 32.6905\% \rightarrow 32.69\%\).  
- TO LIFO: \(2{,}850{,}000 / 495{,}000 = 5.75758\ldots \rightarrow 5.76\).  
- TO FIFO: \(2{,}827{,}000 / 578{,}500 = 4.88678\ldots \rightarrow 4.89\).

**(d) Maplebridge (FIFO peer)**  
Average inventory = \((560{,}000 + 590{,}000)/2 = 575{,}000\)  
Inventory turnover = \(2{,}750{,}000 / 575{,}000 = 4.78261\ldots = \mathbf{4.78}\)  
Average days = \(365 / 4.78 = \mathbf{76.36}\) days  

**(e) Interpretation**  
On **as-reported** bases, Harborcrest’s LIFO turnover (**5.76**) looks much stronger than Maplebridge’s FIFO turnover (**4.78**). That gap is largely **method-driven**: with rising prices, LIFO reports **higher COGS** and **lower average inventory**, both of which **inflate turnover** (and shrink days) even when physical flow is similar. After restating Harborcrest to FIFO, turnover is **4.89** vs Maplebridge **4.78**—nearly comparable velocity. Users should adjust LIFO companies to FIFO (using the LIFO reserve) **before** ranking inventory efficiency.

**Key insight (Demo 9-9):** Ratios differ across methods even when physical flow is identical. Consistency over time and common-basis restatement across companies are essential for interpretation.

### Q4 — MC — Method effect on inventory turnover (rising prices)
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Classification of cost-flow method effects on inventory turnover when acquisition costs are rising  
**Question:**  
Assuming **rising** inventory purchase prices and identical physical inventory activity, which cost-flow assumption typically produces the **highest inventory turnover** ratio?

- A) FIFO  
- B) Weighted-average cost  
- C) LIFO  
- D) Specific identification (always highest regardless of price trend)

**Answer:** **C.** With rising prices, LIFO assigns higher (more recent) costs to COGS and lower (older) costs to ending inventory. Higher COGS and lower average inventory both increase inventory turnover relative to FIFO; average cost typically falls between them. Specific identification depends on which units are sold, not a systematic “always highest” result.

### Q5 — MC — Computing inventory turnover and days
**LO:** LO 9-9  
**Concept:** Correct computation of inventory turnover and average days in inventory  
**Question:**  
**Ironquill Merchants** reports cost of goods sold of **$684,000**, beginning inventory of **$108,000**, and ending inventory of **$132,000**. What are inventory turnover and average days in inventory (use 365 days; round turnover to two decimals and days to two decimals using the rounded turnover)?

- A) Turnover 5.70; days 64.04  
- B) Turnover 5.18; days 70.46  
- C) Turnover 6.33; days 57.66  
- D) Turnover 5.18; days 70.00  

**Answer:** **A.** Average inventory = \((108{,}000 + 132{,}000)/2 = 120{,}000\). Inventory turnover = \(684{,}000 / 120{,}000 = 5.70\). Average days = \(365 / 5.70 = 64.035\ldots = 64.04\) days.  
(B uses ending inventory only as the denominator; C uses beginning only; D mis-rounds days.)
