# Agent 99 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-6

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Explain liability, commitment, and contingency disclosures and analyses using liquidity ratios  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** accrue loss contingency when loss is **probable** and **reasonably estimable** (ASC 450-20); range with better estimate → that amount; no better estimate → **minimum** of range
- **Assurance-type warranty** period-end provision and claims; **warranty liability rollforward** (beginning + provision − claims = ending)
- **Settlement JE:** remove accrued contingency liability; difference vs cash paid → gain or loss on settlement
- **Commitments vs contingencies:** future commitments (plant acquisition, unused LOC, working-capital covenants) generally **disclosed, not accrued** until a present obligation exists; contingencies accrued, disclosed, or neither based on likelihood/estimability
- **Disclosure content:** nature of contingency + estimate of possible loss/range (or statement that estimate cannot be made); SEC 5% current-liability composition; balance-sheet line “Commitments and Contingencies” pointing to notes
- **Liquidity ratios:** current ratio = CA ÷ CL; quick ratio = (Cash + marketable securities + receivables) ÷ CL; impact of accruing contingencies on both ratios

### Q1 — CORE — Contingency initial recognition, warranty schedule, settlement, disclosures & liquidity
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of probable litigation contingency; warranty provision and rollforward; settlement JE; note disclosure; current and quick ratios before/after accruals  
**Scenario:**  
**CedarPeak Appliances Inc.** (calendar-year) is finalizing December 31, **Year 1** financial statements. Statements will be issued March 10, Year 2.

**Unadjusted balances at December 31, Year 1** (after recording warranty *claims* cash payments during the year as Warranty Expense, but **before** year-end contingency and warranty true-up accruals):

| Current assets | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash and cash equivalents | $420,000 |
| Short-term marketable securities | 180,000 |
| Accounts receivable (net) | 960,000 |
| Inventory | 1,480,000 |
| Prepaid expenses | 140,000 |
| **Total current assets** | **$3,180,000** |

| Current liabilities | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Accounts payable | $720,000 |
| Accrued expenses (payroll, interest, taxes) | 480,000 |
| Short-term notes payable | 600,000 |
| Unearned revenue | 200,000 |
| Warranty liability (beginning balance still on books) | 48,000 |
| **Total current liabilities (unadjusted)** | **$2,048,000** |

**Additional facts:**

1. **Litigation (loss contingency).** A customer filed suit in Year 1 alleging injury from a defective appliance. Outside counsel concludes a loss is **probable**. The estimated range of loss is **$200,000–$400,000**; the **best estimate** within the range is **$250,000**. No amount has been recorded.

2. **Assurance-type warranties.** Year 1 product sales were **$4,500,000**. Management estimates total warranty cost at **2.5% of sales**. Actual warranty repair/replacement costs paid in cash during Year 1 were **$95,000** (already debited to Warranty Expense). The January 1 warranty liability of **$48,000** remains on the books; record the year-end adjusting entry so ending warranty liability equals beginning + estimated Year 1 warranty cost − claims paid.

3. **Commitments (not on books).** On December 15, Year 1, CedarPeak signed a noncancelable contract to acquire a new warehouse for **$1,800,000** (closing expected April Year 2). It also has an **unused** bank letter of credit of **$500,000**.

4. **Settlement (Year 2).** On February 20, Year 2 (before statements are issued), the lawsuit settles for cash of **$215,000**. Counsel confirms the settlement relates to conditions existing at December 31, Year 1. (For ratio parts, use the **$250,000** year-end accrual unless instructed otherwise.)

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1 initial recognition JE** for the litigation loss contingency.  
b. Compute the **year-end warranty adjusting amount** and prepare the **period-end adjusting JE**. Prepare a complete **warranty liability rollforward** (beginning, provision, claims, ending).  
c. Prepare the **settlement JE** on February 20, Year 2 (using the Year 1 accrued amount of $250,000 and cash paid of $215,000). Explain briefly whether Year 1 statements (issued March 10) would use $250,000 or $215,000.  
d. **Classification / disclosure:** For each item—(i) litigation accrual, (ii) additional warranty liability, (iii) warehouse purchase commitment, (iv) unused letter of credit—state whether it is **accrued**, **disclosed only**, both, or neither, and what note content is required.  
e. Compute the **current ratio** and **quick ratio** (1) on the **unadjusted** balances and (2) **after** the Year 1 litigation and warranty accruals in (a)–(b). Briefly analyze the effect of the accruals on short-term liquidity measures.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — litigation (probable and reasonably estimable; best estimate in range)**

Best estimate within range = **$250,000** (ASC 450-20-30-1).

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrue loss contingency (initial recognition)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation (Litigation Expense) | 250,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 250,000 |
| *Probable and reasonably estimable loss contingency; best estimate accrued* | | |

**Check:** Dr 250,000 = Cr 250,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Warranty period-end adjustment and rollforward**

Estimated total Year 1 warranty cost = 2.5% × $4,500,000 = **$112,500**  
Claims already expensed in Year 1 = **$95,000**  
Additional period-end provision needed = $112,500 − $95,000 = **$17,500**

Ending warranty liability target:  
$48,000 + $112,500 − $95,000 = **$65,500**  
(Equivalently: $48,000 + $17,500 = **$65,500**)

*December 31, Year 1 — Period-end warranty adjusting entry*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 17,500 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 17,500 |
| *True up liability so ending balance = beg + estimated cost − claims* | | |

**Check:** Dr 17,500 = Cr 17,500. **Balanced.**

**Warranty liability rollforward (subsequent measurement schedule)**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning warranty liability, Jan 1, Year 1 | $48,000 |
| + Warranty provision (total estimated cost for Year 1 sales) | 112,500 |
| − Warranty claims paid in Year 1 | (95,000) |
| **Ending warranty liability, Dec 31, Year 1** | **$65,500** |

**Rollforward check:** 48,000 + 112,500 − 95,000 = **65,500**.

(Presentation: total Warranty Expense for Year 1 = $95,000 already recorded + $17,500 adjust = **$112,500**.)

**c. Settlement JE and recognized subsequent event**

Cash paid $215,000 < accrued $250,000 → **gain on settlement $35,000**.

*February 20, Year 2 — Settle litigation contingency*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated Litigation Liability | 250,000 | |
| Cash | | 215,000 |
| Gain on Litigation Settlement | | 35,000 |
| *Remove accrued liability; difference vs cash is gain* | | |

**Check:** Dr 250,000 = Cr 215,000 + 35,000. **Balanced.**

**Year 1 reporting amount:** Settlement after the balance sheet date but **before issuance** provides evidence of conditions that **existed at Dec 31, Year 1** → **recognized subsequent event**. Year 1 statements should reflect **$215,000** (not $250,000), with the income-statement loss reduced (or remeasured) to $215,000. (If the problem sequence records the $250,000 accrual first for instructional purposes, the recognized subsequent-event adjustment is Dr Estimated Litigation Liability 35,000 / Cr Loss on Litigation 35,000 before issuance.)

*Recognized subsequent-event remeasurement (if adjusting Year 1 books before issuance):*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated Litigation Liability | 35,000 | |
| Loss on Litigation | | 35,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 35,000. **Balanced.** Then settlement entry would clear the remaining $215,000 liability against cash with no gain/loss.

**d. Classification / disclosure**

| Item | Accrue? | Disclose? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) Litigation ($250k best estimate; probable) | **Yes** | **Yes** (nature + that amount accrued; also disclose exposure if range exceeds accrual) | Accrual of $250,000; disclose nature and that reasonably possible losses above accrual may exist up to the high end of the range ($400,000), i.e., additional exposure of up to $150,000 |
| (ii) Warranty liability ending $65,500 | **Yes** | **Yes** (policy + often tabular reconciliation of warranty liability) | Current liability; product warranty guarantee disclosures (ASC 460) |
| (iii) Warehouse purchase commitment $1,800,000 | **No** (not a present liability until title/closing creates obligation under the contract accounting) | **Yes** | Commitment for plant/facility acquisition (ASC 440-10-50) — nature and amount |
| (iv) Unused letter of credit $500,000 | **No** | **Yes** | Unused letters of credit must be disclosed (ASC 440-10-50-1) |

Face of balance sheet may include a **“Commitments and Contingencies”** line (often no dollar amount) directing users to the notes.

**e. Liquidity ratios**

Quick assets = Cash + ST marketable securities + AR  
= $420,000 + $180,000 + $960,000 = **$1,560,000**

**After Year 1 accruals (using instructional $250,000 litigation + $17,500 warranty):**  
Adjusted CL = $2,048,000 + $250,000 + $17,500 = **$2,315,500**  
(CA unchanged at **$3,180,000** for this analysis.)

| Ratio | Formula | Unadjusted | After accruals |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Current ratio** | CA ÷ CL | $3,180,000 ÷ $2,048,000 = **1.55** | $3,180,000 ÷ $2,315,500 = **1.37** |
| **Quick ratio** | Quick assets ÷ CL | $1,560,000 ÷ $2,048,000 = **0.76** | $1,560,000 ÷ $2,315,500 = **0.67** |

**Analysis:** Accruing the litigation contingency and the incremental warranty liability **increases current liabilities** with no matching current asset, so **both** the current ratio and the quick ratio **decline**. The quick ratio is lower than the current ratio in both columns because inventory and prepaids are excluded from the numerator—those assets are less liquid. Users rely on these ratios for **short-term solvency**, not profitability.

**Key insight:** LO 15-6 ties **when to put contingencies on the books** (initial recognition if probable + estimable) to **what still must be told in the notes** (commitments and unaccrued contingencies) and to **how accruals change liquidity ratios** that creditors watch.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Contingency cycle twin + liquidity
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: min-of-range litigation accrual, warranty rollforward, settlement with loss, commitment disclosure, current/quick ratios  
**Scenario:**  
**Northline Outdoor Gear Co.** (calendar-year) prepares December 31, **Year 1** statements (issuance March 5, Year 2).

**Unadjusted balances at December 31, Year 1** (claims already recorded as Warranty Expense; before year-end litigation and warranty true-up):

| Current assets | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash and cash equivalents | $680,000 |
| Short-term investments | 220,000 |
| Accounts receivable (net) | 1,150,000 |
| Inventory | 2,100,000 |
| Other current assets | 250,000 |
| **Total current assets** | **$4,400,000** |

| Current liabilities | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Accounts payable | $980,000 |
| Accrued liabilities | 640,000 |
| Current portion of long-term debt | 400,000 |
| Deferred revenue (current) | 280,000 |
| Warranty liability (Jan 1 balance still on books) | 72,000 |
| **Total current liabilities (unadjusted)** | **$2,372,000** |

**Additional facts:**

1. **Environmental remediation contingency.** Investigation in Year 1 indicates contamination from historical operations. Loss is **probable**. Estimated range **$300,000–$500,000**; **no amount is a better estimate** than any other. Nothing recorded yet.

2. **Assurance-type warranties.** Year 1 sales **$6,000,000**; estimated warranty cost **1.8% of sales**. Claims paid in Year 1 (already expensed) **$90,000**. True up warranty liability at year-end.

3. **Commitment.** Board approved a noncancelable equipment purchase commitment of **$2,400,000** for delivery in Year 2 (not yet a recorded liability).

4. **Settlement.** On January 28, Year 2, the environmental matter is settled for cash **$340,000** (before issuance). Treat as evidence of conditions existing at year-end for disclosure/remeasurement discussion; for parts (a)–(b) first record the year-end accrual per ASC 450 range rule.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **initial recognition JE** for the environmental contingency at Dec 31, Year 1 (apply range rule).  
b. Compute warranty true-up; record **period-end adjusting JE**; complete **warranty rollforward**.  
c. Record **settlement JE** against the amount accrued in (a) when cash of $340,000 is paid (illustrative settlement entry vs. carrying amount).  
d. State note disclosure for: accrued environmental liability, equipment commitment, and any additional loss exposure from the range.  
e. Compute **current** and **quick** ratios unadjusted and after the Year 1 accruals in (a)–(b).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — range with no better estimate → accrue minimum**

Minimum of range = **$300,000**.

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrue environmental loss contingency*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Environmental Remediation Expense (Loss) | 300,000 | |
| Accrued Environmental Liability | | 300,000 |
| *Probable and estimable; no better estimate → accrue minimum of range* | | |

**Check:** Dr 300,000 = Cr 300,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Warranty adjustment and rollforward**

Estimated total Year 1 warranty cost = 1.8% × $6,000,000 = **$108,000**  
Additional provision = $108,000 − $90,000 = **$18,000**  
Ending warranty liability = $72,000 + $108,000 − $90,000 = **$90,000**

*December 31, Year 1 — Warranty adjusting entry*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 18,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 18,000 |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**

**Warranty liability rollforward**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning warranty liability | $72,000 |
| + Provision (1.8% × $6,000,000) | 108,000 |
| − Claims paid | (90,000) |
| **Ending warranty liability** | **$90,000** |

**Check:** 72,000 + 108,000 − 90,000 = **90,000**.

**c. Settlement vs. $300,000 carrying amount**

Cash $340,000 − accrued $300,000 = **loss $40,000**.

*Settlement entry (illustrative against Year 1 minimum accrual)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accrued Environmental Liability | 300,000 | |
| Loss on Environmental Settlement | 40,000 | |
| Cash | | 340,000 |

**Check:** Dr 300,000 + 40,000 = Cr 340,000. **Balanced.**

*(If treated as recognized subsequent event before issuance, Year 1 would be remeasured to $340,000: Dr Loss 40,000 / Cr Accrued Environmental Liability 40,000, then settle Dr Liability 340,000 / Cr Cash 340,000.)*

**d. Disclosure**

- **Accrued environmental liability $300,000:** nature of remediation contingency; amount accrued; **additional reasonably possible loss** of up to **$200,000** ($500,000 − $300,000) above the accrual (or state full range).  
- **Equipment purchase commitment $2,400,000:** disclose as a **commitment** (not accrued as a liability solely because the board approved a future purchase).  
- Warranty: policy and rollforward of warranty liability (common guarantee disclosure).

**e. Liquidity ratios**

Quick assets = $680,000 + $220,000 + $1,150,000 = **$2,050,000**  
Adjusted CL = $2,372,000 + $300,000 + $18,000 = **$2,690,000**

| Ratio | Unadjusted | After accruals |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Current ratio** | $4,400,000 ÷ $2,372,000 = **1.86** | $4,400,000 ÷ $2,690,000 = **1.64** |
| **Quick ratio** | $2,050,000 ÷ $2,372,000 = **0.86** | $2,050,000 ÷ $2,690,000 = **0.76** |

**Key insight:** Same core path as Q1 with **all numbers changed**. When the range has **no better estimate**, accrue the **minimum** and **disclose** the rest of the range—accrual still **worsens** current and quick ratios.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Commitments vs contingencies classification, callable debt, liquidity covenant
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Classification/presentation of commitments and contingencies; period-end reclassification of callable debt; liquidity ratio impact of classification and of accruing a contingency  
**Scenario:**  
**HarborVista Manufacturing LLC** has a December 31, Year 5 balance sheet date. Selected items under review:

| Item | Facts |
|---|---|
| A | Unused revolving line of credit capacity **$2,000,000** (no borrowings outstanding on the unused portion) |
| B | Noncancelable commitment to purchase specialized production equipment in Year 6 for **$1,250,000** |
| C | Pending lawsuit (events occurred Year 5): loss **reasonably possible**, estimated range **$80,000–$150,000**, no best estimate |
| D | Product liability claims: loss **probable**, best estimate **$175,000** (not yet recorded) |
| E | Guarantee of a supplier’s bank loan; likelihood of performance by HarborVista is **remote**; guarantee maximum **$400,000** |

**Unadjusted current assets and liabilities (before Item D accrual and before any debt reclassification):**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Cash | $310,000 |
| Short-term marketable securities | 90,000 |
| Accounts receivable (net) | 540,000 |
| Inventory | 760,000 |
| **Total current assets** | **$1,700,000** |
| Accounts payable and accrued expenses | $520,000 |
| Short-term notes payable | 180,000 |
| Current portion of long-term debt (installments due in Year 6) | 100,000 |
| Long-term note payable (contractual maturity Year 10) | 400,000 *(currently classified noncurrent)* |
| **Total current liabilities (unadjusted)** | **$800,000** |

**Debt covenant:** The long-term note requires a **minimum current ratio of 1.80**. At December 31, Year 5, **before** accruing Item D and **before** reclassifying the note, the unadjusted current ratio is $1,700,000 ÷ $800,000 = **2.13**. On December 31 the company is **in violation** of a separate working-capital covenant on the **same** $400,000 note; the lender has **not** waived the right to call the note, and the violation is not cured within the contractual grace period before issuance. Under ASC 470-10-45, the **entire $400,000** becomes **callable** and must be classified as **current**.

**Required:**  
a. For items **A–E**, complete a classification table: Accrue (yes/no), Disclose (yes/no), and one-line rationale.  
b. Record the **period-end adjusting JE** for Item D only (initial recognition of the probable product-liability contingency).  
c. After accruing Item D **and** reclassifying the $400,000 callable note to current, recompute **total current liabilities**, the **current ratio**, and the **quick ratio**.  
d. Draft the minimum **note disclosure** elements for Item C and for the plant/equipment commitment (Item B).  
e. Suppose instead the lender **waives** the call right for more than one year from the balance sheet date before issuance. Recompute the current ratio after accruing only Item D (note stays noncurrent).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Classification table**

| Item | Accrue? | Disclose? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Unused LOC $2,000,000 | No | **Yes** | Commitment / unused letter of credit capacity — ASC 440 disclosure; not a present liability |
| B Equipment purchase commitment $1,250,000 | No | **Yes** | Future commitment for plant/equipment acquisition — disclose nature and amount |
| C Reasonably possible lawsuit $80k–$150k | No | **Yes** | At least reasonably possible → disclose nature + range (or that estimate cannot be made); do **not** accrue (not probable) |
| D Probable product liability $175,000 | **Yes** | **Yes** | Probable and reasonably estimable → accrue; disclose nature (and any additional exposure if applicable) |
| E Remote supplier-loan guarantee $400,000 | No* | **Yes** | Guarantees require disclosure even when remote (*stand-ready guarantee liability under ASC 460 may apply at fair value of the guarantee—exam core often emphasizes contingency remote → no ASC 450 accrual; still disclose guarantee*) |

**b. Period-end adjusting JE — Item D**

*December 31, Year 5 — Accrue product liability contingency*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Product Liability Loss (Expense) | 175,000 | |
| Accrued Product Liability | | 175,000 |

**Check:** Dr 175,000 = Cr 175,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Ratios after accrual + callable reclassification**

Adjusted current liabilities:  
$800,000 + $175,000 (Item D) + $400,000 (reclass of callable LTD) = **$1,375,000**

Quick assets = $310,000 + $90,000 + $540,000 = **$940,000**  
CA remains **$1,700,000** (no cash paid yet).

| Measure | Amount / ratio |
|---|---:|
| Current liabilities | **$1,375,000** |
| **Current ratio** | $1,700,000 ÷ $1,375,000 = **1.24** |
| **Quick ratio** | $940,000 ÷ $1,375,000 = **0.68** |

**Analysis:** Accruing the contingency alone would reduce liquidity modestly; **reclassifying callable long-term debt to current** sharply increases CL and can destroy covenant headroom (current ratio falls from 2.13 unadjusted to **1.24**). This is why LO 15-6 pairs **classification/disclosure** with **liquidity analysis**.

**d. Minimum disclosure content**

- **Item C (reasonably possible litigation):** (1) nature of the contingency; (2) estimate of possible loss or range (**$80,000–$150,000**) or a statement that an estimate cannot be made.  
- **Item B (equipment commitment):** existence of the commitment, approximate amount (**$1,250,000**), and timing (Year 6 delivery) as a **commitment** note—not recorded as a liability solely from signing the future purchase commitment in this LO framework.

**e. Waiver alternative (note remains noncurrent)**

CL = $800,000 + $175,000 = **$975,000**  
Current ratio = $1,700,000 ÷ $975,000 = **1.74**  
Quick ratio = $940,000 ÷ $975,000 = **0.96**

Even with a waiver, accruing Item D **reduces** the current ratio (2.13 → 1.74) and may still pressure a 1.80 covenant if other adjustments occur.

**Key insight:** Commitments and many contingencies live in the **notes**; only **probable + estimable** losses hit the balance sheet. **Callable-debt reclassification** and contingency **accruals** both inflate current liabilities and are first-order drivers of the **current** and **quick** ratios.

### Q4 — MC — Accrue vs disclose; liquidity impact
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Classification of loss contingency accounting treatment (accrue vs disclose vs neither)  
**Question:** Which of the following loss contingencies is **accrued** (recognized as a liability) at the balance sheet date under U.S. GAAP (ASC 450)?

- A) Loss is **remote**; amount is reasonably estimable  
- B) Loss is **reasonably possible**; amount is reasonably estimable  
- C) Loss is **probable**; amount **cannot** be reasonably estimated  
- D) Loss is **probable**; amount is reasonably estimable  

**Answer:** **D.** Accrual requires **both** (1) probable that a liability has been incurred by the balance sheet date and (2) the amount can be reasonably estimated.  
- A → generally neither accrue nor disclose (except certain guarantees).  
- B → **disclose only** (nature + estimate/range).  
- C → **disclose only** (probable but not estimable).  
- D → **accrue** and typically disclose.

### Q5 — MC — Effect on current ratio
**LO:** LO 15-6  
**Concept:** Effect of accruing a current loss contingency on the current ratio  
**Question:** A company accrues a **$100,000** probable, estimable litigation liability expected to be paid within six months. Current assets are unchanged. What is the effect on the **current ratio** and the **quick ratio**?

- A) Current ratio increases; quick ratio increases  
- B) Current ratio decreases; quick ratio decreases  
- C) Current ratio decreases; quick ratio unchanged  
- D) Both ratios unchanged because the item is only disclosed  

**Answer:** **B.** The credit increases **current liabilities** (denominator of both ratios) with no increase in current or quick assets, so **both ratios decrease**. The item is accrued, not disclosure-only.
