# Agent 322 — CORE DEMO — LO 10-4

**Chapter:** 10  
**LO title:** Demonstrate the gross profit method to estimate inventory  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** period_end_adjusting_JE

## Concept list for this pack
- Gross profit method two-step process: (1) express gross profit as a **percentage of sales**; (2) estimate COGS, then ending inventory
- Convert **markup on cost** to markup on sales: GP%_sales = markup_on_cost ÷ (1 + markup_on_cost); cost % of sales = 1 − GP%_sales
- Estimation identity: Beginning inventory + Net purchases = GAS − Estimated COGS = Estimated ending inventory
- **Period-end adjusting JE (emphasis):** under a periodic system, book estimated ending inventory and estimated COGS at interim (and similar close-style entries); debit ending Inventory + COGS; credit beginning Inventory + Purchases
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** multi-period rollforward where each period’s estimated EI becomes the next period’s BI
- **Initial recognition JE (casualty):** remove estimated inventory on hand at loss date; reclassify salvage; recognize inventory loss
- **Settlement JE:** recognize insurance receivable when recovery is probable; cash settlement when insurer pays
- Multi-class application: when markups differ by line, estimate **separately** and sum (limitation of a single blended rate)
- Uses: interim reporting, casualty/theft estimates, reasonableness tests; limitations: outdated GP rates and sales-mix shifts

### Q1 — CORE — Northvale Outfitters: interim period-end adjusting JEs, multi-period GP schedule, storm loss & insurance

**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JEs for interim inventory under the gross profit method (emphasis); markup-on-cost conversion; subsequent measurement schedule; casualty write-off and insurance settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Northvale Outfitters LLC** uses a **periodic** inventory system and a **December 31** year-end. A full physical count is taken only at year-end. For **interim** reporting, management estimates inventory with the gross profit method. Past experience shows a consistent **markup of 25% of cost**.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Inventory, January 1 (physical count) | $360,000 |

Quarterly activity (net purchases and net sales):

| Period | Net purchases | Net sales |
|---|---:|---:|
| Q1 (Jan 1 – Mar 31) | $420,000 | $500,000 |
| Q2 (Apr 1 – Jun 30) | 390,000 | 480,000 |

**July 12, Year 1:** A severe storm destroys the warehouse inventory. Records for **July 1–12** show net purchases of **$48,000** and net sales of **$90,000**. Undamaged goods salvaged and still salable are valued at **cost of $40,000**. Expected insurance recovery of the book inventory loss is **probable** and reasonably estimable at **$300,000**. On **August 5**, the insurer pays **$300,000** cash in full settlement (equal to the receivable previously recognized).

Assume each interim quarter’s estimated ending inventory is booked via a **period-end adjusting entry** and becomes the beginning inventory carrying amount for the next period’s estimate. Purchases for each period are recorded in **Purchases** (periodic). Ignore income taxes.

**Required:**  
(a) Convert the markup on cost to **gross profit as a percentage of sales** and state the **cost percentage of sales**.  
(b) Prepare a **subsequent measurement schedule** rolling forward **estimated ending inventory** for Mar 31 and Jun 30 (show GAS, estimated COGS, and estimated EI for each quarter).  
(c) Prepare the **period-end adjusting journal entries** for **March 31** and **June 30** that recognize estimated COGS and set ending inventory (**emphasis**).  
(d) Using the Jun 30 estimated inventory as beginning inventory for July, prepare the **gross profit method schedule** estimating inventory on hand at the **July 12 storm date** (before salvage), and compute the **inventory loss**.  
(e) Prepare the **July 12 initial recognition journal entry** to reclassify salvage inventory, write off destroyed inventory, and record the casualty loss (**before** insurance).  
(f) Prepare the **July 12 journal entry** to recognize the **insurance receivable** for the expected recovery.  
(g) Prepare the **August 5 settlement journal entry** when cash is received.  
(h) Report inventory on the balance sheet immediately after the July 12 write-off (before insurance cash is received). State **one use** and **one limitation** of the gross profit method.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Markup conversion**  
\[
\text{GP\% of sales} = \frac{0.25}{1 + 0.25} = \frac{0.25}{1.25} = \mathbf{0.20\ (20\%)}
\]  
Cost percentage of sales = \(1 - 0.20 = \mathbf{80\%}\).

**(b) Subsequent measurement schedule — estimated inventory rollforward**

| Date / period | Beginning inv. | + Net purchases | = GAS | − Est. COGS (sales × 80%) | = Est. ending inv. |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Q1 → Mar 31 | $360,000 | $420,000 | $780,000 | \(500{,}000 \times 0.80 = \$400{,}000\) | **$380,000** |
| Q2 → Jun 30 | 380,000 | 390,000 | 770,000 | \(480{,}000 \times 0.80 = 384{,}000\) | **386,000** |

**Schedule math checks:**  
- Q1: \(780{,}000 - 400{,}000 = 380{,}000\).  
- Q2: \(770{,}000 - 384{,}000 = 386{,}000\).  
Each period’s estimated EI is the next period’s BI (subsequent measurement under the GP method).

**(c) Period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis)**

**March 31 — interim period-end adjusting entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending, estimated) | 380,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 400,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 360,000 |
| Purchases | | 420,000 |
| *Book Q1 estimated EI and COGS under GP method* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(380{,}000 + 400{,}000 = 780{,}000\); Cr \(360{,}000 + 420{,}000 = 780{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**June 30 — interim period-end adjusting entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending, estimated) | 386,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 384,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 380,000 |
| Purchases | | 390,000 |
| *Book Q2 estimated EI and COGS; BI is Q1 estimated EI* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(386{,}000 + 384{,}000 = 770{,}000\); Cr \(380{,}000 + 390{,}000 = 770{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**(d) Storm-date estimate and loss**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory, July 1 (Jun 30 estimated EI) | $386,000 |
| Add: Net purchases, July 1–12 | 48,000 |
| **Cost of goods available for sale** | **434,000** |
| Subtract: Cost of goods sold, estimated \($90{,}000 \times 0.80\) | (72,000) |
| **Ending inventory, estimated (at storm date)** | **$362,000** |

Inventory loss = \(362{,}000 - 40{,}000\) salvage = **$322,000**.

**(e) July 12 — initial recognition of casualty (write-off; no insurance yet)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Salvage | 40,000 | |
| Loss from Storm | 322,000 | |
| Inventory | | 362,000 |
| *Reclassify salvage; remove estimated inventory destroyed; recognize casualty loss* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(40{,}000 + 322{,}000 = 362{,}000\) = Cr \(362{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**(f) July 12 — recognize probable insurance recovery**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Insurance Receivable | 300,000 | |
| Loss from Storm | | 300,000 |
| *Reduce net casualty loss for probable, reasonably estimable recovery* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 300,000. **Balanced.**  
**Net casualty loss** after expected recovery = \(322{,}000 - 300{,}000 = \mathbf{\$22{,}000}\).

**(g) August 5 — insurance settlement (settlement JE)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 300,000 | |
| Insurance Receivable | | 300,000 |
| *Collect insurance claim equal to receivable previously recognized* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 300,000. **Balanced.** (Settlement equals the receivable; no additional gain/loss.)

**(h) Balance sheet — inventory presentation after July 12 write-off**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Inventory—Salvage (undamaged goods at cost) | $40,000 |
| **Total inventory reported** | **$40,000** |

(Insurance receivable of $300,000 is a **receivable**, not inventory.)

**Use (examples):** estimate interim ending inventory when a count is impractical; estimate inventory destroyed by storm/fire for accounting and insurance claims; test reasonableness of a physical count; budgeting.  
**Limitation (examples):** historical GP rate may not hold currently; different markups by product line or **sales-mix shifts** change the average GP rate—apply the method **separately by inventory class** when markups differ.

**Key insight:** Under a periodic system, the **period-end adjusting JE** is how the gross profit method hits the books for interim reporting: estimated COGS and estimated EI replace a physical count. The same two-step engine (convert markup → estimate COGS → plug EI) supports multi-period rollforwards and casualty measurements. Salvage reduces the loss; insurance recovery layers separately when probable, then settles in cash.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Pinnacle Patio: same structure, all numbers changed

**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin—period-end interim adjusting JEs under the gross profit method (emphasis); multi-period schedule; casualty write-off and insurance settlement  
**Scenario:**  
**Pinnacle Patio Furniture Co.** uses a **periodic** inventory system. Management estimates inventory each quarter with the gross profit method. Historical **markup is 50% of cost**. Inventory at **January 1** (physical) is **$180,000**.

| Period | Net purchases | Net sales |
|---|---:|---:|
| Q1 (Jan 1 – Mar 31) | $240,000 | $300,000 |
| Q2 (Apr 1 – Jun 30) | 255,000 | 360,000 |

**September 5:** A flood destroys warehouse inventory. July 1–September 5 records (treat as one post–June 30 measurement interval with BI = June 30 estimated EI): net purchases **$40,000**, net sales **$75,000**. Salvage at cost **$25,000**. Expected insurance recovery is **probable** at **$185,000**. On **October 2**, the insurer pays **$185,000** cash in full settlement.

Purchases are recorded in **Purchases**. Ignore income taxes.

**Required:**  
(a) Convert markup on cost to GP% of sales and cost % of sales.  
(b) Subsequent measurement schedule for Mar 31 and Jun 30.  
(c) Period-end adjusting JEs for March 31 and June 30 (emphasis).  
(d) GP schedule at flood date; inventory loss after salvage.  
(e) September 5 casualty write-off JE (before insurance).  
(f) September 5 insurance receivable JE.  
(g) October 2 cash settlement JE.  
(h) Net casualty loss after expected insurance recovery.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Markup conversion**  
\[
\text{GP\% of sales} = \frac{0.50}{1 + 0.50} = \frac{0.50}{1.50} = \mathbf{\tfrac{1}{3}\ (33\tfrac{1}{3}\%)}
\]  
Cost percentage of sales = \(1 - \tfrac{1}{3} = \mathbf{\tfrac{2}{3}}\) (exactly \(66\tfrac{2}{3}\%\)).

**(b) Subsequent measurement schedule**

| Date / period | Beginning inv. | + Net purchases | = GAS | − Est. COGS (sales × 2/3) | = Est. ending inv. |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Q1 → Mar 31 | $180,000 | $240,000 | $420,000 | \(300{,}000 \times \tfrac{2}{3} = \$200{,}000\) | **$220,000** |
| Q2 → Jun 30 | 220,000 | 255,000 | 475,000 | \(360{,}000 \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 240{,}000\) | **235,000** |

**Checks:** \(420{,}000 - 200{,}000 = 220{,}000\); \(475{,}000 - 240{,}000 = 235{,}000\).

**(c) Period-end adjusting JEs (emphasis)**

**March 31**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending, estimated) | 220,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 200,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 180,000 |
| Purchases | | 240,000 |
| *Book Q1 estimated EI and COGS under GP method* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(220{,}000 + 200{,}000 = 420{,}000\); Cr \(180{,}000 + 240{,}000 = 420{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**June 30**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending, estimated) | 235,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 240,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 220,000 |
| Purchases | | 255,000 |
| *Book Q2 estimated EI and COGS* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(235{,}000 + 240{,}000 = 475{,}000\); Cr \(220{,}000 + 255{,}000 = 475{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**(d) Flood-date estimate and loss**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory, July 1 (Jun 30 estimated EI) | $235,000 |
| Add: Net purchases, Jul 1 – Sep 5 | 40,000 |
| **Goods available for sale** | **275,000** |
| Subtract: Est. COGS \($75{,}000 \times \tfrac{2}{3}\) | (50,000) |
| **Ending inventory, estimated** | **$225,000** |

Loss = \(225{,}000 - 25{,}000 = \mathbf{\$200{,}000}\).

**(e) September 5 — casualty write-off**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory—Salvage | 25,000 | |
| Loss from Flood | 200,000 | |
| Inventory | | 225,000 |

**Check:** Dr \(25{,}000 + 200{,}000 = 225{,}000\) = Cr. **Balanced.**

**(f) September 5 — insurance receivable**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Insurance Receivable | 185,000 | |
| Loss from Flood | | 185,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 185,000. **Balanced.**

**(g) October 2 — settlement**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 185,000 | |
| Insurance Receivable | | 185,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 185,000. **Balanced.**

**(h)** Net casualty loss = \(200{,}000 - 185{,}000 = \mathbf{\$15{,}000}\).

**Key insight:** Same structure as Q1 with every amount and markup rate changed: convert markup → roll estimated EI → **balance the period-end interim adjusting JEs** → estimate at casualty date → subtract salvage → layer insurance receivable and cash settlement.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Harborline Specialty: multi-class GP estimates, period-end adjusting JE, theft reasonableness test

**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Apply the gross profit method **separately by inventory class**; period-end adjusting JE for combined interim inventory; theft/shrinkage test vs physical count; presentation  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Specialty Retail Inc.** uses a **periodic** inventory system. It sells two product lines with **different** historical markups:

| Line | Historical markup |
|---|---|
| Electronics | **30% of sales** (GP% of sales given) |
| Housewares | **40% of sales** (GP% of sales given) |

Data for the **quarter ended March 31**:

| Item | Electronics | Housewares | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Inventory, January 1 | $80,000 | $50,000 | $130,000 |
| Net purchases | 320,000 | 180,000 | 500,000 |
| Net sales | 400,000 | 250,000 | 650,000 |

A physical count is **not** taken at March 31. Management will book a single **period-end adjusting entry** using class-by-class GP estimates (combined totals).

**Additional year-end facts (for theft test):** For the **full year**, combined company data (not by class) show beginning inventory **$130,000**, net purchases **$1,050,000**, and net sales **$1,400,000**. Management incorrectly applied a single blended GP rate of **34% of sales** for a reasonableness estimate. The December 31 **physical count** is **$195,000**. (For the theft comparison only, use the blended rate as given; then discuss why class-by-class would be preferable.)

**Required:**  
(a) Estimate March 31 inventory **separately** for Electronics and Housewares; sum for total estimated inventory.  
(b) Prepare the **March 31 period-end adjusting journal entry** that books total estimated ending inventory and total estimated COGS (debit ending Inventory and COGS; credit beginning Inventory and Purchases for the quarter).  
(c) Using the full-year combined data and the **34% of sales** blended rate, estimate what December 31 inventory **should be**, and compute the estimated **cost of missing inventory** (theft/shrinkage) given the physical count of $195,000.  
(d) Prepare the **December 31 adjusting entry** to write inventory down from the GP-estimated year-end amount to the physical count (recognize shortage loss).  
(e) **Classification / presentation:** How should March 31 estimated inventory be reported? In two to three sentences, explain why Harborline should prefer **separate** class estimates rather than one company-wide GP rate.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Class-by-class GP estimates (March 31)**

**Electronics** (cost % of sales = \(1 - 0.30 = 70\%\)):  
Est. COGS = \(400{,}000 \times 0.70 = \mathbf{\$280{,}000}\).  
GAS = \(80{,}000 + 320{,}000 = \mathbf{\$400{,}000}\).  
Est. EI = \(400{,}000 - 280{,}000 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}\).

**Housewares** (cost % of sales = \(1 - 0.40 = 60\%\)):  
Est. COGS = \(250{,}000 \times 0.60 = \mathbf{\$150{,}000}\).  
GAS = \(50{,}000 + 180{,}000 = \mathbf{\$230{,}000}\).  
Est. EI = \(230{,}000 - 150{,}000 = \mathbf{\$80{,}000}\).

| Line | Est. ending inventory |
|---|---:|
| Electronics | $120,000 |
| Housewares | 80,000 |
| **Total estimated inventory, March 31** | **$200,000** |

Total est. COGS = \(280{,}000 + 150{,}000 = \mathbf{\$430{,}000}\).  
Total GAS = \(130{,}000 + 500{,}000 = \mathbf{\$630{,}000}\).  
Proof: \(630{,}000 - 430{,}000 = 200{,}000\).

**(b) March 31 — period-end adjusting JE (emphasis; combined totals from class estimates)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (ending, estimated) | 200,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 430,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 130,000 |
| Purchases | | 500,000 |
| *Interim close: estimated EI from separate class GP applications; est. COGS by difference* | | |

**Check:** Dr \(200{,}000 + 430{,}000 = 630{,}000\); Cr \(130{,}000 + 500{,}000 = 630{,}000\). **Balanced.**

**(c) Full-year blended estimate vs physical — missing inventory**  
Est. COGS (year) = \(1{,}400{,}000 \times (1 - 0.34) = 1{,}400{,}000 \times 0.66 = \mathbf{\$924{,}000}\).  
GAS (year) = \(130{,}000 + 1{,}050{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}180{,}000}\).  
Est. EI, Dec 31 = \(1{,}180{,}000 - 924{,}000 = \mathbf{\$256{,}000}\).  
Physical count = **$195,000**.  
Estimated missing inventory (theft/shrinkage) = \(256{,}000 - 195{,}000 = \mathbf{\$61{,}000}\).

**(d) December 31 — write down from GP-estimated EI to physical count**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory Shortage Loss (or COGS) | 61,000 | |
| Inventory | | 61,000 |
| *Reduce inventory from GP-estimated $256,000 to physical $195,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 61,000. **Balanced.**  
Ending Inventory after shortage = **$195,000** (physical).

*(Alternative full year-end compound entry from BI + Purchases if books were not yet closed, incorporating physical EI and shortage:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory (physical ending) | 195,000 | |
| Cost of Goods Sold | 924,000 | |
| Inventory Shortage Loss | 61,000 | |
| Inventory (beginning) | | 130,000 |
| Purchases | | 1,050,000 |

Check: Dr \(195{,}000 + 924{,}000 + 61{,}000 = 1{,}180{,}000\); Cr \(130{,}000 + 1{,}050{,}000 = 1{,}180{,}000\). **Balanced.**)*

**(e) Classification / presentation and multi-class preference**  
March 31 estimated inventory of **$200,000** is reported as a **current asset** (merchandise inventory). If material, Harborline may disclose major inventory categories (Electronics $120,000; Housewares $80,000).  

Because Electronics and Housewares have **materially different** gross profit rates (30% vs 40% of sales), Harborline should estimate each class **separately** and **sum** the class estimates. A single blended GP rate is unreliable when product mix shifts toward the higher- or lower-margin class—this is a core textbook **limitation** of the gross profit method. The year-end theft comparison that used a blended 34% rate is only a reasonableness test; class-level rates would improve reliability when markups differ.

**Key insight:** Period-end adjusting entries under the GP method must be **fed by correct class estimates** when markups differ. Separate schedules → sum EI and COGS → one balanced interim JE. Comparing GP-estimated EI to a physical count is a **theft/reasonableness** application of the same method.

### Q4 — MC (method / conversion)

**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Convert markup on cost and select estimated ending inventory under the gross profit method  
**Question:**  
Stonebridge Merchandising reports beginning inventory of **$72,000**, net purchases of **$348,000**, and sales of **$420,000**. Markup on cost has remained constant at **40%**. What is the estimated cost of ending inventory under the gross profit method?

- A) $120,000  
- B) $168,000  
- C) $252,000  
- D) $300,000  

**Answer:** A.  
GP% of sales = \(0.40 / 1.40 = 2/7\).  
Cost % of sales = \(1 - 2/7 = 5/7\).  
Est. COGS = \(420{,}000 \times 5/7 = \mathbf{\$300{,}000}\).  
GAS = \(72{,}000 + 348{,}000 = \$420{,}000\).  
Est. EI = \(420{,}000 - 300{,}000 = \mathbf{\$120{,}000}\).  
Distractors: B multiplies sales by GP% of sales only (\(420{,}000 \times 0.40\)); C uses cost % incorrectly or treats markup as % of sales without conversion (\(420{,}000 \times 0.60\)); D is estimated COGS, not EI.

### Q5 — MC (when multi-class is required)

**LO:** LO 10-4  
**Concept:** Identify when the gross profit method must be applied separately by inventory class  
**Question:**  
Which situation best requires applying the gross profit method **separately to each inventory class** (then summing class estimates) rather than using one company-wide average GP rate?

- A) The company uses a perpetual system and can count inventory monthly.  
- B) Product lines have **widely different markup rates**, and the sales mix can shift across lines.  
- C) Markup is given as a percentage of **cost** rather than sales (single product line).  
- D) Ending inventory is needed only for an internal budget draft with no financial-statement use.  

**Answer:** B.  
Textbook limitation: different markup rates and **sales-mix shifts** change the average GP rate and impair reliability of a single blended rate. Separate class estimates are required; then sum for total inventory. A is about when estimation may be unnecessary; C only requires converting cost markup to sales markup for that one line; D does not create a multi-class requirement.
