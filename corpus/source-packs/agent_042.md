### Q1 — CORE — Credit sales, allowance rollforward, write-off, recovery, aging YE adjust
**LO:** LO 8-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of AR; CECL allowance establishment/adjustment; write-off and recovery; aging schedule  
**Scenario:**  
Harborline Supply Co. uses the **allowance method** under the **Current Expected Credit Loss (CECL)** model for trade accounts receivable. Harborline’s fiscal year ends December 31.

**Balances at January 1, Year 2**
| Account | Balance |
|---|---:|
| Accounts Receivable (gross) | $210,000 |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | $8,400 credit |

**Year 2 activity**
| Event | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Credit sales | $620,000 |
| Cash collections on account (excluding recoveries) | $585,000 |
| Write-offs of specific customer accounts deemed uncollectible | $7,100 |
| Recovery of a previously written-off account (cash collected after write-off) | $1,200 |

**Aging of accounts receivable at December 31, Year 2** (after all of the above activity):

| Age category | Receivable balance | Expected credit loss rate | Estimated uncollectible |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current (not past due) | $175,000 | 1.0% | ? |
| 1–30 days past due | 38,000 | 4.0% | ? |
| 31–90 days past due | 17,400 | 12.0% | ? |
| Over 90 days past due | 7,500 | 40.0% | ? |
| **Total** | **$237,900** | | **?** |

Harborline believes historical rates, adjusted for current conditions and reasonable and supportable forecasts, are appropriate expected credit loss rates (CECL). Receivables are pooled by past-due status.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **initial recognition** journal entry for Year 2 credit sales.  
b. Record cash collections on account (excluding the recovery).  
c. Record the **write-off** of specific uncollectible accounts (settlement/disposal of AR components).  
d. Record the **recovery** of the previously written-off account (two entries).  
e. Prepare an **allowance rollforward schedule** from January 1 through the unadjusted December 31 balance (before the Year 2 adjusting entry). Also prove ending gross AR = $237,900.  
f. Complete the aging schedule; compute the required ending allowance; record the **December 31 period-end adjusting entry** for bad debt expense.  
g. Show **balance-sheet presentation** of receivables at the net amount expected to be collected. Briefly state the effect of the Year 2 write-off on net accounts receivable and on total assets **at the write-off date** (before any later recovery or YE adjust).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Credit sales — initial recognition of AR**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 620,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 620,000 |
| *To record Year 2 credit sales* | | |

*(Dr 620,000 = Cr 620,000)*

**b. Collections on account**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 585,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 585,000 |
| *To record collections on account* | | |

*(Dr 585,000 = Cr 585,000)*

**c. Write-off of specific accounts**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | 7,100 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 7,100 |
| *To write off accounts deemed uncollectible* | | |

*(Dr 7,100 = Cr 7,100)*

**d. Recovery (reinstate, then collect)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 1,200 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 1,200 |
| *To reverse prior write-off and reinstate customer account* | | |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 1,200 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 1,200 |
| *To record cash collection on reinstated account* | | |

*(Each entry: Dr = Cr = 1,200)*

**e. Gross AR proof and allowance rollforward (before YE adjust)**

Gross AR rollforward:
\[
\begin{align*}
\text{Beg AR} &\quad 210{,}000 \\
+ \text{Credit sales} &\quad 620{,}000 \\
- \text{Collections} &\quad (585{,}000) \\
- \text{Write-offs} &\quad (7{,}100) \\
+ \text{Recovery reinstate} &\quad 1{,}200 \\
- \text{Recovery collection} &\quad (1{,}200) \\
\hline
\text{End AR (gross)} &\quad \mathbf{\$237{,}900}
\end{align*}
\]

Allowance rollforward schedule (unadjusted):

| Item | Amount (Cr) / (Dr) |
|---|---:|
| Beginning allowance, Jan 1, Year 2 | $8,400 Cr |
| Write-offs | (7,100) |
| Recoveries (reinstatement) | 1,200 |
| **Unadjusted ending allowance, Dec 31, Year 2** | **$2,500 Cr** |

Check: \(8{,}400 - 7{,}100 + 1{,}200 = 2{,}500\).

**f. Aging schedule and period-end adjusting JE**

| Age category | Receivable balance | Rate | Estimated uncollectible |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current (not past due) | $175,000 | 1.0% | $1,750 |
| 1–30 days past due | 38,000 | 4.0% | 1,520 |
| 31–90 days past due | 17,400 | 12.0% | 2,088 |
| Over 90 days past due | 7,500 | 40.0% | 3,000 |
| **Total** | **$237,900** | | **$8,358** |

Required ending allowance (credit) = **$8,358**  
Unadjusted allowance (credit) = **$2,500**  
Bad debt expense (credit loss expense) = \(8{,}358 - 2{,}500 = \mathbf{\$5{,}858}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bad Debt Expense | 5,858 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 5,858 |
| *To adjust allowance to net amount expected to be collected (aging/CECL)* | | |

*(Dr 5,858 = Cr 5,858)*

Post-adjust allowance T-account:

| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | |
|---:|---:|
| Write-offs 7,100 | Beg 8,400 |
| | Recovery 1,200 |
| | YE adjust 5,858 |
| | **End bal. 8,358** |

Check: \(8{,}400 - 7{,}100 + 1{,}200 + 5{,}858 = 8{,}358\).

**g. Presentation and write-off effect**

Balance sheet (current assets):  
**Trade receivables, net of allowance of $8,358 — $229,542**  
(\(237{,}900 - 8{,}358 = 229{,}542\))

At the **write-off date**, both gross AR and the allowance decrease by the same amount, so **net AR is unchanged** and **total assets are unchanged**. The income-statement effect of expected losses was (or will be) recognized when the allowance was established/adjusted, not when the specific account is written off.

**Key insight:** Under CECL, receivables are reported at the **net amount expected to be collected**. Period-end bad debt expense plugs the allowance from its unadjusted balance (after write-offs/recoveries) to the **desired ending balance** from the aging (or other CECL estimate). Write-offs reclassify within net AR; they do not create additional expense unless estimates prove insufficient.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Full allowance cycle with aging
**LO:** LO 8-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — AR recognition, write-off, recovery, aging YE adjust, net presentation  
**Scenario:**  
Cedar & Oak Retail LLC applies CECL (allowance method) to trade receivables. Year-end is December 31.

**Balances at January 1, Year 3**
| Account | Balance |
|---|---:|
| Accounts Receivable (gross) | $340,000 |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | $12,500 credit |

**Year 3 activity**
| Event | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Credit sales | $890,000 |
| Cash collections on account (excluding recoveries) | $820,000 |
| Write-offs of specific accounts | $11,200 |
| Recovery of previously written-off account (cash after write-off) | $2,000 |

**Aging at December 31, Year 3** (after activity above; totals ending gross AR):

| Age category | Receivable balance | Expected credit loss rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current (not past due) | $280,000 | 0.5% |
| 1–45 days past due | 65,000 | 3.0% |
| 46–90 days past due | 38,000 | 10.0% |
| Over 90 days past due | 15,800 | 45.0% |
| **Total** | **$398,800** | |

**Required:**  
a. Record JEs for: (1) credit sales, (2) collections, (3) write-offs, (4) recovery (two entries).  
b. Allowance rollforward to unadjusted ending balance; prove ending gross AR.  
c. Aging computation; Year 3 adjusting entry for bad debt expense.  
d. Net amount expected to be collected on the December 31, Year 3 balance sheet.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Journal entries**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 890,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 890,000 |

*(Dr = Cr = 890,000)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 820,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 820,000 |

*(Dr = Cr = 820,000)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | 11,200 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 11,200 |

*(Dr = Cr = 11,200)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 2,000 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 2,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 2,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 2,000 |

*(Each recovery entry: Dr = Cr = 2,000)*

**b. Rollforwards**

Gross AR:
\[
340{,}000 + 890{,}000 - 820{,}000 - 11{,}200 + 2{,}000 - 2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$398{,}800}
\]

Allowance (unadjusted):
\[
12{,}500 - 11{,}200 + 2{,}000 = \mathbf{\$3{,}300\ \text{credit}}
\]

**c. Aging and YE adjust**

| Age category | Balance | Rate | Uncollectible |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current | $280,000 | 0.5% | $1,400 |
| 1–45 days | 65,000 | 3.0% | 1,950 |
| 46–90 days | 38,000 | 10.0% | 3,800 |
| Over 90 days | 15,800 | 45.0% | 7,110 |
| **Total** | **$398,800** | | **$14,260** |

Required ending AFDA = **$14,260**  
BDE = \(14{,}260 - 3{,}300 = \mathbf{\$10{,}960}\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bad Debt Expense | 10,960 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 10,960 |
| *To adjust allowance to CECL/aging estimate* | | |

*(Dr 10,960 = Cr 10,960)*

**d. Net presentation**  
Trade receivables, net of allowance of **$14,260** — **$384,540**  
(\(398{,}800 - 14{,}260 = 384{,}540\))

**Key insight:** Changing every input (sales, collections, write-offs, recoveries, aging buckets and rates) changes both the unadjusted allowance and the required ending balance; bad debt expense is always the **plug** that brings the allowance to the CECL estimate—not a fixed percentage of sales unless that proxy yields a materially similar net AR.

### Q3 — CORE — Period-end adjust with debit unadjusted allowance; write-off neutrality; single-rate CECL
**LO:** LO 8-4  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE when unadjusted AFDA is a debit; single-rate estimate; write-off does not change net AR  
**Scenario:**  
Pinecrest Dental Labs Inc. uses a **single expected credit loss rate** of **4%** of year-end gross accounts receivable (one pool; similar risk characteristics). At December 31, Year 1, before adjustment:

| Account | Unadjusted balance |
|---|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | $156,000 debit |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | $750 **debit** |

Write-offs during Year 1 exceeded the prior allowance, leaving a temporary debit balance in AFDA before the year-end CECL estimate is recorded.

**Required:**  
a. Compute the required ending allowance and the December 31 adjusting entry.  
b. Report accounts receivable at the net amount expected to be collected.  
c. On January 12, Year 2, Pinecrest writes off a specific customer balance of **$1,800**. Record the write-off and demonstrate that **net AR is unchanged** immediately before vs. after the write-off (use post-Year-1-adjust balances).  
d. **Classification / presentation:** Where is the allowance reported? Is the direct write-off method acceptable under GAAP for these trade receivables? Briefly explain under CECL.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Required allowance and adjusting JE**  
Required AFDA (credit) = \(156{,}000 \times 0.04 = \mathbf{\$6{,}240}\)  

Unadjusted AFDA is a **$750 debit**, so the credit needed to reach a $6,240 credit balance is:
\[
6{,}240 + 750 = \mathbf{\$6{,}990}
\]

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bad Debt Expense | 6,990 | |
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | | 6,990 |
| *To establish required CECL allowance from a debit unadjusted balance* | | |

*(Dr 6,990 = Cr 6,990)*

Allowance T-account:

| | Allowance for Doubtful Accounts |
|---:|---:|
| Unadj. bal. 750 | |
| | YE adjust 6,990 |
| | **End bal. 6,240** |

**b. Net amount expected to be collected**  
\[
156{,}000 - 6{,}240 = \mathbf{\$149{,}760}
\]  
Balance sheet: **Accounts receivable, net of allowance of $6,240 — $149,760**

**c. Write-off neutrality (Jan 12, Year 2)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | 1,800 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 1,800 |

| | Before write-off | After write-off |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts receivable (gross) | $156,000 | $154,200 |
| Less: Allowance for doubtful accounts | (6,240) | (4,440) |
| **Net accounts receivable** | **$149,760** | **$149,760** |

Net AR and total assets are **unchanged** by the write-off.

**d. Classification / presentation**  
- The allowance is a **contra asset** deducted from gross trade receivables (current assets) so the line reports the **net amount expected to be collected**.  
- Under CECL (ASC 326), an estimate of expected credit losses is required **even if the risk of loss is remote**; the **direct write-off method is not GAAP** for these trade receivables (it is commonly used for **tax** purposes only). Zero-allowance situations are rare.

**Key insight:** When the unadjusted allowance has a **debit** balance (write-offs > prior estimate), bad debt expense equals required ending credit + the debit unadjusted amount. Write-offs never reduce net AR; only the CECL estimate (and collections) change the net amount expected to be collected.

### Q4 — MC — Write-off effect and GAAP measurement basis
**LO:** LO 8-4  
**Concept:** Classification/method: write-off impact; CECL net measurement  

**Question 1:**  
At year-end, after all adjusting entries, Meridian Trails Inc. reports:

- Gross accounts receivable: $80,000  
- Allowance for doubtful accounts: $3,200 credit  

During the next period, Meridian writes off $900 of specific customer accounts (no other activity). Immediately after the write-off, what is the **net amount expected to be collected** on accounts receivable?

- A) $76,800  
- B) $75,900  
- C) $76,800 (unchanged); gross AR and allowance both fall by $900  
- D) $79,100  

**Answer:** **C.**  
Before: net = \(80{,}000 - 3{,}200 = 76{,}800\).  
After: gross \(79{,}100\); allowance \(2{,}300\); net \(79{,}100 - 2{,}300 = 76{,}800\).  
Write-off reduces gross AR and AFDA equally; **net AR is unchanged**. Choice A states the correct net amount but omits the equal reduction mechanism; C is the complete correct description. B incorrectly subtracts the write-off from net. D is post-write-off gross, not net.

**Question 2:**  
Which statement best describes measurement of trade accounts receivable under U.S. GAAP (CECL)?

- A) Report gross receivables only; recognize losses only when a specific account is written off (direct write-off).  
- B) Report receivables at the **net amount expected to be collected**, using an allowance for expected credit losses over the contractual life, adjusted each reporting date.  
- C) Report receivables at fair value through net income each period.  
- D) Establish an allowance only when a loss is probable and reasonably estimable under the incurred-loss model still required for all trade receivables.

**Answer:** **B.**  
CECL (ASC 326-20) requires an allowance so that amortized-cost financial assets (including most trade receivables) are presented at the **net amount expected to be collected**, with expected credit losses estimated over the contractual term and updated each period. A is the direct write-off method (not GAAP for these receivables). C is not the general trade-receivable model. D describes the superseded incurred-loss timing emphasis; CECL recognizes expected losses even if remote.
