# Agent 38 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-7

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Prepare the operating cash flow section of the statement of cash flows using the direct method  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Convert accrual sales to **cash collected from customers** (AR rollforward; write-offs and recoveries are noncash)
- Convert COGS to **cash paid to suppliers** via inventory purchases and accounts payable
- Convert operating expenses to cash paid to employees / other suppliers (exclude bad debt expense; adjust prepaid and accruals)
- Convert interest and tax expense to **cash paid for interest** and **cash paid for taxes** (premium/discount amortization; payables)
- Direct-method operating section: major classes of gross receipts and payments; **same net OCF** as indirect
- Required **reconciliation of net income to operating cash flows** when direct method is used
- Noncash items (depreciation, amortization, gains/losses) **do not appear** as line items under the direct method
- Bond interest: cash coupon under direct method; discount/premium amortization affects reconciling schedule only
- Zero-coupon (or insignificant-coupon) settlement: bifurcate accreted interest (operating) vs principal (financing)

### Q1 — CORE — Full direct-method operating section with AR write-offs, schedules, and reconciling disclosure
**LO:** LO 22-7  
**Concept:** Prepare operating cash flows under the direct method from income statement and comparative working-capital balances  
**Scenario:**

**Required:**

Prepare the **December 31, 20X5** period-end adjusting entries for (i) bad debt expense and (ii) bond interest (cash interest already paid during the year equals the amount computed in (b); record remaining accrual and premium amortization as of year-end in a single compound entry that explains the $2,000 increase in interest payable and $2,000 premium amortization).

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Direct-method operating section with discount amortization
**LO:** LO 22-7  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — convert full income statement to direct-method operating cash flows (discount bonds; inventory decrease)  
**Scenario:**  
Pinecrest Outfitters Inc. uses the **direct method** for operating cash flows for the year ended December 31, 20X6.

**Income statement**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales revenue | $920,000 |
| Cost of goods sold | (410,000) |
| Depreciation expense | (70,000) |
| Trademark amortization expense | (12,000) |
| Operating expenses (includes $5,000 bad debt expense) | (95,000) |
| Gain on insurance settlement (noncash) | 8,000 |
| Loss on disposal of equipment (noncash) | (4,000) |
| Interest expense | (25,000) |
| Income tax expense | (80,000) |
| **Net income** | **$232,000** |

Operating expenses of $95,000: bad debt **$5,000**, salaries **$60,000**, other operating **$30,000**.

**Selected balances**

| Account | Jan 1, 20X6 | Dec 31, 20X6 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts receivable | $55,000 | $70,000 |
| Allowance for doubtful accounts | (6,000) | (9,000) |
| Inventory | 40,000 | 35,000 |
| Prepaid operating expenses | 5,000 | 8,000 |
| Accounts payable (inventory) | 28,000 | 22,000 |
| Salaries payable | 8,000 | 11,000 |
| Interest payable | 5,000 | 3,000 |
| Income taxes payable | 20,000 | 25,000 |
| Discount on bonds payable | 9,000 | 6,000 |

**Additional information**
- Write-offs **$4,000**; recoveries **$2,000**.
- Discount amortization for the year **$3,000**.
- All sales on account.

**Required:**  
a. Compute cash collected from customers (show AR rollforward).  
b. Compute cash paid to suppliers, cash paid for other operating items, cash paid for interest, and cash paid for taxes.  
c. Prepare the direct-method operating activities section.  
d. Prepare the NI-to-OCF reconciliation and prove equality.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Cash collected from customers**

AR: beg 55,000 + sales 920,000 + recoveries 2,000 − write-offs 4,000 − collections **X** = end 70,000  
\(55{,}000 + 920{,}000 + 2{,}000 - 4{,}000 - X = 70{,}000\) → \(X = 903{,}000\).

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales | $920,000 |
| Less: increase in AR | (15,000) |
| Less: write-offs | (4,000) |
| Add: recoveries | 2,000 |
| **Cash collected from customers** | **$903,000** |

ADA proof: \(6{,}000 + 5{,}000 - 4{,}000 + 2{,}000 = 9{,}000\).

**b. Other cash payments**

Cash paid to suppliers:  
COGS 410,000 − inventory decrease 5,000 + accounts payable decrease 6,000 = **$411,000**.

Cash paid for other operating items:  
95,000 − BDE 5,000 + prepaid increase 3,000 − salaries payable increase 3,000 = **$90,000**.  
(Other cash 30,000 + 3,000 = 33,000; salaries cash 60,000 − 3,000 = 57,000; total 90,000.)

Cash paid for interest:  
Interest expense 25,000 − discount amortization 3,000 + decrease in interest payable 2,000 = **$24,000**.  
Proof: beg IP 5,000 + (25,000 − 3,000) − 24,000 = end 3,000.

Cash paid for taxes:  
80,000 − increase in taxes payable 5,000 = **$75,000**.

**c. Direct-method operating section**

```
Cash flows from operating activities
  Cash collected from customers                     $903,000
  Cash paid to suppliers                            (411,000)
  Cash paid for other operating items                (90,000)
  Cash paid for interest                             (24,000)
  Cash paid for income taxes                         (75,000)
  Net cash provided by operating activities         $303,000
```

**d. Reconciliation**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net income | $232,000 |
| Depreciation | 70,000 |
| Trademark amortization | 12,000 |
| Amortization of bond discount | 3,000 |
| Gain on insurance settlement | (8,000) |
| Loss on disposal of equipment | 4,000 |
| Increase in AR, net \((70{,}000-9{,}000)-(55{,}000-6{,}000)=12{,}000\) | (12,000) |
| Decrease in inventory | 5,000 |
| Increase in prepaid operating expenses | (3,000) |
| Decrease in accounts payable | (6,000) |
| Increase in salaries payable | 3,000 |
| Decrease in interest payable | (2,000) |
| Increase in income taxes payable | 5,000 |
| **Net cash provided by operating activities** | **$303,000** |

**Proof:** \(232 + 70 + 12 + 3 - 8 + 4 - 12 + 5 - 3 - 6 + 3 - 2 + 5 = 303\).

**Key insight:** Discount amortization **increases** interest expense relative to cash interest; under the direct method only the **cash** coupon/settlement interest appears. Inventory decreases and payable decreases move cash paid to suppliers in opposite directions.

### Q3 — CORE — Bond interest schedule, zero-coupon settlement split, and classification/presentation
**LO:** LO 22-7  
**Concept:** Cash paid for interest under the direct method; amortization schedule; classification of settlement and required major cash-flow classes  
**Scenario:**  

**Part A — Discount bonds (subsequent measurement / interest paid)**  
On January 1, 20X4, Ridgeview Foods issued **$200,000** face, 5-year bonds. Stated rate **6%** paid annually each December 31; market yield **8%**. Issue proceeds **$184,058** (PV of principal and interest at 8%). Effective-interest amortization applies.

Partial amortization schedule (complete Year 1–2 columns as needed):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, 20X4 | — | — | — | $184,058 |
| Dec 31, 20X4 | ? | ? | ? | ? |
| Dec 31, 20X5 | ? | ? | ? | ? |

**Part B — Zero-coupon settlement (disposal/maturity classification)**  
On December 31, 20X9, Summit Logistics settles a **zero-coupon** note by paying **$150,000** cash. Carrying amount immediately before settlement equals face **$150,000**. Of the total accreted discount over the life, **$42,000** is accreted interest attributable to the discount (the remainder is principal return of original proceeds). Per ASC 230 for zero-coupon instruments, classify the settlement cash payment between operating and financing.

**Part C — Presentation / disclosure**  
Summit uses the **direct method** for operating activities.

**Required:**  
a. Complete the Year 1 and Year 2 amortization schedule for Ridgeview.  
b. For 20X4 and 20X5, state the amount reported as **cash paid for interest** in the direct-method operating section, and the related **financing** cash flow at issuance (20X4 only).  
c. Prepare the journal entry on Dec 31, 20X4 for Ridgeview’s cash interest payment and discount amortization (compound).  
d. For Summit’s zero-coupon settlement, allocate the $150,000 cash payment between operating and financing; prepare the settlement journal entry (assume no gain/loss; carrying amount = $150,000).  
e. List the **minimum major classes** of operating cash receipts and payments ASC 230 encourages/requires under the direct method, and state what **additional schedule** must accompany a direct-method SCF for a business entity.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Amortization schedule**

| Date | Cash (6% × $200,000) | Interest exp. (8% × CV) | Discount amort. | Carrying value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1, 20X4 | — | — | — | $184,058 |
| Dec 31, 20X4 | $12,000 | \(0.08 × 184{,}058 = 14{,}725\) | $2,725 | $186,783 |
| Dec 31, 20X5 | $12,000 | \(0.08 × 186{,}783 = 14{,}943\) | $2,943 | $189,726 |

**Checks:** \(12{,}000 + 2{,}725 = 14{,}725\); \(184{,}058 + 2{,}725 = 186{,}783\); \(12{,}000 + 2{,}943 = 14{,}943\); \(186{,}783 + 2{,}943 = 189{,}726\).

**b. Direct-method interest and issuance classification**  
- 20X4 cash paid for interest (operating): **$12,000**  
- 20X5 cash paid for interest (operating): **$12,000**  
- 20X4 cash proceeds from bond issuance (financing): **$184,058**  

Interest expense (14,725 / 14,943) is **not** the direct-method line; only cash paid is reported. Discount amortization appears only in the NI reconciliation (add to NI under indirect/reconciliation).

**c. Dec 31, 20X4 compound JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest Expense | 14,725 | |
| Discount on Bonds Payable | | 2,725 |
| Cash | | 12,000 |
| *Record cash interest and amortize discount* | | |

**Check:** Dr 14,725 = Cr \(2{,}725 + 12{,}000\). Balanced.

**d. Zero-coupon settlement classification and JE**

| Classification | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Operating — cash paid for interest (accreted interest portion) | $42,000 |
| Financing — repayment of principal (proceeds portion) | 108,000 |
| **Total cash paid** | **$150,000** |

Settlement JE (balance sheet view):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Notes Payable | 150,000 | |
| Cash | | 150,000 |
| *Settle zero-coupon note at maturity* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 150,000. On the **statement of cash flows**, split the single cash outflow: operating $42,000 and financing $108,000 (ASC 230 bifurcation for zero-coupon / insignificant-coupon instruments).

**e. Minimum major classes and required disclosure**  
Minimum classes under the direct method (ASC 230-10-45-25):  
1. Cash collected from customers  
2. Interest and dividends **received**  
3. Other operating cash receipts (if any)  
4. Cash paid to employees and other suppliers of goods or services  
5. Interest **paid**  
6. Income taxes paid  
7. Other operating cash payments (if any)  

Required additional disclosure for non-NFP entities using the direct method: a **separate schedule reconciling net income to net cash flow from operating activities** (the indirect-method reconciliation).

**Key insight:** Direct method reports **cash** interest only; amortization is reconciling, not a cash line. Zero-coupon settlements uniquely split accreted interest (operating) from principal (financing). Direct method always pairs with the NI reconciliation disclosure.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method presentation)
**LO:** LO 22-7  
**Concept:** Classification of items under the direct-method operating section vs noncash reconciling items  

**Question 1:**  
Which item is reported as a **separate line** in the operating activities section when the **direct method** is used?

- A) Depreciation expense added back to net income  
- B) Cash collected from customers  
- C) Gain on sale of land subtracted from net income  
- D) Increase in accounts payable added to net income  

**Answer:** B.  
Under the direct method, major classes of gross operating cash receipts and payments are listed (e.g., cash collected from customers). Depreciation, gains, and working-capital *adjustments to NI* appear in the **reconciliation schedule** (or under the indirect method), not as direct-method operating lines.

---

**Question 2:**  
Maple Co. reports interest expense of $40,000, amortization of bond **discount** of $3,500, and an **increase** in interest payable of $1,200 for the year. What amount of **cash paid for interest** is reported in the direct-method operating section?

- A) $40,000  
- B) $43,500  
- C) $35,300  
- D) $38,300  

**Answer:** C.  
Cash paid for interest = interest expense − discount amortization − increase in interest payable  
\(= 40{,}000 - 3{,}500 - 1{,}200 = 35{,}300\).  
(Discount amortization is noncash; an increase in interest payable means cash interest was less than the accrual-basis interest cost net of amort.)
