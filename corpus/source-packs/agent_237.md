# Agent 237 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-1

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Describe and report key components of stockholders’ equity  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Five key components of stockholders’ equity:** paid-in capital (capital stock + APIC), retained earnings, accumulated OCI (AOCI), treasury stock (contra equity), noncontrolling interest
- **Initial recognition JE:** issuance of common and preferred stock (par to capital stock; excess to APIC)
- **Share terminology:** authorized vs issued vs outstanding; treasury reduces outstanding but not issued
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period statement of stockholders’ equity roll-forward (beginning → activity → ending) for each major equity component
- **Period-end adjusting/closing JEs:** close net income to retained earnings; close current OCI into AOCI; cash dividends reduce RE
- **Disposal / reacquisition JE:** treasury stock purchase (cost method) and reissuance (above or below cost)
- **Classification / presentation:** equity section of the balance sheet with par, authorized, issued, and outstanding disclosures; APIC aggregated; AOCI and treasury deducted when debit balances; NCI presented separately

**Key insight:** The **subsequent measurement schedule** (statement of stockholders’ equity) is the core LO 19-1 deliverable: each equity component rolls forward independently. Multi-period schedules must **tie** ending totals of period *n* to beginning totals of period *n*+1.

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 12,000. **Balanced.**

(Dividends already reduced RE by $50,000 when declared/paid.)

**Key insight:** LO 19-1 centers on **identifying and reporting** the five components. **RE** and **AOCI** each need a **subsequent measurement roll-forward** (beginning ± period activity = ending). Capital stock and APIC are **paid-in capital** (APIC usually **aggregated** on the face); treasury is a **contra equity** deduction; **NCI** is presented **separately** under controlling interest, then summed for total equity.

### Q4 — MC (classification / presentation)
**LO:** LO 19-1  
**Concept:** Classification of treasury stock within the five key components of stockholders’ equity  
**Question:**  
On the balance sheet, treasury stock acquired for purposes other than retirement and accounted for under the **cost method** is reported as:

- A) An asset (investment in the company’s own shares)  
- B) A reduction of retained earnings only  
- C) A **contra equity** deduction in arriving at total stockholders’ equity  
- D) A liability until the shares are reissued  

**Answer:** **C.** Under the cost method, Treasury Stock is a **contra equity** account deducted in the stockholders’ equity section. It is not an asset, not a liability, and is not limited to a RE-only reduction (though RE may be charged on below-cost reissuance when APIC—Treasury is unavailable).

### Q4b — MC (share terminology)
**LO:** LO 19-1  
**Concept:** Computing outstanding shares for equity-section disclosure  
**Question:**  
**Fircrest Systems Corp.** has 200,000 common shares **authorized**, 90,000 shares **issued**, and holds 6,000 shares as **treasury stock**. How many common shares are **outstanding**, and which amount is used for basic EPS?

- A) Authorized 200,000; EPS uses authorized shares  
- B) Issued 90,000; EPS uses issued shares  
- C) Outstanding **84,000**; EPS uses **outstanding** shares  
- D) Outstanding 96,000; EPS uses issued plus treasury  

**Answer:** **C.** Outstanding = issued − treasury = 90,000 − 6,000 = **84,000**. Basic EPS uses **weighted-average shares outstanding**, not authorized or issued.
