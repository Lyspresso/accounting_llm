# Agent 225 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-1

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Record accounts payable and sales taxes payable  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** trade purchases create Accounts Payable at invoice face (gross method, perpetual); freight-in capitalized to Inventory
- **Sales taxes payable at point of sale:** company acts as agent; credit Sales Taxes Payable for tax portion; Sales = pre-tax selling price
- **Period-end adjusting JE (alternate policy):** record gross collections in Sales, then reclassify tax via Sales → Sales Taxes Payable using Total ÷ (1 + rate)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period liability rollforwards for AP and Sales Taxes Payable (begin + recognize − settle = end); no interest accretion on ordinary short-term trade payables (ASC 835-30 scope exception)
- **Settlement / remittance JE:** pay supplier (with or without cash discount under gross method); remit sales taxes to taxing authority (not settled when customer pays the retailer)
- **Classification:** both AP and Sales Taxes Payable are **current liabilities** when outstanding
- **Number-variant twin:** same multi-period path with all amounts changed

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-period AP & sales taxes: initial recognition, subsequent measurement schedule, settlement/remittance

### Q4 — MC — Classification and measurement of AP and sales taxes payable
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Classification / presentation — current liability nature; agency role for sales taxes; face measurement of short-term trade AP  
**Question:**  
Which statement best describes the balance-sheet classification and measurement of **accounts payable** from ordinary inventory purchases due in 30 days and **sales taxes payable** collected from customers but not yet remitted?

- A) Accounts payable is a current liability measured at present value; sales taxes payable is deferred revenue until remitted.  
- B) Both are current liabilities measured at settlement/face amount; sales taxes payable is an agency obligation to the taxing authority, not revenue of the seller.  
- C) Both are noncurrent liabilities because remittance timing to suppliers and tax authorities is uncertain.  
- D) Accounts payable is recorded at fair value each period; sales taxes payable is an equity appropriation until paid.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Ordinary trade accounts payable due within the operating cycle/year are **current liabilities** measured at **settlement/face amount** (ASC 835-30 generally does not require separate interest for normal short-term supplier payables). Sales taxes collected are held as an **agent** for the taxing authority and reported as **Sales Taxes Payable (current liability)** until remitted—they are **not** the seller’s revenue and are not deferred revenue for goods/services.

### Q4b — MC — Period-end adjusting method for sales taxes
**LO:** LO 15-1  
**Concept:** Classification of method — period-end adjusting entry when sales taxes were not isolated at the point of sale  
**Question:**  
A retailer collects **$10,600** cash from customers on a day when the sales tax rate is **6%**. Company policy is to credit the **entire $10,600** to Sales at the point of sale. At month-end, the correct adjusting entry to isolate sales taxes payable is:

- A) Debit Sales Taxes Payable $600; credit Sales $600.  
- B) Debit Sales $600; credit Sales Taxes Payable $600.  
- C) Debit Sales $10,000; credit Sales Taxes Payable $10,000.  
- D) Debit Cash $600; credit Sales Taxes Payable $600.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Pre-tax sales = \(10{,}600 / 1.06 = \mathbf{\$10{,}000}\); tax = \(10{,}600 - 10{,}000 = \mathbf{\$600}\) (or \(10{,}000 \times 0.06\)). The adjusting entry **reduces Sales** and **increases Sales Taxes Payable** so that revenue is stated net of tax and the agency liability is reported. Option A reverses the accounts; C uses the wrong amount; D double-counts cash already recorded.
