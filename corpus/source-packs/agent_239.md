# Agent 239 — CORE DEMO — LO 19-3

**Chapter:** 19  
**LO title:** Account for reacquisition of common stock  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** reacquire common shares under the **cost method** — debit Treasury Stock (contra equity) at acquisition cost; credit Cash
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-lot treasury cost rollforward (FIFO layers), PIC—Treasury Stock, and retained earnings effects across purchases, reissuances, and remaining balance
- **Period-end presentation:** stockholders’ equity section with issued vs outstanding shares; treasury stock deducted from total equity (not an asset)
- **Disposal / settlement JE:** reissue treasury stock above cost (credit PIC—Treasury Stock) and below cost (debit PIC—TS then Retained Earnings); subsequent formal retirement of remaining treasury shares
- **Direct retirement alternative:** derecognize Common Stock and PIC in Excess of Par at original issuance amounts; balance to PIC—Retired Stock or Retained Earnings; shares return to unissued
- Classification: no gain/loss in net income on treasury transactions under the cost method

### Q1 — CORE — Treasury stock (cost method): multi-lot purchases, FIFO reissuance schedule, period-end equity, and subsequent retirement
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for multi-lot treasury stock (FIFO); reissuance above/below cost; year-end equity presentation; formal retirement of remaining treasury shares  
**Scenario:**  
**Rivermark Forge Inc.** (calendar-year company) uses the **cost method** for treasury stock and **FIFO** to assign cost when multiple lots exist.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Authorized common stock | **100,000** shares, **$1** par |
| Jan 5, Year 1 — original issuance | **40,000** shares issued for cash at **$10** per share |
| Mar 12, Year 1 — treasury purchase | **3,000** shares reacquired at **$12** per share |
| May 8, Year 1 — treasury purchase | **2,000** shares reacquired at **$9** per share |
| Aug 20, Year 1 — reissuance | Sold **4,000** treasury shares for cash at **$11** per share (FIFO cost) |
| Retained earnings, Dec 31, Year 1 **before** any treasury-related debits | **$150,000** |
| Feb 14, Year 2 — subsequent retirement | Remaining treasury shares formally **retired** and returned to unissued status |

No other equity transactions occurred in Year 1. There was no prior balance in Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 5, Year 1** journal entry for the original issuance of common stock.  
b. Record the **March 12** and **May 8** journal entries for reacquisition of shares for the treasury (**initial recognition** under the cost method).  
c. Prepare a **subsequent-measurement treasury stock cost schedule** (emphasis) through August 20 showing: date, event, shares in/(out), unit cost (or layers), change in Treasury Stock ($), ending treasury shares, ending Treasury Stock ($), and effects on PIC—Treasury Stock and Retained Earnings.  
d. Record the **August 20** journal entry for the FIFO reissuance of 4,000 treasury shares.  
e. Prepare the **December 31, Year 1** stockholders’ equity section (period-end presentation), including shares authorized, issued, and outstanding.  
f. Record the **February 14, Year 2** entry for subsequent retirement of the remaining treasury shares.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial issuance (context for original capital amounts)**

*January 5, Year 1 — Issue 40,000 shares at $10*

Common Stock = \(40{,}000 \times \$1 = \$40{,}000\)  
PIC in Excess of Par—CS = \(40{,}000 \times (\$10 - \$1) = \$360{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 400,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 40,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 360,000 |
| *Issue 40,000 shares, $1 par, at $10 cash* | | |

**Check:** Dr 400,000 = Cr 40,000 + 360,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Initial recognition — reacquisition for treasury (cost method)**

*March 12 — Purchase 3,000 shares @ $12*

Treasury Stock = \(3{,}000 \times \$12 = \$36{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 36,000 | |
| Cash | | 36,000 |
| *Reacquire 3,000 shares for treasury at cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 36,000. **Balanced.**

*May 8 — Purchase 2,000 shares @ $9*

Treasury Stock = \(2{,}000 \times \$9 = \$18{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 18,000 | |
| Cash | | 18,000 |
| *Reacquire 2,000 shares for treasury at cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 18,000. **Balanced.**  
**Combined treasury after both purchases:** 5,000 shares; cost \(36{,}000 + 18{,}000 = \$54{,}000\).

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — treasury cost rollforward (FIFO)**

| Date | Event | Shares in/(out) | Unit cost / layers | Δ Treasury Stock ($) | End TS shares | End TS ($) | PIC—TS end | RE effect (cumulative) |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 5 | Issue (no TS) | — | — | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Mar 12 | Purchase lot 1 | +3,000 | $12 | +36,000 | 3,000 | 36,000 | 0 | 0 |
| May 8 | Purchase lot 2 | +2,000 | $9 | +18,000 | 5,000 | 54,000 | 0 | 0 |
| Aug 20 | Sell 4,000 FIFO | −4,000 | 3,000 @ $12 + 1,000 @ $9 | −45,000 | 1,000 | 9,000 | 0 | **−1,000** |
| **Y1 end** | **Remaining lot 2** | | **1,000 @ $9** | | **1,000** | **9,000** | **0** | **−1,000** |
| Feb 14 Y2 | Retire remaining | −1,000 | $9 | −9,000 | 0 | 0 | **1,000** | −1,000 |

**FIFO cost of 4,000 shares sold:**  
\(3{,}000 \times \$12 = \$36{,}000\)  
\(1{,}000 \times \$9 = \$9{,}000\)  
**Total cost removed = $45,000**  
Cash proceeds = \(4{,}000 \times \$11 = \$44{,}000\)  
Deficiency = \(45{,}000 - 44{,}000 = \$1{,}000\) → debit **Retained Earnings** (no prior PIC—TS credit balance).

**Remaining treasury:** \(5{,}000 - 4{,}000 = 1{,}000\) shares from lot 2 at **$9** → **$9,000**.

**Roll-forward check:** \(54{,}000 - 45{,}000 = 9{,}000\). **OK.**

**d. August 20 reissuance JE**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 44,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | 1,000 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 45,000 |
| *Reissue 4,000 TS shares @ $11; FIFO cost $45,000; excess of cost over proceeds to RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 44,000 + 1,000 = Cr 45,000. **Balanced.**

**e. December 31, Year 1 — stockholders’ equity (period-end)**

Shares issued remain **40,000** (treasury does not reduce issued shares).  
Shares outstanding = \(40{,}000 - 1{,}000 = 39{,}000\).  
RE = \(150{,}000 - 1{,}000 = \$149{,}000\).

| Stockholders’ Equity | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Common stock, $1 par value, 100,000 shares authorized, 40,000 shares issued, 39,000 shares outstanding | $40,000 |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par—common stock | 360,000 |
| Retained earnings | 149,000 |
| Less: Treasury stock, 1,000 shares (at cost) | (9,000) |
| **Total stockholders’ equity** | **$540,000** |

**Check:** \(40{,}000 + 360{,}000 + 149{,}000 - 9{,}000 = 540{,}000\).

**f. February 14, Year 2 — subsequent retirement of remaining 1,000 treasury shares**

Original issuance capital per share = **$10** (par $1 + PIC $9).  
Treasury cost of remaining shares = \(1{,}000 \times \$9 = \$9{,}000\).  
Original capital removed = \(1{,}000 \times \$10 = \$10{,}000\).  
Excess of original capital over TS cost = **$1,000** → credit **Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock** (same “balancing” approach as a reissuance above cost).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock (1,000 × $1) | 1,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock (1,000 × $9) | 9,000 | |
| Treasury Stock (1,000 × $9) | | 9,000 |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock (to balance) | | 1,000 |
| *Retire remaining treasury shares; return to unissued* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,000 + 9,000 = Cr 9,000 + 1,000. **Balanced.**  
After retirement: **39,000** shares issued and outstanding; authorized still 100,000; Treasury Stock balance **$0**. Net stockholders’ equity is **unchanged** by the retirement entry alone (only reclassifies within equity and changes issued count).

**Key insight:** Under the cost method, Treasury Stock is carried at acquisition cost and deducted from total equity. Multi-lot reissuances require a cost-flow assumption (FIFO here). “Gains” never go through income—excess of reissue proceeds (or original capital on retirement) over cost credits PIC—Treasury Stock; shortfalls first reduce that PIC, then Retained Earnings.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Single-lot treasury lifecycle: purchase, reissue above and below cost, equity balances, retirement
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — cost-method reacquisition, reissuance above cost then below cost (absorb PIC—TS then RE), ending equity accounts, subsequent retirement  
**Scenario:**  
**Lakewood Precision LLC** accounts for treasury stock under the **cost method**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Authorized common | **75,000** shares, **$2** par |
| Jan 1, Year 1 — issuance | **15,000** shares issued at **$22** cash per share |
| July 1, Year 1 — treasury purchase | **2,500** shares reacquired at **$25** per share |
| Oct 1, Year 1 — reissue (above cost) | **600** treasury shares sold at **$28** per share |
| Dec 1, Year 1 — reissue (below cost) | **900** treasury shares sold at **$20** per share |
| RE balance before any treasury-related adjustments | **$80,000** |
| Mar 5, Year 2 | Remaining **1,000** treasury shares formally **retired** |

**Required:**  
a. Journal entries for **January 1** issuance and **July 1** reacquisition.  
b. Journal entry for **October 1** reissuance (sales price > cost).  
c. Journal entry for **December 1** reissuance (sales price < cost), applying available PIC—Treasury Stock first.  
d. Ending balances of Common Stock, PIC in Excess of Par—CS, PIC—Treasury Stock, Retained Earnings, and Treasury Stock after the December 1 entry; shares issued and outstanding.  
e. Journal entry for **March 5, Year 2** subsequent retirement of the remaining treasury shares.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Issuance and reacquisition**

*January 1 — Issue 15,000 @ $22*  
CS = \(15{,}000 \times \$2 = \$30{,}000\)  
PIC-CS = \(15{,}000 \times (\$22 - \$2) = \$300{,}000\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 330,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 30,000 |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 300,000 |

**Check:** 330,000 = 30,000 + 300,000. **Balanced.**

*July 1 — Buy 2,500 @ $25*  
TS = \(2{,}500 \times \$25 = \$62{,}500\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 62,500 | |
| Cash | | 62,500 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 62,500. **Balanced.**

**b. October 1 — reissue 600 @ $28 (above cost)**

Cash = \(600 \times \$28 = \$16{,}800\)  
TS cost = \(600 \times \$25 = \$15{,}000\)  
PIC—TS = \(16{,}800 - 15{,}000 = \$1{,}800\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 16,800 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 15,000 |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | | 1,800 |
| *Reissue 600 TS shares above cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 16,800 = Cr 15,000 + 1,800. **Balanced.**

**c. December 1 — reissue 900 @ $20 (below cost)**

Cash = \(900 \times \$20 = \$18{,}000\)  
TS cost = \(900 \times \$25 = \$22{,}500\)  
Shortfall = \(22{,}500 - 18{,}000 = \$4{,}500\)  
Debit PIC—TS limited to its **$1,800** credit balance; remainder \(4{,}500 - 1{,}800 = \$2{,}700\) to **Retained Earnings**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 18,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock | 1,800 | |
| Retained Earnings | 2,700 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 22,500 |
| *Reissue 900 TS shares below cost; absorb PIC—TS then RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 + 1,800 + 2,700 = Cr 22,500. **Balanced.**

**d. Ending equity accounts after December 1**

| Account | Balance | Computation |
|---|---:|---|
| Common Stock | **$30,000** | unchanged (issued shares still 15,000) |
| PIC in Excess of Par—CS | **$300,000** | unchanged |
| PIC—Treasury Stock | **$0** | 1,800 − 1,800 |
| Retained Earnings | **$77,300** | 80,000 − 2,700 |
| Treasury Stock (contra) | **$25,000** Dr | 62,500 − 15,000 − 22,500; **1,000** shares × $25 |
| Shares issued | **15,000** | |
| Shares outstanding | **14,000** | 15,000 − 1,000 treasury |

**Total SE check:** \(30{,}000 + 300{,}000 + 77{,}300 - 25{,}000 = \$382{,}300\).

**e. March 5, Year 2 — retire remaining 1,000 shares**

Original capital per share = **$22** → \(1{,}000 \times \$2 = \$2{,}000\) CS; \(1{,}000 \times \$20 = \$20{,}000\) PIC-CS.  
TS cost = \(1{,}000 \times \$25 = \$25{,}000\).  
Excess of cost over original capital = \(25{,}000 - 22{,}000 = \$3{,}000\) → debit **Retained Earnings** (PIC—TS balance is zero).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 2,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 20,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | 3,000 | |
| Treasury Stock | | 25,000 |
| *Subsequent retirement of remaining treasury shares* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,000 + 20,000 + 3,000 = Cr 25,000. **Balanced.**  
After retirement: **14,000** shares issued and outstanding; RE = \(77{,}300 - 3{,}000 = \$74{,}300\).

**Key insight:** Reissuance “gains” build PIC—Treasury Stock; reissuance “losses” first reverse that PIC, then hit Retained Earnings. Retirement of remaining treasury uses the same balancing logic against original issue capital.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Direct retirement of common stock (above and below original issue price) vs holding as treasury
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Direct retirement when cash paid differs from original issuance capital; contrast with cost-method treasury purchase; shares reclassified to unissued  
**Scenario:**  
**Northshore Alloys Corp.** has the following equity history (no prior treasury or retired-stock PIC balances):

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Common stock | **$5** par; **8,000** shares issued and outstanding at **$40** cash per share |
| Common Stock account | \(8{,}000 \times \$5 = \$40{,}000\) |
| PIC in Excess of Par—CS | \(8{,}000 \times \$35 = \$280{,}000\) |
| Transaction A — June 30 | Reacquires and **immediately retires** **1,200** shares, paying **$45** cash per share |
| Transaction B — September 15 | Reacquires and **immediately retires** **500** shares, paying **$32** cash per share |
| Alternative for A | Same 1,200 shares reacquired at **$45** but held as **treasury** (cost method), not retired |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **June 30** journal entry for **direct retirement** of 1,200 shares at $45 (cash paid **above** original issuance price of $40).  
b. Record the **September 15** journal entry for **direct retirement** of 500 shares at $32 (cash paid **below** original issuance price).  
c. After both retirements, compute shares **issued** and **outstanding**, and balances of Common Stock and PIC in Excess of Par—CS.  
d. Alternatively for June 30 only: record the entry if the 1,200 shares are held as **treasury stock** (cost method) rather than retired. In one sentence, state how issued and outstanding shares differ under this alternative.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Direct retirement — cash paid > original issue price**

Cash paid = \(1{,}200 \times \$45 = \$54{,}000\)  
Original capital = \(1{,}200 \times \$40 = \$48{,}000\) → CS \(1{,}200 \times \$5 = \$6{,}000\); PIC-CS \(1{,}200 \times \$35 = \$42{,}000\)  
Excess paid = \(54{,}000 - 48{,}000 = \$6{,}000\) → debit **Retained Earnings** (no prior PIC—Retired Stock).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 6,000 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 42,000 | |
| Retained Earnings | 6,000 | |
| Cash | | 54,000 |
| *Direct retirement of 1,200 shares at $45; excess of cost over original capital to RE* | | |

**Check:** Dr 6,000 + 42,000 + 6,000 = Cr 54,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Direct retirement — cash paid < original issue price**

Cash paid = \(500 \times \$32 = \$16{,}000\)  
Original capital = \(500 \times \$40 = \$20{,}000\) → CS \(500 \times \$5 = \$2{,}500\); PIC-CS \(500 \times \$35 = \$17{,}500\)  
Excess of original capital over cash = \(20{,}000 - 16{,}000 = \$4{,}000\) → credit **Paid-In Capital—Retired Stock**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common Stock | 2,500 | |
| Paid-In Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | 17,500 | |
| Paid-In Capital—Retired Stock | | 4,000 |
| Cash | | 16,000 |
| *Direct retirement of 500 shares at $32; excess of original capital over cash to PIC—Retired Stock* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,500 + 17,500 = Cr 4,000 + 16,000. **Balanced.**

**c. After both direct retirements**

Shares retired = \(1{,}200 + 500 = 1{,}700\).  
Shares issued = outstanding = \(8{,}000 - 1{,}700 = 6{,}300\) (retired shares revert to **unissued**).  
Common Stock = \(6{,}300 \times \$5 = \$31{,}500\) (or \(40{,}000 - 6{,}000 - 2{,}500\)).  
PIC in Excess of Par—CS = \(280{,}000 - 42{,}000 - 17{,}500 = \$220{,}500\).  
PIC—Retired Stock = **$4,000** (credit).

**d. Alternative — hold as treasury (cost method) on June 30 only**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treasury Stock | 54,000 | |
| Cash | | 54,000 |
| *Reacquire 1,200 shares for treasury at $45 cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 54,000. **Balanced.**

**Issued vs outstanding:** Issued shares remain **8,000**; outstanding fall to \(8{,}000 - 1{,}200 = 6{,}800\). Under direct retirement in (a), both issued and outstanding fall to **6,800** after the June 30 retirement alone.

**Key insight:** Direct retirement removes capital accounts and reduces **issued** shares (shares return to unissued). Treasury stock under the cost method leaves **issued** shares unchanged, reduces only **outstanding** shares, and records a contra-equity cost balance until reissued or later retired.

### Q4 — MC (classification / method)
**LO:** LO 19-3  
**Concept:** Classification of treasury “gain” and balance-sheet presentation under the cost method  

**Question 1:**  
On reissuance of treasury stock **above** acquisition cost under the cost method, the excess of cash received over the carrying amount of Treasury Stock is:

- A) Recognized as a realized gain in net income  
- B) Credited to Paid-In Capital—Treasury Stock (additional paid-in capital)  
- C) Credited to Retained Earnings  
- D) Deferred as a liability until the shares are retired  

**Answer:** **B.** Under ASC 505-30 and the cost method, excess of reissue proceeds over treasury cost is credited to additional paid-in capital (PIC—Treasury Stock), never to income. Losses (excess of cost over proceeds) reduce that PIC first, then Retained Earnings.

**Question 2:**  
Under the cost method, how is treasury stock reported on the balance sheet?

- A) As a current asset at cost  
- B) As a noncurrent investment at fair value  
- C) As a deduction from total stockholders’ equity (capital stock + APIC + RE)  
- D) As a deduction only from the Common Stock account at par  

**Answer:** **C.** Cost of treasury stock is shown separately as a deduction from the total of capital stock, additional paid-in capital, and retained earnings. Shares remain **issued** but not outstanding; treasury stock is not an asset.
