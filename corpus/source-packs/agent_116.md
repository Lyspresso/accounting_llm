# Agent 116 — CORE DEMO — LO 20-2

**Chapter:** 20  
**LO title:** Account for stock options  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition / measurement:** measure total compensation cost at the **grant date** using an option-pricing model (e.g., Black-Scholes); **no journal entry** at grant date for equity-classified options
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** allocate grant-date fair value straight-line over the **requisite service period** (typically the vesting period)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** Dr Compensation Expense / Cr Paid-in Capital—Stock Options each period
- **Exercise (settlement) JE:** Dr Cash (exercise price × shares) + Dr PIC—Stock Options (pro rata grant-date cost) / Cr Common Stock + Cr PIC in Excess of Par
- **Expiration (maturity) JE:** reclassify remaining PIC—Stock Options to PIC—Expired Stock Options (equity stays in equity; no P&L impact)
- **Forfeitures:** as-incurred (revise cumulative expense in period of forfeiture) or estimate forfeitures at grant and revise estimates prospectively/cumulatively
- **Performance conditions:** accrue compensation only when achievement is **probable**; reverse/stop if not probable
- **Classification / presentation:** equity-classified awards → equity (PIC—Stock Options), not a liability; compensation expense in net income over service period

### Q1 — CORE — Grant-date measurement, service-period schedule, exercise & expiration
**LO:** LO 20-2  
**Concept:** Initial measurement of stock-option compensation at grant-date fair value (no grant JE); straight-line recognition schedule; exercise and expiration JEs; equity classification  
**Scenario:**  
**Cascade Forge Industries Inc.** (calendar-year) adopts an employee stock option plan. On **January 1, Year 1**, Cascade grants **80,000** stock options to employees. Each option allows purchase of one share of **$1 par** common stock at an exercise price of **$12**. The market price of Cascade common stock on the grant date is also **$12** (intrinsic value = $0). Options become exercisable after **January 1, Year 5** (cliff vesting; **four-year** requisite service period) and expire on **December 31, Year 12** (or earlier if the employee leaves before vesting). Using a Black-Scholes option-pricing model, management estimates the **fair value of each option at $6.50**, so total grant-date compensation cost is **$520,000**. Cascade elects to recognize forfeitures **as incurred**. Assume all employees remain through the service period unless a part states otherwise. On **January 1, Year 5**, employees exercise **60,000** options (75% of the award) when the market price is **$20** per share. The remaining **20,000** options (25%) are never exercised and expire on **December 31, Year 12**.

**Required:**  
a. Compute total compensation cost at the grant date. Record any journal entry on **January 1, Year 1** (grant date).  
b. Prepare the **four-year compensation recognition schedule** (years and amounts). Record the **December 31, Year 1** period-end adjusting entry.  
c. Record the **January 1, Year 5** journal entry for exercise of 60,000 options.  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 12** journal entry for expiration of the remaining 20,000 options.  
e. **Classification / presentation:** State how Paid-in Capital—Stock Options is classified on the balance sheet during Years 1–4, and where compensation expense appears. Does the grant create a liability?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Grant-date measurement and initial recognition JE**

Total compensation cost (grant-date fair value)  
= 80,000 options × $6.50 = **$520,000**

*January 1, Year 1 — Grant date*  
**No journal entry.** Total cost is measured at grant date, but equity-classified option compensation is recognized as expense over the requisite service period, not at grant.

**Check:** No Dr/Cr. **N/A balanced.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule and Year 1 period-end JE**

Straight-line annual compensation = $520,000 ÷ 4 years = **$130,000** per year.

| Year | Compensation expense | Cumulative PIC—Stock Options |
|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 | $130,000 | $130,000 |
| Year 2 | 130,000 | 260,000 |
| Year 3 | 130,000 | 390,000 |
| Year 4 | 130,000 | 520,000 |
| **Total** | **$520,000** | **$520,000** |

*December 31, Year 1 — To record compensation expense*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compensation Expense | 130,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | | 130,000 |
| *Grant-date FV $520,000 / 4-year service period* | | |

**Check:** Dr 130,000 = Cr 130,000. **Balanced.**  
(Identical $130,000 entries at Dec 31 of Years 2, 3, and 4.)

**c. Exercise (settlement) JE — January 1, Year 5**

Cash collected = 60,000 × $12 = **$720,000**  
PIC—Stock Options released = $520,000 × 75% = **$390,000**  
Common Stock = 60,000 × $1 = **$60,000**  
PIC in Excess of Par (plug) = $720,000 + $390,000 − $60,000 = **$1,050,000**

*January 1, Year 5 — To record exercise of stock options*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 720,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | 390,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 60,000 |
| Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 1,050,000 |
| *Exercise 60,000 options @ $12; release 75% of PIC—SO* | | |

**Check:** Dr 720,000 + 390,000 = 1,110,000; Cr 60,000 + 1,050,000 = 1,110,000. **Balanced.**  
(Market price of $20 is **not** used in the equity issuance entry; grant-date FV already drove compensation expense.)

**d. Expiration (maturity) JE — December 31, Year 12**

Remaining PIC—Stock Options = $520,000 × 25% = **$130,000**

*December 31, Year 12 — To record expiration of unexercised stock options*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | 130,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Expired Stock Options | | 130,000 |
| *Reclassify equity within equity; no income statement effect* | | |

**Check:** Dr 130,000 = Cr 130,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification / presentation**

- **Paid-in Capital—Stock Options** is reported in **stockholders’ equity** (additional paid-in capital), not as a liability.  
- **Compensation Expense** is recognized in **net income** over the requisite service period (typically operating expense / SG&A or a separate share-based compensation line).  
- The grant does **not** create a liability for equity-classified employee stock options under ASC 718; the credit is to equity as services are rendered.

**Key insight:** Measure at grant-date fair value (option-pricing model), book **nothing** at grant, then Dr Comp Exp / Cr PIC—SO over the service period. Exercise combines cash at the strike price with a release of PIC—SO into permanent capital accounts; unexercised options reclassify PIC—SO to PIC—Expired (still equity).

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Full option life cycle (different numbers)
**LO:** LO 20-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: grant-date FV measurement (no grant JE), multi-year recognition schedule, exercise and expiration JEs  
**Scenario:**  
**Northline Analytics Corp.** grants **50,000** stock options on **January 1, Year 1**. Each option allows purchase of one share of **$2 par** common stock at an exercise price of **$20**. Market price on the grant date is **$20**. Options cliff-vest after a **three-year** requisite service period (exercisable beginning January 1, Year 4) and expire December 31, Year 10. An option-pricing model values the award at **$9 per option** (total compensation cost **$450,000**). Northline recognizes forfeitures as incurred; assume no forfeitures through vesting. On **January 1, Year 4**, employees exercise **40,000** options (80%) when the market price is **$28**. The remaining **10,000** options (20%) expire unexercised on **December 31, Year 10**.

**Required:**  
a. Compute total compensation cost and record any **January 1, Year 1** grant-date entry.  
b. Prepare the **three-year compensation schedule** and the **December 31, Year 1** adjusting JE.  
c. Record the **January 1, Year 4** exercise entry for 40,000 options.  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 10** expiration entry for 10,000 options.  
e. Briefly state the financial statement classification of the credit balance in PIC—Stock Options at December 31, Year 2.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Grant-date measurement**

Total compensation = 50,000 × $9 = **$450,000**  
*January 1, Year 1:* **No journal entry.**

**b. Subsequent measurement schedule and Year 1 adjusting JE**

Annual expense = $450,000 ÷ 3 = **$150,000**

| Year | Compensation expense | Cumulative PIC—Stock Options |
|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 | $150,000 | $150,000 |
| Year 2 | 150,000 | 300,000 |
| Year 3 | 150,000 | 450,000 |
| **Total** | **$450,000** | **$450,000** |

*December 31, Year 1 — To record compensation expense*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compensation Expense | 150,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | | 150,000 |

**Check:** Dr 150,000 = Cr 150,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Exercise JE — January 1, Year 4**

Cash = 40,000 × $20 = **$800,000**  
PIC—Stock Options = $450,000 × 80% = **$360,000**  
Common Stock = 40,000 × $2 = **$80,000**  
PIC in Excess of Par = $800,000 + $360,000 − $80,000 = **$1,080,000**

*January 1, Year 4 — To record exercise of stock options*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 800,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | 360,000 | |
| Common Stock | | 80,000 |
| Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par—Common Stock | | 1,080,000 |

**Check:** Dr 800,000 + 360,000 = 1,160,000; Cr 80,000 + 1,080,000 = 1,160,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Expiration JE — December 31, Year 10**

PIC—Stock Options remaining = $450,000 × 20% = **$90,000**

*December 31, Year 10 — To record expiration of stock options*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | 90,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Expired Stock Options | | 90,000 |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Classification at December 31, Year 2**

PIC—Stock Options balance = $300,000 (after two years of recognition). Reported in **stockholders’ equity** (APIC), not as a liability or asset.

**Key insight:** Same accounting skeleton as Q1 with new counts, par, strike, FV, and service years — always measure at grant FV, expense over service, then settle via exercise or reclassify on expiration.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Forfeitures as incurred, estimated forfeitures, and performance conditions
**LO:** LO 20-2  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JEs with forfeiture policy election; cumulative catch-up; performance-condition probability gate  
**Scenario:**  
**Helix Biomedical Inc.** grants **120,000** stock options on **January 1, Year 1**. Grant-date fair value (option-pricing model) totals **$720,000**. Requisite service period is **three years** (cliff vesting on January 1, Year 4). Common stock is **$1 par**; exercise price is **$15**. Helix’s default policy is to recognize forfeitures **as incurred**.

**Part A — Forfeitures as incurred**  
Year 1: no forfeitures.  
During Year 2, employees holding **18,000** options (15% of the award) leave; those options are forfeited.

**Part B — Estimated forfeitures (alternate policy)**  
Ignore Part A. Instead, at grant Helix estimates **10%** forfeitures and expenses based on expected awards. No actual forfeitures occur in Year 1.

**Part C — Performance condition**  
Ignore Parts A–B. Same $720,000 grant-date fair value and three-year service period, but options vest only if Helix’s annual revenue grows by at least **8%** in each of Years 1–3.  
(i) At December 31, Year 1, management concludes achievement is **not probable**.  
(ii) At December 31, Year 2, management concludes achievement **is probable** (and remains probable).

**Required:**  
a. Record compensation expense at **December 31, Year 1** and **December 31, Year 2** under the as-incurred policy in Part A. Show the revised total cost and cumulative catch-up logic.  
b. Record compensation expense at **December 31, Year 1** under the estimated-forfeiture policy in Part B.  
c. For Part C: state (and record, if any) Year 1 and Year 2 compensation expense under the performance condition.  
d. **Classification:** If the options later expire unexercised after full vesting and full expense recognition, does expiration reverse previously recognized compensation expense? Explain briefly.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Forfeitures as incurred (Part A)**

*December 31, Year 1 — To record compensation expense*  
Original annual amount = $720,000 ÷ 3 = **$240,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compensation Expense | 240,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | | 240,000 |

**Check:** Dr 240,000 = Cr 240,000. **Balanced.**

Year 2 forfeiture: 15% of options → revised total compensation cost  
= $720,000 × 85% = **$612,000**

After two years of service, cumulative PIC—SO / expense should be  
$612,000 × 2/3 = **$408,000**

Year 2 expense (catch-up) = $408,000 − $240,000 already recognized = **$168,000**

*December 31, Year 2 — To record compensation expense adjusted for forfeitures*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compensation Expense | 168,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | | 168,000 |
| *Cumulative to $408,000 after 15% forfeiture; catch-up from $240,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr 168,000 = Cr 168,000. **Balanced.**  
(Year 3 would then be $612,000 − $408,000 = **$204,000**.)

**b. Estimated forfeitures at grant (Part B)**

Expected cost = $720,000 × 90% = **$648,000**  
Year 1 expense = $648,000 ÷ 3 = **$216,000**

*December 31, Year 1 — To record compensation expense (estimated forfeitures)*  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compensation Expense | 216,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | | 216,000 |

**Check:** Dr 216,000 = Cr 216,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Performance condition (Part C)**

(i) *December 31, Year 1* — Achievement **not probable** → **no compensation expense** recorded (no JE).

(ii) *December 31, Year 2* — Achievement becomes **probable**. Cumulative expense should equal two years of service on full grant-date cost:  
$720,000 × 2/3 = **$480,000**  
(Recognize the full catch-up in Year 2 because probability flipped.)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compensation Expense | 480,000 | |
| Paid-in Capital—Stock Options | | 480,000 |
| *Probable performance condition; cumulative 2/3 of $720,000* | | |

**Check:** Dr 480,000 = Cr 480,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Expiration classification / income-statement effect**

**No.** Expiration after full vesting and full expense recognition does **not** reverse compensation expense. The company reclassifies PIC—Stock Options to PIC—Expired Stock Options **within equity**. Net income is unaffected by the reclassification.

**Key insight:** Forfeiture policy changes the timing/amount of period-end catch-ups; performance conditions gate whether any cost is accrued until probable. Expiration never “undoes” expense already earned under the service condition.

### Q4 — MC — Grant-date recognition for equity stock options
**LO:** LO 20-2  
**Concept:** Classification of the accounting event at the grant date for equity-classified employee stock options  
**Question:**  
On the **grant date** of an equity-classified employee stock option award measured using an option-pricing model, which statement is correct?

- A) Debit Compensation Expense and credit a liability for the full grant-date fair value.  
- B) Debit Unearned Compensation (contra-equity) and credit Common Stock for the grant-date fair value.  
- C) Measure total compensation cost at grant-date fair value, but record **no** journal entry until the service period begins to be recognized (typically at period-end).  
- D) Measure compensation cost at intrinsic value only; if strike equals market, total cost is zero and remains zero even if an option-pricing model yields a positive fair value.

**Answer:** **C.** Under ASC 718 / core Demo 20-2 treatment, total compensation is measured at grant-date fair value from an option-pricing model, but the journal entry recognizing expense and PIC—Stock Options is made over the requisite service period—not at grant. A is wrong (equity-classified awards are not recorded as liabilities). B confuses restricted stock award mechanics with options. D is the outdated intrinsic-value approach; options can have positive fair value even when intrinsic value is zero.

### Q5 — MC — Exercise vs expiration classification of the equity credit
**LO:** LO 20-2  
**Concept:** Classification of settlement outcomes for vested stock options (exercise vs time-lapse expiration)  
**Question:**  
After a stock option award has fully vested and all related compensation cost has been recognized in PIC—Stock Options, what is the correct accounting when some options **expire unexercised** at the contractual expiration date?

- A) Debit Compensation Expense and credit PIC—Stock Options to reverse the original expense.  
- B) Debit PIC—Stock Options and credit PIC—Expired Stock Options (equity reclassification; no income effect).  
- C) Debit PIC—Stock Options and credit Gain on Expiration of Options.  
- D) Debit Retained Earnings and credit Common Stock for the grant-date fair value of the expired options.

**Answer:** **B.** Expiration is a **within-equity** reclassification from PIC—Stock Options to PIC—Expired Stock Options. Compensation expense is not reversed, and no gain is recognized.
