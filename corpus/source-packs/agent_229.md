# Agent 229 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-5

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Describe accounting for subsequent events and contingencies including litigation, warranties, and other contingencies  
**Critical gap LO:** no  
**Emphasis angle:** subsequent_measurement_schedule

## Concept list for this pack
- **Loss contingency criteria (ASC 450):** accrue only if **probable** and **reasonably estimable**; otherwise disclose if at least reasonably possible; remote → generally neither accrue nor disclose (except certain guarantees)
- **Range of loss:** accrue the **better estimate** within the range; if no amount is better, accrue the **minimum** and disclose exposure above the minimum
- **Initial recognition JE:** debit loss/expense, credit estimated liability (litigation) or warranty liability (assurance-type warranty)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-year **warranty liability rollforward** (beginning balance + accruals − claims = ending balance)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** year-end warranty accrual (and year-end litigation accrual when criteria met)
- **Settlement / maturity JE:** pay warranty claims against the liability; settle litigation (difference from carrying amount → gain/loss change in estimate, or adjust pre-issuance via recognized subsequent event)
- **Recognized subsequent events:** post–balance-sheet evidence of conditions existing at year-end → **adjust** year-end amounts
- **Nonrecognized subsequent events:** conditions arising after year-end → **disclose** (do not accrue in prior year) if needed to avoid misleading FS
- **Assurance-type warranty:** loss contingency; full estimated cost accrued in year of sale
- **Service-type warranty:** separate performance obligation; cash → deferred revenue; recognize revenue over coverage (not a loss contingency)
- **Gain contingencies:** **never accrued**; may disclose carefully

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-year assurance warranty: sale, accruals, claims, liability schedule (emphasis)
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Assurance-type warranty loss contingency — initial recognition of sales and estimated liability; subsequent measurement schedule (warranty liability rollforward); period-end adjusting accruals; settlement of claims against the liability  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedarline Outdoor Gear** (calendar-year company) sells camping equipment that includes a **two-year assurance-type warranty** (not sold separately; protects against manufacturing defects). Management estimates warranty costs at **1.5% of sales** based on historical experience. Actual warranty costs are paid in cash when claims are processed. Ignore cost of goods sold.

| Year | Product sales (cash) | Actual warranty claims paid |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $2,400,000 | $12,000 |
| 2 | 2,800,000 | 48,000 |
| 3 | 3,000,000 | 55,000 |

Warranty estimates remain 1.5% of that year’s sales each year. No service-type (extended) warranties are sold.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **Year 1** journal entry for **product sales** (initial revenue recognition).  
b. Record the **December 31, Year 1 period-end adjusting entry** to accrue estimated warranty cost.  
c. Record the **Year 1** entry to **settle / pay** actual warranty claims.  
d. Prepare a complete **subsequent measurement schedule** for the **warranty liability** for Years 1–3 showing beginning balance, accruals, claims, and ending balance (**emphasis**).  
e. Record the **December 31, Year 2** period-end adjusting entry for the Year 2 warranty accrual.  
f. Record the **Year 3** entry to pay Year 3 warranty claims (using the liability first).  
g. In one or two sentences, explain why an assurance-type warranty is treated as a **loss contingency** rather than deferred revenue.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — Year 1 sales**

*Year 1 — Product sales (assurance warranty included in price)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 2,400,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 2,400,000 |
| *Record product sales; assurance warranty not separately priced* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,400,000 = Cr 2,400,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Period-end adjusting JE — Year 1 warranty accrual**

Estimated warranty cost = \(\$2{,}400{,}000 \times 0.015 = \$36{,}000\).

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrue assurance-type warranty*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 36,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 36,000 |
| *Accrue probable and reasonably estimable warranty loss contingency* | | |

**Check:** Dr 36,000 = Cr 36,000. **Balanced.**

**c. Settlement of Year 1 claims**

*Year 1 — Pay warranty claims*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 12,000 | |
| Cash | | 12,000 |
| *Settle claims against estimated warranty liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr 12,000 = Cr 12,000. **Balanced.**

**d. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — Warranty liability rollforward**

| Year | Beginning liability | + Accrual (1.5% × sales) | − Claims paid | Ending liability |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 36,000 | 12,000 | **24,000** |
| 2 | 24,000 | 42,000 | 48,000 | **18,000** |
| 3 | 18,000 | 45,000 | 55,000 | **8,000** |
| **Totals** | | **123,000** | **115,000** | |

**Computations:**  
- Y2 accrual = \(\$2{,}800{,}000 \times 0.015 = \$42{,}000\)  
- Y3 accrual = \(\$3{,}000{,}000 \times 0.015 = \$45{,}000\)  
- Y2 end: \(24{,}000 + 42{,}000 - 48{,}000 = 18{,}000\)  
- Y3 end: \(18{,}000 + 45{,}000 - 55{,}000 = 8{,}000\)  

**Roll-forward check:** Total accruals − total claims = ending liability when beg = 0: \(123{,}000 - 115{,}000 = \$8{,}000\). **OK.**

**e. Period-end adjusting JE — Year 2**

*December 31, Year 2 — Accrue warranty*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 42,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 42,000 |

**Check:** Dr 42,000 = Cr 42,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Settlement of Year 3 claims**

*Year 3 — Pay warranty claims \( \$55{,}000 \)*  
Beginning Y3 liability after Y3 accrual = \(18{,}000 + 45{,}000 = 63{,}000\) (claims still reduce the liability; accrual timing within the year does not change the net YE schedule). Claims of $55,000 do not exceed the liability available under the year-end rollforward model used in (d); record full reduction of liability:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 55,000 | |
| Cash | | 55,000 |

**Check:** Dr 55,000 = Cr 55,000. **Balanced.**  
(If claims were recorded before the Y3 accrual and temporarily exceeded the unadjusted liability, any excess would be charged to Warranty Expense as a change in estimate; the year-end schedule in (d) still holds.)

**g. Why assurance-type is a loss contingency**  
An assurance-type warranty relates to **existing but unidentified defects** in products already sold; the obligation is estimated and **accrued as expense** in the period of sale when the loss is probable and reasonably estimable. It is **not** a separate performance obligation, so cash is not deferred as unearned warranty revenue.

**Key insight:** The **warranty liability subsequent measurement schedule** is the core control: every period’s accrual increases the liability; claims reduce it; the ending balance must equal beginning + accruals − claims. Assurance warranties load estimated cost into **expense of the sale year**, even when cash repairs occur later.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Multi-year warranty schedule twin
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: assurance-type warranty initial recognition, subsequent measurement liability schedule, period-end accruals, and claim settlements with one period where claims exceed the pre-claim liability balance  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Peak Electronics** (calendar year) includes a **three-year assurance-type warranty** in the selling price of its devices. Estimated warranty cost = **2.5% of sales**. Claims are paid in cash. Ignore COGS.

| Year | Product sales (cash) | Actual warranty claims paid |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $1,600,000 | $8,000 |
| 2 | 2,000,000 | 52,000 |
| 3 | 1,800,000 | 45,000 |

**Required:**  
a. Record Year 1 **sales** and the **December 31, Year 1** warranty **accrual**.  
b. Record Year 1 **claims** settlement.  
c. Prepare the **warranty liability subsequent measurement schedule** for Years 1–3.  
d. Record **December 31, Year 2** accrual and **Year 2 claims** (assume Year 2 claims are paid **after** the Year 2 accrual is recorded).  
e. Record Year 3 **accrual** and Year 3 **claims**.  
f. Reconcile: total warranty **expense** recognized over Years 1–3 equals total **accruals** on the schedule; total cash claims equal total reductions of liability (no excess-claim expense under the timing in d–e).

Y1 accrual = \(\$1{,}600{,}000 \times 0.025 = \$40{,}000\).

| Cash | 1,600,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 1,600,000 |

**Check:** Dr 1,600,000 = Cr 1,600,000. **Balanced.**

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrue warranty*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Expense | 40,000 | |
| Warranty Liability | | 40,000 |

**Check:** Dr 40,000 = Cr 40,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Year 1 claims settlement**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 8,000 | |
| Cash | | 8,000 |

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis)**

| 2 | 32,000 | 50,000 | 52,000 | **30,000** |
| 3 | 30,000 | 45,000 | 45,000 | **30,000** |
| **Totals** | | **135,000** | **105,000** | |

**Computations:**  
- Y2 accrual = \(\$2{,}000{,}000 \times 0.025 = \$50{,}000\)  
- Y3 accrual = \(\$1{,}800{,}000 \times 0.025 = \$45{,}000\)  
- Y2 end: \(32{,}000 + 50{,}000 - 52{,}000 = 30{,}000\)  
- Y3 end: \(30{,}000 + 45{,}000 - 45{,}000 = 30{,}000\)

**d. Year 2 period-end accrual and claims**

|---|---:|---:|

**Check:** Dr 50,000 = Cr 50,000. **Balanced.**

*Year 2 — Claims (after accrual; liability before claims = \(32{,}000 + 50{,}000 = 82{,}000\))*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 52,000 | |
| Cash | | 52,000 |

**Check:** Dr 52,000 = Cr 52,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Year 3 accrual and claims**

*December 31, Year 3 — Accrue*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Warranty Liability | 45,000 | |
| Cash | | 45,000 |

**Check:** Dr 45,000 = Cr 45,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Reconciliation**  
Total warranty expense (accruals) Years 1–3 = **$135,000**.  
Total claims paid = **$105,000**.  
Ending warranty liability = **$30,000** = \(135{,}000 - 105{,}000\). Books reconcile.

**Key insight:** Changing every sales, rate, and claim amount still produces the same control structure: **schedule first**, then every JE must keep the liability rollforward intact (Dr = Cr on each entry).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Litigation contingency, range accrual, recognized subsequent event, settlement
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Loss contingency initial recognition (range minimum); period-end accrual; recognized subsequent event measurement update before FS issuance; settlement JE; contrast with nonrecognized subsequent event and gain contingency  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborstone Chemicals** is a calendar-year company. Year 1 financial statements are **issued March 12, Year 2**.

**Litigation facts (parts a–c):**  
In **November Year 1**, a plant accident injured a third party. A lawsuit seeking **$500,000** was filed in December Year 1. Counsel advises that a loss is **probable**. At December 31, Year 1, the reasonably estimable range of loss is **$150,000 to $400,000**, and **no amount within the range is a better estimate** than any other.

On **February 8, Year 2** (before issuance), the parties agree to settle for **$210,000** cash, payable **April 1, Year 2**.

**Additional independent situations (parts d–f):**  
d. Same accident and lawsuit timing as above, but at December 31 Year 1 management concludes loss is **probable** yet **not reasonably estimable**. Settlement of **$90,000** cash occurs **June 30, Year 2** (after FS issuance).  
e. On **January 20, Year 2**, a warehouse fire (no Year 1 condition) destroys inventory with carrying amount **$75,000** (uninsured). FS still issued March 12 Year 2.  
f. Harborstone’s attorneys believe it is **highly probable** Harborstone will **win** a countersuit and collect **$120,000** in Year 2.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **December 31, Year 1** journal entry for the litigation contingency (before considering the February settlement).  
b. Explain and record the **recognized subsequent event** effect on the **Year 1** financial statements after the February 8 settlement agreement (measurement date before issuance).  
c. Record the **April 1, Year 2 settlement** payment.  
d. For the “probable but not estimable” path: what is recorded at December 31 Year 1, and record the **June 30, Year 2** settlement.  
e. Is the January 20 fire a **recognized** or **nonrecognized** subsequent event for Year 1? What is the Year 1 accounting?  
f. What is the Year 1 accounting for the **gain contingency**?

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition — range minimum (period-end adjusting JE)**

No better estimate → accrue **minimum** of range = **$150,000**. Disclose possible additional loss up to the top of the range (not required as a JE).

*December 31, Year 1 — Accrue loss contingency (minimum of range)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation | 150,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 150,000 |
| *Probable and reasonably estimable; accrue minimum of range* | | |

**Check:** Dr 150,000 = Cr 150,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Recognized subsequent event — adjust Year 1 measurement**

The accident and related obligation existed at December 31 Year 1. Settlement **before FS issuance** provides additional evidence of the year-end condition → **recognized subsequent event**. Adjust Year 1 liability from $150,000 to the settlement amount **$210,000** (additional loss \(210{,}000 - 150{,}000 = \$60{,}000\)).

*Adjusting entry dated December 31, Year 1 (recognized subsequent event)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation | 60,000 | |
| Estimated Litigation Liability | | 60,000 |
| *Update Year 1 accrual to post–balance-sheet settlement evidence* | | |

**Check:** Dr 60,000 = Cr 60,000. **Balanced.**  
Carrying amount of Estimated Litigation Liability for Year 1 BS = **$210,000**.  
Total Year 1 litigation loss = **$210,000**.

**c. Settlement JE (disposal / maturity of contingency)**

*April 1, Year 2 — Pay settlement*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estimated Litigation Liability | 210,000 | |
| Cash | | 210,000 |
| *Settle accrued litigation liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr 210,000 = Cr 210,000. **Balanced.**  
No Year 2 gain or loss (amount matches carrying amount).

**d. Probable but not reasonably estimable**

*December 31, Year 1:* **No accrual** (estimability condition not met). **Disclose** nature of contingency and that an estimate cannot be made (or range if available).

*June 30, Year 2 — Record settlement when amount becomes known*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Loss on Litigation | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 90,000 |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**  
Loss is recognized in **Year 2** (period when estimable/settled), not Year 1.

**e. Nonrecognized subsequent event**  
The fire arose from conditions **after** the balance sheet date → **nonrecognized** subsequent event. **Do not** record a Year 1 loss or write-down. **Disclose** the nature of the event and an estimate of financial effect ($75,000 inventory loss) if needed to keep Year 1 FS from being misleading. The loss is recorded in **Year 2** when the fire occurs.

**f. Gain contingency**  
**No accrual** of the $120,000 in Year 1 (gain contingencies are **never accrued**). Careful **disclosure** may be provided without implying realization is certain.

**Key insight:** For loss contingencies, **when** information arrives matters as much as **what** it says: evidence before issuance about year-end conditions **adjusts** Year 1 (recognized subsequent event); new conditions after year-end are **disclosed**, not accrued backward. Range accruals start at the **minimum** (or better estimate), then settlement updates measurement.

### Q4 — MC — Subsequent event classification
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Distinguish recognized vs nonrecognized subsequent events for litigation and other post–balance-sheet events  
**Question:**  
**Riverglen Packaging** has a December 31 Year 1 year-end. Financial statements are issued February 28 Year 2. Which event is a **recognized subsequent event** that should **adjust** amounts in the December 31 Year 1 financial statements?

- A) On January 15 Year 2, Riverglen issues $2,000,000 of bonds at par to refinance short-term working capital.  
- B) On February 5 Year 2, Riverglen settles a lawsuit arising from a **Year 1** product injury for $180,000 cash; at year-end the loss was probable and a $100,000 liability had been accrued.  
- C) On January 8 Year 2, a flood (new post–year-end casualty) destroys a warehouse; uninsured carrying amount of inventory lost is $40,000.  
- D) On February 10 Year 2, the market price of Riverglen’s publicly traded stock declines 22%.

**Answer:** **B.**  
Settlement of litigation whose **underlying events occurred before the balance sheet date**, occurring **before FS issuance**, provides additional evidence about conditions existing at year-end. Riverglen should adjust the Year 1 estimated litigation liability from $100,000 to **$180,000** (additional Year 1 loss $80,000).  
A and C are **nonrecognized** subsequent events (disclose if material/misleading to omit); D is neither recognized nor typically disclosed as a subsequent event in the notes (market price changes).

### Q5 — MC — Contingency reporting treatment
**LO:** LO 15-5  
**Concept:** Apply probable / reasonably possible / remote and estimability grid for loss contingencies  
**Question:**  
At December 31, **Northbridge Tools** is a defendant in a suit over a **Year 1** contract dispute. Management and counsel conclude a loss is **reasonably possible** (not probable). A reasonable estimate of loss is **$55,000**. What is the correct Year 1 financial reporting?

- A) Accrue a $55,000 liability and expense; no disclosure needed beyond the accrual.  
- B) Accrue $55,000 and disclose the nature of the contingency.  
- C) Do **not** accrue; **disclose** the nature of the contingency and an estimate of possible loss ($55,000) or state that an estimate cannot be made.  
- D) Neither accrue nor disclose because the loss is not probable.

**Answer:** **C.**  
Accrual requires **both** probable **and** reasonably estimable. **Reasonably possible** → **disclose only** (no accrual), including nature and estimate or statement that estimate is not possible. Remote would generally require neither accrual nor disclosure (except certain guarantees).
