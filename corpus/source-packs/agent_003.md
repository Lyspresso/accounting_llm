# Agent 3 — CORE DEMO — LO 14-2

**Chapter:** 14  
**LO title:** Account for debt securities measured at FV-NI with fair value adjustments at sale date and period-end  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Initial recognition of trading debt securities (purchase at par, including between interest dates)
- Interest revenue on TS debt (cash interest vs. interest earned; reverse accrued interest receivable)
- Period-end Fair Value Adjustment (FVA) through net income (FV-NI)
- Sale-date FV-NI: re-measure to fair value, then record sale and eliminate FVA (no separate realized gain/loss if already at FV)
- Subsequent measurement when purchased at discount (effective interest amortized cost + FVA)
- Fair value option election on debt that would otherwise be HTM (same FV-NI mechanics as TS)
- Classification / presentation: TS as current assets; unrealized holding G/L in other income (NI, not OCI)

### Q1 — CORE — TS purchase between interest dates, year-end FVA, and sale (FV-NI)

**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of trading debt securities + period-end and sale-date FV-NI adjustments  
**Scenario:** On **October 1, Year 1**, **Northline Capital LLC** purchased **at par** **$50,000** face of **Cascade Industrial** **6%** bonds (bonds dated **July 1, Year 1**; mature **June 30, Year 8**). Cash interest is paid **semiannually on June 30 and December 31**. Northline classifies the investment as **trading securities (TS)** and measures it at **FV-NI**. Northline’s accounting period ends **December 31**. Brokerage fees are zero.  
Additional facts:  
- Fair value of the Cascade bonds on **December 31, Year 1** is **$48,200**.  
- On **January 15, Year 2**, Northline sells the entire holding for **$48,800** cash (no accrued interest on this date for simplicity—assume sale coincides with a settlement convention that prices clean; or treat quoted amount as dirty price equal to clean fair value with no coupon accrual since last payment date is Dec 31).  

**Required:**  
(a) Record the **October 1, Year 1** purchase entry (include accrued interest).  
(b) Record receipt of cash interest on **December 31, Year 1**.  
(c) Record the **December 31, Year 1** entry to adjust the TS investment to fair value (show FVA worksheet).  
(d) Show how the investment and related income statement items appear for Year 1 (balance sheet carrying amount; interest revenue; unrealized holding G/L).  
(e) Record the **January 15, Year 2** sale: first adjust to fair value using FV-NI, then record the sale and eliminate the associated FVA balance. Reconcile total G/L recognized over the holding period to (proceeds − amortized cost).  

**Answer key:**  

**(a) October 1, Year 1 — Purchase between interest dates**  
Accrued interest from July 1 to October 1 = 3 months:  
\(50{,}000 \times 0.06 \times 3/12 = \$750\)  
Cash paid = \(50{,}000 + 750 = \$50{,}750\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in TS—Cascade Bonds | 50,000 | |
| Interest Receivable | 750 | |
|  Cash | | 50,750 |

*Check: Dr 50,750 = Cr 50,750.*

**(b) December 31, Year 1 — Receipt of semiannual cash interest**  
Cash (full 6 months) = \(50{,}000 \times 0.06 \times 6/12 = \$1{,}500\)  
Interest revenue earned since purchase (3 months, Oct–Dec) = \(50{,}000 \times 0.06 \times 3/12 = \$750\)  
Reverse Interest Receivable \(750\).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 1,500 | |
|  Interest Receivable | | 750 |
|  Interest Revenue | | 750 |

*Check: Dr 1,500 = Cr 1,500.*

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — Adjust TS to fair value (FV-NI)**  

| TS Investment | Amortized cost | Fair value YE | Unrealized holding G/(L) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cascade bonds | $50,000 | $48,200 | $(1,800)$ |
| Existing FVA balance | | | 0 |
| **Increase (decrease) to FVA needed** | | | **$(1,800)$** |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income | 1,800 | |
|  Fair Value Adjustment—TS | | 1,800 |

*Check: Dr 1,800 = Cr 1,800. Carrying amount = amortized cost − credit FVA = 50,000 − 1,800 = **$48,200** = FV.*

**(d) Year 1 financial statement presentation**  

**Balance sheet (Dec 31, Year 1) — Current assets**  
Investment in trading securities …… **$48,200**  
*(Investment in TS $50,000 less Fair Value Adjustment—TS credit $1,800)*

**Income statement (Year ended Dec 31, Year 1) — Other revenues (expenses)**  
Interest revenue ……………………………… **$750**  
Unrealized holding loss on trading securities … **$(1,800)**

**(e) January 15, Year 2 — Sale (adjust to FV, then eliminate FVA)**  

Required FVA at sale date\* = \(48{,}800 - 50{,}000 = \$(1{,}200)\) (credit)  
Less existing FVA balance = \((1{,}800)\) credit  
**Amount of increase (decrease) to FVA needed** = **$600** (debit FVA / credit unrealized G/L)

\*$48,800 fair value − $50,000 amortized cost.  
*Proof of YE amount:* \(50{,}000 - 48{,}200 = 1{,}800\) (not \(2{,}000\)).

*Adjust to fair value at sale date:*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fair Value Adjustment—TS | 600 | |
|  Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income | | 600 |

*Check: Dr 600 = Cr 600. FVA now credit $1,200; carrying amount = 50,000 − 1,200 = $48,800.*

*Record sale and eliminate FVA (no separate realized G/L—already in earnings via FV-NI):*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 48,800 | |
| Fair Value Adjustment—TS | 1,200 | |
|  Investment in TS—Cascade Bonds | | 50,000 |

*Check: Dr 50,000 = Cr 50,000.*

**Holding-period G/L reconciliation:**  
Unrealized loss in Year 1 income …… $(1,800)  
Unrealized gain in Year 2 income …… 600  
**Net loss over ownership period** …… **$(1,200)**  
= Cost $50,000 − proceeds $48,800. ✓  

**Key insight:** For TS measured at FV-NI, purchase between interest dates capitalizes only the bond cost in the investment account and records **Interest Receivable** for accrued coupon paid; period-end and sale-date **FVA** moves push all fair-value changes into **net income**. The sale entry eliminates amortized cost and FVA against cash—any “realized” economic result already hit earnings through prior FV-NI entries.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — TS between interest dates, FVA, sale

**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of trading debt securities + period-end and sale-date FV-NI adjustments (number twin)  
**Scenario:** On **October 1, Year 1**, **Harborcrest Partners** purchased **at par** **$60,000** face of **Meridian Utilities** **8%** bonds (dated **July 1, Year 1**; mature **June 30, Year 9**). Cash interest is paid **semiannually on June 30 and December 31**. Classified as **TS (FV-NI)**. Year-end is **December 31**.  
- Fair value on **December 31, Year 1** is **$61,500**.  
- On **February 1, Year 2**, Harborcrest sells the bonds for **$61,100** cash (no additional accrued-interest complexity beyond the post–Dec 31 clean price given).  

**Required:**  
(a) Purchase entry on October 1, Year 1.  
(b) Interest receipt entry on December 31, Year 1.  
(c) Year-end fair value adjusting entry (with FVA worksheet).  
(d) Balance sheet carrying amount and Year 1 income-statement effects.  
(e) February 1, Year 2 sale: FV-NI adjustment then sale with FVA elimination; reconcile holding-period G/L.  

**Answer key:**  

**(a) October 1, Year 1**  
Accrued interest (3 months) = \(60{,}000 \times 0.08 \times 3/12 = \$1{,}200\)  
Cash paid = \(60{,}000 + 1{,}200 = \$61{,}200\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in TS—Meridian Bonds | 60,000 | |
| Interest Receivable | 1,200 | |
|  Cash | | 61,200 |

*Dr = Cr = 61,200.*

**(b) December 31, Year 1**  
Cash (6 months) = \(60{,}000 \times 0.08 \times 6/12 = \$2{,}400\)  
Interest revenue (3 months earned) = \(60{,}000 \times 0.08 \times 3/12 = \$1{,}200\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 2,400 | |
|  Interest Receivable | | 1,200 |
|  Interest Revenue | | 1,200 |

*Dr = Cr = 2,400.*

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — FV adjustment**  

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|

| Account | Debit | Credit |

**(d) Presentation**  
**BS (current assets):** Investment in trading securities **$61,500**  
**IS other income:** Interest revenue **$1,200**; Unrealized holding gain on trading securities **$1,500**

**(e) February 1, Year 2 — Sale**  
Required FVA at sale = \(61{,}100 - 60{,}000 = \$1{,}100\) debit

| Account | Debit | Credit |

|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 61,100 | |

|  Investment in TS—Meridian Bonds | | 60,000 |

*Dr 61,100 = Cr 61,100.*

**Holding-period reconciliation:**  
Year 1 unrealized gain …… $1,500  
Year 2 unrealized loss …… (400)  
**Net gain over ownership** …… **$1,100** = proceeds $61,100 − cost $60,000. ✓  

**Key insight:** Same FV-NI mechanics as Q1 with all different amounts: when fair value **rises** above amortized cost, FVA is a **debit** adjunct; a later partial reverse before sale still ensures net income over the life equals cash profit or loss on the position.

### Q3 — CORE — Discount TS: effective-interest amortized cost, year-end FVA, disposal

**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule (discount amortization) + period-end FVA + disposal under FV-NI  
**Scenario:** On **January 1, Year 1**, **Westbrook Advisors Inc.** purchases **$100,000** face of **Oakridge Logistics** **5%** bonds for **$93,225.58**. The bonds pay **cash interest annually each December 31** and mature on **December 31, Year 4** (four years remaining). The market (effective) rate on the purchase date is **7%**. Westbrook classifies the bonds as **trading securities** and uses the **effective interest method**. Year-end is December 31.  
- Fair value on **December 31, Year 1** is **$96,000**.  
- On **January 1, Year 2**, Westbrook sells the entire investment for **$95,500** cash.  

**Required:**  
(a) Verify (show computation) that the purchase price is the present value of the bonds at 7%. Record the January 1, Year 1 purchase.  
(b) Prepare the **Year 1 row** of the amortization schedule (cash interest, effective interest revenue, discount amortization, ending amortized cost). Record the December 31, Year 1 interest entry.  
(c) Record the December 31, Year 1 fair value adjusting entry (FVA worksheet using **amortized cost**, not original cost).  
(d) Record the January 1, Year 2 sale (FV-NI adjust, then sale with FVA elimination).  
(e) Compute net income effects in Year 1 and Year 2 from this investment (interest + unrealized G/L only).  

**Answer key:**  

**(a) Purchase price verification and entry**  
Annual cash interest = \(100{,}000 \times 0.05 = \$5{,}000\)  
\[
PV = 100{,}000 \times (1.07)^{-4} + 5{,}000 \times \frac{1-(1.07)^{-4}}{0.07}
\]  
\((1.07)^4 = 1.31079601\)  
\(PV_{\text{principal}} = 100{,}000 / 1.31079601 = 76{,}290.16\) (rounded to cent in combined PV)  
\(PVAF(7\%,4) = [1 - (1.07)^{-4}] / 0.07 = 3.387211\)  
\(PV_{\text{interest}} = 5{,}000 \times 3.387211 = 16{,}936.06\)  
**Combined PV (ROUND_HALF_UP) = $93,225.58** ✓  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in TS—Oakridge Bonds | 93,225.58 | |
|  Cash | | 93,225.58 |

*Dr = Cr = 93,225.58.* (Recorded net of discount; amortized cost basis = $93,225.58.)

**(b) Year 1 amortization + interest entry**  

| Date | Cash interest | Effective interest (7%) | Discount amort. | Amortized cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1/1/Y1 | — | — | — | 93,225.58 |
| 12/31/Y1 | 5,000.00 | \(93{,}225.58 \times 0.07 = \mathbf{6{,}525.79}\) | 1,525.79 | **94,751.37** |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 5,000.00 | |
| Investment in TS—Oakridge Bonds | 1,525.79 | |
|  Interest Revenue | | 6,525.79 |

*Dr = Cr = 6,525.79. Ending amortized cost = 93,225.58 + 1,525.79 = **$94,751.37**.*

**(c) December 31, Year 1 — FV-NI adjustment**  

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Amortized cost (after amort.) | $94,751.37 |
| Fair value | 96,000.00 |
| Required FVA (debit) | 1,248.63 |
| Existing FVA | 0 |
| **Increase to FVA** | **$1,248.63** |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fair Value Adjustment—TS | 1,248.63 | |
|  Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income | | 1,248.63 |

*Dr = Cr = 1,248.63. BS carrying amount = 94,751.37 + 1,248.63 = **$96,000**.*

**(d) January 1, Year 2 — Sale at $95,500**  
Required FVA = \(95{,}500.00 - 94{,}751.37 = \$748.63\) debit  
Existing FVA = \(1{,}248.63\) debit  
**Decrease to FVA** = **$(500.00)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income | 500.00 | |
|  Fair Value Adjustment—TS | | 500.00 |

*Dr = Cr = 500.00.*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 95,500.00 | |
|  Fair Value Adjustment—TS | | 748.63 |
|  Investment in TS—Oakridge Bonds | | 94,751.37 |

*Dr 95,500.00 = Cr 95,500.00.*

**Economic check:** Proceeds $95,500 − amortized cost $94,751.37 = **$748.63** net holding gain.  
Recognized: Y1 unrealized +1,248.63 + Y2 unrealized −500.00 = **+748.63** ✓  

**(e) Income effects**  

| Component | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest revenue | $6,525.79 | $0 |
| Unrealized G/(L)—Income | +1,248.63 | (500.00) |
| **Total pretax NI effect** | **$7,774.42** | **$(500.00)** |

**Key insight:** FV-NI always measures **fair value against current amortized cost** (after effective-interest amortization of discount/premium). Interest still runs through NI via the effective interest method; FVA only bridges amortized cost to fair value. Sale still: **adjust FVA → remove investment at amortized cost and reverse FVA against cash**.

### Q4 — CORE — Fair value option on HTM-classified debt + classification/presentation

**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Fair value option election (FV-NI on otherwise HTM debt) and classification/presentation  
**Scenario:** On **July 1, Year 1**, **Summit Ridge Holdings** purchases **at par** **$200,000** face of **Lakeshore Energy** **4%** bonds that mature on **June 30, Year 6**. Cash interest is paid **semiannually on June 30 and December 31**. Management has **positive intent and ability** to hold the bonds to maturity and therefore **classifies** them as **held-to-maturity (HTM)**. On the **acquisition date**, Summit Ridge **irrevocably elects the fair value option** for this investment under ASC 825 (so subsequent measurement is **FV-NI**, same mechanics as trading securities). Interest for the second half of Year 1 has already been recorded properly.  
On **December 31, Year 1**, fair value of the Lakeshore bonds is **$206,400**. Beginning FVA related to this investment is zero.  

**Required:**  
(a) Record the July 1, Year 1 purchase (ignore any presentation of the HTM vs TS **label**—focus on cost).  
(b) Record the December 31, Year 1 fair value option adjusting entry.  
(c) State the **carrying amount** reported on the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet and whether the unrealized holding gain is reported in **net income** or **OCI**.  
(d) Briefly disclose **two** points management should include regarding the fair value option election (purpose-level, not full ASC laundry list).  
(e) **Contrast:** If Summit Ridge had **not** elected the fair value option and kept pure HTM amortized-cost accounting, what year-end entry (if any) would be made for the $6,400 unrealized gain?  

**Answer key:**  

**(a) July 1, Year 1 — Purchase at par**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Investment in Debt Securities—Lakeshore (HTM, FVO) | 200,000 | |
|  Cash | | 200,000 |

*Dr = Cr = 200,000.*

**(b) December 31, Year 1 — Fair value option (FV-NI)**  
Unrealized holding gain = \(206{,}400 - 200{,}000 = \$6{,}400\)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fair Value Adjustment—Fair Value Option | 6,400 | |
|  Unrealized Gain or Loss—Income | | 6,400 |

*Dr = Cr = 6,400.*

**(c) Presentation**  
- **Carrying amount:** **$206,400** (amortized cost $200,000 + FVA debit $6,400).  
- Unrealized holding gain of **$6,400** is recognized in **net income** (other income), **not** OCI—because the fair value option produces **FV-NI** measurement (same income treatment as trading securities).  

**(d) Disclosure points (examples)**  
1. Management **elected the fair value option** for this specific debt investment **on the acquisition date**; the election is **generally irrevocable** and applied **instrument-by-instrument**.  
2. The investment is subsequently measured at **fair value with changes in fair value recognized in earnings**; disclose reasons for the election and how fair value is determined (level in the fair value hierarchy as applicable).  

**(e) Contrast without FVO (pure HTM)**  
**No year-end fair value entry.** HTM debt remains at amortized cost; unrealized fair value changes are **not** recognized in the accounts (only possible footnote fair-value disclosure). No FVA; no unrealized G/L in NI or OCI.  

**Key insight:** The fair value option is **not** available as a “second coat” on TS (already FV-NI), but it **is** the LO 14-2 path that puts otherwise amortized-cost (or FV-OCI) debt onto **FV-NI**. Accounting entries for FVO debt mirror TS: debit FVA for holding gains, credit Unrealized G/L—**Income**.

### Q5 — MC — Classification of unrealized holding gains (TS / FV-NI vs AFS)

**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Classification—where unrealized holding gains/losses on FV-NI debt securities are reported  
**Question:** **Pinebridge Mutual Fund Corp.** holds two debt portfolios at year-end:  
- Portfolio T: debt securities classified as **trading** (measured at FV-NI).  
- Portfolio A: debt securities classified as **available-for-sale** (measured at FV-OCI).  

During Year 1, Portfolio T’s fair value rose by **$18,000** above amortized cost (no sales). Portfolio A’s fair value rose by **$18,000** above amortized cost (no sales). Ignoring interest and taxes, which statement is **correct**?

- A) Both portfolios report the $18,000 unrealized gain in **net income**.  
- B) Portfolio T reports $18,000 in **net income**; Portfolio A reports $18,000 in **other comprehensive income**.  
- C) Portfolio T reports $18,000 in **OCI**; Portfolio A reports $18,000 in **net income**.  
- D) Neither portfolio recognizes unrealized gains until sale; both wait to record a realized gain in net income.  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Trading / FV-NI debt (LO 14-2) recognizes unrealized holding gains and losses **in earnings** each period. Available-for-sale debt (LO 14-3) recognizes unrealized holding gains and losses **in OCI** until realized (subject to impairment rules). Option A confuses AFS with TS; C reverses the categories; D describes neither US GAAP model for TS/AFS subsequent measurement.

### Q6 — MC — Sale of TS: which entry pattern is correct?

**LO:** LO 14-2  
**Concept:** Disposal under FV-NI—adjust FVA first, then eliminate investment and FVA  
**Question:** **Copperline Trading Desk** holds a TS debt investment with amortized cost **$40,000** and a **credit** Fair Value Adjustment balance of **$1,200** from the prior period-end (carrying amount $38,800). Today the bonds are sold for **$39,500** cash. Copperline adjusts fair value through NI at the sale date before recording the sale. Which description of the **sale-date sequence** is correct?

- A) Debit Cash $39,500; credit Investment $39,500; leave the $1,200 FVA outstanding until year-end.  
- B) First reduce the required FVA to a $500 credit (unrealized gain $700 through NI); then debit Cash $39,500, debit FVA $500, credit Investment $40,000.  
- C) Debit Cash $39,500; debit Loss on Sale $500; credit Investment $40,000; ignore FVA.  
- D) Debit Cash $39,500; debit FVA $1,200; credit Investment $40,000; credit Gain $700—without a separate pre-sale FV adjustment entry.  

**Answer:** **B.**  
Required FVA at sale = \(39{,}500 - 40{,}000 = \$(500)\) credit. Existing FVA = $\(1{,}200\) credit. Increase FVA (reduce the credit) by **$700**: Dr FVA $700 / Cr Unrealized G/L—Income $700. Then sale: Dr Cash $39,500 + Dr FVA $500 = Cr Investment $40,000. No freestanding “Loss on Sale” is used when FV-NI has already updated carrying amount to exit price; FVA must be **cleared** on disposal.

*(Verification: Dr/Cr on adjust $700; Dr/Cr on sale $40,000. Holding loss since last report: economic exit $39,500 vs prior carrying $38,800 → $700 gain in current period, matching the FVA true-up.)*
