# Agent 226 — CORE DEMO — LO 15-2

**Chapter:** 15  
**LO title:** Record customer deposits and advances  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** cash received from customer → credit **Liability—Returnable Deposit**, **Deferred Revenue**, or **Deferred Gift Card Revenue** (not Sales until earned/settled)
- **Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis):** multi-period roll-forward of deferred revenue / gift-card liability (beginning balance, cash advances or sales, revenue recognized or redemptions/breakage, ending balance)
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** recognize revenue for performance obligations satisfied (or proportional gift-card breakage) at year-end
- **Settlement JE:** return of deposit (Dr liability / Cr cash); forfeiture of deposit (Dr liability / Cr sales + COGS if inventory); final period of advance (Dr deferred revenue / Cr revenue); gift-card redemption
- **Returnable deposits:** liability until returned or forfeited; forfeited deposit → revenue; related inventory cost → COGS when forfeited
- **Customer advances:** contract liability (deferred revenue) until goods/services transferred
- **Gift cards (core special case):** sale → deferred gift-card revenue; redemption → sales; breakage under **proportional method** (if criteria met) vs when redemption is **remote**
- Classification: customer deposits/advances/gift-card liabilities are **current** when settlement expected within one year (or operating cycle)

### Q1 — CORE — Multi-period customer advance: deferred revenue schedule (emphasis: subsequent measurement)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Subsequent measurement schedule for deferred revenue on a multi-month customer advance; initial recognition JE; period-end adjusting JE; final-period settlement JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborline Fitness Studios** (calendar-year company) sells prepaid training packages.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Date cash collected | **October 1, Year 1** |
| Cash advance received | **$9,000** |
| Service period | **9 months** evenly (October Year 1 – June Year 2) |
| Recognition pattern | Straight-line monthly (performance obligation satisfied evenly over time) |
| Year-end | **December 31** |
| Final service month ends | **June 30, Year 2** |

No refunds; no significant financing component (core Demo 15-2B path only).

**Required:**  
a. Record the **October 1, Year 1** journal entry for **initial recognition** of the customer advance.  
b. Compute **monthly service revenue** and the amount of revenue earned in **Year 1** vs **Year 2**.  
c. Prepare a complete **subsequent-measurement schedule** of the deferred revenue liability for each of the **9 months** (beginning balance, revenue recognized, ending balance).  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 1** period-end adjusting entry for revenue earned in Year 1 (company recognizes revenue via year-end adjusting entries, not monthly books).  
e. State the **December 31, Year 1** balance-sheet amount and classification of any remaining deferred revenue.  
f. Record the **June 30, Year 2** entry that settles the **final month** of the performance obligation (assume May 31 Year 2 books already recognized revenue through May).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*October 1, Year 1 — Collect customer advance*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 9,000 | |
| Deferred Revenue | | 9,000 |
| *Customer prepayment before performance; contract liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr 9,000 = Cr 9,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Monthly and annual revenue**

Monthly service revenue = \(\$9{,}000 / 9 = \$1{,}000\).

Year 1 months satisfied (Oct–Dec) = \(3 \times \$1{,}000 = \$3{,}000\).

Year 2 months satisfied (Jan–Jun) = \(6 \times \$1{,}000 = \$6{,}000\).

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis) — Deferred Revenue liability**

| Month | Beginning Deferred Revenue | Revenue recognized | Ending Deferred Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Oct Y1 | 9,000 | 1,000 | 8,000 |
| Nov Y1 | 8,000 | 1,000 | 7,000 |
| Dec Y1 | 7,000 | 1,000 | **6,000** |
| Jan Y2 | 6,000 | 1,000 | 5,000 |
| Feb Y2 | 5,000 | 1,000 | 4,000 |
| Mar Y2 | 4,000 | 1,000 | 3,000 |
| Apr Y2 | 3,000 | 1,000 | 2,000 |
| May Y2 | 2,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| Jun Y2 | 1,000 | 1,000 | **0** |
| **Totals** | | **9,000** | |

**Roll-forward check:** Beginning cash advance \(-\) total revenue recognized = \(\$9{,}000 - \$9{,}000 = \$0\) ending liability.  
Year 1 revenue on schedule = \(3 \times \$1{,}000 = \$3{,}000\); ending YE balance = **\$6,000**.

**d. Period-end adjusting entry — December 31, Year 1**

*December 31, Year 1 — Recognize 3 months of training revenue*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Revenue | 3,000 | |
| Service Revenue | | 3,000 |
| *\$1,000 × 3 months (Oct–Dec)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,000 = Cr 3,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Balance sheet, December 31, Year 1**

Remaining deferred revenue = \(\$9{,}000 - \$3{,}000 = \$6{,}000\).  
All remaining service is due within the next year → report **\$6,000 as a current liability** (Deferred Revenue / contract liability).

**f. Settlement — final month of performance (June 30, Year 2)**

After May recognition, liability balance = **\$1,000**.  
June satisfies the last month:

*June 30, Year 2 — Recognize final month of prepaid training*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Revenue | 1,000 | |
| Service Revenue | | 1,000 |
| *Settle remaining contract liability as performance obligation completed* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,000 = Cr 1,000. **Balanced.** Ending deferred revenue = **\$0**.

**Key insight:** A customer **advance** is initially a **liability**, not revenue. The **subsequent measurement schedule** tracks how the deferred revenue balance declines each period as the seller satisfies performance obligations; year-end adjusting entries pull the earned portion into revenue, and the final period **settles** the remaining liability to zero.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Multi-period advance, schedule, YE adj, settlement
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: subsequent measurement schedule for deferred revenue; initial recognition; period-end adjusting JE; final settlement JE  
**Scenario:**  
**Cedar & Pine Landscaping Co.** (calendar year) collects a cash prepayment for seasonal grounds maintenance.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Date cash collected | **August 1, Year 1** |
| Cash advance | **$6,000** |
| Service period | **10 months** evenly (August Year 1 – May Year 2) |
| Recognition | Straight-line monthly |
| Year-end | **December 31** |
| Package fully performed | **May 31, Year 2** |

**Required:**  
a. Record **August 1, Year 1** initial recognition.  
b. Compute monthly revenue; revenue earned in Year 1 and Year 2.  
c. Prepare the **subsequent-measurement schedule** of Deferred Revenue for all **10 months**.  
d. Record **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entry (revenue recognized via YE adjusting entry only).  
e. Compute December 31, Year 1 deferred revenue balance.  
f. Record **May 31, Year 2** entry for the **final month** (assume revenue through April Year 2 already recognized).

| Cash | 6,000 | |
| Deferred Revenue | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr 6,000 = Cr 6,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Monthly and annual amounts**

Monthly revenue = \(\$6{,}000 / 10 = \$600\).

Year 1 (Aug–Dec) = \(5 \times \$600 = \$3{,}000\).  
Year 2 (Jan–May) = \(5 \times \$600 = \$3{,}000\).

**c. Subsequent measurement schedule (emphasis)**

| Month | Beginning Deferred Revenue | Revenue recognized | Ending Deferred Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Aug Y1 | 6,000 | 600 | 5,400 |
| Sep Y1 | 5,400 | 600 | 4,800 |

| Mar Y2 | 1,800 | 600 | 1,200 |
| Apr Y2 | 1,200 | 600 | 600 |
| May Y2 | 600 | 600 | **0** |
| **Totals** | | **6,000** | |

**Roll-forward check:** \(\$6{,}000 - \$6{,}000 = \$0\). YE ending balance after 5 months = **\$3,000**.

**d. December 31, Year 1 adjusting entry**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Revenue | 3,000 | |
| Service Revenue | | 3,000 |
| *\$600 × 5 months (Aug–Dec)* | | |

**Check:** Dr 3,000 = Cr 3,000. **Balanced.**

**e. December 31, Year 1 deferred revenue balance**

\(\$6{,}000 - \$3{,}000 = \$3{,}000\) **current liability**.

Remaining liability after April = **\$600**.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Revenue | 600 | |
| Service Revenue | | 600 |

**Check:** Dr 600 = Cr 600. **Balanced.** Liability = **\$0**.

**g. Life-of-contract reconciliation**

Service Revenue: Year 1 \$3,000 + Year 2 \$3,000 = **\$6,000** = original cash advance.  
All JEs balance; schedule rolls to zero at maturity of the performance period.

**Key insight:** Changing only the **cash amount**, **start date**, and **contract length** changes monthly revenue and the split of earned vs deferred at year-end, but the mechanics are identical: **Dr Cash / Cr Deferred Revenue**, then **Dr Deferred Revenue / Cr Revenue** as obligations are satisfied until settlement.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Returnable deposits: collection, return, forfeiture (settlement)
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of returnable deposits; settlement by cash return; settlement by forfeiture with sales and COGS  
**Scenario:**  
**Summit Peak Equipment Rentals** rents camping and trail equipment. Customers pay a **returnable deposit** that is refunded if gear is returned undamaged. Damaged or unreturned gear causes **forfeiture** of the deposit. Gear is carried in **Inventory** until a related deposit is forfeited.

**June activity (summary):**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Customer deposits collected in June | **$18,000** |
| Deposits returned in cash (gear returned in good condition) | **$15,750** |
| Deposits forfeited (gear damaged or not returned) | **$2,250** |
| Cost of gear related to forfeited deposits | **60%** of the forfeited deposit amount |
| Usable gear recovered on forfeitures | **None** |

**Required:**  
a. Record the **June summary entry** for **collection** of customer deposits.  
b. Record the **June summary entry** for **return** of deposits to customers.  
c. Record the **June entries** for **forfeiture** of deposits (revenue and COGS).  
d. Prepare a short **deposit liability roll-forward** for June (beginning \$0).  
e. Compute the **net cash effect** of all deposit activity in June and the **net income effect** of forfeitures only.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Collection of customer deposits**

*June — Collect returnable deposits*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 18,000 | |
| Liability—Returnable Deposit | | 18,000 |
| *Cash held as refundable deposit; not revenue* | | |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Return of deposits (settlement by refund)**

Deposits returned = **\$15,750**.

*June — Return deposits to customers*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability—Returnable Deposit | 15,750 | |
| Cash | | 15,750 |
| *Settle liability by cash refund when gear returned undamaged* | | |

**Check:** Dr 15,750 = Cr 15,750. **Balanced.**

**c. Forfeiture of deposits (settlement by revenue recognition)**

Forfeited deposits = **\$2,250**.  
COGS = \(0.60 \times \$2{,}250 = \$1{,}350\).

*June — Recognize revenue on deposit forfeiture*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Liability—Returnable Deposit | 2,250 | |
| Sales | | 2,250 |
| *Customer fails to perform; deposit forfeited → revenue* | | |

**Check:** Dr 2,250 = Cr 2,250. **Balanced.**

*June — Recognize COGS on deposit forfeiture*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 1,350 | |
| Inventory | | 1,350 |
| *Remove inventory cost of gear not recovered* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,350 = Cr 1,350. **Balanced.**

**d. Liability roll-forward (June)**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Liability—Returnable Deposit | 0 |
| + Collections | 18,000 |
| − Cash returns | (15,750) |
| − Forfeitures reclassified to Sales | (2,250) |
| **Ending Liability—Returnable Deposit** | **0** |

**Check:** \(\$18{,}000 - \$15{,}750 - \$2{,}250 = \$0\).

**e. Cash and income effects**

Net cash from deposit activity = collections − returns = \(\$18{,}000 - \$15{,}750 = \$2{,}250\) cash retained (equals forfeitures).  
Income effect of forfeitures: Sales \$2,250 − COGS \$1,350 = **\$900** gross profit.

**Key insight:** Returnable deposits start as **liabilities**. **Settlement** is either a cash refund (Dr liability / Cr cash) or **forfeiture** (Dr liability / Cr sales), with inventory cost moved to COGS when the company no longer expects recovery of the asset.

### Q4 — CORE alternate angle — Gift cards: sale, redemption, proportional breakage schedule
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Gift-card deferred liability subsequent measurement schedule; initial sale JE; redemption settlement; period-end proportional breakage adjusting JE; remote alternative  
**Scenario:**  
**Brightline Bistro** (calendar year) sells nonrefundable gift cards. Management can estimate breakage and it is **probable** that a significant reversal of recognized breakage will **not** occur → **proportional method** (core Demo 15-2C).

| Fact | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Gift cards sold for cash during Year 1 | **$12,000** |
| Gift cards redeemed during Year 1 | **$7,200** |
| Estimated portion of sold value that will **never** be redeemed (breakage rate) | **20%** |
| Estimated portion that will eventually be redeemed | **80%** |

Ignore COGS on redemptions.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **Year 1 summary entry** for sale of gift cards.  
b. Record the **Year 1 summary entry** for redemptions.  
c. Compute expected total redemptions, expected total breakage, the Year 1 **redemption rate**, and **proportional breakage revenue**.  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 1** adjusting entry for gift-card breakage under the proportional method.  
e. Prepare a **subsequent-measurement schedule** (liability roll-forward) for Deferred Gift Card Revenue for Year 1.  
f. **Alternative:** Assume instead the company does **not** meet proportional-method criteria and the chance of redeeming the **remaining unredeemed** cards is **remote**. Record the December 31 adjusting entry under that assumption (ignore the proportional entry in part d).

**Answer key:**  

**a. Sale of gift cards**

*During Year 1 — Sale of gift cards*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 12,000 | |
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | | 12,000 |
| *Advance for future goods/services; contract liability* | | |

**Check:** Dr 12,000 = Cr 12,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Redemption settlement**

*During Year 1 — Redemption of gift cards*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 7,200 | |
| Sales | | 7,200 |
| *Performance obligation satisfied on redemption* | | |

**Check:** Dr 7,200 = Cr 7,200. **Balanced.**

**c. Proportional breakage calculations**

Expected total redemptions = \(0.80 \times \$12{,}000 = \$9{,}600\).  
Expected total breakage = \(0.20 \times \$12{,}000 = \$2{,}400\).

\[
\text{Redemption rate} = \frac{\text{Actual redemptions}}{\text{Expected total redemptions}} = \frac{\$7{,}200}{\$9{,}600} = 0.75 = 75\%
\]

\[
\text{Proportional breakage revenue} = \$2{,}400 \times 0.75 = \$1{,}800
\]

**d. Period-end adjusting entry — proportional breakage**

*December 31, Year 1 — Gift card breakage revenue (proportional method)*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 1,800 | |
| Gift Card Breakage Revenue | | 1,800 |
| *Recognize breakage in proportion to pattern of rights exercised* | | |

**Check:** Dr 1,800 = Cr 1,800. **Balanced.**

**e. Subsequent measurement schedule — Deferred Gift Card Revenue**

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning balance | 0 |
| + Gift card sales (cash advances) | 12,000 |
| − Redemptions → Sales | (7,200) |
| − Proportional breakage → Breakage revenue | (1,800) |
| **Ending Deferred Gift Card Revenue** | **3,000** |

**Checks:**  
Unredeemed before breakage entry = \(\$12{,}000 - \$7{,}200 = \$4{,}800\).  
After breakage = \(\$4{,}800 - \$1{,}800 = \$3{,}000\).  
Remaining expected redemptions = \(\$9{,}600 - \$7{,}200 = \$2{,}400\); remaining expected breakage not yet recognized = \(\$2{,}400 - \$1{,}800 = \$600\); \(2{,}400 + 600 = 3{,}000\). ✓

**f. Alternative — redemption remote (not proportional)**

Remaining unredeemed after redemptions only = **\$4,800**. If likelihood of further redemption is **remote**:

*December 31, Year 1 — Breakage when redemption remote*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Deferred Gift Card Revenue | 4,800 | |
| Gift Card Breakage Revenue | | 4,800 |

**Check:** Dr 4,800 = Cr 4,800. **Balanced.** Ending deferred gift-card liability = **\$0**.

**Key insight:** Gift cards are a form of **customer advance**. The **subsequent measurement schedule** reduces the liability for **redemptions** and, when criteria are met, for **proportional breakage** aligned with the redemption pattern—not all at once—unless redemption is remote.

### Q5 — MC — Classification of cash received from customers
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Classify initial credit for returnable deposit vs customer advance vs immediate revenue  
**Question:**  
On March 5, **Riverbend Outfitters** receives **\$800 cash** from a customer. Which situation correctly pairs the facts with the **initial** credit account?

- A) Customer prepays for a guided fishing trip next month → credit **Sales Revenue** for \$800  
- B) Customer pays a refundable damage deposit on rented kayaks (to be returned if undamaged) → credit **Liability—Returnable Deposit** for \$800  
- C) Customer buys a nonrefundable gift card → credit **Sales Revenue** for \$800  
- D) Customer prepays for six months of storage starting today → credit **Liability—Returnable Deposit** for \$800  

**Answer:** **B.** A **returnable / refundable deposit** is credited to **Liability—Returnable Deposit** until returned or forfeited. Customer **advances** and **gift card** sales credit **Deferred Revenue** (or Deferred Gift Card Revenue), not sales, until performance (or breakage rules) allow revenue recognition. Immediate credit to Sales (A, C) is incorrect when performance has not occurred; a prepaid service package (D) is deferred revenue, not a returnable deposit.

### Q6 — MC — Gift card breakage method
**LO:** LO 15-2  
**Concept:** Proportional method vs remote method for gift-card breakage  
**Question:**  
**Northline Books** sold gift cards during the year, can reasonably estimate unused amounts, and it is probable that a significant reversal of recognized breakage will **not** subsequently occur. Customers have already redeemed a portion of cards sold. Which treatment is correct for **unused** gift-card balances at year-end?

- A) Recognize **all** unused balances as Gift Card Breakage Revenue immediately under the proportional method  
- B) Recognize **no** breakage until every card expires by its printed date  
- C) Recognize breakage **in proportion** to the pattern of actual redemptions relative to total **expected** redemptions  
- D) Always wait until the chance of redemption is remote before recognizing any breakage, even when proportional-method criteria are met  

**Answer:** **C.** Under the **proportional method** (core Demo 15-2C), breakage revenue = total estimated breakage × (actual redemptions ÷ total expected redemptions). The company does **not** take all unused balances to revenue at once (A). Expiration-date rules (B) are not the proportional model. Waiting only for remote redemption (D) applies when proportional-method criteria are **not** met.
