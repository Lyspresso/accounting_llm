### Q1 — CORE — HTM discount: purchase, schedule, period-end JE, maturity & early sale
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** Initial recognition JE + effective-interest subsequent measurement (discount)  
**Scenario:** On January 1, Year 1, Meridian Forge Holdings purchased $250,000 face of Cascade Utilities 5% bonds. The bonds mature on December 31, Year 4, and pay cash interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The market yield for similar risk on the purchase date is **6%**. Meridian has the **positive intent and ability** to hold the bonds to maturity and classifies them as **held-to-maturity (HTM)**. Meridian uses the **effective interest method** and records the investment **net** (no separate discount account). Meridian’s fiscal year ends December 31.

**Required:**  
**(a)** Compute the January 1, Year 1 purchase price and record the purchase journal entry.  
**(b)** Prepare the full effective-interest amortization schedule (8 semiannual periods).  
**(c)** Record the June 30, Year 1 interest and discount amortization entry.  
**(d)** Record the December 31, Year 1 period-end interest and amortization entry.  
**(e)** Report the investment on the December 31, Year 1 balance sheet. Fair value of the bonds on that date is $239,000. How (if at all) is fair value used? What note disclosure is appropriate?  
**(f)** Record the maturity settlement on December 31, Year 4 (assume the final interest entry for that date has already been recorded).  
**(g)** **Alternative:** Instead of holding to maturity, Meridian sells the entire investment on **July 1, Year 3** for **$245,000** cash (immediately after the June 30, Year 3 interest entry). Record the sale.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Purchase price and initial recognition JE**  
Semiannual market rate = 6% ÷ 2 = **3%**  
Semiannual cash interest = 5% ÷ 2 × $250,000 = **$6,250**  
Number of periods = 4 × 2 = **8**  

Purchase price = PV of interest annuity + PV of principal at 3%, 8 periods:  
PV = $6,250 × PVA(3%,8) + $250,000 × PV(3%,8) = **$241,225**  
(Discount = $250,000 − $241,225 = **$8,775**)

| Jan 1, Y1 | Investment in HTM Securities—Cascade Bonds | 241,225 | |
| | Cash | | 241,225 |

*(Dr 241,225 = Cr 241,225)*

**(b) Effective interest amortization schedule — Discount**

| Date | Cash (2.5% × Face) | Interest revenue (3% × Beg. AC) | Discount amort. | Amortized cost |
|------|-------------------:|--------------------------------:|----------------:|---------------:|
| Jan 1, Y1 | | | | 241,225 |
| June 30, Y1 | 6,250 | 7,237 | 987 | 242,212 |
| Dec 31, Y1 | 6,250 | 7,266 | 1,016 | 243,228 |
| June 30, Y2 | 6,250 | 7,297 | 1,047 | 244,275 |
| Dec 31, Y2 | 6,250 | 7,328 | 1,078 | 245,353 |
| June 30, Y3 | 6,250 | 7,361 | 1,111 | 246,464 |
| Dec 31, Y3 | 6,250 | 7,394 | 1,144 | 247,608 |
| June 30, Y4 | 6,250 | 7,428 | 1,178 | 248,786 |
| Dec 31, Y4 | 6,250 | 7,464 | 1,214 | 250,000 |
| **Totals** | **50,000** | **58,775** | **8,775** | |

*Check: Interest revenue = cash + discount amort each period; ending AC = face; total discount amort = $8,775.*

**(c) June 30, Year 1**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 6,250 | |

*(Dr 7,237 = Cr 7,237)*

**(d) December 31, Year 1 (period-end adjusting / interest entry)**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 6,250 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Cascade Bonds | 1,016 | |

*(Dr 7,266 = Cr 7,266)*  
Year 1 total interest revenue = $7,237 + $7,266 = **$14,503**.

**(e) Presentation and fair value**  
Balance sheet (assets): **Investment in HTM securities — $243,228** (amortized cost after Dec 31, Y1).  
Fair value is **ignored** for measurement of HTM under amortized cost. No fair-value adjustment is recorded.  
**Note disclosure:** Aggregate fair value $239,000; unrecognized holding **loss** = $243,228 − $239,000 = **$4,228** (disclosed, not recognized in NI or OCI for pure HTM amortized-cost accounting).

**(f) Maturity — December 31, Year 4** (after final interest entry; AC = face)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|

*(Dr 250,000 = Cr 250,000)*

**(g) Sale before maturity — July 1, Year 3**  
Carrying amount after June 30, Y3 interest entry = **$246,464**.  
Cash proceeds $245,000 → **realized loss** = $246,464 − $245,000 = **$1,464** (other income/expense in net income).

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 245,000 | |
| Loss on Sale of Investment | 1,464 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Cascade Bonds | | 246,464 |

*(Dr 246,464 = Cr 246,464)*

**Key insight:** HTM debt is recognized at acquisition cost (here a discount to face). Subsequent measurement uses amortized cost: effective-interest revenue increases the carrying amount each period until face at maturity. Fair-value changes are **not** booked; sale before maturity (if it occurs) produces a realized gain or loss equal to cash minus amortized cost.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — HTM premium (all numbers changed)
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin — premium purchase, effective-interest schedule, amortization JE  
**Scenario:** On January 1, Year 1, Northwind Partners purchased $200,000 face of Summit Rail 7% bonds. The bonds mature December 31, Year 3, and pay interest **semiannually** on June 30 and December 31. The market yield on the purchase date is **5%**. Northwind has positive intent and ability to hold to maturity (HTM) and uses the **effective interest method** (net method). Fiscal year ends December 31.

**Required:**  
**(a)** Compute the purchase price and record the January 1, Year 1 purchase entry.  
**(b)** Prepare the full effective-interest amortization schedule (6 periods).  
**(c)** Record the June 30, Year 1 interest and premium amortization entry.  
**(d)** Record the December 31, Year 1 period-end interest entry.  
**(e)** Record the maturity entry on December 31, Year 3 (final interest already recorded).  
**(f)** **Alternative:** Sell on January 1, Year 2 (immediately after Dec 31, Y1 entries) for **$208,000**. Record the sale.

**Answer key:**

**(a)**  
Semiannual market rate = 5% ÷ 2 = **2.5%**  
Semiannual cash interest = 7% ÷ 2 × $200,000 = **$7,000**  
Periods = 3 × 2 = **6**  
Purchase price = PV at 2.5%, 6 periods of $7,000 annuity + $200,000 principal = **$211,016**  
(Premium = $211,016 − $200,000 = **$11,016**)

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Period-end accrual (interest date ≠ year-end) + presentation
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** Period-end adjusting JE for HTM interest accrual; amortized-cost presentation  
**Scenario:** On October 1, Year 1, Solstice Partners purchased **at par** $90,000 face of Northfield Corp. 8% bonds. Interest is paid **annually each September 30**. The bonds mature on September 30, Year 5. Solstice has positive intent and ability to hold to maturity and classifies the bonds as **HTM**. Solstice’s fiscal year ends **December 31**. Because the bonds were purchased at par, stated rate equals market rate (8%).

**Required:**  
**(a)** Record the purchase on October 1, Year 1.  
**(b)** Record the December 31, Year 1 **period-end adjusting entry** for accrued interest.  
**(c)** Record the September 30, Year 2 cash interest receipt (reverse/settle the accrual and recognize the remaining interest earned in Year 2).  
**(d)** On December 31, Year 1, fair value of the bonds is $91,200. How is the investment reported on the balance sheet, and what disclosure is required?  
**(e)** Record maturity settlement on September 30, Year 5 (assume the final interest entry that day is recorded separately).

**Answer key:**

**(a) October 1, Year 1 — purchase at par**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Investment in HTM Securities—Northfield Bonds | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 90,000 |

*(Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000)*

**(b) December 31, Year 1 — period-end accrual**  
Interest for 3 months (Oct 1–Dec 31): $90,000 × 8% × 3/12 = **$1,800**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Interest Receivable | 1,800 | |
| Interest Revenue | | 1,800 |

*(Dr 1,800 = Cr 1,800)*  
Amortized cost remains **$90,000** (par; no discount/premium).

**(c) September 30, Year 2 — annual cash coupon $7,200**  
Interest earned in Year 2 (Jan 1–Sep 30): $90,000 × 8% × 9/12 = **$5,400**  
Cash = prior receivable $1,800 + Year 2 revenue $5,400 = **$7,200**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 7,200 | |
| Interest Receivable | | 1,800 |
| Interest Revenue | | 5,400 |

*(Dr 7,200 = Cr 7,200)*

**(d) Presentation / disclosure**  
Balance sheet: **Investment in HTM securities $90,000** (amortized cost = par).  
**Interest receivable $1,800** (current asset).  
Fair value **$91,200** is **not** used to remeasure the HTM asset. Notes should disclose fair value and unrecognized holding **gain** of $91,200 − $90,000 = **$1,200**.

**(e) Maturity — September 30, Year 5**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|------:|-------:|
| Cash | 90,000 | |
| Investment in HTM Securities—Northfield Bonds | | 90,000 |

*(Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000)*

**Key insight:** When the fiscal year-end falls **between** interest payment dates, HTM still requires a period-end entry to recognize interest revenue earned. At par there is no amortization, but the same period-split logic applies to discount/premium amortization when rates differ. Measurement remains amortized cost; FV is disclosure only.

### Q4 — MC (classification / measurement method)
**LO:** LO 14-1  
**Concept:** HTM amortized-cost classification criteria and pricing outcome  

**Question 1:** Which condition is **required** for an investor to measure a debt security at **amortized cost** as a held-to-maturity investment?  
- A) The investor plans to sell within 90 days if rates move favorably  
- B) The investor has the **positive intent and ability** to hold the security to maturity  
- C) The security’s fair value equals face value at every reporting date  
- D) The investor elects the fair value option at acquisition  

**Answer:** **B.** ASC 320 restricts amortized-cost HTM classification to debt securities for which management has both positive intent **and** ability to hold to maturity. Uncertainty about holding to maturity precludes amortized cost. Fair value option (D) would move measurement to FV-NI, not pure amortized cost. A is inconsistent with HTM. C is neither required nor typical.

---

**Question 2:** On the acquisition date, a bond’s **market (effective) yield exceeds its stated (coupon) rate**. All else equal, the HTM investment is initially recognized:  
- A) At a **premium** to face  
- B) At a **discount** to face  
- C) Always exactly at face  
- D) At fair value with an unrealized loss in OCI  

**Answer:** **B.** Market rate > stated rate → present value of contractual cash flows < face → **discount**. Premium arises when market < stated. Face (par) only when rates are equal. Pure HTM amortized-cost accounting does not book initial FV-OCI unrealized amounts (D is AFS-style subsequent measurement).
