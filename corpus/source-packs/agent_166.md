# Agent 166 — CORE DEMO — LO 22-3

**Chapter:** 22  
**LO title:** Prepare the investing activities section of the statement of cash flows  
**Critical gap LO:** yes

## Concept list for this pack
- Investing activities report **gross** cash inflows and outflows from long-lived productive assets, nontrading investments, intangibles acquired/sold for cash, and nontrade loans
- **Subsequent measurement schedules / T-accounts** for PPE, Accumulated Depreciation, investments, and notes receivable solve for cash purchases, cost/AD removed on disposal, and principal collections
- Reconstruct **cash proceeds** from PPE sale or insurance settlement from book value ± gain/loss; report full proceeds (not the gain/loss) as investing
- **Initial recognition JEs** for cash purchases of PPE, investments, and loans made
- **Disposal / maturity / settlement JEs** for sale of equipment, sale of investments, insurance recovery, and principal collection
- Only cash paid at (or soon before/after) purchase of productive assets is an investing outflow; seller-financed or stock-financed portion is **noncash** (disclosed)
- Collection of nontrade loan **principal** = investing inflow; interest = operating
- Equity/debt investments **not held specifically for resale** → purchase, sale, and maturity cash flows are investing (gains/losses reconciling items under the indirect method)
- Patent amortization and depreciation are **not** investing cash flows (noncash operating adjustments)

### Q1 — CORE — Harborcrest Fabrication: investing section with full subsequent-measurement schedules
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Prepare cash flows from investing activities — emphasize PPE/AD/investment/notes **rollforward schedules** to plug cash purchases and verify disposals; record JEs; report gross investing flows  
**Scenario:**  
**Harborcrest Fabrication Inc.** is preparing its statement of cash flows for the year ended **December 31, 2025**. Selected comparative balances and additional information follow.

| Account | Dec 31, 2024 | Dec 31, 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | $520,000 | $750,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation—equipment | 175,000 | 210,000 |
| Debt investments (amortized cost; **not** held for trading/resale) | 60,000 | 45,000 |
| Long-term notes receivable (nontrade) | 90,000 | 65,000 |
| Patent, net | 48,000 | 36,000 |

**Additional information for 2025:**
1. Equipment that cost **$90,000** with accumulated depreciation of **$55,000** was sold for **$28,000** cash.  
2. Depreciation expense on equipment was **$90,000**.  
3. All equipment purchases were for **cash** (no notes or stock issued for equipment).  
4. Debt investments were purchased for **$20,000** cash. Investments with a carrying amount of **$35,000** were sold for **$38,000** cash. Ending carrying amount reflects only purchases and sales.  
5. Harborcrest made a new nontrade loan of **$25,000** cash and collected **$50,000** of principal on existing long-term notes receivable. Interest collected is **not** included in the $50,000.  
6. Patent amortization expense was **$12,000**. There were no patent purchases or sales.  
7. There were no other investing activities.

**Required:**  
(a) Prepare the **December 31, 2025** depreciation adjusting entry.  
(b) Reconstruct the **Equipment** and **Accumulated depreciation—equipment** subsequent-measurement schedules (T-accounts). Solve for **cash purchases of equipment**.  
(c) Reconstruct the **Debt investments** and **Long-term notes receivable** rollforward schedules to verify purchases, sales, loans advanced, and principal collections.  
(d) Prepare the journal entry to record the **sale of equipment**.  
(e) Prepare the journal entries for the **purchase of equipment**, **purchase of debt investments**, **sale of debt investments**, **loan advanced**, and **principal collection** on notes receivable.  
(f) Prepare the **Cash flows from investing activities** section of the 2025 statement of cash flows.  
(g) Briefly state how the **equipment sale loss**, **investment sale gain**, and **patent amortization** are treated under the **indirect** operating section (do not prepare the full operating section). Explain why patent amortization does **not** appear in investing.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Period-end adjusting entry — depreciation**  
```
Dr Depreciation Expense ..................... 90,000
   Cr Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment .......... 90,000
```
(Dr = Cr = 90,000)

**(b) PPE subsequent-measurement schedules**

**Equipment**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | 520,000 | |
| Purchases (plug) | **320,000** | |
| Cost of equipment sold | | 90,000 |
| End. bal. | 750,000 | |

Check: \(520{,}000 + 320{,}000 - 90{,}000 = 750{,}000\).

**Accumulated depreciation—equipment**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | | 175,000 |
| Depreciation expense | | 90,000 |
| AD removed on sale | 55,000 | |
| End. bal. | | 210,000 |

Check: \(175{,}000 + 90{,}000 - 55{,}000 = 210{,}000\).

**(c) Investment and notes receivable subsequent-measurement schedules**

**Debt investments**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | 60,000 | |
| Purchases | 20,000 | |
| Cost (carrying amount) sold | | 35,000 |
| End. bal. | 45,000 | |

Check: \(60{,}000 + 20{,}000 - 35{,}000 = 45{,}000\).

**Long-term notes receivable (nontrade)**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | 90,000 | |
| Loans advanced | 25,000 | |
| Principal collections | | 50,000 |
| End. bal. | 65,000 | |

Check: \(90{,}000 + 25{,}000 - 50{,}000 = 65{,}000\).

**Patent, net (for contrast — not an investing cash plug):** \(48{,}000 - 12{,}000\ \text{amortization} = 36{,}000\). No cash investing effect.

**(d) Disposal JE — sale of equipment**  
Book value \(= 90{,}000 - 55{,}000 = 35{,}000\); proceeds \(28{,}000\); **loss = $7,000**.  
```
Dr Cash ..................................... 28,000
Dr Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment ...... 55,000
Dr Loss on Sale of Equipment ................  7,000
   Cr Equipment ...................................... 90,000
```
(Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000)

**(e) Initial recognition / settlement JEs**

Purchase of equipment:  
```
Dr Equipment ............................... 320,000
   Cr Cash ......................................... 320,000
```

Purchase of debt investments:  
```
Dr Debt Investments ........................ 20,000
   Cr Cash .......................................... 20,000
```

Sale of debt investments (carrying amount $35,000; proceeds $38,000; **gain $3,000**):  
```
Dr Cash ..................................... 38,000
   Cr Debt Investments .............................. 35,000
   Cr Gain on Sale of Investments ...................  3,000
```
(Dr 38,000 = Cr 38,000)

Loan advanced (nontrade):  
```
Dr Long-Term Notes Receivable .............. 25,000
   Cr Cash .......................................... 25,000
```

Principal collection:  
```
Dr Cash ..................................... 50,000
   Cr Long-Term Notes Receivable .................... 50,000
```

**(f) Cash flows from investing activities — 2025**

```
Cash flows from investing activities
  Proceeds from sale of equipment ............... $  28,000
  Proceeds from sale of debt investments ........    38,000
  Collection of long-term notes receivable ......    50,000
  Purchase of equipment .........................  (320,000)
  Purchase of debt investments ..................   (20,000)
  Loans to others ...............................   (25,000)
Net cash used by investing activities .......... $(249,000)
```

Inflows \(28{,}000 + 38{,}000 + 50{,}000 = 116{,}000\); outflows \(320{,}000 + 20{,}000 + 25{,}000 = 365{,}000\); net \(116{,}000 - 365{,}000 = \mathbf{\$(249{,}000)}\).

**(g) Indirect operating treatment / non-investing items**  
- **Loss on sale of equipment $7,000:** added back to net income in the operating section (full cash proceeds appear in investing).  
- **Gain on sale of investments $3,000:** deducted from net income in the operating section (full cash proceeds appear in investing).  
- **Patent amortization $12,000:** added back to net income in the operating section as a noncash expense. It is **not** an investing cash flow because no cash changed hands for the patent this period (amortization only reduces the net patent carrying amount).

**Key insight:** Investing activities list **gross cash** amounts, not net book-value changes. Use subsequent-measurement (T-account) schedules for PPE, AD, investments, and notes receivable to solve for purchases and verify sales/collections; report **full cash proceeds** (not the gain or loss) as the investing inflow.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Stonebridge Alloys twin

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Northfield Packaging: insurance settlement, construction, maturity proceeds, partial noncash PPE
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Investing section with insurance recovery, cash construction, investment maturities, land sale, and cash-only portion of mixed PPE acquisition; PPE/AD subsequent-measurement schedules; noncash disclosure  
**Scenario:**  
**Northfield Packaging Co.** provides the following PPE balances and investing-related events for the year ended **December 31, 2025**.

| Account | Jan 1, 2025 | Dec 31, 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | $1,200,000 | $1,620,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation—equipment | 480,000 | 165,000 |

**Events during 2025:**
1. A hurricane completely destroyed factory equipment. Original cost **$650,000**; accumulated depreciation at the date of the storm **$410,000**. Insurance paid Northfield **$220,000** cash in full settlement.  
2. Northfield constructed replacement equipment for **$890,000** cash.  
3. Purchased additional equipment with a cash price of **$180,000**. Payment: **$120,000** cash and **$60,000** of Northfield common stock (fair value equals cash price residual).  
4. Sold vacant land (cost **$95,000**) for **$110,000** cash.  
5. Purchased equity investments (**not** held specifically for resale) for **$55,000** cash.  
6. Received **$40,000** cash from **maturities** of debt investments (principal only; carrying amount equaled proceeds).  
7. Depreciation expense on remaining equipment was **$95,000**.  
8. No other PPE or investing transactions.

**Required:**  
(a) Reconstruct the **Equipment** and **Accumulated depreciation** subsequent-measurement schedules; verify that the ending balances are consistent with the events above.  
(b) Prepare the journal entry to remove the destroyed equipment and record the **insurance settlement**.  
(c) Prepare the **initial recognition** journal entries for (i) cash construction of replacement equipment and (ii) the mixed cash/stock equipment purchase.  
(d) Prepare the land-sale JE, equity investment purchase JE, and debt investment maturity collection JE.  
(e) Prepare the **Cash flows from investing activities** section for 2025.  
(f) Prepare the **noncash investing and financing activities** disclosure related to the equipment purchase.  
(g) Explain why only **$120,000** (not $180,000) of the mixed equipment purchase is an investing cash outflow, and why the **$60,000** stock issuance does not appear as a financing cash inflow.

**Answer key:**

**(a) Subsequent-measurement schedules**

**Equipment**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | 1,200,000 | |
| Construction (cash) | 890,000 | |
| Purchase (cash + stock) | 180,000 | |
| Cost destroyed | | 650,000 |
| End. bal. | 1,620,000 | |

Check: \(1{,}200{,}000 + 890{,}000 + 180{,}000 - 650{,}000 = 1{,}620{,}000\).

**Accumulated depreciation—equipment**

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beg. bal. | | 480,000 |
| Depreciation expense | | 95,000 |
| AD on destroyed equipment | 410,000 | |
| End. bal. | | 165,000 |

Check: \(480{,}000 + 95{,}000 - 410{,}000 = 165{,}000\).

**(b) Insurance settlement / disposal of destroyed equipment**  
Carrying amount \(= 650{,}000 - 410{,}000 = 240{,}000\); cash proceeds \(220{,}000\); **loss = $20,000**.  
```
Dr Cash .................................... 220,000
Dr Accumulated Depreciation—Equipment ..... 410,000
Dr Loss on Insurance Settlement ............. 20,000
   Cr Equipment ..................................... 650,000
```
(Dr 650,000 = Cr 650,000)

**(c) Initial recognition — construction and mixed purchase**  
```
Dr Equipment ............................... 890,000
   Cr Cash ......................................... 890,000

Dr Equipment ............................... 180,000
   Cr Cash ......................................... 120,000
   Cr Common Stock (and/or APIC) ...................  60,000
```
(Construction: Dr = Cr = 890,000; Mixed: Dr 180,000 = Cr 180,000)

**(d) Land sale, investment purchase, maturity collection**  
```
Dr Cash .................................... 110,000
   Cr Land ........................................... 95,000
   Cr Gain on Sale of Land ........................... 15,000

Dr Equity Investments ...................... 55,000
   Cr Cash .......................................... 55,000

Dr Cash ..................................... 40,000
   Cr Debt Investments .............................. 40,000
```
(Land: Dr 110,000 = Cr 110,000; others balance as shown)

**(e) Cash flows from investing activities — 2025**

```
Cash flows from investing activities
  Proceeds from insurance settlement ............ $  220,000
  Proceeds from sale of land ....................    110,000
  Proceeds from maturities of debt investments ..     40,000
  Purchase of equipment (cash portion) .......... (1,010,000)
  Purchase of equity investments ................    (55,000)
Net cash used by investing activities .......... $ (695,000)
```

Cash equipment outflows: construction \(890{,}000\) + cash paid on mixed purchase \(120{,}000\) = **$1,010,000** (may be shown as one combined line or two separate purchase lines; total cash out is the same).

Inflows \(220{,}000 + 110{,}000 + 40{,}000 = 370{,}000\); outflows \(1{,}010{,}000 + 55{,}000 = 1{,}065{,}000\); net \(\mathbf{\$(695{,}000)}\).

**Note:** Insurance proceeds are investing inflows because they compensate for loss of a productive asset (related loss is added back in the operating section under the indirect method). Maturities of debt investments not held for trading are investing inflows (gross, separately from purchases).

**(f) Noncash disclosure**

```
Noncash investing and financing activities
  Purchased equipment by paying cash and issuing common stock:
    Cost of equipment ........................... $180,000
    Cash paid ...................................  120,000
    Common stock issued ......................... $ 60,000
```

**(g) Classification rationale**  
ASC 230 reports as investing outflows only amounts paid **at the time of purchase or soon before or after** for PPE. The **$60,000** stock-financed portion is a noncash exchange (asset acquired by issuing equity). It is disclosed as a noncash investing and financing activity and is **not** reported as an investing cash outflow or a financing cash inflow on the face of the SCF. Including $180,000 as investing would overstate the cash effect of the acquisition.

**Key insight:** Subsequent-measurement schedules prove the asset account rollforward when destruction, construction, and mixed purchases coexist. The investing section remains a **cash** statement: full insurance/land/maturity proceeds in, cash portion of PPE and investment purchases out, and equity- or note-financed acquisition costs **disclosed as noncash**.

### Q4 — MC — Classification of investing cash flows
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Identify which items are correctly included as investing inflows or outflows (gross presentation; principal vs interest)  
**Question:**  
Which of the following is **correctly** reported in the **investing activities** section of the statement of cash flows under U.S. GAAP (ASC 230)?

- A) Depreciation expense of $72,000 is subtracted as an investing cash outflow for use of equipment.  
- B) Purchase of equipment for $150,000 cash and a contemporaneous sale of different equipment for $40,000 cash are netted, showing a single $110,000 investing outflow.  
- C) Cash proceeds of $52,000 from sale of equipment (book value $44,000; gain $8,000) are reported as a **$52,000** investing inflow.  
- D) Principal collection of $18,000 and interest collection of $2,200 on a nontrade note receivable are both reported as investing inflows totaling $20,200.

**Answer:** **C.** Investing activities report the **full cash proceeds** from sale of PPE as an inflow; the gain is not the investing amount (the gain is removed from NI in the operating section under the indirect method).  

- A is wrong: depreciation is a noncash expense adjusted in **operating** (indirect method), not an investing flow.  
- B is wrong: ASC 230 requires **gross** presentation of investing inflows and outflows (purchase and sale reported separately).  
- D is wrong: **interest** collected is an **operating** inflow; only principal collection is investing.

### Q5 — MC — Net cash used by investing (insurance + rebuild + land)
**LO:** LO 22-3  
**Concept:** Compute net cash used by investing activities when insurance proceeds, cash reconstruction, and another investing inflow all occur  
**Question:**  
A flood destroyed a building with original cost **$1,100,000** and carrying amount **$380,000**. Insurance paid the company **$310,000** cash. The company then constructed a replacement building for **$1,400,000** cash and sold surplus land for **$75,000** cash. Ignoring all other transactions, what is **net cash used by investing activities** related to these events?

- A) $1,400,000  
- B) $1,090,000  
- C) $1,015,000  
- D) $1,470,000  

**Answer:** **C. $1,015,000.**  
Investing inflows: insurance proceeds **$310,000** + land sale **$75,000** = **$385,000**.  
Investing outflow: cash paid to construct building **$1,400,000**.  
Net cash used = \(1{,}400{,}000 - 385{,}000 = \mathbf{\$1{,}015{,}000}\).  

(The carrying amount and any insurance loss of $70,000 affect the income statement / operating reconciling items, not the investing cash totals. Choice A ignores both inflows; B ignores the land sale; D incorrectly adds cost to proceeds or confuses carrying amount with cash.)
