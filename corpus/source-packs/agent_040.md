# Agent 40 — CORE DEMO — LO 8-2

**Chapter:** 8  
**LO title:** Account for sales and collections on account including the impact of cash discounts  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- Initial recognition of trade receivables and sales under the **gross method** (AR and sales at invoice/gross)
- Initial recognition under the **net method** (AR and sales at gross less available cash discount)
- Cash collections **within** the discount period (Sales Discount contra under gross; no extra revenue under net)
- Cash collections **after** the discount period (full cash under gross; Sales Discount Forfeited revenue under net)
- Accounts receivable rollforward schedule (subsequent measurement / settlement tracking)
- Period-end adjusting entries for expected cash discounts still open (gross: reduce AR/revenue; net: increase AR/revenue for expected forfeitures)
- Income statement presentation: net sales (gross method) vs sales + sales discounts forfeited (net method)
- Trade vs cash discounts: trade discounts define invoice price only (not recorded separately)

### Q1 — CORE — Gross method: initial sale, partial early collection, late settlement, AR schedule, IS presentation
**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Initial recognition under gross method; settlement with and without cash discount; AR rollforward; net sales presentation  
**Scenario:**  
Cedar Ridge Outfitters transfers control of merchandise to Maple Peak Co. on **September 3**. Gross invoice price is **$48,000**; cost is **$28,800**. Credit terms are **2/10, n/30**. Cedar Ridge accounts for cash discounts using the **gross method**.

- **September 10:** Maple Peak pays **50%** of the gross balance (within the discount period) and takes the available cash discount.
- **September 28:** Maple Peak pays the **remaining** balance in full (after the discount period).

**Required:**  
a. Prepare the September 3 journal entries (sale and cost of goods sold).  
b. Prepare the September 10 collection entry.  
c. Prepare the September 28 collection entry.  
d. Prepare an accounts receivable rollforward for Maple Peak from September 3 through September 28.  
e. Prepare the income statement excerpt for **net sales revenue** related to this customer for September (gross method presentation).

**Answer key:**  

**a. September 3 — initial recognition (gross method)**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 48,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 48,000 |
| *To record credit sale at gross invoice price* | | |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 28,800 | |
| Inventory | | 28,800 |
| *To remove inventory and recognize COGS* | | |

**Check:** Sale JE Dr = Cr = 48,000; COGS JE Dr = Cr = 28,800.

**b. September 10 — partial collection within discount period**  
Gross amount collected against AR = 50% × $48,000 = **$24,000**  
Sales discount = $24,000 × 0.02 = **$480**  
Cash received = $24,000 − $480 = **$23,520**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 23,520 | |
| Sales Discount | 480 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 24,000 |
| *Partial collection within 2/10; Sales Discount is contra-revenue* | | |

**Check:** Dr 23,520 + 480 = Cr 24,000. Balanced.

**c. September 28 — settlement after discount period**  
Remaining AR = $48,000 − $24,000 = **$24,000** (no discount)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 24,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 24,000 |
| *Final collection after discount period; full remaining gross collected* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 24,000.

**d. Accounts receivable rollforward (Maple Peak)**  

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Beginning AR (Sept 3, before sale) | $0 |
| + Credit sale (gross) | 48,000 |
| − Collection allocated to AR (Sept 10) | (24,000) |
| − Collection allocated to AR (Sept 28) | (24,000) |
| **Ending AR** | **$0** |

**Cash total collected:** $23,520 + $24,000 = **$47,520**  
**Sales Discount taken:** **$480**  
**Reconciliation:** Cash + Sales Discount = $47,520 + $480 = $48,000 (original AR).

**e. Income statement presentation — gross method**  

| Income Statement — Excerpt (September) | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales revenue | $48,000 |
| Less: Sales discounts | (480) |
| **Net sales revenue** | **$47,520** |

**Key insight:** Under the gross method, AR and sales start at the full invoice amount. A cash discount is recognized **only if** the customer pays within the discount window (contra-revenue). Payment after the window leaves revenue at the gross amount for that portion. Total cash collected plus discounts taken equals the original receivable.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — Gross method twin with different terms and split collection
**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin of gross-method sale, early discount take, and late settlement  
**Scenario:**  
Brookhaven Supply sells merchandise to Harborline Distributors on **October 5**. Gross invoice price is **$90,000**; cost is **$54,000**. Credit terms are **3/10, n/30**. Brookhaven uses the **gross method**.

- **October 12:** Harborline pays **40%** of the gross balance within the discount period.
- **October 30:** Harborline pays the remaining **60%** after the discount period.

**Required:**  
a. October 5 sale and COGS entries.  
b. October 12 collection entry.  
c. October 30 collection entry.  
d. Compute net sales revenue for this customer for October.  
e. Prove that total cash + sales discounts = original AR.

**Answer key:**  

**a. October 5 — initial recognition**  

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 90,000 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 90,000 |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 54,000 | |
| Inventory | | 54,000 |

**Check:** 90,000 = 90,000; 54,000 = 54,000.

**b. October 12 — 40% within discount period**  
Gross AR relieved = 0.40 × $90,000 = **$36,000**  
Sales discount = $36,000 × 0.03 = **$1,080**  
Cash = $36,000 − $1,080 = **$34,920**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 34,920 | |
| Sales Discount | 1,080 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 36,000 |

**Check:** 34,920 + 1,080 = 36,000.

**c. October 30 — remaining 60% after discount period**  
Remaining AR = 0.60 × $90,000 = **$54,000**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 54,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 54,000 |

**Check:** 54,000 = 54,000.

**d. Net sales**  
Sales revenue $90,000 − Sales discounts $1,080 = **$88,920**

**e. Proof**  
Cash total = $34,920 + $54,000 = **$88,920**  
Cash + Sales Discount = $88,920 + $1,080 = **$90,000** = original AR. Ending AR = **$0**.

**Key insight:** Changing the discount rate (here 3%) and the early-pay fraction scales Sales Discount and cash, but the gross-method pattern is unchanged: record gross at sale; record discount only on amounts paid inside the window.

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Net method cycle, period-end adjusting entries (gross vs net), and presentation
**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Net-method initial recognition and settlement; year-end adjusting for expected discounts; classification of Sales Discount vs Sales Discount Forfeited  
**Scenario:**  

**Part A — Net method sales and collections**  
Pinecrest Merchandising transfers control of goods to Riverbend LLC on **June 1**. Gross invoice price is **$25,000**; cost is **$15,000**. Terms **2/10, n/30**. Pinecrest uses the **net method**.

- **June 8:** Riverbend pays **half** of the **net** receivable (within the discount period).  
- **June 28:** Riverbend pays the **remaining** balance after the discount period (pays the full remaining **gross** share).

**Part B — Period-end adjusting entries (open AR)**  
At **December 31**, two unrelated open trade receivables exist (year-end is Dec 31). Ignore COGS. Discount terms on both are **2/10, n/30**, and the discount period for each still extends into January.

| Customer | Gross amount still open | Management expectation at Dec 31 |
|---|---:|---|
| Solstice Co. | $40,000 | Will take the cash discount on **100%** of this balance |
| Northvale Inc. | $20,000 | Will **forfeit** the cash discount on **100%** of this balance |

1. Assume the company uses the **gross method** for all sales. Record the single combined year-end adjusting entry for expected cash discounts on open receivables (only Solstice is expected to take the discount).  
2. Assume instead the company uses the **net method** for all sales (AR already recorded net of available discount). Record the year-end adjusting entry for expected **forfeitures** (only Northvale is expected to forfeit).

**Part C — Income statement composition**  
Using **only Part A** results, show how total revenue related to Riverbend appears under the **net method** for June.

**Required:**  
a. Part A: all June journal entries under the net method (sale, COGS, both collections).  
b. Part B: both year-end adjusting entries (gross-method version and net-method version).  
c. Part C: net-method revenue presentation for Part A.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Part A — Net method**  

**June 1 — initial recognition at net**  
Net AR/Sales = $25,000 × 0.98 = **$24,500**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 24,500 | |
| Sales Revenue | | 24,500 |
| *Sale recorded at gross less available 2% cash discount* | | |

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of Goods Sold | 15,000 | |
| Inventory | | 15,000 |

**Check:** 24,500 = 24,500; 15,000 = 15,000.

**June 8 — half of net within discount period**  
Cash / AR = 0.50 × $24,500 = **$12,250**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 12,250 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | 12,250 |
| *No Sales Discount account under net when customer pays on time* | | |

**Check:** 12,250 = 12,250.

**June 28 — remaining half after discount period**  
Remaining net AR = **$12,250**  
Gross remaining half = 0.50 × $25,000 = **$12,500**  
Sales Discount Forfeited = $12,500 − $12,250 = **$250** (or $12,500 × 0.02)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 12,500 | |
| Sales Discount Forfeited | | 250 |
| Accounts Receivable | | 12,250 |
| *Late payment: cash at gross remaining share; forfeited discount is other revenue* | | |

**Check:** Dr 12,500 = Cr 250 + 12,250. Balanced. Ending AR = **$0**.

**Total cash:** $12,250 + $12,500 = **$24,750**  
**Total revenue (sales + forfeited):** $24,500 + $250 = **$24,750**

**b. Part B — period-end adjusting entries**  

**1. Gross method — reduce AR/revenue for expected discounts still available**  
Expected discount to be taken = $40,000 (Solstice) × 0.02 = **$800**  
(Northvale: $0 expected take → no reduction)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales Discount | 800 | |
| Accounts Receivable (or Allowance for Sales Discounts) | | 800 |
| *YE adjust: reduce revenue and AR for cash discounts expected on open gross AR* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 800.

**2. Net method — increase AR/revenue for expected forfeitures**  
Expected forfeiture = $20,000 (Northvale) × 0.02 = **$400**  
(Solstice: expected to take discount → already correctly at net; no forfeit accrual)

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts Receivable | 400 | |
| Sales Discount Forfeited | | 400 |
| *YE adjust: increase AR and revenue for discounts expected to be forfeited next period* | | |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 400.

**c. Part C — Net method income statement excerpt (Part A only)**  

| Income Statement — Excerpt (June) | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales revenue | $24,500 |
| Sales discounts forfeited | 250 |
| **Total revenue** | **$24,750** |

*(Sales discounts forfeited is typically reported in other revenues/expenses, not as a reduction of sales.)*

**Key insight:** Net method books the receivable **as if the discount will be taken**. Early payment simply clears AR for cash. Late payment records **Sales Discount Forfeited**. At year-end, gross method **pulls down** open AR/revenue for expected takes; net method **pulls up** AR/revenue for expected forfeits. Both methods ultimately report the same total cash-based consideration when outcomes are known.

### Q4 — MC — Classification / method presentation (2 items)
**LO:** LO 8-2  
**Concept:** Classification of cash-discount accounts and method differences  

**Question 1:**  
Under the **gross method**, when a customer pays within the cash discount period, the debit to **Sales Discount** is best described as:  
- A) An operating expense  
- B) A contra-revenue account that reduces sales to net sales  
- C) Other revenue, similar to interest income  
- D) A direct reduction of inventory  

**Answer:** B. Sales Discount is a contra account to sales; net sales = sales revenue − sales discounts. It is not an operating expense and is not other revenue (that role belongs to **Sales Discount Forfeited** under the **net** method).

**Question 2:**  
On March 1, a company records a $10,000 credit sale, terms 2/10, n/30, under the **net method**. If the customer pays in full on March 25 (after the discount period), which statement is correct for the **March 25** entry only?  
- A) Debit Cash $9,800; credit Accounts Receivable $9,800  
- B) Debit Cash $10,000; credit Accounts Receivable $10,000  
- C) Debit Cash $10,000; credit Sales Discount Forfeited $200; credit Accounts Receivable $9,800  
- D) Debit Cash $9,800; debit Sales Discount $200; credit Accounts Receivable $10,000  

**Answer:** C. Net method initially records AR at $9,800. After the discount period, the customer remits the full $10,000; the $200 difference is **Sales Discount Forfeited** (revenue). Option D is the **gross**-method early-pay pattern (wrong method and wrong timing). Option A would be correct only if paid **within** the discount period.
