# Agent 89 — CORE DEMO — LO 13-4

**Chapter:** 13  
**LO title:** Account for change in estimate in finite life intangible assets  
**Critical gap LO:** no

## Concept list for this pack
- **Initial recognition JE:** finite-life intangible (patent / license / customer list) at cash-equivalent cost
- **Subsequent measurement schedule:** straight-line amortization of cost (less residual) over estimated useful life; credit often direct to the intangible asset
- **Change in estimate (prospective):** revise useful life and/or residual value; do **not** restate prior periods or adjust beginning retained earnings
- New periodic amortization = **(carrying amount at revision date − revised residual) ÷ revised remaining useful life**
- **Change in amortization method** = change in accounting estimate **effected by** a change in accounting principle → also **prospective**
- **Period-end adjusting JE:** amortization expense after the revision
- **Disposal JE:** update amortization to disposal date; remove carrying amount; gain or loss = proceeds − carrying amount
- Classification / presentation: prospective treatment disclosure concept; amortization expense in income from continuing operations; carrying amount on the balance sheet (net)

### Q1 — CORE — Patent purchase, amortization, shortened life, disposal (emphasis: initial recognition)
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of finite-life patent; multi-year amortization schedule; prospective change in useful life; period-end amortization after revision; disposal with loss  
**Scenario:**  
**CedarWave Analytics**, a calendar-year company, acquires a finite-life patent on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase price of patent | **$72,000** |
| Original estimated useful life | **8 years** |
| Original residual value | **$0** |
| Amortization method | Straight-line; company credits **Patent** directly |
| Revision date | **January 1, Year 5** (after four full years of amortization) |
| Revised total useful life from acquisition | **6 years** (so **2 years** remain at the revision date) |
| Revised residual value | Still **$0** |
| Disposal | **January 1, Year 6**, sold for **$16,500** cash (after Year-5 amortization has been recorded) |

No impairment indicators arise at any date.

**Required:**  
a. Record the **January 1, Year 1** journal entry for **initial recognition** of the patent.  
b. Compute annual amortization for Years 1–4 and the **carrying amount on January 1, Year 5** (immediately before the estimate revision).  
c. Prepare a **subsequent-measurement amortization schedule** for Years 1–5 showing beginning carrying amount, amortization expense, and ending carrying amount (apply the revised estimate beginning Year 5).  
d. Record the **December 31, Year 5** period-end adjusting entry for amortization.  
e. Record the **January 1, Year 6** disposal entry.  
f. In one or two sentences, explain why Years 1–4 amortization is **not** restated when the useful life is revised.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition (emphasis angle)**

*January 1, Year 1 — Purchase patent*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patent | 72,000 | |
| Cash | | 72,000 |
| *Record finite-life patent at cash-equivalent cost* | | |

**Check:** Dr 72,000 = Cr 72,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Pre-revision amortization and carrying amount**

Original annual amortization = \(\$72{,}000 / 8 = \$9{,}000\) per year.

Accumulated amortization, Years 1–4 = \(4 \times \$9{,}000 = \$36{,}000\).

Carrying amount, January 1, Year 5 = \(\$72{,}000 - \$36{,}000 = \$36{,}000\).

**c. Amortization schedule (prospective revision at start of Year 5)**

At revision: remaining useful life = \(6 - 4 = 2\) years; residual = $0.  
Revised annual amortization (Years 5–6) = \(\$36{,}000 / 2 = \$18{,}000\).

| Year | Beginning CA | Amortization expense | Ending CA | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 72,000 | 9,000 | 63,000 | Original estimate |
| 2 | 63,000 | 9,000 | 54,000 | Original estimate |
| 3 | 54,000 | 9,000 | 45,000 | Original estimate |
| 4 | 45,000 | 9,000 | 36,000 | Original estimate |
| 5 | 36,000 | **18,000** | **18,000** | **Revised life (prospective)** |
| **Total through Y5** | | **54,000** | | |

**Roll-forward check:** Cost − total amort through Y5 = \$72,000 − \$54,000 = **\$18,000** ending CA.

**d. Period-end adjusting entry after change in estimate**

*December 31, Year 5 — Amortization under revised remaining life*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 18,000 | |
| Patent | | 18,000 |

**Check:** Dr 18,000 = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**

**e. Disposal**

Carrying amount on January 1, Year 6 = **\$18,000**.  
Cash proceeds = **\$16,500**.  
Loss on disposal = \$18,000 − \$16,500 = **\$1,500**.

*January 1, Year 6 — Sell patent*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 16,500 | |
| Loss on Sale of Patent | 1,500 | |
| Patent | | 18,000 |

**Check:** Dr 16,500 + 1,500 = Cr 18,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Prospective treatment**  
A change in estimated useful life of a finite-life intangible is a **change in accounting estimate**. Prior periods are **not** restated and beginning retained earnings is **not** adjusted; the remaining carrying amount is amortized over the **revised remaining** useful life only.

**Key insight:** At **initial recognition**, capitalize the patent at cost. When useful life is later shortened, leave historical amortization alone and spread the **current carrying amount** over the **new remaining life**—then update amortization through disposal before recognizing gain or loss.

### Q2 — CORE number variant — License purchase, shortened life, disposal twin
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Number-variant twin: initial recognition of finite-life license; amortization schedule; prospective useful-life revision; period-end JE; disposal with gain (vs Q1 loss)  
**Scenario:**  
**NovaShell Robotics** (calendar year) purchases an exclusive technology **license** (finite-life intangible) on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash purchase price | **$96,000** |
| Original estimated useful life | **8 years** |
| Original residual value | **$0** |
| Amortization | Straight-line; credit **License** directly |
| Revision date | **January 1, Year 5** (after four full years) |
| Revised total useful life from acquisition | **6 years** (**2 years** remaining) |
| Revised residual | Still **$0** |
| Disposal | **January 1, Year 6**, sold for **$26,000** cash (Year-5 amortization already recorded) |

**Required:**  
a. Record January 1, Year 1 **initial recognition**.  
b. Compute carrying amount on January 1, Year 5 before the revision.  
c. Complete amortization schedule Years 1–5 (apply revision from Year 5).  
d. Record December 31, Year 5 amortization.  
e. Record January 1, Year 6 disposal.  
f. Compute **total amortization expense** recognized over the holding period (Years 1–5) and reconcile to cost − disposal carrying amount removed.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| License | 96,000 | |
| Cash | | 96,000 |

**Check:** Dr 96,000 = Cr 96,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Carrying amount at revision date**

Original annual amort = \(\$96{,}000 / 8 = \$12{,}000\).  
Accumulated amort (4 years) = \(4 \times \$12{,}000 = \$48{,}000\).  
CA, January 1, Year 5 = \(\$96{,}000 - \$48{,}000 = \$48{,}000\).

**c. Schedule**

Revised remaining life = 2 years; revised annual amort = \(\$48{,}000 / 2 = \$24{,}000\).

| Year | Beginning CA | Amortization expense | Ending CA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 96,000 | 12,000 | 84,000 |
| 2 | 84,000 | 12,000 | 72,000 |
| 3 | 72,000 | 12,000 | 60,000 |
| 4 | 60,000 | 12,000 | 48,000 |
| 5 | 48,000 | **24,000** | **24,000** |
| **Total Y1–Y5** | | **72,000** | |

**Check:** \$96,000 − \$72,000 = **\$24,000** ending CA.

**d. December 31, Year 5**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 24,000 | |
| License | | 24,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 24,000.

**e. Disposal with gain**

CA = **\$24,000**; proceeds = **\$26,000**.  
Gain on disposal = \$26,000 − \$24,000 = **\$2,000**.

*January 1, Year 6 — Sell license*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 26,000 | |
| License | | 24,000 |
| Gain on Sale of License | | 2,000 |

**Check:** Dr 26,000 = Cr 24,000 + 2,000. **Balanced.**

**f. Holding-period reconciliation**  
Total amortization expense Years 1–5 = **\$72,000**.  
Cost − CA removed at disposal = \$96,000 − \$24,000 = **\$72,000**. Matches.

**Key insight:** Same mechanics as Q1 with **all numbers changed**. Shortening total useful life **increases** post-revision periodic amortization; cash proceeds **above** updated carrying amount produce a **gain** (Q1’s lower proceeds produced a **loss**).

### Q3 — CORE alternate angle — Dual estimate revision (life + residual), mid-year disposal, presentation
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Initial recognition of customer list; dual prospective revision of useful life and residual; period-end amortization; mid-year disposal; classification/presentation of estimate change  
**Scenario:**  
**LumenForge Media** (calendar year) acquires a finite-life **customer list** on **January 1, Year 1**.

| Fact | Amount / term |
|---|---|
| Cash cost of customer list | **$90,000** |
| Original useful life | **9 years** |
| Original residual value | **$0** |
| Amortization | Straight-line; credit **Customer List** directly |
| **Revision (January 1, Year 4)** after three full years | Total useful life revised to **12 years** from acquisition (**9 years** remaining); residual revised to **$6,000** |
| Disposal | **July 1, Year 6** sold for **$48,000** cash |

Amortization is recorded annually on December 31, except Year 6 requires **half-year** amortization through the July 1 disposal date.

**Required:**  
a. Record January 1, Year 1 initial recognition.  
b. Compute carrying amount on January 1, Year 4 before revision; compute **revised annual amortization** beginning Year 4.  
c. Record December 31, Year 4 period-end amortization.  
d. Compute carrying amount immediately **before** the July 1, Year 6 sale (after partial Year-6 amortization), and record (1) the July 1 amortization update and (2) the disposal.  
e. **Classification / presentation:** State (i) whether the dual revision is retrospective or prospective, (ii) whether beginning retained earnings is adjusted at the revision date, and (iii) where amortization expense appears in the income statement.

**Answer key:**  

**a. Initial recognition**

*January 1, Year 1*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Customer List | 90,000 | |
| Cash | | 90,000 |

**Check:** Dr 90,000 = Cr 90,000. **Balanced.**

**b. Revision math (dual: life + residual)**

Original annual amort = \(\$90{,}000 / 9 = \$10{,}000\).  
Accumulated amort, Years 1–3 = \(3 \times \$10{,}000 = \$30{,}000\).  
CA, January 1, Year 4 = \(\$90{,}000 - \$30{,}000 = \$60{,}000\).

Revised remaining life = \(12 - 3 = 9\) years.  
Revised residual = **\$6,000**.  
Revised annual amortization = \((\$60{,}000 - \$6{,}000) / 9 = \$54{,}000 / 9 = \$6{,}000\).

**c. December 31, Year 4**

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 6,000 | |
| Customer List | | 6,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 6,000.

CA, December 31, Year 4 = \$60,000 − \$6,000 = **\$54,000**.  
CA, December 31, Year 5 (after another \$6,000) = **\$48,000**.

**d. Mid-year disposal, Year 6**

Partial Year-6 amortization (½ year) = \(\$6{,}000 \times 6/12 = \$3{,}000\).

*July 1, Year 6 — Update amortization to disposal date*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amortization Expense | 3,000 | |
| Customer List | | 3,000 |

**Check:** Dr = Cr = 3,000.

Carrying amount at sale = \$48,000 − \$3,000 = **\$45,000**.  
Proceeds = **\$48,000**.  
Gain = \$48,000 − \$45,000 = **\$3,000**.

*July 1, Year 6 — Sell customer list*

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 48,000 | |
| Customer List | | 45,000 |
| Gain on Sale of Customer List | | 3,000 |

**Check:** Dr 48,000 = Cr 45,000 + 3,000. **Balanced.**

**Post-revision schedule excerpt (through disposal)**

| Period | Beginning CA | Amortization | Ending CA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan 1 Y4 (post-revision base) | 60,000 | — | 60,000 |
| Year 4 | 60,000 | 6,000 | 54,000 |
| Year 5 | 54,000 | 6,000 | 48,000 |
| Jan 1–Jun 30 Y6 | 48,000 | 3,000 | **45,000** (sold) |

**e. Classification / presentation**  
(i) **Prospective** only (current and remaining periods).  
(ii) **No** beginning retained earnings adjustment at the revision date.  
(iii) Amortization expense is reported in **income from continuing operations** (typically operating expense); the gain on sale is also a component of income from continuing operations.

**Key insight:** When **both** remaining life and residual change, recompute only once at the revision date: **(CA − new residual) ÷ new remaining life**. Lengthening life and/or raising residual **lowers** future amortization; mid-period disposal still requires amortization **through the sale date**.

### Q4 — MC — Prospective treatment of useful-life revision
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Classification of accounting treatment for change in estimated useful life of a finite-life intangible  
**Question:**  
On January 1, Year 1, a company purchased a patent for \$40,000 and amortized it straight-line over 10 years with zero residual value. On January 1, Year 6, management revises the patent’s **total** useful life to 8 years from acquisition (3 years remaining). Which statement is correct?

- A) Restate Years 1–5 amortization as if the 8-year life had always been used, with an offset to beginning retained earnings.  
- B) Leave Years 1–5 unchanged; amortize the January 1, Year 6 carrying amount of \$20,000 over the remaining 3 years (\$6,667 per year, nearest dollar).  
- C) Immediately expense the entire remaining carrying amount of \$20,000 as an impairment loss because the life was shortened.  
- D) Credit patent and debit retained earnings for the cumulative difference between old and new amortization for Years 1–5 only (no Year-6 amortization until year-end).

**Answer:** **B.**  
A change in estimated useful life is a **change in accounting estimate** applied **prospectively**. CA at revision = \$40,000 − 5 × (\$40,000/10) = \$20,000; new amort ≈ \$20,000 / 3. Prior periods are not restated (rejects A and D). Shortening life alone is not an automatic impairment write-off (rejects C).

### Q5 — MC — Change in amortization method
**LO:** LO 13-4  
**Concept:** Change in amortization method as estimate effected by principle (prospective)  
**Question:**  
A company has amortized a finite-life franchise license using the straight-line method. On January 1 of the current year it switches to an accelerated method that better reflects the pattern of economic benefits for the **remaining** life. Under U.S. GAAP, how is this change treated?

- A) Retrospective restatement of all prior periods presented.  
- B) Prospective application from the carrying amount at the change date (change in estimate effected by a change in accounting principle).  
- C) Cumulative catch-up through current-period amortization expense equal to the entire difference for all prior years.  
- D) Cumulative catch-up through beginning retained earnings, with no effect on current amortization.

**Answer:** **B.**  
A change in amortization (or depreciation) method is a **change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle** and is applied **prospectively**—same orientation as a pure life/residual revision. No full retrospective restatement and no RE catch-up for prior years’ amortization.
